## BLOCK 3 — RESEARCH NOTES

### What I could and could not reach

**Reached and heavily weighted.** `spottingmode.com/wro` was by far the most productive source and carried the whole spine of this package. Its TLS does fail modern OpenSSL, and `curl --ciphers "DEFAULT:@SECLEVEL=0"` was required throughout. It holds **113 Iranian locations and 150 linked airframes**, each with per-location coordinates, construction numbers, dated "last update" fields, and status codes (`pre`, `std`, `dum`, `dlt`, `i/a`) that let me separate exhibits from stored hulks mechanically rather than by guesswork. Crucially it also distinguishes an *asserted identity* (serial in the registration field) from an *observed marking* (registration `...` plus a remark "As 3-xxxx"), which is exactly the distinction the brief demands and which drove most of my blank tail numbers.

**OpenStreetMap via Overpass.** `overpass-api.de` and `overpass.kumi.systems` both failed repeatedly through the agent proxy (`ws_closed_mid_exchange`); `overpass.private.coffee` worked and is what I used, though it 504'd on `around:` queries and only reliably served bbox queries. Iranian OSM coverage is good and Persian-language names are rich. Two sweeps were run: a tag sweep for `historic=aircraft` / `memorial=aircraft` / `museum=aviation`, and a Persian/English name sweep. The name sweep is extremely noisy in Persian — **میگو** (shrimp) collides with **میگ** (MiG) and **آژانس هواپیمایی** (travel agency) with **هواپیما** (aircraft), so roughly 95% of ~200 hits were shrimp farms and ticket offices.

**Esri World Imagery.** The `identify` endpoint returned per-tile acquisition dates reliably and was my main currency instrument. The `export` endpoint let me actually **look at** sites, which mattered more than the dates. It fails with `Error: bytes` (HTTP 500) at high zoom outside metropolitan areas — a cache limit, not a transient — so provincial gate guards had to be viewed at roughly half the resolution of the Tehran sites, which is usually too coarse to identify a fighter-sized airframe.

**Could not reach.** Persian-language news and municipal sources were the biggest loss. `eghtesadonline.com` was rejected by the fetch proxy (403); `aftabnews.ir`, `tehranpress.com` and `safarbazi.com` all failed robots.txt resolution; `tehranica.info` reset the connection. Wikimedia Commons' API returned 429 and its category pages are cache-only and unfetchable. Persian Wikipedia's article on the Tehran aviation exhibition was likewise cache-only. **No Iranian museum's own website was reachable** — I have `iranhdm.ir` and `sadmu.ir` only as URLs recorded by third parties, never fetched. This means opening hours, admission, and current exhibit lists for every Iranian museum here are unverified, and provincial Sacred Defence branch holdings are largely unknown to me.

### Corrections, with evidence

**The brief's central premise about the anchor collection is wrong, and this is the most important finding.** The brief describes "The Iranian Air Force Museum / موزه هوانوردی, Tehran (Mehrabad, Azadi/Ekbatan area)" as holding F-4, F-5, F-14, MiG-21 and Su-22. Two distinct institutions have been conflated:

- **Tehran Aerospace Exhibition Center** (نمایشگاه هوانوردی), at 35.71311/51.29311 by Ekbatan north of Mehrabad, is a **civil and utility collection** — 707, 727s, 737s, 747F, two L-1011 TriStars, Fokkers, Aero and Rockwell Commanders, Pipers, Cessnas — with exactly **one** jet fighter, F-86F 3-118. Its roster in spottingmode, in aviationmuseum.eu's independent listing, and in Atlas Obscura's description all agree, and the October 2025 satellite view shows an airliner park. There is **no F-14, F-4, MiG-21 or Su-22 here.**
- **موزه نیروی هوایی ارتش**, at 35.70455/51.48042 in the Niruye Havayi district of east Tehran beside IRIAF Headquarters, is the military museum. OSM names the feature outright; spottingmode carries it as an unnamed "Tehran, various" location with 11 airframes and a contributor comment "Location is Iran AF HQ, not public." November 2025 imagery shows an unmistakable display park: ~10-12 airframes laid out around a circular central pavilion. This is where the F-84Gs, RT-33s, Il-14, Tiger Moth and the captured Iraqi Su-22M are.

I found **no evidence of an F-14 Tomcat on display anywhere in Iran.** Given Iran still operates the type, every F-14 visible in Iran should be presumed a serving aircraft until proven otherwise.

**No MiG-23, no Mirage F1 on display.** Wikipedia's list of displayed MiG-23s contains no Iranian entry at all. Despite the brief's expectation, the captured-Iraqi display category resolves to just three airframes: Su-22M 22635 and Mi-25 3128 (both firm serials) and Su-22UM3K 22533 (fuselage only).

**The MiG-21U's identity is genuinely contested.** Iranian state media (Tehran Times, 1 July 2025) describes the Sacred Defence museum's MiG-21 as "captured from Iraqi forces." spottingmode records the same airframe as c/n 664617, an **ex-East German** MiG-21U-600 numbered 288, with the remark "In Iranian AF c/s, no markings." A construction number is much harder to fabricate than a museum caption. I left the tail number blank and put both claims in the description rather than pick a side.

**Serial 3-7150 is worn by three different F-5s** — at Qazvin (Abbas Babaei's mausoleum), Takestan, and Damghan. The source itself flags the Qazvin/Takestan pair as sharing a number. I blanked all three tail numbers, including Damghan's, even though the database carries that one as an identity: with a known three-way collision, asserting it for any single airframe would be the kind of permanent damage the brief warns against. Likewise **AH-1J 2-4173** appears both at Chahar Zebar (as identity) and in Tehran (as observed marking) — I kept it only on the former.

**Construction number 566 is duplicated** in the anchor collection between DHC-2 Beaver 6-9701 and "FH-227B 6-9704," and serial 6-9704 also belongs to the Beaver at Sa'dabad (c/n 558). The FH-227 record is corrupt, so I recorded the aircraft with a blank tail number. Similarly **c/n 12680** appears against both C-47A EP-TWB and A-26B N956R; I flagged it rather than silently picking one.

**Khorramshahr Holy Defence Museum has no aircraft.** Its own attraction listing describes tanks, burned vehicles and trenches only. That branch is a genuine negative result, not an omission.

### Display versus stored — what I rejected, by name

The status codes made this unusually clean. Excluded as stored, dumped, deleted or instructional:

- **Tu-154Ms EP-MBQ, EP-MBT, EP-MCT and Il-76TD 15-2285** — parked at the Tehran Aerospace Exhibition Center coordinates but coded `std`, not `pre`. They are stored airliners at the museum's edge, not exhibits. **EP-EKD and EP-EKE** are marked "Removed" and EP-EKD explicitly scrapped.
- **Isfahan 3x T-33 at 32.77133/51.89259** — coded `std` despite an October 2025 update. A stored trio at TFB 8, not a display.
- **Saha University, Mehrabad** (B707 5-8315, Tu-154 EP-CPO, Cessna, HH-43) and the **Civil Aviation Technology College** (Aero Commanders EP-AGT, EP-AHT, EP-FSS) — all coded `i/a`, instructional airframes in college hangars. This is precisely the distinction the brief asked me to be careful about, and both fail the public-presentation test.
- **Samaparan Aviation Training Center, west Tehran** — U-17 6-4025 is coded `pre` but the site is a working training centre with an `i/a` An-2 alongside. Left out as too ambiguous to call a display.
- **Mehrabad and Tehran-Ahmadabad storage** — EP-IAG, EP-IAM, EP-MOP, EK-30018/30068/30098, F-OJHI, EP-IBL/IBM/IBN, EP-FQL, EP-MHF and the rest. Classic stored/dumped airliner populations, exactly the material the brief warned circulates in lists.
- **Kerman, Kish and Mashhad airliner dumps** (EP-MHE, F-WQAJ, three A310s, the Fokker 50 group EP-FQA/FQC/LCG, LZ-EFA, LZ-FDB, LZ-LDD, TC-SGC, UR-BXL, UR-CHZ, EP-TBA, EP-ASD, EP-ZAB, EP-MCR), **Shiraz Il-76s** 5-8201/8202/8204/8207, **Shiraz C-130s and Mehrabad C-130Es** 5-8528/8538/8548, **Zahedan Fokker 50s** EP-TFN/TFT, **Karaj** EK-74711 and EP-TPL, **Tehran B747s** EP-MNB/MNC. All `std`, `dum` or `dlt`.
- **Yazd An-74 15-2254** — OSM tags a `historic=aircraft` node at 31.90661/54.28096, but spottingmode codes the airframe `dlt` (deleted). The OSM node is probably stale. Excluded, flagged below as an open question.
- **Pioneer 2 EP-AHD** at the IRIAF Museum — coded `std` with the remark "wreck." Excluded even though it sits inside a museum, because it is a stored hulk rather than an exhibit.
- **Kermanshah city museum UH-1** — the source says outright "not sure this is it, picture not clear enough." No row; a lead only.
- **RH-53D 158686** at the Sacred Defence museum — potentially the single most historically significant airframe in Iran, an **Operation Eagle Claw** Sea Stallion abandoned at Desert One in 1980. The source carries it with the caveat "Need confirmation this one is really here," and Tehran Times' 2025 walkthrough does not mention it. Too important to record on a flagged-doubtful entry; it is an open question, not a row.

### Access types

Judged by how a member of the public actually gets in. `public` for the Sacred Defence and Sa'dabad museums (both ticketed public institutions), the Tehran Aerospace Exhibition Center (Atlas Obscura records 8am-3pm Saturday-Wednesday, 5,000 rial, guide assigned), and for everything on a roundabout, boulevard, cemetery or hotel forecourt. `restricted` for airframes inside air-base perimeters or base housing towns (Doshan Tappeh, Nojeh, both Isfahan TFB 8 sites, Bandar Abbas airport housing, Manzariyeh, Shiraz), military university campuses (Sattari, AJA Command and Staff), and the IRIAF Museum, where the only evidence I have is a contributor's flat statement that it is not public — I did not assume the OSM museum tag implies access. `appointment` for Pardis Technology Park, a working business campus. Bushehr's F-86 at the Parvaz Hotel is marked public and is an unusually accessible Iranian military airframe.

### Coordinate and imagery provenance

Every coordinate comes from **spottingmode's per-location field**, except the Tehran Aerospace Exhibition Center, where I used the OSM `amenity=exhibition_centre` node named نمایشگاه هوانوردی (35.71311/51.29311); spottingmode's 35.71470/51.29372 and Atlas Obscura's 35.713423/51.293791 agree within ~200 m, all inside the display area. City and province for every site were established by **Nominatim reverse geocoding**, which also produced several substantive identifications rather than just addresses: Dezful's F-5 stands in **میدان نیروی هوایی** (Air Force Square); Qarchak's in **میدان شهید ستاری**; Pishva's in **میدان شهید اردستانی**; Qazvin's F-5 at **آرامگاه شهید خلبان عباس بابایی**, the mausoleum of the IRIAF pilot killed in 1987; the Tehran F-5E at **Behesht-e Zahra**; and the two "Tehran" fighter sites at 35.691 resolved to **دانشگاه جنگ**, the Army Command and Staff University.

**Postal codes are deliberately blank throughout.** Nominatim returns Iranian postal zones for these points, but the Iranian 10-digit postcode is address-specific, and an area code reverse-geocoded from a coordinate is not the site's postcode. Blank beats a plausible-looking wrong number.

Imagery-confirmed sites and dates: Tehran Aerospace Exhibition Center **29 Oct 2025** (20+ airframes in a landscaped park); IRIAF Museum **6 Nov 2025** (~10-12 airframes around a pavilion); Sacred Defence Museum **21 Nov 2025** (two plinth-mounted swept-wing jets plus a line of armour, visitors on the paths); Bustan-e Besat **21 Nov 2025** (unmistakable DC-3 planform with footpaths to it); Kish Tu-154 and Malekabad BAe 146 both directly visible as intact airliners. Sattari **29 Oct 2025**, Behesht-e Zahra **21 Nov 2025**, Takestan **14 Oct 2025**, Qarchak **3 Dec 2024**, Mahabad **18 Jul 2025**, Kermanshah **8 May 2025**, Bandar Abbas **25 Sep 2025** — recent coverage exists at all of these, but resolution was insufficient to identify the airframes at most, so imagery there establishes only that nothing has obviously been cleared.

### Deliberate blanks

Fourteen aircraft rows carry a blank `tail_number`. Every one is either an explicit "As ####" observed-marking record, a serial in a known collision (3-7150 ×3, 2-4173 ×2), a serial the source itself calls probably fake (Sattari's 3-7374/3-7365), or a corrupt database entry (the FH-227). `year_built` is blank on every row without exception — no Iranian source I reached gives build years, and inferring them from construction numbers would be exactly the fabrication the brief forbids. The Cessna 414 and AVA-101 have registrations held in aliases rather than the tail field because I could not confirm the marks are still worn.

### Open questions, ranked

1. **Sari riverside aircraft park, Mazandaran (36.58040/53.08116).** Four `historic=aircraft` nodes clustered in a riverside park in the Zeytun district of Sari, mapped by a local contributor, entirely absent from spottingmode. February 2023 imagery shows several dark winged shapes on a paved apron beside new landscaping. This looks like a recently built Sacred Defence display park and could be a whole unrecorded site. No types identifiable; no Persian source reachable. **Highest-value single lead in this sweep.**
2. **RH-53D 158686 (c/n 65.359).** Is an Operation Eagle Claw Sea Stallion on display at the Sacred Defence museum? The source doubts its own entry. Related: spottingmode's Tabas location (20811) records "F.27 fuselage, also some fake CH-53s," which hints at Eagle Claw commemoration at the Desert One site itself.
3. **The provincial Sacred Defence branch network.** Ahvaz, Kerman, Mashhad, Isfahan, Shiraz, Tabriz and Bandar Abbas branches were unreachable. Khorramshahr is confirmed aircraft-free; the others are simply unknown. The brief was right that this is the most likely reservoir of uncatalogued records, and I could not open it.
4. **Bell and Cobra helicopters at the Sacred Defence museum, Tehran.** Tehran Times (July 2025) states they are on display; spottingmode links none. Almost certainly real records with no identities attached.
5. **رستوران هواپیما قمصر** (33.74282/51.43355), an "Airplane Restaurant" at Qamsar near Kashan, Isfahan province, named in OSM but absent from spottingmode. Esri export failed at that location. Unconfirmed whether a real airframe is involved or the name is decorative.
6. **Kish "Raspina" air café.** A Persian travel article describes a café inside an aircraft on Kish; the page was unreachable. It is plausibly the Tu-154 EP-LBR I did record, but I did not assert the link.

9. **The F-84 at the Army Command and Staff University**, queried with a question mark by the source and never resolved.
10. **HESA and IAIO display airframes at Isfahan/Shahin Shahr.** The only spottingmode entry near Hesa (30807, 32.92505/51.56353) is "several Tu-154, An-140," coded civil and consistent with production storage rather than a heritage display. No IAIO display collection found.

### Honest summary of confidence

The four Tehran museums, Bustan-e Besat, Kish, and Malekabad are well evidenced — multiple sources, recent updates, and in several cases direct visual confirmation on 2025 imagery. The martyrs'-square and mausoleum monuments are well located and well characterised but mostly serial-less.
