# Kansas

25 museums, 286 aircraft. Before this pass the state held a single record — the
Cosmosphere, with no aircraft attached.

Built as a civilian-museum pass plus a combined base-and-monument pass; the
discovery sweep in the first pass found so much that the second was small.

## Sources and their weight

Kansas is unusually well served by **museums that publish airframe-level
lists**. The Combat Air Museum's "Aircraft Master 2024" PDF gives serial,
hangar, ownership and loan code for every airframe; Mid-America's three
collection pages define its holdings; the Kansas National Guard museum lists
its statics. Where those exist they outrank everything else here.

The **FAA registry** did most of the work at Liberal: nearly every Mid-America
N-number is deregistered with the City of Liberal or the MAAM Foundation as
last owner, which independently confirms presence for ~80 airframes at once.
The same trick at the Kansas Aviation Museum produced a registry-only tier of
about twenty light aircraft with no other evidence they are on display — those
were **not** imported (see below).

**silverhawkauthor's Kansas list** is the usual stale ancestor: it still places
B-29 "Doc" at the Kansas Aviation Museum (it has its own hangar across the
airport), lists a Beech T-34B at Liberal that no other source has, and carries
Girard's Huey under a serial with a digit dropped.

## Corrections made

- **The Augusta thread is closed.** The Kansas Museum of Military History is
  defunct; CNHI reporting from July 2023 confirms the American Military Heritage
  Museum in Enid acquired "a museum in Augusta, Kansas, that went defunct" —
  naming the Huey, the Jenny and the cockpit sections the Oklahoma pass recorded
  there. Serials 65-9700 and 69-16123 could not be tied to Enid by number.
- **Girard's Huey is 64-13632, not 64-3632** — read off the door markings in a
  2019 photograph.
- **McConnell's F-86L is 52-4256**; one directory transposes it to 52-4265.
- **The Kansas Aviation Museum's F-84F is 52-9089** (NMUSAF loan list); the
  Wikipedia survivor table gives 47-1513, which is actually the F-84C at
  McConnell.
- **The Guard museum's F-105 is 61-0106**, the GF-105D that left the Kansas
  Aviation Museum — resolving a conflict with a single source claiming F-105F
  63-8366.
- **Independence's F-100 is an F-100F** — the two-seat serial block and the
  HMdb marker agree; the Wikipedia table says D.
- **Combat Air Museum's Harvard Mk IV cannot have been built in 1947**; the
  Mk IV was not produced until 1951-54. Year left blank.
- **The "Cosmosphere T-38 N948NS"** that two directories cite is not in the FAA
  register. Tail left blank.

## Judgment calls

**Cosmosphere.** The distinction that matters there is flown versus not: the
genuine flown Liberty Bell 7, Gemini X and Apollo 13's Odyssey are recorded with
their spacecraft numbers; the Voskhod, Sputniks, Surveyor, Pioneer 4 and LTA-3A
are recorded as engineering or test articles; the Shuttle facade, LM, Apollo CM,
Vanguard, Explorer and Mars rover are recorded as replicas. Two items the museum
calls genuine and Wikipedia calls replica — the Titan II and the Mercury-Redstone
— are recorded with both claims in aliases. The X-15 (possibly sub-scale) and a
standalone Mercury replica were left out as unconfirmed. The Volga airlock is a
component of the Voskhod display and is mentioned there, not recorded separately.

**Kansas Aviation Museum.** 37 airframes imported: the ramp and Vintage Room
collections, plus eight light aircraft with both an FAA registration to the
museum *and* a visitor sighting. The remaining ~14 registry-only entries (a
Swallow TP, a Stearman C3B, two American Eagles, a Culver V, a Swift, an AT-17,
a Rawdon R-1 and others) are not recorded until someone confirms they are
displayed rather than stored or parted out.

**Mid-America Air Museum.** 107 of roughly 112 candidates imported. Left out:
the "Koala II" (unidentifiable), "Firefly 7" and "Raven Model" (probably not
airframes), a Mooney M22 and a DK-1 Der Kricket registered to the museum but
absent from its own list, and the T-34B, Travel Air, Ercoupe and Avro 504K that
exist only in directories.

**Replicas** are recorded and flagged throughout — the Combat Air Museum's
eleven WWI replicas (mostly 7/8-scale flying replicas by Dick and Sharon
Starks), the Bf 109 mockup, the S.E.5 under construction, Liberal's S.E.5a and
Nieuport 11 and 2/3-scale Mustang, Goodland's Purvis-Wilson helicopter, and the
Laird Swallow at Wichita.

**The American Flight Museum** is a flying club as much as a museum; its fleet
rotates. The twelve recorded are those FAA-registered to it or on its current
site. A BAC 1-11 and two Hueys that one directory lists there are not on the
museum's site and were not recorded.

## Excluded, and why

- **Kansas Museum of Military History, Augusta** — defunct; collection to Enid.
- **Forbes Field 190th ARW** — every static there belongs to the Guard museum
  and stands on museum grounds; no separate record.
- **Fort Riley** and **Fort Leavenworth** — no airframes found on either post.
- **Eisenhower Presidential Library** — no aircraft; "Columbine" there is an
  archival finding aid.
- **Museum of World Treasures**, **Wichita Art Museum**, **Amelia Earhart
  Birthplace Museum**, **Great Bend B-29 Memorial** (a sculpture), **Exploration
  Place** (a business-jet cabin section) — no full airframes.
- **Pioneer-Krier's Chipmunk N6311V** — FAA-registered to the Airpower Museum in
  Ottumwa Iowa since 2018; presence at Ashland unverified.
- **Private aircraft** — P-51A "Polar Bear" (Syracuse), the CAF Great Plains
  Wing's P-51D that only visits Wichita with Doc, and member-owned aircraft
  hangared at the two CAF wings.
- **Combat Air Museum departures** — O-47B 39-0098 (sold 2014), B-26C N99420,
  the loaned F4F and N2S (returned 2005), and OH-6A 67-16066, which is now at
  the Guard museum.

## Blank fields, deliberately

`year_built` on military airframes comes only from a dated acceptance or
delivery (the Combat Air Museum pages give these for most of its fleet; McConnell's
KC-135A 55-3118, the first ever built, first flew 31 August 1956). On civil
aircraft it comes from the FAA YEAR-MFR field. One FAA year was refused: an
Aeronca K given as 1945, which is impossible for a 1937-38 type.

Tails are blank where identity is disputed or unpublished: the Combat Air
Museum's MiG-21 (museum marking versus Aerial Visuals c/n), Liberal's PT-23
(the registration Wikipedia gives is on a Pennsylvania aircraft), the Cosmosphere
T-38, the Allied A-2 Balentine, Wichita's WASP Beech 18, and the Guard museum's
L-19 and OH-23.

## Needs a human on site

- **McConnell's air park** was assembled from survivor lists and satellite pins
  because the NMUSAF loan PDFs were unreachable; eleven airframes are recorded and
  there may be more. The B-47E and KC-135A sit in a second cluster by the main
  gate at roughly 37.6403 / -97.2455.
- **Pratt** — all three serials (F-4C 63-7702, T-38A 71-0814, HH-3F 1469) rest on
  a single source, and the Cobra has none.
- **Naval Air Park, New Century** — three BuNos from a single source, not
  re-verified for 2025.
- **Coffeyville's "Jenny"** is almost certainly a replica; nobody has said so in
  print.
- **The Cosmosphere's Titan II and Mercury-Redstone** — genuine or replica.
- **Combat Air Museum's CH-54** — the museum's own documents disagree on A or B.
- **WaKeeney's F-14** is mid-restoration after 2017 hail damage; recorded
  `under_restoration` on a June 2026 report.
- Coordinates that are centroids rather than fixes: WaKeeney and Girard (park and
  courthouse), Dodge City (airport reference point, not the pylon), the American
  Flight Museum (Forbes Field reference), and the two New Century sites.
