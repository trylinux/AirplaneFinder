# PANAMA — preserved / publicly displayed aircraft
Research date: 2026-09-10. Researcher pass: greenfield (database had zero Panama records).

## HEADLINE RESULT

**Zero (0) site records and zero (0) airframe records can be entered at the required
evidence standard.** Every lead in the brief was chased in Spanish and English; none
produced a second independent source or a dated photograph confirming a publicly
presented, deliberately retained airframe currently standing in Panama.

This is a negative finding, not an unfinished one. The single most likely candidate
found (the Naos Island airliner) is confirmed **removed and scrapped**, and belongs on
the exclude list rather than in the database. Nothing has been invented to fill the gap.

### 3. EXCLUDED — named, with reasons

- **Fokker 100, Naos Island, Amador Causeway, Panama City.** A Venezuelan businessman
  placed a commercial airliner on Naos c. 2021 as "Captain Flags / The Planet
  Restaurant" — a themed restaurant explicitly billed in press coverage as including a
  *museo aeronáutico*. This is the closest Panama ever came to a qualifying record.
  **It no longer exists.** *La Prensa* investigative piece (21 May 2026) reports the
  aircraft sat ~6 years, the Autoridad de Aeronáutica Civil then ordered its removal
  and formally classified it as **chatarra (scrap)**, and it has been hauled away; the
  site is now slated for a boutique hotel and marina. Type given as Fokker 100 by
  *Crítica* (22 Sep 2021); registration never published in either source. **EXCLUDE:
  removed, scrapped, no longer publicly viewable.** Do not enter.
- **SENAN active flightline** — Bell UH-1H, UH-1ST, Bell 212/407/412, CASA C-212,
  BN-2 Islander, T-35 Pillán, PA-31/PA-34, King Air 250. All operational aircraft.
  EXCLUDE per brief.
- **"Museo Aéreo Fénix"** — surfaced repeatedly in searches for "Panama City". It is in
  **Panama City, FLORIDA, USA**, not Panama. False positive; flagged so a later pass
  does not re-chase it.
- **Panamanian scale-model collection** (*La Prensa*: "La colección de aviones a escala
  más grande de la región está en Panamá"). Scale models, not airframes. EXCLUDE.
- **Tocumen fire-training fuselage** — no evidence one exists in Panama; the Benemérito
  Cuerpo de Bomberos 2025 exercises describe joint drills, not a retained airframe.
  Even if one exists it is a training prop, not a public display. EXCLUDE.
- **1981 Torrijos DHC-6 Twin Otter (FAP-205)** crash wreckage — destroyed in the Cerro
  Marta impact, not preserved. EXCLUDE.

### 4. Captured FAP / PDF airframes in US museums

The brief asked specifically for these so they can be kept out of Panama's records.
**No such airframe could be confirmed.** Searches of Operation Just Cause sources
(ARSOF history, Air & Space Forces Magazine Dec 2009 retrospective, Tank Encyclopedia's
1989 invasion inventory, Capture of Torrijos Airport) describe FAP aircraft being
**destroyed on the ground at Paitilla, Tocumen/Torrijos and Río Hato** in Dec 1989, and
surviving airframes passing to the new Servicio Aéreo Nacional rather than to US
collections. No US museum was found claiming a Panamanian T-33, U-17, UH-1H or
Cessna 210 as a Just Cause trophy. **Nothing to exclude, and nothing that could be
mistakenly filed under Panama.** This remains an open question rather than a settled
negative — see Q4.

### 5. Leads from the brief that did NOT survive

- **Albrook gate-guard T-33 / UH-1 / OV-10 / C-47.** Not corroborated by any source.
  The two authoritative type-survivor lists (T-33, C-47) both omit Panama entirely.
  Albrook AFS closed 1997/1999; the site is now Marcos A. Gelabert airport, Albrook
  Mall and the SENAN base. Neither the airport's Spanish or English Wikipedia articles,
  the airport guides, nor panama50.com's Gelabert feature mention a displayed aircraft.
  Judgment: the "documented preserved airframes in the Albrook area" premise in the
  brief is **unsupported** on current evidence.
- **Howard AFB / Panamá Pacífico post-1999 leftovers.** Coverage of the handover
  (Wikipedia, globalsecurity.org, DVIDS "A Goodbye Mission", Coronado Concierge history)
  describes complete US withdrawal and conversion to a business park. No display
  aircraft reported as remaining.
- **Museo del Canal Interoceánico / Biomuseo / Museo de la Libertad y la Democracia /
  Museo Histórico de Panamá.** Checked. None holds an aircraft.
- **Universidad Tecnológica de Panamá — Escuela de Aviación y Logística / Técnico en
  Mantenimiento de Aeronaves (FIM).** Programme pages confirmed to exist. **No airframe
  on campus is documented anywhere**, and no photograph was found. Plausible that
  instructional airframes exist; unverified, so not entered. See Q1.

### 6. Method limits (read before trusting the zero)

- OpenStreetMap Overpass API (`historic=aircraft` and name-regex over the Panama
  admin area) was attempted three times against two endpoints and is **blocked by this
  session's egress proxy** (`ws_closed_mid_exchange` on overpass-api.de; HTTP 403 on
  the WebFetch route). DuckDuckGo HTML/lite endpoints are bot-blocked. Facebook group
  posts are robots-disallowed. **An Overpass sweep is the single highest-value
  unexecuted check** and should be the first action of any follow-up pass — plinthed
  aircraft are very commonly the only OSM-tagged trace of a monument that has no web
  presence.
- Google Street View / satellite imagery was not available to this session. For Panama
  specifically, imagery inspection of the Albrook SENAN compound, the Gelabert terminal
  frontage, the Río Hato / Scarlett Martínez apron, and the Panamá Pacífico gate would
  likely settle several of the questions below in minutes.
- The available web index for Panamanian aviation heritage is genuinely sparse — this
  is not merely a language problem. The national civil aviation authority's own history
  page mentions no preserved aircraft at all.

### 8. Ranked open questions for a follow-up pass

1. **Does the Universidad Tecnológica de Panamá hold instructional airframes, and are
   they publicly viewable?** Highest probability of a real record. Facultad de
   Ingeniería Mecánica / Escuela de Aviación y Logística, Campus Víctor Levi Sasso,
   Vía Puente Centenario, Panamá. https://eal.utp.ac.pa/ and https://fim.utp.ac.pa/ ;
   UTP switchboard +507 560-3000. Ask: "¿Tienen aeronaves completas para práctica en el
   campus, y son visibles al público?"
2. **Does SENAN retain any withdrawn airframe as a monument at Albrook or at the
   Academia de Formación Aeronaval?** SENAN, Base Aeronaval Albrook, Ciudad de Panamá.
   http://www.aeronaval.gob.pa/ ; press/comms via Ministerio de Seguridad Pública,
   https://www.minseg.gob.pa/ . Ask specifically about aircraft of the pre-1990 Fuerza
   Aérea Panameña (T-33, U-17, UH-1H, Cessna 210) retained as *monumento* or
   *exhibición estática*.
3. **Run the OSM Overpass sweep** (`historic=aircraft`, `name~"[Aa]vi[óo]n"`,
   `tourism=museum` + aero name, within ISO3166-1=PA) plus a satellite sweep of
   Albrook, Panamá Pacífico/Howard, Río Hato and Enrique Malek (David). Blocked here.
4. **Did any captured Panamanian Defense Forces airframe reach a US collection?**
   Best contacts: US Army ARSOF History Office (https://arsof-history.org/), National
   Museum of the USAF research division, Airborne & Special Operations Museum. Needed
   only to keep such an airframe correctly filed under the USA, not Panama.
5. **The "avioneta en el Colegio Italiano" Facebook thread** (group 57020997026, post
   10159452374107027) — a light aircraft standing in a Colegio Italiano schoolyard. The
   group's country could not be determined (Facebook is robots-disallowed to this
   session) and there are Colegios Italianos across Latin America, so this may well not
   be Panama at all. Worth one manual look; do not assume Panama.
6. **Autoridad de Aeronáutica Civil records office** — the AAC ordered the Naos Fokker
   100 scrapped, so it tracks derelict/retained airframes nationally. It is the single
   best institutional source for "what old aircraft still stand in Panama."
   https://www.aeronautica.gob.pa/ , +507 501-9000.
