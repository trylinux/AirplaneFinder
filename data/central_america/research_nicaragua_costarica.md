## NOTES

### Sources that proved empty or stale
- **aviationmuseum.eu** — both the Costa Rica page (/World/Latin_America/Costa_Rica/index.htm) and the Nicaragua page exist but their "Museum links" sections are **completely empty**. No lead value at all for either country; the /North_America/Central_America/Nicaragua path 404s.
- **Wikipedia "List of displayed Lockheed T-33 Shooting Stars"** — no Central American entries at all; no Nicaraguan or Costa Rican T-33 exists on it. The T-33 lead in the brief did not pan out.
- **Wikipedia "List of surviving Douglas C-47 Skytrains"** — no Nicaragua or Costa Rica entries.
- **Wikipedia "List of museums in Nicaragua"** — no aviation museum; the military/revolution entries (Museo de la Revolución León, Museo General Sandino Jinotega, the Estelí ex-combatants museum) show no aircraft holdings in any source found.
- **oldjets.net San José page** placed the Curtiss Robin TI-BGZ at the Juan Santamaría domestic terminal as of 23 February 2024. **This is now stale** — Vintage Aviation News (4 December 2025) reports the aircraft was disassembled and moved to IFA Heredia in May 2025. Anyone importing from oldjets alone would put it at the wrong site.
- **Museo Aeronáutico de Costa Rica's own website** still carries a COVID-19 closure notice, i.e. the site is not maintained; do not read that notice as current fact without contacting them.

### Corrections and identity cautions
- **El Avión's C-123 is NOT the Hasenfus aircraft.** The aircraft shot down on 5 October 1986 was Corporate Air Services HPF821 (previously N4410F, c/n 20128, ex-USAF 54-679) and was destroyed in Nicaragua. Several travel pages and one USAFA alumni page conflate the two and attribute N4410F/HPF821 to the restaurant. The Quepos airframe is the **sister ship**, and its own identity is not established in any source I could confirm. A 2019 dated photograph reads 54-663 on the fuselage; that is a single-source marking and is recorded in aliases only, never as tail_number.
- **C-123 variant is unresolved.** The two CIA-contracted aircraft were C-123K models; the 2019 Flickr caption calls the Quepos airframe a C-123B. Variant left blank rather than guessed.
- **Costa Verde 727 registration** is given as HK-3133X by AirlineGeeks and the identity chain ZS-DYP → HK-3133X by jordi-batlle.com. The trailing X is normal for Colombian provisional marks. The hotel's own page names only "Avianca" and "1965"; the SAA origin is confirmed by two independent sources. Model recorded as 727 / variant 100 per the base-designation rule; the customer designation 727-44 is in aliases.
- **Managua 737-200: N103HA vs TG-REX.** oldjets gives both, with TG-REX applied in November 2010 after export. tail_number set to TG-REX (last-worn identity), N103HA in aliases. No photograph I found shows the registration legibly — the airframe now wears political slogans — so this pairing rests on one source and should be re-checked.
- **Condega: everything left blank.** Spanish-language sources say only "avión bimotor de la FAN". The Cessna 337/O-2A is the plausible type but is unconfirmed, so manufacturer, model, variant, tail_number and year_built are all blank. role_type set to recon because every account agrees it was flying a reconnaissance mission for the Guardia Nacional; that is the one attribute the sources do support.
- **No year_built was taken from any serial.** The three years populated (1965 Costa Verde 727, 1979 Suites Charter 727, 1984 MD-82, 1975 Managua 737, 1929 Curtiss Robin) all come from stated build/delivery dates in the cited sources.

### Judgment calls
- **Hotel/restaurant conversions counted as sites.** Costa Verde, El Avión and Suites Charter are deliberate retention plus public presentation — the whole reason the brief flags them. El Avión is a walk-in restaurant, hence `public`; the two hotels gate their airframes behind a booking, hence `appointment`.
- **Costa Verde vs El Avión treated as two sites.** The Costa Verde website counts the C-123 among "four aircraft," and ruudleeuw.com describes it as "part of the Hotel Costa Verde," so common ownership is likely. But El Avión has its own name, address, website and public access model, so it is a separate site record. Flag for a future merge decision if ownership is confirmed.
- **Costa Verde MD-82 = one airframe, two suites** (Cockpit Cabana + Skyloft). One aircraft record, not two.
- **Costa Verde DC-9 and MD-82 are partial airframes** (nose section, split fuselage). Recorded because they are deliberately retained and presented as aircraft, but the description says so plainly.
- **Condega is a shootdown relic, not a scrap hulk.** It was deliberately relocated by a civic committee, given a park, a viewing tower and picnic facilities, and is promoted by municipal and national media as a monument. It counts.
- **Managua 737 is an installed public exhibit**, not stored equipment — boarded by visitors, painted with state slogans, in place 2012-2024.

### EXCLUDED — and why
- **Exposición Estática del Ejército de Nicaragua**, Plaza La Fe, Managua (annual, most recently July 2025 and 2026, 134 items incl. Mi-17s and other helicopters). Excluded: this is a temporary open-days display of **currently operational** air force equipment that returns to service, not deliberate retention of a retired airframe.
- **Nicaraguan Air Force ramp, Augusto C. Sandino International Airport, Managua** — An-2 "FAN-79" and Russian helicopters logged by oldjets in February 2024. Excluded: an active military ramp, no evidence any of it is retained as a heritage display or gate guard. Worth re-checking, since an An-2 in old FAN marks could well be a preserved airframe rather than a live one.
- **COOPESA MRO, San José Juan Santamaría** — Embraers, an ex-Ukraine International Boeing and other airframes in maintenance/storage/conversion. Excluded: transient commercial storage, not preservation.
- **Museo Nacional de Costa Rica (Cuartel Bellavista), San José** — checked directly; its permanent exhibitions are archaeology, colonial history, natural history and the "De Cuartel a Museo" army-abolition gallery. **No aircraft.** The brief's lead does not hold.
- **Museo Histórico Cultural Juan Santamaría, Alajuela** — 1856-57 National Campaign museum; no aviation content found. Not carried as a site.
- **Museo de la Revolución (León), Museo General Sandino (Jinotega), Galería/museo de héroes y mártires (Estelí)** — revolution museums with photographs and small arms; no aircraft or aircraft sections found in any source.
- **Servicio de Vigilancia Aérea (Costa Rica) bases at Pavas/Tobías Bolaños and Liberia** — searched specifically for a gate guard or retired airframe display; nothing found. All SVA aircraft located are in service.
- **"Avión Pirata" (Lockheed Constellation N2520B)** — surfaced repeatedly in searches; it is in **Santa Cruz, Bolivia**, not Central America. Not a record here.
- **La Casa de los Aviones (Nicaragua, Facebook/TikTok)** — appears to be a shop/business name, not an aircraft display. No airframe evidence.
- **Grokipedia** — not used, per instruction.

### Deliberately blank fields
- All coordinates except El Avión: no sourced lat/long found for Costa Verde, Suites Charter, IFA Heredia, Parque Mirador El Avión or the Managua Malecón exhibit. Condega's town centroid (13.35, -86.40) is published but the park is on a separate hill and the cemetery, so it was **not** used.
- Postal codes: only Dominical (60504) is published.
- `model_name` left blank throughout except the C-123 ("Provider"); none of the other types carry a manufacturer popular name.
- `aircraft_name` populated only for the Curtiss Robin ("Espíritu Tico II"), which genuinely carries a name.
- Condega: every identity field blank, by design.
- DC-9 TI-AZS: variant and year_built blank — sources give "first flew 1968 with SAS" but no series and no build year, and a first-flight-with-operator year is not a build year.
- Websites: Suites Charter Boutique has no confirmed first-party site (only OTA listings and a Facebook page); Parque Mirador El Avión and the Managua exhibit have none.

### Open questions, ranked
1. **What type is the Condega aircraft?** Highest-value unknown — it is the only genuine war-relic monument in either country and currently has no identity at all. Contact: Alcaldía de Condega (Facebook: Alcaldía Municipal de Condega) or INTUR Estelí. A single clear photograph of the nose and engine arrangement would settle Cessna 337/O-2A vs something else.
2. **What is the El Avión C-123's real identity (serial, c/n, B or K)?** Ask the restaurant directly — elavion.net, +506 2777 3378 (number appears on the restaurant's own site; verify before publishing). US civil registry and C-123 production-list research on the second Southern Air Transport / Corporate Air Services airframe would also close it.
3. **Is the Museo Aeronáutico de Costa Rica actually open, and what else does it hold?** Email info@museoaeronauticocr.com. Its website advertises "Exhibiciones Físicas" but publishes no inventory; there may be additional airframes or major components at IFA Lagunilla beyond the Curtiss Robin, which would materially expand Costa Rica's coverage. Also settle the correct access_type.
4. **Coordinates for all five uncoordinated sites** — resolvable with satellite imagery: the Managua 737 and the Condega hilltop airframe are both large enough to identify from above.
5. **Is the Managua 737's registration still TG-REX, and does the airframe still carry any legible mark?** A recent spotter photograph would confirm. Also confirm the exact enclosure (Plaza La Fe vs Puerto Salvador Allende ticketed area) and whether entry is free.
6. **Is the An-2 "FAN-79" at Managua airport preserved or operational?** If preserved, it is a seventh site (air base heritage display, access `restricted`). Contact: Fuerza Aérea del Ejército de Nicaragua public affairs via ejercito.mil.ni.
7. **Does the Costa Verde DC-9 have a fuller identity** (series, c/n, build year)? Costa Rican registry TI-AZS records via DGAC (sub.dgac.go.cr) would settle it.
8. **Any airframes at Costa Rican aviation training schools** — IFA, ECDEA, AENSA, INA. Maintenance programmes usually hold retired airframes; none was documented publicly. Worth a direct enquiry round; only publicly viewable ones count.
