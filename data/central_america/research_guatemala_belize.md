## NOTES

### Sources used, and which proved stale or wrong

- **AirHistory.net** — the single most useful source for Guatemala. Its photo captions carry curated display histories ("on display outside the entrance to La Aurora Air Base since 2010", "displayed outside the entrance to La Aurora Air Base from 1993 until 2015", "on display since the late 1970s"). Its search and marks-listing endpoints return HTTP 403 to automated fetching, so only individual photo and location pages were reachable; a human browsing the site would likely find more Guatemalan airframes than I did.
- **aviationmuseum.eu** — used as a lead list only, as instructed, and it earned that treatment. Its Guatemala entry gives coordinates of roughly 14.628 N, 90.619 W for the Museo Militar. The Centro Cultural Miguel Ángel Asturias is at roughly longitude −90.52; −90.619 puts the pin about 11 km west of the actual site, matching the site's known coordinate-error pattern. I therefore discarded its coordinates entirely and did not carry its two serials into tail_number. It also merges "Museo del Ejército de Guatemala" and "Museo Militar y Ex Fuerte de San José" into one title without saying they are the same institution (they are — the fort site now holds the Centro Cultural).
- **silverhawkauthor.com** — thin and stale for Guatemala. Its Guatemala page documents exactly one airframe (P-51D FAG 336, "La Aurora International Airport") and otherwise only names types the FAG once flew (B-26, C-47) without serials or locations. It added nothing I did not already have and the author himself solicits corrections. Contributed no records.
- **Warbird Registry** — its FAG 336 page stops at 1987 and is internally inconsistent with AirHistory on whether the airframe is 336 or 360. Stale, but valuable precisely because the inconsistency is the evidence that the painted serial is unreliable.
- **Wikipedia** — actively unhelpful here. *List of surviving North American P-51 Mustangs* has no Guatemala section at all despite a Mustang standing in public view in Guatemala City for ~47 years. *List of displayed Lockheed T-33 Shooting Stars* has no Guatemala or Belize entry. *Guatemalan Air Force*, *RAF Belize*, *Price Barracks* and *Hawker Siddeley Harrier* contain no preservation content. These are gaps in Wikipedia, not evidence of absence.
- **Thunder & Lightnings** (thunder-and-lightnings.co.uk) — the only structured register that carries the Belize Harrier. Fetched the survivors table directly and confirmed the row. Entry last updated 1 Apr 2018.
- **sicultura.gob.gt (SIC, Guatemalan Ministry of Culture directory)** — the best Spanish-language systematic source for Guatemalan museums and monuments; how I found and then eliminated the Totonicapán "El Aviador" monument, and how I confirmed the Museo de los Niños address and hours.
- **Overpass API / OpenStreetMap** — attempted, blocked by the outbound proxy in this environment. This is the obvious next tool for coordinates and would likely close several of the open questions below.

### Corrections made, with evidence

1. **The Mustang's serial is not its identity.** The painted "336" is described by AirHistory as a fake serial applied in the mid-1980s over an earlier "360". tail_number left blank; both numbers filed as aliases. Anyone importing "336" as the tail number is importing paint, not provenance.
2. **A-37B A-416's "69-6404" is a fiscal-year order serial, not a build year.** year_built left blank throughout.
3. **"Museo del Ejército de Guatemala" and "Museo Militar y Ex Fuerte de San José" are the same institution**, also carried by SIC as "Museo Servicio de Historia Militar". Recorded once, with the other names available as alternates, rather than as two sites.
4. **Monumento "El Aviador", Totonicapán is not an aircraft.** SIC's own description says the central element is a helicopter propeller with a plaque and railings, honouring Col. Jacinto Rodríguez Díaz, dedicated 30 Jun 1933. Rejected — a propeller is not an airframe.
5. **The Cobán monument's type was left blank rather than assumed.** Both Guatemalan sources say only "avioneta". The published 12 m span and ~2,700 kg contradict the Arava and are too large for a Cessna 206, so any specific type would have been invention.
6. **Belize's Harrier is a GR.3, serial ZD669** — the operational 1997 AirHistory photo at Belize is of the same airframe but does not by itself establish preservation; the preservation claim rests on Thunder & Lightnings plus laststandonzombieisland, which is why the site is medium and not high confidence.

### Judgment calls

- **The four zona 13 airframes are ONE site, not four.** They stand along a single public street frontage of one air base and were installed by the same institutional programme. Splitting them into four monument records would double-count the same air park. If the database's convention is one record per plinth, this splits cleanly into four.
- **`access_type: public` for the zona 13 row** — these are street-side monuments on a public thoroughfare, visible and photographable without entering the base. Photographers have shot the Mustang from the public side repeatedly.
- **`access_type: restricted` for the Belize Harrier** — chosen conservatively. Neither source says whether it is inside Price Barracks/Airport Camp (military, not open) or on the civil airport side. If it turns out to be outside the wire this should become `public`.
- **The Museo de los Niños Cessna is `on_display`, not an amusement ride.** It is a real airframe deliberately retained and presented, with a simulator fitted inside. It qualifies.
- **No replicas, composites or nose sections were identified** in either country. Every airframe recorded here is claimed by its sources to be a complete airframe.
- **`military_civilian: military` for the Cessna 210** on the strength of its FAG donation, flagged in the description as possibly having a prior civil identity.

### EXCLUDED — everything considered and rejected, and why

- **Monumento "El Aviador", 17 avenida entre 4a y 6a calle, zona 2, Totonicapán** — a helicopter propeller on a plinth, not an aircraft. SIC listing, dedicated 30 Jun 1933.
- **"Quetzaltenango 1", the first aircraft built in Guatemala** — guatemala.com reports the original survives but "remains in a private hangar in Quetzaltenango". Not publicly viewable, no public presentation. Excluded, but this is the single most interesting exclusion in either country: if it were ever put on public display it would be a first-order national record.
- **"Panchito el Avioncito" DC-3, Guatemala City** — a DC-3 that served as the "Drive In DC-3" restaurant near the Yurrita church, then a children's party venue, moved to zona 13 near Parque Berlín and finally to 25 calle at the end of Avenida Hincapié. Dismantled around the 1980s and its parts donated to the FAG. The airframe no longer exists.
- **Sikorsky UH-19B Chickasaw "110" (US serial 55393), La Aurora Air Base** — was a genuine display airframe outside the base entrance from 1993 until 2015 per AirHistory. Removed in 2015; subsequent fate not established by any source I reached. Excluded as a current site/airframe, but it is a live open question: if it was moved rather than scrapped it is a record somewhere.
- **Cessna A-37B A-448 (ex-USAF 73-1658), La Aurora** — restored to taxiable condition for the FAG's 2021 centenary. That makes it a maintained heritage airframe rather than a publicly presented static display, and there is no evidence it is accessible to the public. Excluded pending better information; a strong candidate for inclusion if the FAG presents it at a fixed public location.
- **Museum of Belize, Belize City (NICH)** — checked directly. A former colonial prison holding Maya, colonial and philatelic collections. No aircraft. Rejected.
- **Maritime Museum, Belize; Old Belize (Belize City)** — searched, no aircraft holdings surfaced in any source. Rejected on current evidence, not exhaustively cleared.
- **Abandoned aircraft found in Escuintla, Retalhuleu and Petén** (Prensa Libre / elPeriódico, 2019) — narco-trafficking airframes abandoned in fields and seized. Not preserved, not presented. Explicitly excluded per the brief.
- **Guatemalan Air Force active flightline at La Aurora (Bell 212, Bell 412EPX, Bell 407GX, King Airs, Cessna Caravans, PC-12, and the still-flying C-47B and T-33A "700" c/n 580-6974 seen on JetPhotos)** — operational aircraft, not preserved. Excluded. The T-33A "700" is worth re-checking in a few years: it is the type most likely to become the FAG's next plinth.
- **IRTRA Xetulul / Xocomil theme parks, Retalhuleu** — searched specifically because theme parks are common aircraft-monument hosts. No aircraft exhibit found; the 2026 "Historia del IRTRA" gallery at Xetulul is a photographic exhibition. Rejected.
- **Escuela Militar de Aviación / Escuela Politécnica** — mindef.mil.gt is unreachable (robots.txt fetch timeout through this proxy) and no secondary source mentions a display airframe at either. Not cleared; see open questions.
- **Belize Defence Force Air Wing, Price Barracks** — the operational Air Wing itself is not a record. Only the ZD669 Harrier is.

### Deliberately blank fields, and why

- **Every latitude and longitude in this file is blank.** I could not reach OpenStreetMap/Overpass through the proxy, and the only published coordinates I found (aviationmuseum.eu's for the Museo Militar) are demonstrably about 11 km wrong. Guessing from street addresses would have produced exactly the class of error the brief warns about.
- **Every year_built is blank.** No source gave a construction, rollout, first-flight or delivery date for any airframe here. The only date-like numbers available were FY order prefixes and painted serials, neither of which is a build year.
- **P-51D tail_number** — the painted serial is documented as false and the two best sources disagree on the true one.
- **A-37B (Museo Militar) and UH-1H (Museo Militar) tail_numbers** — available only from aviationmuseum.eu, a source the brief flags as unreliable and which I independently caught with an 11 km coordinate error on this very entry.
- **IAI Arava and zona 13 UH-1H tail_numbers** — no Guatemalan source published a serial for either.
- **Cobán monument manufacturer / model / variant / tail_number / model_name** — the type is genuinely unknown.
- **Cessna 210 variant and tail_number** — the sub-variant suffix and registration were not published.
- **Museum websites** — only the Museo de los Niños has one. The Museo Militar has no site I could verify; its phone number (+502 4497 4080, per aviationmuseum.eu) is unconfirmed.

### Open questions, ranked — one call or one visit closes several

1. **Visit or photograph 11 calle y 15 avenida, zona 13, Guatemala City.** One walk down this street resolves: how many airframes are actually there in 2026; the serials on the Arava and the Huey II; whether the A-37B is A-416; whether the Mustang still wears "336"; whether the 2016 Arava mentioned by relato.gt is a fifth airframe; and coordinates for all of them. This is by far the highest-value single action in this research.
2. **Call the Museo Militar y Ex Fuerte de San José: +502 4497 4080** (number unconfirmed — obtained from aviationmuseum.eu). Ask: what type is the aircraft in the courtyard, what type is the helicopter, and what serials do they carry. That single call converts two low-confidence airframe records into high-confidence ones and independently tests aviationmuseum.eu's Guatemala data quality.
3. **Establish where the Belize Harrier ZD669 actually stands and whether it is still there.** Contact the Belize Defence Force (https://www.bdf.mil.bz/) or BATSUB. This decides the site's coordinates, its access_type (public vs restricted), and whether the entry survives at all — the most recent register update is 1 Apr 2018 and the only narrative source is secondary. This is the whole of Belize's national record; it deserves the call.
4. **Identify the Cobán roundabout aircraft.** Contact the Municipalidad de Cobán, Alta Verapaz. Two questions: what type is it, and is it still on the roundabout after six years and at least one vandalism incident. A single photograph resolves the type.
5. **Where did the UH-19B Chickasaw "110" (55393) go after 2015?** If it was relocated rather than scrapped, that is an entire additional site. The FAG or Guatemalan aviation historians Tulio Soto and Mario E. Overall — both credited on the Warbird Registry's Guatemala entries — are the people who would know.
6. **Get the Museo de los Niños Cessna 210's registration:** info@museodelosninos.gt / +502 2213-8989.
7. **Clear the Escuela Militar de Aviación and the Escuela Politécnica** for gate-guard airframes. mindef.mil.gt was unreachable from this environment; a browser visit would settle it.
8. **Ask about the "Quetzaltenango 1"** — who owns the private hangar in Quetzaltenango, and is there any public-access arrangement. Long shot, high payoff.
9. **Resolve the Museo Militar street number:** 24 calle 3-81 (aviationmuseum.eu) vs 24 calle 0-81 (Agencia Guatemalteca de Noticias, 2021), both zona 1.

#### Corrections made to the OSM data

1. **`aircraft:type=airliner` on node 13610218828 is wrong.** A Piper PA-23-250 is a six-seat light twin, not an airliner. Recorded here as role_type `private`. Worth fixing upstream in OSM.
2. **PA-23-250 is the Aztec, not the Apache.** The PA-23-150/160 are Apaches; the -235 and -250 are Aztecs. model_name recorded as Aztec accordingly, which is a type fact and not a guess about this airframe.
