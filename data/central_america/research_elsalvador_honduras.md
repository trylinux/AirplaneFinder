# Preserved & Displayed Aircraft — EL SALVADOR and HONDURAS
Research date: 2026-09-10. Region for all records: Central America.
Greenfield: airplane.museum previously held zero records for both countries.

Primary source for El Salvador is **Flotilla Aérea** (flotilla-aerea.com), a Salvadoran
aviation-history site run by Mario Alfaro. Its article "Aeronaves de El Salvador en
exposición" (28/09/2018, revised through 2025 — it cites events of Aug 2024 and Jul 2025)
is the single best inventory that exists for the country: airframe-by-airframe with c/n,
USAAF/BuNo serial, delivery date, withdrawal reason and current display location, and it
explicitly flags its own uncertainties. It has been cross-checked below against the FAS
official site, Wikipedia and photo archives.

## NOTES

### Source assessment

**flotilla-aerea.com — outstanding, and the backbone of the El Salvador entries.**
"Aeronaves de El Salvador en exposición" (28 September 2018, revised through 2025) is
airframe-level research by a Salvadoran author with access to FAS records: c/n, USAAF
serial or BuNo, delivery date, withdrawal cause, display date and location for every
airframe. Crucially it also documents its own uncertainties, explaining that FAS tail
booms were swapped in maintenance so fuselage and tail serials disagree, that ID plates
were removed or damaged on pre-1980 deliveries, and that some airframes were renumbered
to commemorate historic events with no record of the change. That is exactly the kind of
warning most secondary sources omit, and I have carried each specific instance into the
relevant description. The article's revision currency is demonstrable: it cites the
Plaza Bicentenario installation of 31 August 2024 and links an article dated July 2025.

**fas.gob.sv/museo — official but STALE, and it must not be used as an inventory.**
The museum's own "Aeronaves en Display" page is written in the future tense ("En esta área
estarán ubicadas las aeronaves…") and lists a Socata Rallye FAS 58, MD-450 Ouragan
**FAS 714**, CM-170 Fouga FAS 500, Douglas A/C-47 **FAS 106**, UH-1M FAS 320 and UH-1H
FAS 260, with additional images captioned **T-41A** and **A-26**. Three problems:
- **FAS 714** does not match any displayed Ouragan. The Ouragan in the Plaza de la
  Aviación is FAS 713 (series 381). 714 is likely a typo or an earlier plan.
- **C-47 FAS 106** does not match any preserved Salvadoran C-47. The three preserved
  Dakotas are FAS 109 (El Zapote, moved 2017), FAS 114 (paratrooper monument) and
  FAS 115 (cockpit, museum interior). The page appears to predate the 2017 move.
- **T-41A** appears nowhere in Flotilla Aérea's inventory. I have NOT created a record
  for it. See open questions.
The same site's contact/hours page (mna.html) is, by contrast, current and usable, and
is the source of the address, phone, e-mail, hours and admission price above.

**aviationmuseum.eu — used as a lead list only, and it does not survive checking.**
Its Museo del Aire de Honduras page claims **37 aircraft including a Northrop F-5E
Tiger**. The museum's own inventory, the enabling Congress decree (144-2003) and both
Wikipedia versions all give **25** transferred airframes, plus C-130 FAH-558 added later.
I could find no reporting anywhere of an F-5 being retired to the museum, and the FAH
still operates F-5s. The F-5E claim is treated as unverified and is NOT recorded. The
site's contact details (museo_del_aire@live.com, +504 9992 4543) are used, flagged.

**silverhawkauthor.com — lead list only, and it conflicts on a serial.**
It gives the museum's Kingcobra as **FAH-403, USAAF 43-11732**, against **FAH-402** in
the museum's own inventory. It also calls the Corsair an **F4U-5NL** named
**"The Weak Spot"**. Its BuNo for the Corsair, 124715, IS independently confirmed by the
Warbird Registry, so the source is not worthless — but the uncorroborated claims are held
in aliases and description, never in the identity fields.

**Warbird Registry / airhistory.net — used to confirm the Corsair.** The Corsair registry
page for FAH-609 confirms BuNo 124715, USN then FAH from March 1956, in service to 1980,
displayed at the Museo del Aire since 1990. An airhistory.net photograph dated
**2 March 2011** shows it at Toncontín with three kill marks, and notes a restoration
started in 2012 after which it is displayed under cover.

**Grokipedia was not consulted, per instruction.**

### Corrections to the brief's starting assumptions

The brief listed the Ilopango museum's "known/claimed holdings" as including **Cavalier
F-51D Mustangs, an FG-1D Corsair, a Fouga Magister, an O-2A, an A-37B, a UH-1H, a C-47,
a T-34 and a Rallye**. Several of those are wrong for the museum specifically, and the
Football War fighters are wrong for the country entirely:

- **There is no Mustang in El Salvador and no Corsair in El Salvador.** Every ex-FAS
  piston fighter left the country decades ago (details in the exclusion list below).
  The confusion the brief warned about is real and this research resolves it: the only
  Football War piston fighter still in Central America is Honduras's **F4U-5N FAH-609**.
- The **Fouga, O-2A, A-37B, UH-1H, C-47 and Rallye** all do exist in El Salvador, but
  mostly NOT inside the museum building — they are spread across the Plaza de la
  Aviación, the Primera and Segunda Brigadas Aéreas, the El Zapote military museum and
  three public parks. Treating them all as "museum holdings" would have collapsed nine
  separate sites into one.
- I found **no evidence of a T-34** anywhere in El Salvador. No record created.
- The **Museo Militar de El Salvador (Cuartel El Zapote)** is confirmed and is a
  substantial aviation site in its own right — five airframes, including a C-47 that only
  arrived in 2017.
- The brief also expected an **F-51D Mustang at the Museo del Aire de Honduras**. It is
  not in the museum's inventory; the Honduran Mustangs were sold abroad.

### Judgment calls

**Perquín wreckage — INCLUDED.** The Museo de la Revolución Salvadoreña displays recovered
wreckage of the UH-1H in which Colonel Domingo Monterrosa died on 23 October 1984. This is
not a hulk left where it fell: it was recovered, moved, and is deliberately presented as
the interpretive centrepiece of a museum open to the public. That meets the definition of
a site. The airframe row carries no serial because none is published.

**Base Aérea de Ilopango consolidated into ONE site.** Ten airframes sit at four separate
spots inside the same closed perimeter — the PBA guard post, the Casino de Aerotécnicos,
the GOE and the GOA. Recording four sites would over-fragment a single installation, so
they are one restricted site with the exact unit given per airframe.

**Plaza de la Aviación kept SEPARATE from the museum.** Flotilla Aérea consistently
distinguishes the two, and the Plaza holds eight airframes of its own. If airplane.museum
prefers a single Ilopango museum record, site 2 folds into site 1 cleanly.

**AAC / ICCAE / COCESNA marked `restricted`.** Working civil-aviation authority and
training-institute grounds at Ilopango, not a visitor attraction. Both the Ouragan and the
DC-9 sit inside that compound.

**Boeing 727 at the aviation museum — INCLUDED but flagged.** It is a Universidad Don
Bosco ground-instruction airframe parked at the museum's west side. It is retained and
publicly visible at a museum, so it is recorded, but its live function is training. If
airplane.museum excludes instructional airframes, drop this one row.

**UH-1H FAS 299 — `in_storage`, and the Escuela Militar is NOT a site.** It was displayed
at the Escuela Militar "Capitán General Gerardo Barrios" from 2006, but was removed in
March 2022 when that ground was redeveloped, and returned to Ilopango. The source confirms
the return, not a new display, so it is in_storage and the Escuela Militar is delisted.

**Cabins and cockpits recorded as airframes.** Four Salvadoran records are partial
airframes — C-47 FAS 115, MD-450 FAS 705, UH-1D FAS 209 and BT-67 FAS 117 — each is
deliberately preserved and displayed, and each description says plainly that only a
section survives.

**False markings.** Four airframes wear numbers that are not their identity, and in every
case the true identity is in `tail_number` and the false marking is in `aliases`:
Hughes 500D **FAS 30** wears **FAS 41**; BT-67 **FAS 117** wears **125**; MD-450 **FAS 705**
wore **007** at the San Jacinto cable car in 1982–83; DC-6B **FAS 301** wore **FAS 302**
between 2013 and its recent repaint. The unserialled UH-1M at the GOE has **230** under
black paint on its tail boom, but UH-1C FAS 230 is a separate identified airframe on the
same base, so 230 is recorded as an alias, not a tail number.

**A-26 "FAS 601" — recorded, heavily caveated.** ID plate deliberately destroyed; civil
registration N-86482 found under the paint; and Castle Air Museum in California displays
a different A-26 that it ALSO calls "ex-FAS 601". Both cannot be right. The number is kept
in tail_number because it is the airframe's universal reference, with the doubt spelled
out in the description. Flagged here as the weakest identity in the El Salvador set.

**Deliberately blank fields.**
- `year_built` on all but six airframes. A serial is never a year, and no FAS or FAH
  inventory publishes construction dates. The six populated are sourced to explicit
  construction or first-flight statements: C-47 FAS 114 (1944), C-47 FAS 115 (1944),
  C-47 FAS 109 (1945), BT-67 FAS 117 (airframe built 1944), A-37B FAS 428 (built 1968),
  B727 N469FE (first flight 22 Jan 1979), DC-9 N813TL (built June 1966).
- The **Tin Marín 727** gets NO year_built. Its source says "built in 1960 in Seattle",
  which is impossible — the 727 first flew in February 1963. That is a source error, not
  a fact, and it is the clearest example in this dataset of why blank beats a guess.
- The **NA-16 FAH-21** gets no year_built: the museum says it entered Honduras in 1935 and
  was registered in 1937, which are inconsistent and neither is a build date.
- Coordinates blank for six sites: Plaza de la Aviación, the paratrooper monument, Base
  Aérea de Ilopango, the AAC compound, Plaza Bicentenario and the Perquín museum. In each
  case geocoding returned either nothing or a town/base centroid that is not the display
  location. No coordinate has been derived, interpolated or estimated anywhere in this file.
- `variant` blank where the sub-type is genuinely unknown: A-26 FAS 601, C-130 FAH-558.
- `military_civilian` and `role_type` blank for the Beechcraft Twin Bonanza at the Museo
  del Aire — the museum lists it with no registration and no source states whether it is
  ex-FAH or a private donation.

### EXCLUDED — and why

**Ex-FAS airframes now in the USA or abroad (NOT Central American records):**
- **Goodyear FG-1D Corsair, c/n 3951, BuNo 92690** — currently wears **FAS 220** but its
  real FAS identity was **FAS 207**. Delivered to the FAS 1957, displayed at the San
  Jacinto cable car entrance until replaced by MD-450 FAS 705 in 1982–83, then moved to
  Ilopango and on to the Segunda Brigada Aérea, and **sold to North American collectors in
  the early 1990s**. No longer in El Salvador. The 220/207 mismatch is exactly the kind of
  error that would put a phantom Corsair into the Salvadoran database.
- **Goodyear FG-1D Corsair, BuNo 92460, ex-FAS 217** — bought in 1969 by Nick Mainero, a
  wartime Corsair pilot, and moved to **Sikorsky Memorial Airport (KBDR), Stratford,
  Connecticut**, where it stands as a memorial. A US record, not Salvadoran.
- **North American F-51 Mustang, 44-73458, ex-FAS 404** — received 1969, erected as a
  monument in the **central park of Ilopango in 1971** to mark the municipality's
  elevation to city status, removed and sold abroad in **1974**. Flew in the USA, last
  registered **N4151D**, and was **totally destroyed in a crash at Galveston, Texas, on
  23 October 2013**. It no longer exists anywhere. Ilopango central park is therefore NOT
  a site — this is a monument that ended fifty years ago.
- **Douglas A-26 Invader at Castle Air Museum, California**, also claimed as "ex-FAS 601".
  Whatever its true provenance, it is in California.
- **Mil Mi-8MTV-1, msn 40705, ex-YS-1006P** — ex-Cuban DAAFAR and Sandinista Air Force,
  bought by HELICA of El Salvador in April 1993, sold on to Helistar Helicopters in July
  1993, and now displayed at **Huka Falls, Wairakei Park, New Zealand**.

**Firefighter training hulks at Comalapa (MSLP) — not display, not records:**
- **DC-6B YS-39C "General Manuel José Arce"** (c/n 773) — ex-Northeast Airlines N6581C,
  TACA Cargoliner and AESA; famously **stolen from Miami International on the night of
  29 November 1979** and recovered the same night at Fort Lauderdale. Now used for
  aeronautical firefighter training at MSLP. Consumed as a training prop, not presented.
- **Bell UH-1H FAS 288** (c/n 9153, BuNo 66-16959) — same site, same firefighter
  training role.

**Museo de Historia Militar, Parque Valle, Tegucigalpa — EXCLUDED, models only.**
An El Heraldo feature of 13 October 2018 describes an aviation room there curated by
Colonel José Antonio Meza Mejía, but everything in it is **scale models** — T-41
Mescalero, C-47 "El Brujo", C-101, Arava 201, A-37, Tucano T-27, C-130, Bell 412 — with
further models (Corsair, Mustang, T-28, L-410) in the colonel's own home. No real
airframes, so no site record. This is a trap for a lead-list-driven survey: the article
reads exactly like an aircraft collection until you notice the word "maquetas".

**Not excluded so much as not found:** no evidence of preserved aircraft at Soto Cano /
Palmerola, at the Academia Militar de Aviación (which Spanish Wikipedia places at
Palmerola, Comayagua, since 1980), at Base Aérea Armando Escalón Espinal (San Pedro Sula)
or Base Aérea Héctor Caraccioli Moncada (La Ceiba), or at the Museo para la Identidad
Nacional. Absence of evidence only — see open questions.

### Open questions, ranked

1. **Honduras outside Tegucigalpa is almost certainly under-recorded.** Twelve searches in
   Spanish across San Pedro Sula, La Ceiba, Comayagua/Palmerola, Catacamas and Juticalpa
   produced no monument or gate-guard record at all, which is implausible for an air force
   with four bases and a 1969 combat history. The Salvadoran picture only became clear
   because one enthusiast published a national inventory; Honduras appears to have no
   equivalent. **Highest-value action: contact the Fundación Museo del Aire de Honduras
   directly** — museo_del_aire@live.com, +504 9992 4543, facebook.com/museo.del.aire.hn —
   and ask both for a current airframe list and for what the FAH displays at its other
   bases.
2. **The T-41A on the FAS museum website.** fas.gob.sv/museo/aeronaves.html carries an
   image captioned T-41A, but no T-41 appears in Flotilla Aérea's national inventory. Ask
   the museum: museodeaviacion@gmail.com, (503) 2250-0070 ext. 1119.
3. **Coordinates for six sites** (Plaza de la Aviación; the paratrooper C-47 monument on
   Boulevard del Ejército; Plaza Bicentenario on Boulevard Luis Poma, Antiguo Cuscatlán;
   the Perquín museum; the AAC compound; the Ilopango base unit displays). All are
   physically findable on satellite imagery by someone able to verify what they are
   looking at; none should be guessed from a street name.
4. **Kingcobra FAH-402 vs FAH-403 / 43-11732** at the Museo del Aire. Resolvable with one
   dated photograph of the tail.
5. **The Museo del Aire's unregistered airframes** — Twin Bonanza, C-46 Commando, DC-6.
   The C-46 (possibly 44-78708) and DC-6 (possibly BuNo 152688, which would make it a
   C-118B) need first-hand confirmation.
6. **Perquín's helicopter: serial and variant.** The museum should be able to say what the
   wreck is; UH-1H is currently inferred, not confirmed.
7. **Current status of the two park Magisters.** FAS 507 in the Parque Infantil is sourced
   to its 2012 installation with no later confirmation. FAS 503 at Plaza Bicentenario is
   solid to 31 August 2024.
8. **Museo del Aire opening hours conflict** between the Spanish Wikipedia figures and the
   2022 press reports. Most recent evidence for the museum overall is October 2022 —
   the oldest "current status" date in this file and the one most worth refreshing.
9. **Boeing 727 at Tin Marín: identity unknown.** No registration, c/n or line number has
   ever been published. The museum (tinmarin.org) may hold acquisition paperwork from 1999.
