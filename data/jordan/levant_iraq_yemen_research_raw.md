## BLOCK 3 — RESEARCH NOTES

### Sources and the weight given to each

**spottingmode.com/wro was the backbone**, as expected — reached with `curl --ciphers "DEFAULT:@SECLEVEL=0"`, which worked first time. All five countries are present in it. Its value here is the per-airframe status code, which does most of the display-versus-derelict work automatically: I treated `pre` as a display candidate, and `std`, `dum`, `dlt` and `i/a` as automatic rejections pending contrary evidence. Its per-location "last update" dates are the difference between a usable record and a stale one — Jordan has entries refreshed on 25 January 2026, while much of Syria and Yemen is frozen at 2013–2018.

**The International F-104 Society (i-f-s.nl) was the single most valuable specialist source for Jordan** and outranked spottingmode wherever the two disagreed. Jordan's preserved population is dominated by ex-9 Squadron F-104s and the F-104 society tracks them by construction number with provenance. WebFetch was blocked by a robots.txt timeout; plain `curl` with a browser user-agent worked.

**Esri World Imagery was decisive and is the reason several rows exist at all.** The `identify` endpoint returns per-tile acquisition dates, and I fetched and actually looked at the tiles rather than just reading dates. Acquisition dates obtained: Baghdad 30 Apr 2025; Aleppo 23 Apr 2024; Damascus 31 May 2025; Amman/Marka 18 Sep 2024; Amman west, Madaba, Ajloun, Royal Automobile Museum 7 May 2025; Mafraq/King Hussein 3 Jul 2025; Prince Hassan 5 Jun 2025; Halabja 9 Apr 2025; Sana'a 7 Oct 2025; Rayak 2 Aug 2025; Aden 29 Dec 2019 (the worst coverage anywhere in the package).

**An OpenStreetMap Overpass sweep** for `historic=aircraft` / `memorial=aircraft` / museum tags found three things nothing else did: the **Iraqi Military Aviation Museum** node in Baghdad (`museum=aerospace`, node 7774624293, last edited 14 December 2025) plus six unnamed `historic=aircraft` nodes beside it; the Aden and Sana'a military museum nodes; and the October War Panorama's three mapped airframes, one tagged `destroyed=yes; ruins=yes`. Name-regex Overpass queries timed out repeatedly against both the main and Kumi endpoints — bbox queries on tag keys worked. I also pulled OSM node edit timestamps via the API as an independent currency signal; the Aleppo aircraft node was last touched 17 June 2025, the Madaba roundabout node created 24 March 2026.

**aviationmuseum.eu was treated strictly as a lead**, and it proved stale in a specific and instructive way: its Jordan page lists exactly one site (the Martyr's Memorial) and **no Royal Jordanian Air Force museum at all**, and its Syria page lists only two Damascus sites and **no Aleppo air force museum**. Where I used it — the ten Damascus Military Museum aircraft and the Sana'a MiG-17F — every row carries a currency caveat, because that is all the source can support.

**airhistory.net and jetphotos were unusable**: airhistory returns Cloudflare 403 to every request path I tried, so the dated-photographer-log evidence the brief hoped for was not available to me. Nominatim reverse geocoding substituted usefully for naming and administrative assignment, and confirmed the Baghdad museum name in Arabic and the University of Jordan / Jubeiha identification that settles the 912 dispute below.

### Country by country: what could and could not be established

**Jordan — established, and richer than the databases alone suggest.** Twenty-four sites and twenty-seven airframes. The gate-guard and roundabout population is real, well-documented, and mostly verifiable from imagery: I positively identified aircraft on current imagery at the Martyrs' Memorial (two of them), the Royal Automobile Museum, the Marka gate, Madaba roundabout, Kerak roundabout, Ajloun road, the Ramtha Hunter and the Bait Ali An-2.

**The one thing I could NOT establish in Jordan is the Royal Jordanian Air Force Museum at Marka.** This is the largest negative in the package and it contradicts the brief's premise. Four independent lines of evidence point the same way: aviationmuseum.eu's Jordan country page lists no air force museum; the Jordanian embassy's own list of fifteen national museums lists no air force museum; OSM has no such feature anywhere in Amman despite mapping the Martyrs' Memorial, the Royal Automobile Museum, the Royal Tank Museum and even the Hedjaz Railway Museum; and spottingmode's three Marka-area locations resolve to a withdrawn-fleet storage area, not a display line. The nearest thing to the brief's description is the International F-104 Society's phrase "preserved inside the memory lane at King Abdullah AB (Amman-Marka IAP)" for F-104A 918 — but spottingmode carries that same 918 as **std** (stored) alongside a stored C-212A and a derelict Boeing 737, which reads as a storage compound rather than a curated collection. Esri imagery of Marka from 18 September 2024 shows a working ramp with operational and stored airliners and turboprops, not a museum line. I therefore did not create the museum, and I did not create rows for the Hunters, Vampires, Mirage F1, Dove fleet, Tri-Star and Dakota the brief attributed to it. If that collection exists it is on the military side of King Abdullah II Air Base and is invisible to every public source I could reach; that is my top open question.

**Lebanon — established, small, and appointment-only.** The Lebanese Air Force Museum at Rayak is real, occupies a single hangar on the active air base, and is explicitly not open to the public: visitors must obtain permission from air force authorities. Nine airframes with full serials, seven of them with construction numbers. Two sources (spottingmode, aviationmuseum.eu) agree on eight; spottingmode adds Magister L-601 and is alone on it. Wikipedia's Rayak article adds a T-6 Texan and a de Havilland Dove that neither database supports — the Dove is contradicted outright (see corrections). Beyond Rayak, Lebanon yielded **nothing**: no Hunter or Mirage III on a plinth anywhere, no aircraft at the Beirut National Museum, and Beirut's only two aviation entries are a scrapped 707 storage line and a Boeing 737 fuselage prop.

**Syria — partially established, and the answer to the headline question is negative.** I found **no evidence that a Syrian Air Force Museum at Aleppo Nayrab currently exists, and only weak evidence that it ever did in the form described.** Neither aviationmuseum.eu's Syria page, nor Wikipedia's list of museums in Syria, nor spottingmode carries such an institution; spottingmode's Aleppo entries are two individual roadside airframes plus an apron dump of MiG-23s, L-39s and helicopters. What I can establish positively, from 23 April 2024 Esri imagery, is that **four displayed airframes in Aleppo survived the war**: the Il-28 in a landscaped plot on the west side of the airport, a MiG-17 on a pad by the airport approach road, a MiG-21 on a traffic island in Ramouseh, and a MiG-17 on a circular forecourt in the 1070 Apartments district — the last two in areas that were front lines from 2012 to 2016. Whether these are the dispersed remnants of a museum collection or simply municipal monuments, I cannot say, and I will not assert either. Meanwhile the **October War Panorama in Damascus is confirmed intact** — Esri imagery of 31 May 2025 shows the MiG-21, a rotor-bearing helicopter and the rows of armoured vehicles still on the northern hardstand. The **Damascus Military Museum is the weak link**: the institution stands, but z19 imagery of 31 May 2025 over both the Tekkiye and the Madrasa Sulaymaniyah courtyards resolves **no aircraft outdoors at all**. Its ten rows rest entirely on a 2015–16 compilation and every one says so.

**Iraq — established, and it produced the single best find in the package.** The **Iraqi Military Aviation Museum (متحف الطيران الحربي العراقي)** in the Al-Zawra quarter of Karkh, Baghdad, beside the Monument to the Unknown Soldier, is a real, current, curated open-air display. It is in no aviation compilation I checked; I found it through the Overpass museum sweep, confirmed the name by Arabic reverse geocoding, and then **saw five to six aircraft laid out in a deliberate display line on Esri imagery of 30 April 2025** — a swept-wing jet, a straight-wing four-engined type consistent with the Heron, and two or more deltas consistent with MiG-21s. OSM carries six aircraft nodes there. Only two of the airframes can be named. Beyond that, Iraq gave a PC-9 gate guard at Baghdad International and the Halabja Sukhoi. Everything else is wreckage, and there is a lot of it.

**Yemen — a documented negative, as expected.** Two records, both weak. Yemen's 70-plus spottingmode locations are almost entirely bombed airfields: Hodeidah, Al Anad, Ta'izz, Riyan and Sana'a between them hold scores of MiG-15/17/21, Su-22, Il-28 and Mi-8 hulks, several explicitly described as "in destroyed shelter" or "half destroyed". The **Aden Military Museum in Crater is real** (OSM node, Arabic reverse geocode confirms المتحف العسكري on Museum Street) but I found **no evidence it holds any aircraft**, and it gets no site record for that reason. Yemen is the country where the brief's expectation of "very little" is most accurate.

### Corrections, with evidence

**The Balila / University of Jordan F-104 identity conflict.** Spottingmode links serial 912 and construction number 183-1085 to the Starfighter on the University of Jordan campus at Jubeiha (32.00938N 35.87664E — Nominatim confirms الجامعة الأردنية) and links no airframe at all to Balila. The International F-104 Society records 912 (c/n 1085, ex 56-797) as the monument at Balila on Highway 35, and separately carries an entry headed "Unknown Jordan 2 — F-104A wearing '912' at the university campus of Amman – Jubajha, first seen in 2008, identity still unknown." Both databases describe the same two locations; only the F-104 registry distinguishes the aircraft from the paint. I followed the registry: Balila gets 912, the University of Jordan row gets a **blank tail number**.

**Prince Hassan F-104A 924 has probably moved and I did not record it.** Spottingmode places it at Prince Hassan air base on a record last updated in 2012. The F-104 society says it is now on a roundabout near the Knowledge Station in the town of Sabha, Mafraq governorate, ex Safawi-H5 base on a pole. I could not obtain a verified coordinate for Sabha, and creating a row at a location the more current source says it has left would be worse than omitting it. **Open item: F-104A 924, c/n 183-1122, ex USAF 56-834, believed at a roundabout in Sabha, Mafraq governorate. Needs a coordinate.**

**The Rayak de Havilland Dove is gone.** Wikipedia's Rayak Air Base article lists a Dove among the museum's aircraft. Spottingmode carries L-110, c/n 04299, at its own separate Rayak location with the status **dlt** — deleted, i.e. no longer present. I excluded it and did not create the T-6 Texan Wikipedia also lists, since no database carries it.

**The two King Hussein C-101s are self-contradictory in the source and got no rows.** Spottingmode's location description for 32.35225N 36.27262E says "2x C-101" and was refreshed 25 January 2026, but the only airframe linked, C-101CC 1164 c/n 109, carries the status **dlt**, and the UH-1H alongside it is marked "std / Removed". A location description saying two aircraft are present and an airframe record saying the only identified one is gone cannot both be right, and I would not guess which.

**Aqaba C-130 sinking date.** Arab Divers gives 16 November 2017; Divernet gives November 2018. Both are in the row. I did not pick one.

**Baghdad Heron 393.** Spottingmode's own remark on the airframe is "Identity not sure", so the tail number is blank and 393 and c/n 14105 sit in aliases.

### Judgment calls

**The Aqaba underwater aircraft: INCLUDED, all four airframes, as three sites.** The test in the brief is deliberate retention plus public presentation, and these pass it more cleanly than several things on dry land. They are not crash sites and not abandonment. The Aqaba Special Economic Zone Authority bought a Tri-Star specifically to sink it, stripped it of hazardous materials, and placed it; the Jordanian armed forces donated a C-130 for the same purpose; and the 2019 Underwater Military Museum is a curated formation of 21 machines arranged over 140 m of seabed and marketed under the word "museum". They are ticketed, mapped, actively presented attractions. The only thing unusual about them is the medium. Access is recorded as `public` because any member of the public can buy a dive and see them — that is precisely the test the brief specifies for `access_type`. I put the two Cobras at the Underwater Military Museum's own coordinate and the two fixed-wing scuttlings at their own separate sites, because they are separate dive sites at separate depths several kilometres apart.

**Museum-displayed war trophies: INCLUDED even though they are wreckage.** The Israeli Mirage IIIBJ and F-4E at the Damascus Military Museum, and the F-4 remains at the October War Panorama, are recorded as wrecks by their sources. But a shot-down enemy aircraft that a state has deliberately recovered, transported, arranged and put on public exhibition inside a museum is an exhibit, not battlefield debris. The wreckage exclusion in the brief targets hulks left where they fell or where they were withdrawn; it does not target curation. These are curated.

**Aleppo 1070 Project MiG-17: INCLUDED, with a caveat.** It sits on a circular paved pad in front of a war-damaged building. Deliberate positioning on a purpose-built circular plinth is the signature of a monument, so it is recorded — but the ruined surroundings mean I cannot vouch for public access or upkeep, and the row says so.

**Halabja Sukhoi: INCLUDED despite being dismantled.** Spottingmode describes it as dismantled and lying on the asphalt, which on its face reads like a hulk. But Rudaw reported in March 2017 that the museum brought it from Kirkuk specifically to add it to its collection of weapons used in the 1988 attack, and it is beside the Halabja Martyrs Monument in April 2025 imagery. Deliberate acquisition and deliberate presentation, poorly executed, is still a display.

**Ataq MiG-15, Yemen: EXCLUDED.** Spottingmode has it as a MiG-15 at 14.54559N 46.82449E. Esri imagery of 8 January 2025 shows an aircraft-shaped object in bare desert beside vehicle tracks and what appears to be a revetment berm — no plinth, no paving, no path, no landscaping. That is an abandoned airframe at an airfield, not a monument, and it fails the test.

**Suwaqa Lockheed TriStar N766BE, Jordan: EXCLUDED.** Spottingmode codes it `i/a` — in use as — not `pre`. It stands in the desert at 31.38577N 36.06593E near a Jordanian security-forces training area, in the same category as the Airbus A300 at the KASOTC special forces training centre at 32.03350N 35.97586E, also `i/a`. Training props are not public displays.

**Beirut Kidzmondo Boeing 737 fuselage: EXCLUDED.** A fuselage section used as a play structure in a children's edutainment park, with no airframe linked, at a venue that has since closed.

### Deliberate blanks

Tail numbers are blank on eleven rows and every one is deliberate: the Marka F-5E and the University of Jordan, Ajloun and Umm al-Aswad F-104s (painted marks contradicted or unconfirmed by the specialist registry); the Baghdad Heron (source flags its own identity as unsure); the Ramtha Hunter, Al Karak UH-1, Dabouq Bulldog and Bait Ali An-2 (locations with a type but no linked airframe); both Aqaba Cobras and the Aqaba C-130 (no serials published anywhere); the four Aleppo airframes and the October War Panorama Phantom. **No `year_built` is populated anywhere in this package** — I had construction numbers and USAF fiscal-year serials in quantity but not one build year I could source, and a fiscal-year serial is not a build year. Website is blank throughout; I could not verify a single official URL and would not guess one. ### Ranked open questions

1. **Does the Royal Jordanian Air Force Museum at Marka exist?** If it does, it is inside King Abdullah II Air Base and invisible to every public source. Resolving it needs a Jordanian armed forces contact or a dated visit report, and it would likely double Jordan's aircraft count. Everything the brief attributed to it — Hunters, Vampires, Mirage F1, F-5, Dove fleet, the royal Tri-Star and Dakota — is unrecorded here.
2. **What are the other four airframes at the Iraqi Military Aviation Museum?** Six are mapped and five to six are visible on 2025 imagery; two can be named. This is the most productive single lead in the package.
3. **Do the ten Damascus Military Museum aircraft still exist?** The negative imagery result over both Tekkiye courtyards on 31 May 2025 needs resolving one way or the other — indoors, moved, or gone. A single visitor photograph would settle ten rows.
4. **The second aircraft at the Martyrs' Memorial, Amman.** Esri imagery of 18 September 2024 clearly shows two airframes on the plaza; every source describes one Hunter. Type and identity unknown.
5. **Is the Amman Martyrs' Memorial Hunter really ex-RAF F.4 XF946?** Spottingmode gives a serial and a Hawker Blackpool construction number; the museum and Arabic sources say it is the RJAF aircraft flown at Samu in 1966, and Jordan's 1966 Hunters were Mk.6 and Mk.73, not F.4s. One of these is wrong.
6. **Where is F-104A 924 in Sabha, Mafraq governorate?** Needs a coordinate.
7. **What is the Baghdad University of Technology aircraft monument?** OSM node 8709359393, نصب الطائرة, at 33.31138N 44.44736E, tagged `memorial=aircraft` but also `material=steel` and `tourism=artwork`. Imagery of 30 April 2025 is inconclusive under tree cover. It may be a sculpture of an aircraft rather than an airframe, which is why it got no record.
