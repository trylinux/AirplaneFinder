# Colorado

24 museums, 201 aircraft. Greenfield — the state held nothing before this pass.

Built as a civilian pass (Wings Over the Rockies, Pueblo, the National Museum of
World War II Aviation, the CAF wings and a sweep) and a combined military-base
and monument pass.

## Sources and their weight

Three institutions publish airframe-level inventories that outrank everything
else: **Wings Over the Rockies'** exhibit pages (sitemap dated September 2026),
**Pueblo Weisbrod's** collection page, and the **National Museum of World War II
Aviation's** "Aircraft Collection Roster" PDF dated 30 April 2026, which
separates 26 flying aircraft from 10 restoration projects. **Aerial Visuals'**
location dossiers carry per-airframe coordinates for the Academy, Peterson and
Buckley, and its Colorado locator was queried in full for the monument sweep.
The **FAA registry** did the identity work for the flying collections. The
**NMUSAF loan list** was reachable in its April 2016 edition only.

**silverhawkauthor's Colorado pages** are stale and were used as a cross-check
only: they place aircraft at the Academy that are at Pueblo, credit Peterson
with a P-47 that is actually the WWII museum's flyer, and list a Fort Carson
"Third Cavalry Museum" inventory from before the 3rd ACR left in 2011.

## Corrections made

- **The Eaglerock NC2568 is recorded once, at Wings Over the Rockies**, whose
  own 2026 exhibit page lists it — not at Pueblo, where a 2013 transfer was
  reported but the museum's current list does not show it.
- **Wings' F-4E is 66-0287**, not the 66-0286 in Wikipedia's survivor table.
- **Pueblo's website misprints two BuNos** — the F-8A is 145349 (site: 145394)
  and the TV-2 is 137939 (site: 137936). Its "F-80C 49-1872" is painted as that
  serial; Aerial Visuals identifies the airframe as ex-Ecuadorian TF-872.
- **The Academy's F-16 is the full-scale-development airframe 75-0748**,
  repainted in 2023 as 84-1390, the 10th TFS aircraft Capt William Andrews was
  shot down in during Desert Storm.
- **Peterson's F-104C 56-0936 wears 56-0808; its F-4C 64-0799 wears 63-7589;
  its P-40 is a fibreglass replica** — the genuine P-40E 40-390 left for Robins
  about 1994 though it still appears on the 2016 loan list.
- **Buckley's two F-100s both wear other aircraft's serials**: F-100A 53-1578 is
  painted as 54-1897 (Maj Clyde Seiler's loss) and F-100D 56-3299 as 54-1953.
- **The WWII museum's P-38F "White 33" has left.** Re-registered 24 April 2026
  to Comanche Maverick Air, Houston (the Friedkin family); absent from the
  30 April roster. The brief's expectation of a P-40, Spitfire, Wildcat,
  Bearcat and Grasshopper there was wrong — none has ever been at the museum.
- **Spirit of Flight moved to Idaho in 2019** and is already recorded at Nampa;
  nothing remains at Erie.
- **The Fruita Huey is 70-16329**, painted as 68-16307, under which Wikipedia
  lists it.

## Judgment calls

**The Academy is `public`**: visitors enter through the North Gate with ID and
reach the Honor Court on foot from the Visitor Center. The B-52D and HH-3E at
the North Gate are a separate record because they stand outside the cadet area.
The F-100F at the Preparatory School is recorded with the main cluster and
flagged.

**Peterson is `appointment`**: non-DoD visitors must email identity details
three business days ahead for a pass. The museum is closed for construction
with a tentative 7 October 2026 reopening; the outdoor displays remain.

**Wings Over the Rockies' Block B** — eleven airframes (F-105D, F-101B, T-33A,
EB-57E, F-102A, AT-11, U-3A, Nord 3202, KR-1, Moni, Nieuport 28 replica) that
are absent from the museum's exhibit pages but photographed on site between
2011 and 2023 — is recorded, each flagged with its last photograph date. Nine
further legacy entries with no photograph since 2011 are not.

**The National Museum of World War II Aviation** owns only six airframes
outright; everything else is on loan, principally from Jim Slattery's Greatest
Generation Naval Museum and related LLCs, WestPac's principals, the Tobul family
and private owners. Loans are recorded where the aircraft are. Two Hellcat
projects with no published BuNos are recorded as distinguishable rows.

**Butts Army Airfield's three helicopters** rest on unreviewed Aerial Visuals
imports and are flagged as such; three more with no mapped position are not
recorded.

## Excluded, and why

- **Fort Carson's 4th Infantry Division Museum** — reviews confirm helicopters
  outdoors but no source identifies type or serial. Needs a site visit.
- **Spirit of Flight, Erie** — moved to Idaho.
- **Colorado Aviation Historical Society** — a society, not a museum; its
  aircraft are loaned to Wings and Pueblo and recorded there.
- **Watkins, Colorado Air and Space Port A-4C 148600** — a 2013 sighting only.
- **Rangely CNCC fleet** — college maintenance-training airframes.
- **Pueblo departures**: F7U-3 128451 (to USS Midway), F-101A 53-2418 (to
  Marana), RB-34 44-449 (to NMUSAF), PT-13D 42-17701. **The Pueblo F-14 has not
  arrived** — the fundraiser stood at about $65,000 of $245,000 in September
  2026.
- **Wings departures**: CH-21C 55-4218, FG-1D 92050, F-100D 56-3299 (now at
  Buckley), A-7D 69-6188 (to March Field). Its Star Wars X-Wing and podracer,
  the Boom XB-1 mockup, and the Titan engines are not aircraft.
- **Vintage Aero departures**: Fokker D.VIII N8105D (to the Military Aviation
  Museum, Virginia) and BT-13A N54679 (to Texas). Wikipedia's claim that the
  collection holds original WWI aircraft is wrong — all are replicas.
- **Academy departures**: the Minuteman III (removed 2008), the X-4 (returned to
  Edwards 1998), GF-104A 55-2967 and F-106A 58-0761 (the F-104 is now at
  Pueblo). A TG-10C and a T-38A fuselage are on loan to the Academy but have no
  known display location.
- **No airframes**: Schriever, Cheyenne Mountain, Fort Leavenworth-style checks
  at Pueblo Chemical Depot, the Otero Museum, Fort Collins-Loveland, Longmont,
  and about thirty towns swept in the monument pass.

## Blank fields, deliberately

`year_built` is populated from first-flight, acceptance or delivery dates and
from FAA YEAR-MFR on the flying collections. Blank where none was sourced —
including the NB-47E, the Neptune, most of Pueblo, and every monument. One FAA
year was refused: 1948 for the CAF Rocky Mountain Wing's TBM, which is not
credible for BuNo 53503.

Tails are blank where identity is disputed or unpublished: Wings' F-16D (ex-
Thunderbirds, serial unpublished), Pueblo's A-10A, J-3, Learjet 25 and F-4
cockpit, the WWII museum's N3N, D18S, CG-4A, Hellcats and Skyshark, the Academy
SV-5J, the Buckley T-6 (SNJ-5 90769 versus AT-6D 42-86196), the Fort Garland
T-33 (57-6560 versus TV-2 138064), and the Cañon City Huey.
