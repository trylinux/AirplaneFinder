# Hawaii

7 museums, 66 aircraft (65 new plus one pre-existing Zero, corrected in place).

Before this pass the state held a single stub under the museum's pre-2019 name,
"Pacific Aviation Museum Pearl Harbor", with one untailed Zero attached.

## Sources and their weight

The Pearl Harbor Aviation Museum publishes serial-level collection pages and a
blog that records arrivals by date, and those outrank everything else here.
Aerial Visuals' location dossiers for Hickam, Kaneohe and Wheeler carry
per-airframe coordinates; JetPhotos and ABPic supplied dated photographs (2016-17
for the bases, 2023 for the Hickam F-86). silverhawkauthor's Hawaii page is
mostly historical Hawaii ANG photographs and should not be read as a display
inventory.

## Corrections made

- **The museum was renamed in 2019.** The stub was updated in place — name,
  address (319 Lexington Boulevard, Ford Island), ZIP, website and coordinates —
  rather than duplicated. Its Zero is now identified as the **composite rebuild
  5356**, c/n 4461, painted as BII-120 of the Hiryu.
- **The Niihau Zero is a separate record** — the as-found wreckage of the
  aircraft Shigenori Nishikaichi crash-landed on Niihau on 7 December 1941,
  which happens to be the real BII-120 the composite is painted to represent.
- **Huey 411's painted serial is bogus.** The museum's own 2015 research traced
  the drive-train data plate to 68-16411; the airframe wears 69-15705.
- **The MiG-15 is a Polish Lim-2**, c/n 1B01524, painted as North Korean Red
  1524.
- **Serial conflicts recorded in aliases rather than resolved:** the F-86E
  "Nina II" (museum 51-2832, Aerial Visuals and Wikipedia 51-2841 — the marking),
  the F-5A (museum and Baugher 63-8393, Aerial Visuals 68-9085), the Stearman
  (museum BuNo 92468, Aerial Visuals 07103 for the same c/n), and the Hickam
  F-86E painted 50-0625 whose true identity some sources give as 50-0653 or a
  Canadair Sabre.
- **The PHAM O-2A is in storage, not on display** — reported on a pallet at Fort
  Kamehameha in May 2023.

## Access

Ford Island is inside Joint Base Pearl Harbor-Hickam, but the museum is a
ticketed public attraction reached by the free shuttle from the Pearl Harbor
National Memorial visitor centre, so it is `public`; private vehicles need a
base pass. Hickam's heritage line, Kaneohe's flightline displays and Wheeler's
Kawamura Gate air park are `restricted`.

## Excluded, and why

- **Naval Air Museum Barbers Point, Kalaeloa — closed.** Shut November 2019,
  evicted by the state DOT on 31 October 2020. Collection dispersed: an A-4E to
  Ford Island; DC-8-62 N799AL moved to the Kalaeloa soundstages as a film prop in
  February 2024; UH-1H 69-15708 relocated. The remaining hulks (P-3C 160770,
  UP-3A 152169, F-4N 152291, an HC-130H, SH-2F 150158) were slated for scrapping
  with no confirmation of survival.
  Five airframes were reported in 2021 as bound for **Castle Air Museum**, but an
  audit of Castle in September 2026 found **only two ever arrived** — AH-1W
  162558 and SH-60B 162102, both on Castle's own collection page and in the
  February 2022 arrival reporting, and both already in the database. CH-53D
  156964, UH-3H 148043 and a second A-4E rest on pre-shipment press only; the
  CH-53D and UH-3H were Pensacola loans that would have needed re-issuing to
  Castle, and no such re-issue is documented.
- **PBY-5A 46602** — loaned to the museum by the Navy in 2023 but physically at
  the Air Zoo in Kalamazoo for restoration. Recorded nowhere until it moves.
- **An A-4 Skyhawk** named in the museum's June 2025 suspension appeal — no
  identity or provenance published.
- **SBD-5 36177** — displayed at Ford Island 2009-2022; now at the American
  Heritage Museum in Massachusetts, replaced by SBD-2P 2173.
- **Tropic Lightning Museum, Schofield Barracks** — open, but its outdoor
  exhibits are tanks and artillery; the helicopters older lists put at
  "Schofield" are the Wheeler air park.
- **Wheeler's second cluster** (UH-1 70-27548, AH-1S 70-16098, UH-1 76-22594) —
  Aerial Visuals only, not recorded.
- **A UH-1A 59-1659 at Fort DeRussy** and **UH-1K 157200 at Ford Island** —
  single-source, unconfirmed.
- **Emair MA-1B, a Boeing F4B-4 replica, a P-38 forward fuselage and a Tiger
  Moth** at Ford Island — each seen on one visitor list, no serials, no current
  status.
- **No airframes**: Battleship Missouri, the Bishop Museum, the Honolulu airport
  (the Pacific Aerospace Museum there closed), PMRF Barking Sands, the Big Island,
  Maui, Molokai and Lanai. Hawaiian Airlines' Bellanca CH-300 is a private
  airworthy corporate aircraft.
- **USS Bowfin** is recorded for its Regulus I cruise missile only, consistent
  with the SRAM at Stillwater and the ACM at Tinker.

## Needs a human on site

- **Hangar 79 is mid-campaign.** A $51M restoration launched December 2025 and
  the 2025 "Lifting History" project plans to hang eleven aircraft from the
  ceiling; positions will shift through 2026. The F-4C was photographed outdoors
  in August 2025 during the works.
- **Kaneohe and Wheeler** rest on 2016-17 photographs. Nothing suggests removal,
  but nothing confirms presence either.
- **Kauai's A-4E 151036** — last dated evidence 2019.
- The **Hickam RB-26C** and the two contested F-86E identities.
