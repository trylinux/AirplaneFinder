## BLOCK 3 — RESEARCH NOTES

### Sources and the weight given to each

**`spottingmode.com/wro` is the spine of this package and by a very wide margin the best Israeli source in existence.** Reached with `curl --ciphers "DEFAULT:@SECLEVEL=0"`, its Israel country page carries **77 locations**, each with an 8-decimal coordinate, a category, a "last update" date and a per-airframe table of registration, construction number, operator country, service, a status code (`pre`/`std`/`dlt`/`dum`/`i/a`) and a dated "last noted" field. Across those 77 locations it holds **557 airframe records** — 218 `pre`, 286 `std`, 28 `dlt`, 21 `i/a`, 4 `dum`. Nothing else about Israel comes close. Critically, **its editors ran a satellite-imagery review of Israel on 22 May 2026** — sixteen locations carry that update date, one of them explicitly annotated "visible on latest GE image", one downgraded to "Removed" (Mystère IVA 50 at Ramat David), one flagged "still here?" (the Nir David Alouette), and seven brand-new locations (IDs 32601–32607) were created for airframes spotted on imagery but not yet identified. That is a genuine 2026 currency pass and it is the evidence behind every "confirmed present by the spottingmode Israel review of 22 May 2026" note in Block 2.

**Ynet (Udi Etzion), 13 February 2022 and 6 May 2022** — two articles that overturn the single largest block of data in the country. Read in full from the pages' embedded JSON-LD, not from search snippets. Weighted above every directory because they are contemporaneous reporting on the disposal itself.

**Hebrew Wikipedia** (`מוזיאון חיל האוויר`, `מנחת ראשון לציון`, `בח"א 21`) — used for facts English Wikipedia does not carry. It is the source that identified the Rishon LeZion museum and confirmed the Phantom at the Be'er Sheva branch of the Technical School.

**`iafe.net`** (Israeli Air Force Enthusiasts, Amos Dor) — the PDF **"אנדרטאות בהן משולבים מטוסי חיל האויר או חלקיהם"** ("Monuments incorporating IAF aircraft or parts thereof") is a purpose-built register of exactly the third-phase material this brief asked for. It produced the Tel Nof Super Frelon 006, named the Kfar Giladi Vautour "Pogrom", and explained the Rehovot and Karmiel monuments. Most of its ~25 entries are wreckage fragments rather than airframes; it is nevertheless the best monument index for Israel and the site also hosts per-type IAF history PDFs (Spitfire, Vautour, Meteor, Catalina, 707) that were used for cross-checking.

**ABPic** — 578 dated photographs at "Hatzerim AFB (LLHB)". Pagination is `/<offset>/`, not `?page=`. Its date distribution is 2004–2018 with **nothing after 2018**, so it corroborates identity but is worthless for currency. Used for the F-4E and A-4 construction numbers and to confirm Meteor F.8 06 c/n 2173 and the Auster VQ-PAH c/n 627.

**English Wikipedia's IAF Museum article** — used as a lead list only, but it is the sole source for the three Spitfire RAF identities (SL653 / TE554 / EN145) and the TBM Bureau Number 69355, and it carries the February 2022 disposal fact with its citation.

**OpenStreetMap / Overpass** — very thin for Israel: a bbox sweep of `historic=aircraft`, `memorial=aircraft`, `aeroway=aircraft` returned **only nine elements**. It added no new site but confirmed three (Technoda, Be'er Sheva college, Karmiel) with `tourism=attraction` tagging, and supplied the Karmiel memorial text naming Cpl Gil Blau plus its Izkor register link. Note: `overpass-api.de` and `kumi.systems` both failed through the agent proxy; **`overpass.private.coffee` worked**.

**Esri World Imagery `identify`** — acquisition dates pulled for 30 sites. Most of the country is covered by 2024–2025 imagery (IAF Museum 7 Nov 2024, Haifa Technical School 22 Oct 2024, Rishon LeZion and Rehovot 26 Feb 2025, Hulata and Ma'ayan Baruch 7 Jan 2025, Palmachim 26 Feb 2025, Tel Nof 24 Dec 2024). The oldest are Ramon Airbase (22 Feb 2021), Ovda (3 Jun 2021), Nevatim and Dimona (18 Aug 2021), Eilat (30 Dec 2022) — the Negev base displays are precisely where imagery-based currency is weakest.

**`aviationmuseum.eu` was consulted and used for nothing.** Its Israel pages surfaced in search but contain no construction numbers and no dating; every fact it offers was already in spottingmode with a c/n attached.

**Grokipedia appeared in one search result (Sde Dov Airport) and was not opened or used.**

### Corrections, with evidence

1. **The brief's "F-16A 78-0322, the aircraft that flew the Osirak raid" is not in Israel and is not the museum aircraft.** 78-0322 is **Netz 129**, one of four ex-IAF F-16A/Bs (129/78-0322, 220/78-0327, 250/78-0346, B-model 017/78-0362) transferred to the US contractor Top Aces as aggressors; Netz 129 was flown on Operation Opera by Hagai Katz, fourth of the eight to release. The Osirak veteran at Hatzerim is **Netz 107** (c/n 6V-4), the first F-16 to land in Israel (2 July 1980, Ramat David), flown on the raid by Amos Yadlin, later credited with 6.5 kills — a world record for the type — and retired to the museum in 2015.

2. **The Boeing 707 "Re'em" the brief expects to find on display no longer exists as an aircraft.** In February 2022 the IAF condemned 20 museum aircraft to scrap, the most historically significant being **707 serial 008**, the Entebbe airborne command post, which by then also served as the museum's film theatre; the stated reason was fear that its deteriorated structure would collapse on visitors. At the May 2022 reopening Ynet found **its forward fuselage sawn off and parked behind the display fence**, earmarked for a future separate Entebbe exhibit alongside a Hercules. Recorded as `in_storage` with the description saying exactly that. This is the single most consequential correction in the package.

3. **spottingmode's entire Hatzerim museum table is dated "l/n Oct 21" — before the disposal.** Its 103 present rows therefore describe the museum *as it stood four months before twenty of its aircraft were condemned*. The Ynet report names the condemned types as Ouragan, Mystère and Phantom fighters, helicopters, and transports "including a Nord". The museum still shows three Ouragans (49, 80, 113), two Mystère IVAs (09, 60), seven Phantoms and one Noratlas in the Oct 2021 table, and **I cannot tell which individual airframes went**. Every one of those rows is carried with the caveat in its description, and the Noratlas 045 row says so explicitly. **This is open question #1 below.**

4. **Three museum aircraft were not on the display line at the 2022 reopening and are recorded accordingly.** The **Avia S-199 D-120** and the **PBY-6A Catalina N285RA** were found by Ynet parked beyond the exhibition fence in an area closed to the public awaiting repainting (`under_restoration`). The **Mi-24D** was found stripped and awaiting rebuild by a visitor in March 2020 (`under_restoration`).

5. **The museum's three airworthy aircraft left the museum in 2022.** After the Stearman fire, the **Black Spitfire (2057 / TE554)**, the **yellow Harvard (G-BDZZ, displayed as 001)** and the rebuilt **Stearman** were moved to permanent parking inside the operational area of Hatzerim; they are brought to the museum only for Independence Day and Passover. All three are `in_storage`, not `on_display`.

6. **English Wikipedia says the Stearman "was destroyed in a ground fire in 2015". Ynet says it was rebuilt from parts of two airframes.** Recorded as a composite with a **blank tail_number** — 2752/52 cannot honestly be attached to a two-airframe rebuild.

7. **spottingmode gives construction number 4023 to two different F-4E(S) aircraft** — RF-4E(S) 498 at the museum and F-4E(S) 492 at Ovda. Only three were built. 498 keeps the c/n (it is the better-documented airframe); **492's row carries no construction number** and says why.

8. **The museum's F-15A is neither 398 nor 695.** It is USAF **73-0098** (c/n 0040). A spottingmode contributor established that it went to Israel and was allocated 398 but was never taken up in service; it is displayed in the markings of Baz 695 with kill symbols. Filed under the USAF serial with both Israeli numbers explained in the description.

9. **MiG-23 number conflict.** spottingmode gives **2786** with c/n 0390324522; the oldjets.net March 2019 inventory reads **2781**. spottingmode preferred because it carries the construction number; the conflict is stated in the row.

10. **Ouragan 80's construction number is contested on spottingmode's own page** — the table says 234, a contributor comment argues 218. Neither is asserted; both are in the description.

11. **The Eilat monument's type is contested.** spottingmode records a C-47, serial 040, c/n 6223, displayed as 4X-AES; the OpenStreetMap node 11 m away is named **"DC-4"**. Recorded as a C-47 because spottingmode carries a construction number, with the conflict stated.

12. **A large number of Israeli museum airframes wear serials that are not theirs.** Twenty-two rows in Block 2 record a false marking. Examples: A-4H 270 as 272; Mirage III 111 as 158; Kfir C1 568 at Hatzor as 529 (which is the museum's Kfir C7); Vautour c/n-41 airframe 12; F-15A 393 as 021; Ouragan 65 as 85; F-4E 331 as 119; Kfir C1 841 as 827; Alouette II 12 as 03 and 19 as 05; Harvards 1114/1125/1110 as 14/25/10. In every case the true identity is in `tail_number` and the painted number is in `aliases` and the description. Where the true identity could not be established at all — the Meteor F.8 gate guard "40", the Nevatim and Ramat David Mystères, the Ovda Cobra "115", three Kfirs, the Givatayim Tzukit "095", the Nevatim Skyhawk "116", the Revivim Super Cub "A-13" — **`tail_number` is blank**.

13. **The Argentine and Chilean airframes are foreign, not Israeli.** Mirage III **C-713** (painted as Shahak 159 with 13 kills, in mixed Israeli and Argentine roundels) is filed under the Argentine serial. Hunter **J-747** is ex-Chilean, displayed in Jordanian markings with no serial. The Vampire FB.52 is ex-SAAF **255** shown as Lebanese L158; the Venom is ex-Swiss **J-1516** shown as Iraqi 353; the Vampire T.55 is ex-Venezuelan **0053** shown as Egyptian; the MiG-15UTI is ex-Polish **307** shown as Egyptian.

### Judgment calls

**Access types.** `public` for museums with published hours and for street-side monuments; `restricted` for everything inside an active air base (Hatzerim base gate, Haifa Technical School, Nevatim, Hatzor, Ramat David, Ovda, Ramon, Tel Nof, Palmachim) regardless of the fact that they are heritage displays; `appointment` for the two private-airfield collections (Rishon LeZion and Habonim), because both have been visited by enthusiast groups by arrangement and the Rishon operator was reported unwelcoming to a group that had permission. The IAF Museum at Hatzerim sits **outside** the base security perimeter and is `public`; it reopened to the public on Monday 9 May 2022 and Israeli business directories list current hours.

**Site splitting.** Where one base holds physically separated clusters (Ramat David has three, ~1.4 km apart; Ovda two; Ramon three, 2–3 km apart; Hatzor three; Tel Nof three; Nevatim two; Palmachim two), each is a separate site record with its own coordinate, following the brief's rule that a single displayed airframe is a site record. Conversely, the four spottingmode locations inside the **Haifa Technical School** (14979, 20197, 21143, 32607) are one campus and were merged into one site.

**Coordinates.** Every coordinate in Block 1 comes from a spottingmode contributor's 8-decimal marker, except: **Ein Harod** (32.566, 35.390) which comes from the Amud Anan POI record and is ~100 m precision, stated here rather than dressed up; the **Be'er Sheva Technological College** which uses the OpenStreetMap `historic=aircraft` node for the Phantom (31.25469, 34.75684) rather than spottingmode's college marker 150 m away. **Three sites deliberately carry blank coordinates**: Hulata, Ma'ayan Baruch and the Palmachim Bell 212, because spottingmode's own text on all three says "exact location TBC" and its markers sit at village level — that is a guess at the spot, and a blank is the honest answer. The **Tel Nof Night Leaders Memorial** is also blank: the iafe.net register places it "at Tel Nof airbase" and publishes no fix. All 77 spottingmode coordinates were reverse-geocoded through Nominatim, which is how the Haifa, Rishon LeZion, Be'er Sheva, Karmiel, Eilat and Sde Teyman sites were identified; postal codes in Block 1 come from those same reverse geocodes and are only given where Nominatim returned a specific street address.

**Repaint versus identity** — handled as described in correction 12. **No replicas were found in Israel.** The nearest things are two composite/rebuild airframes (the Auster "VQ-PAH", the Tiger Moth "VQ-PAU") and the composite Skyhawks at Nevatim, all flagged in prose with blank or qualified tails.

### Open questions, ranked

1. **Which twenty aircraft were scrapped at Hatzerim in February 2022?** This is the highest-value unknown in the country. It affects up to 20 of the 104 museum rows, and no published list exists — Ynet names only types and the 707. A single dated 2023–2026 walkround of the museum, or one email to the museum, resolves it. Everything else here is secondary.
2. **What was the effect of the war on the base displays?** The Negev bases (Nevatim, Ovda, Ramon) rest on spottingmode records last noted 2013–2018 with Esri imagery no fresher than 2021, and Nevatim was struck by Iranian missiles in April 2024 and October 2024. Thirty-one of the 209 rows sit at those three bases.
3. **The seven unidentified spottingmode locations added on 22 May 2026** — 32601 (Ovda, "F-16?"), 32602/32603/32604 (Hatzerim, "6× A-4", "A-4", "A-4 and F-4 visible on latest GE image"), 32605 (Hatzor, "F-16"), 32606 (Ben Gurion, "2× B747"), 32607 (Haifa Technical School, "F-16"). Real airframes seen on imagery with no identity attached. Several are probably decoys or stored aircraft rather than displays; the Hatzor and Haifa ones are most likely genuine additional displays.
4. **Is the Nir David Alouette II still there?** spottingmode itself asked the question at its 2026 review and did not answer it. Esri imagery there is 21 Oct 2023.
5. **The Ein Harod Kfir needs a serial.** Two Hebrew sources give two different installation dates (10 Nov 2021 per Walla, May 2022 per Amud Anan) and neither publishes the airframe number. It came from the museum, so it is very likely one of the airframes moved out during the 2021–22 refurbishment — which would also help answer question 1.
6. **Rishon LeZion's light-aircraft rows are unresolved.** spottingmode codes eight further airframes there `pre` (CH-701s 4X-HFD/4X-HFZ/4X-HGO, MCR-01 4X-OTY, Sling 4 4X-HRU, S-12 4X-HDU, RJJ-1 4X-HUR) but they may be active aircraft on an active ULM strip rather than exhibits. Excluded pending better evidence; the museum's own site would settle it.
7. **What is the Habonim collection, and is it still intact?** Nine preserved airframes including an ex-Czech MiG-23ML, a Tiger Moth, a Fleet 1 and a Porterfield 35-70 — the best pre-1948 civil aviation group in Israel. All spottingmode dates are 2021–2023; no owner, no website, no opening arrangement could be established, and the location was not touched by the 2026 satellite review.
8. **The Mi-24D's identity and status.** Serial 4010 / c/n M344010 is single-sourced through Wikipedia to armedconflicts.com, the arrival year is given as both 2004 and 2011, and the only visitor report (March 2020) found it stripped. spottingmode does not list it at all.
