## NOTES

### Sources and their weight

**What carried each country.**

- **Hungary** was carried by OpenStreetMap. All three countries were swept with Overpass, but Hungary
  is mapped at the level of the individual airframe: 162 objects inside the Hungarian bounding box,
  with the big collections (Kecel, Szolnok RepTár, Dunavarsány, Budaörs, Vecsés) each broken out into
  one node per aircraft, many carrying `model:wikidata`, a `reptar.hu`/`aeropark.hu` deep link, or a
  registration. Second-line Hungarian sources that earned their place: **kozterkep.hu** (the national
  public-art register — gives erection dates, construction numbers and service histories for monument
  aircraft, and is the single best Hungarian source found for this work), **hu.wikipedia** (detailed,
  current collection lists for both Szolnok museums, including which airframes stayed behind at the
  old site), **hampage.hu** (an illustrated walk-through of Kecel), and **veol.hu** for the 2025 status
  of the Pápa store.
- **Switzerland** was the *weakest* of the three despite the brief's expectation. Only 42 objects came
  back inside the Swiss bounding box, and a third of those turned out to be in France, Germany or Italy.
  The country carried on **fr.wikipedia** (an excellent, internally consistent serial table for Clin
  d'Ailes), OSM, and the museums' own sites. See "EXCLUDED" and "Needs a human" for why the expected
  large population of plinthed Hunters, Venoms and Mirages did not materialise in the sources reachable
  from here.
- **Austria** carried on OSM plus **en.wikipedia "List of surviving Saab 35 Drakens"**, which turned out
  to be the highest-value Austrian source in the pass: it gives construction numbers and locations for
  twelve Drakens and cross-confirmed four of the five OSM Draken points independently.

**Compilations that proved stale or wrong, specifically.**

- **aviationmuseum.eu**: two measured coordinate failures in this pass. Its Zeltweg page gives
  47.19766 / 14.61374 — **11 km west** of the actual museum (OSM node 1617661077 at 47.19725 / 14.75314,
  which carries the museum's own e-mail address). Its Technisches Museum Wien page gives
  48.190941 / 16.177971 — **11 km west** of Mariahilfer Straße 212 (48.190895 / 16.317945). Its RepTár
  Szolnok page gives 47.167605 / 19.903486 — **21 km west** of the museum (47.16779 / 20.18022). All
  three errors are the same shape: a longitude that has drifted west. Coordinates from this source were
  discarded wholesale.
- **aviationmuseum.eu serial tables are column-shifted at Dübendorf and Luzern.** At the Flieger-Flab-
  Museum it pairs "Dassault Mirage 3RS" with **J-4204** (a Hunter T.68 serial — Swiss Mirage IIIRS are
  R-21xx), "Pilatus PC-9" with **J-3098** (an F-5E serial — Swiss PC-9 are C-4xx), and "Northrop F-5E"
  with **J-3202**. At the Verkehrshaus it pairs "Bücker Bü 133 Jungmeister" with **HB-ICC**, which OSM
  proves on the ground is the Convair CV-990 Coronado, and "Mudry CAP 231EX" with **U-288**, while OSM
  gives the CAP-231EX as HB-MSC — at Grenchen, not Luzern. Because the shift is not uniform (some rows,
  e.g. C-3603 / C-537 and Fi 156 / A-97, look correct), **every serial from these two tables was
  discarded** and only three Verkehrshaus registrations (HB-ICC, HB-IRN, HB-JRA, all from OSM) and one
  Dübendorf serial (J-4001, confirmed by hermannkeist.ch) are recorded.
- The same source's **Zeltweg** table, by contrast, is internally consistent: every Austrian serial
  prefix matches the right type (3D- Alouette II, 3E- Alouette III, 3C- AB206, 4D- AB204/Fouga,
  3B- OH-13, 3A- L-19/G.46/Yak-18, 5C- Vampire, 4C- Yak-11/LT-6G, 3F- Safir, 2A- Zlin, 5S- Skyvan),
  and four of its Draken numbers (08, 09, 14, 21) are independently confirmed by Wikipedia. Those
  serials were kept, each flagged in its description as single-sourced.
- **wings.hu** proved a useful Hungarian *lead* list of wrecks-and-relics sites (Alsónémedi, Békéscsaba,
  Bócsa, Börgönd, Budaörs, Csepel, Dunaújváros, Jakabszállás, Kecskemét, Pápa, Solt, Székesfehérvár,
  Tápiószecső, Tököl) but it is undated, several entries are known to be historical, and only Bócsa
  could be corroborated. It is recorded here as a lead list, not as facts.

   `addr:housenumber=Hangar - 8` and `email=mlfz@hgm.at`).
2. **Technisches Museum Wien coordinate** moved 11 km east to the OSM museum node at
   48.190895 / 16.317945, matching the published street address.
3. **RepTár Szolnok coordinate** moved 21 km east to 47.167791 / 20.180221 (hu.wikipedia and the OSM
   airframe cluster agree).
4. **Clin d'Ailes address corrected.** Every English-language summary calls it "Payerne air base". The
   museum's OSM node and its own website give **Route de la Petite-Glâne 62, 1541 Morens FR** — a
   Fribourg address, not a Vaud one. `state_province` is recorded as Fribourg accordingly, with Payerne
   kept as the city because that is how the museum names itself.
5. **Two Drakens moved off "Graz Museum".** en.wikipedia places Draken 07 and Sk 35C 35804 at a "Graz
   Museum". That is the Österreichisches Luftfahrtmuseum at **Thalerhof / Feldkirchen bei Graz**
   (46.9868 / 15.4480), not the separate Draken that OSM maps 5 km north on Eggenberger Allee in
   Graz-Eggenberg (47.0695 / 15.4075). The two are recorded as different sites.
6. **False markings flagged, not recorded as identity.** The Zeltweg gate guard is an ex-Swedish J 35D,
   c/n 35339, *painted as Austrian 25*; the Voitsberg shopping-centre aircraft is an ex-Swedish J 35J,
   c/n 35601, *painted as Austrian 16*. In both cases `tail_number` is left blank, the construction
   number and the worn number go in `aliases`, `operator_country` is **SE**, and the description says
   the marking is not the airframe's identity.
7. **Aeropark second Yak-40 conflict recorded rather than resolved.** OSM maps it as HA-YLR; en.wikipedia
   lists OK-EED. Both are carried in the row (registration HA-YLR, OK-EED in aliases) with the conflict
   stated in the description.
8. **4C-AH conflict recorded.** The same lead list gives the Austrian serial 4C-AH to a Yak-11 at
   Zeltweg *and* to a Yak-11 at Graz-Thalerhof. Both rows carry the conflict in their description.
9. **Zeltweg MiG-21 flagged.** The inventory lists a MiG-21bis "110". Austria's best-known MiG-21 was
   the aircraft flown to Klagenfurt by the Croatian defector Rudolf Perešin, and Austria returned that
   airframe to Croatia. Whether "110" is that aircraft or a different one could not be settled, so
   `operator_country` is left blank rather than guessed at HR, YU or RU.
10. **Novapark Graz second aircraft identified.** OSM calls it only "80s Flieger Bar & Restaurant". The
    hotel's own page names it as an **Ilyushin Il-62M**, alongside the Boeing 727 restaurant.

    (a 1944 build against a 17 June 1948 service entry). Per the contract a blank is the correct answer.

### Judgment calls

- **Hangar-7 recorded as a site with all aircraft `on_display`**, per the brief, even though the entire
  Flying Bulls fleet is airworthy and flying. Hangar-7 is a permanent, free, publicly-open exhibition
  hall that exists to display these aircraft, which is a different thing from a warbird kept on a
  flightline. Every row says "Airworthy" in its description so a later reader is not misled.
- **Clin d'Ailes Mirage IIIDS J-2012 and Hunter T.68 J-4203** are likewise airworthy museum aircraft and
  are recorded `on_display` with the flying status stated.
- **Mirage-Verein Buochs recorded `appointment`**, not public: the association's own page says visits
  are arranged individually through a request form, with priced modules.
- **Zánkai Haditechnikai Park recorded `appointment`**, not public: it is inside the Erzsébet children's
  camp and serves camp programmes, so a member of the public cannot simply walk up.
- **Militärluftfahrtmuseum Zeltweg recorded `public`** despite standing inside an active air base. The
  test in the contract is how a member of the public actually gets in: they arrive during published
  opening hours, walk through the main gate and continue 400 m on foot. The base perimeter airframes,
  which have no such access, are a separate site recorded `restricted`.
- **The former Szolnok museum site is recorded, not dropped.** Eight large airframes (Tu-134, Il-18,
  Il-14, Il-28, An-24/An-26, JA 37 Viggen, Hawker Hunter) are still standing where the museum left them
  in 2015. hu.wikipedia states plainly that the site "már nem látogatható". This is deliberate retention
  without public presentation, so it is `restricted` rather than excluded — a researcher looking for
  Hungary's only Viggen needs to find it here.
- **Replicas and reconstructions flagged in the description, not excluded**: the Kvasz I at RepTár, the
  Lohner-Etrich Taube and Pischof Autoplan and Santos-Dumont Demoiselle at Aviaticum, the Häfeli DH-1
  and DH-5 at Dübendorf, and the Lilienthal glider at the Technisches Museum.
- **Cockpit and nose sections recorded as full rows** with "cockpit section only" in the description:
  Saab 35J 35531 and Saab 105Öe RI-29 at Zeltweg, MiG-21bis 4029 and MiG-21MF 8111 at RepTár.
- **`Unidentified` used eleven times**, all at well-located single-airframe sites where OSM records a
  `historic=aircraft` node with no type tag. Per the contract these are recorded with a descriptive
  model rather than dropped.
- **The Breitling Orbiter 1 gondola at Château-d'Oex is recorded as `lighter_than_air`** with a blank
  `wing_type`, as is the 1796 French balloon Intrépide at the HGM.
- **`operator_country` on every Hungarian airframe is `HU`** except where the airframe genuinely was
  not Hungarian: the Il-2M3 wreck (`RU`), the Kecel MiG-23UB that came from the Soviet Air Force in
  1991 (`RU`), the ex-Swedish Viggen and Kecel Draken (`SE` / blank), the ex-Swiss Hunter (`CH`), and
  the Berekfürdő Mi-24D described as the first export Mi-24 (`RU`). This is exactly the collision case
  the field exists for — Hungarian MiG-21 tactical numbers such as 4605 and 813 repeat across Poland,
  Romania and the former USSR.

### Blank fields, deliberately

- **All Flieger-Flab-Museum and Verkehrshaus serials except four** — the only inventory found is
  column-shifted (see above). A blank imports safely; a shifted serial does not.
- **All Kecel and KOMO-Sky tactical numbers.** Neither the park operators, hampage.hu, the news
  coverage nor OSM publishes them for these two collections.
- **Coordinates for four sites**: Voitsberg Draken, Salzburg-barracks Draken, Berekfürdő, and the
  Közlekedési Múzeum store. In each case the source names a place but not a point, and a guessed
  coordinate would be worse than none.
- **`year_built` almost everywhere.** Only where a source gave a build, delivery or first-flight date
  (Pápa MiG-21 1971; the Flying Bulls fleet years, which are the manufacturer's build years quoted by
  the operator; Aeropark's fleet-entry years, labelled as such in the descriptions) is it populated.
  No serial anywhere in this file was converted into a year.
- **`operator_country` blank** on: the Basel Fouga Magister (Switzerland never flew the type and the
  real operator is unknown), the Zeltweg MiG-21 (see corrections), the Kecel Draken, the Aviaticum
  Demoiselle and SZD-22, the Novapark 727 and Il-62M, the FFA Museum's civil types, and every
  `Unidentified` row.

### Needs a human, ranked

1. **Switzerland's monument jets are almost certainly under-counted here by an order of magnitude.**
   The brief expected a very large population of ex-Swiss Hunters, Venoms, Mirages and Tigers in towns,
   schools, scrapyard art and restaurants. Only ~15 could be confirmed. The two most promising leads
   that could not be read from here are the **flugzeugforum.de thread "Gate Guards in der Schweiz"**
   (https://www.flugzeugforum.de/threads/gate-guards-in-der-schweiz.16645/ — the fetch returned only
   navigation chrome) and **swissair00.ch** (http://swissair00.ch/ — per-type pages for the Hunter,
   Venom, Vampire and Mirage; the site's TLS certificate is too weak for the fetch tool: "EE certificate
   key too weak"). A human with a normal browser could probably double the Swiss count in an afternoon
   from those two pages alone. Third lead: **hermannkeist.ch** "Ausgemusterte Mittel der Schweizer
   Luftwaffe" and "Ausgemusterte Flugplätze".
2. **Espace Passion, Sion.** clindailes.ch/espace-passion/ returned 404. Phone Clin d'Ailes
   (via clindailes.ch) and ask what is displayed at Sion, whether it is publicly visitable, and its
   street address. One call closes an entire missing site.
3. **Közlekedési Múzeum aviation collection.** The national collection came out of the Petőfi Csarnok
   in 2016 and is in storage at the Északi Járműjavító while a new Kőbánya building is built. A call to
   kozlekedesimuzeum.hu would establish (a) what airframes exist, (b) whether any are viewable by
   appointment, (c) the opening date of the new site. This is the single largest unlisted Hungarian
   collection.
4. **Berekfürdő "A szovjet repülőtér titkai".** soviet-airforce.com gives no street address. Contact
   the founder Károly Vándor through the site for the address, coordinate and the tactical numbers of
   the Mi-24D and MiG-21MF.
5. **KOMO-Sky 51-es Bázis (komosky51bazis.hu, +36 30 269 8306) and Pintér Művek Kecel.** Between them
   these two parks hold roughly 45 airframes and not one tactical number is published. Both are
   commercially operated and would probably supply an inventory on request. Same for **HarckocsiPark**
   (harckocsi.com — the site's self-signed certificate blocked the fetch).
6. **Zeltweg MiG-21 "110".** Ask the Militärluftfahrtmuseum (mlfz@hgm.at, +43 664 8876 3887) which
   MiG-21 they hold and its provenance, so `operator_country` can be filled.
7. **Voitsberg and Salzburg Drakens.** Two airframes named by Wikipedia with no coordinate. The
   Voitsberg one is at a shopping centre; the Salzburg one at an unnamed barracks. Both are trivially
   findable on the ground and would complete the Austrian Draken set.
