# SOUTH KOREA and TAIWAN — Museums, Base Displays and Monuments (single pass)

Both countries were at ZERO records. Sites are grouped by country: SOUTH KOREA first, then
TAIWAN (Republic of China, including Kinmen and Penghu). Aircraft blocks follow the same
order and are grouped under `### <site name>` headings.

`region` is the literal string `Asia` throughout, per the override in the brief.

Research was done in Korean and Traditional Chinese. English sources were used only as
cross-checks. The single most productive sources were the Chinese Wikipedia per-airframe
survivor tables (`中華民國空軍F-104`, `C-119運輸機`) and a 2024-2025 OpenStreetMap survey of
Taiwan, which between them fix roughly 60 Taiwanese airframes to a named location with a
tactical number. Korean per-airframe evidence is far thinner and most Korean rows carry a
blank `tail_number` deliberately — see NOTES.

## NOTES

### Sources and their weight

**What carried Taiwan.** Two Chinese Wikipedia articles did most of the work:
- `中華民國空軍F-104` carries a `除役展示機列表` (retired display aircraft list) of about 45 airframes,
  each with type; ROCAF tactical number; named display location; display mode (indoor / open-air /
  sheltered) and an access note. It is sourced to two Chinese-language F-104 monographs
  (王長河 & 葛惠敏 2021 ISBN 978-986-5568-29-0; 傅鏡平 2000 ISBN 9578628188) plus two Japanese
  Bunrindo titles; and it is internally consistent with the article's own 120-entry loss table.
  Several entries carry the kind of detail that only comes from someone who went and looked —
  the 2016 re-reading of the engine-bay data plate at Jiji; the wrong number painted on the
  Chenggongling airframe; the 2017 relocation from Shuili to Yunlin.
- `C-119運輸機` carries a 14-entry display list giving BOTH the ROCAF tactical number and the USAF
  construction serial for most entries; and flags the two it is unsure of.
These two are the reason the Taiwanese rows carry real identities while the Korean ones mostly
do not.

**What carried the OpenStreetMap side.** A Taiwanese surveyor (or small group) mapped roughly 60
preserved airframes across Taiwan between about 2020 and 2024 and, unusually, typed the tactical
number into the `name` tag — `F-5E(5308 Tiger 2000原型機)`; `F-104J(4519)` with a note that the
number painted on it is wrong; `TF-104G(4193)`. Where those names overlap the Chinese Wikipedia
tables the two agree every single time (4147; 4189; 4193; 4301; 4179; 4378; 4417; 4519). That is
two genuinely independent sources agreeing and it is why the Taiwanese confidence ratings are high.

**What carried South Korea.** Almost nothing did. The War Memorial of Korea is the exception: it has
been mapped in OSM to an exceptional standard, with serials; manufacturer; old_operator and linked
Wikimedia Commons filenames on nearly every airframe, and re-surveyed in October 2024. Boramae Park
was mapped by type in 2023. Beyond those two, Korean coverage collapses: Korean Wikipedia has
one-paragraph stubs for its aviation museums with no aircraft lists at all, and namu.wiki — which is
where Korean enthusiast detail actually lives — publishes type lists but essentially never serials.

**What proved stale or wrong.**
- The `정석항공관` (Jeongseok Aviation Museum) run by Korean Air on Jeju appears in every English
  aviation-museum directory. namu.wiki records that it was **demolished in 2025**. Its holdings
  included the Korean Air A300 HL7218 nose and gear and the 747 HL7463. EXCLUDED.
- The `中正航空科學館` / Taoyuan International Airport Aviation Science Museum, Asia's first aviation
  science museum (opened 1981-10-31), **closed 2014-10-31** for Terminal 3 works; its 18 real
  aircraft began moving to the former ROC Navy Taoyuan base on 2015-03-01 and are recorded here as
  a storage site, not a museum.
- The `國軍歷史文物館` / Armed Forces Museum in Taipei (Guiyang Street), which the brief names,
  **closed 2021-12-30** and demolition began in February 2025. It held no aircraft in any case.
  Its collection is destined for the planned 國家軍事博物館 (National Military Museum) which was
  approved in 2015 and is still not open. EXCLUDED.
- The `C-119L 3144` at the former 高苑科技大學 is recorded by Chinese Wikipedia as "reportedly
  returned to Pingtung and broken up for campus expansion". EXCLUDED as scrapped; the RF-104G 4378
  at the same (renamed) university IS still there and is recorded.

### Corrections made
1. **War Memorial of Korea C-119G** — this airframe is universally assumed to be American. It is
   not. Chinese Wikipedia records it as **ROCAF 3199 / USAF 53-3199**, supplied by the Republic of
   China on **1992-02-25** in a swap for a ROKAF C-46 destined for the Gangshan display hall; and
   records that after Seoul recognised Beijing on 1992-08-23 the ROC national insignia was
   **overpainted with a US star-and-bar** while nothing else on the airframe was changed.
   `operator_country` is therefore `TW`, and the US markings it wears are recorded in the
   description as applied-later decoration. This also identifies the Gangshan C-46 as ex-ROKAF.
2. **Chenggongling F-104J** — displayed as **4522**. It is **4519**. Both the OSM surveyor
   (修復外觀時，漆錯編號為4522) and Chinese Wikipedia (曾經誤植編號為4522) record the error
   independently. True identity in `tail_number`; 4522 in `aliases`.
3. **Jiji Military History Park F-104J** — displayed for years as **4303**. It is **4506**,
   established in 2016 by retired servicemen and ROCAF staff re-reading the engine-bay data plate.
4. **National Science and Technology Museum F-104J** — displayed as **4303**. It is **4522**.
   Note the consequence: the number 4303 was worn simultaneously by two different F-104Js in
   Taiwan, and 4522 was worn by an airframe that is actually 4519 while the real 4522 wore 4303.
   The ROCAF F-104J display fleet has a systemic mis-marking problem and NO Taiwanese F-104J
   should be identified from its paint.
5. **Pingtung AC-119L** — the airframe on the south apron is **3181**, not 3183. After a battery
   short-circuit cockpit fire on 1996-06-01 the unit repainted 3181 as the just-retired 3183 to
   dodge responsibility; a legislator exposed it and the wing commander was suspended. 3183 is a
   FALSE serial and is recorded as an alias.
6. **Jeju "F-86F from Red Muffler"** — the film markings from 빨간 마후라 (1964) are applied
   decoration; recorded in the description; not treated as identity.
7. **War Memorial B-52D** — OSM gives 55-0105 as the primary ref but the linked Wikimedia Commons
   photograph is filed under 50-1512. One of the two is the painted number and one is the serial;
   this is NOT resolved. 55-0105 is recorded as the identity and both other numbers as aliases,
   with the doubt stated in the description.
8. **Bagua Mountain F-104G 4417** — OSM places it 1.1 km from the C-119 that gives the park its
   name. Chinese Wikipedia places both in "八卦山風景區軍機公園". Recorded as one site with the
   discrepancy stated rather than split into two sites on a 1 km gap inside one scenic area.

### Judgment calls
- **Replicas recorded and flagged**: the Buhwalho at the War Memorial; at Jeju and at Sacheon are
  replicas (the original is at the ROKAF museum in Cheongju, National Registered Cultural Heritage
  No. 411) and each row says REPLICA in the description. The Yeouido C-47 is recorded as a replica
  because that is the more likely reading of a commemorative exhibition hall built around a 1945
  event; it is flagged as unconfirmed.
- **Test articles**: the AIDC Aerospace Park F-CK-1 row covers a static structural test model and an
  ejection-seat test model displayed together — not a flying airframe. `role_type` is `test`.
  The F-5E 5308 Tiger 2000 prototype is likewise `test` rather than `fighter`.
- **A fuselage is a record**: the Do 228-212 B-55561 at Beinan Elementary School is explicitly a
  fuselage only (機身) and is recorded as such.
- **Aircraft in another use**: the Boeing 747-121 at Deokso Church, Namyangju is recorded per the
  contract's rule about aircraft converted into other uses and publicly visitable.
- **Never-served airframes**: F-104J 4526 (Nanhua) and F-104J 4547 (NDU) were brought to Taiwan as
  spares-recovery hulks and never flew for the ROCAF. They are recorded with `operator_country` TW
  because the ROCAF owned and displays them, with the fact stated in the description. Arguably they
  should be `JP`; a later pass may want to change this.
- **`Unidentified` rows**: 27 rows are `Unidentified` + a descriptive model. Every one of them is a
  surveyed OSM aircraft object at a fixed coordinate with no type recorded. Per the contract these
  are recorded rather than dropped and no type is guessed.
- **Jaundae, Daejeon**: six aircraft-tagged objects were found but they are tagged `building=yes`
  as well, which in OSM usually means an aircraft-shaped structure or a hulk. ONE representative
  Unidentified row is recorded rather than six, because asserting six airframes on that evidence
  would be inventing five.
- **`access_type` choices**: Taiwanese base and school displays annotated
  "位於基地內部，僅有開放活動時始能參觀" (inside the base; visitable only during open events) are
  `restricted`. Ones annotated "參觀需徵詢警衛同意" (must ask the guard) are `appointment`.
  Municipal 軍人忠靈祠 (military cemeteries), 軍機公園 (military aircraft parks) and civic parks are
  `public`. The Goseong Unification Observatory is `restricted` because it is inside the Civilian
  Control Line and entry requires registration and an ID check even though it is a tourist site.
- **Kinmen and Penghu** are recorded with `country: Taiwan`. Kinmen is administratively Fujian
  Province of the Republic of China; the two sites there are flagged in their `state_province`.

### Blank fields, deliberately
- **`tail_number` is blank on nearly every South Korean row.** This is not an oversight. Korean
  sources publish type lists, not serials. The one exception is the War Memorial of Korea where a
  meticulous OSM survey recorded them.
- **`latitude` / `longitude` are blank on 14 Taiwanese sites.** Those sites are known from the
  Chinese Wikipedia survivor tables by name only and are not in OpenStreetMap. A guessed coordinate
  would be worse than a blank one — the Europe passes measured directory coordinates 12 km off.
- **`year_built` is blank on every single row.** No source consulted gave a construction; roll-out
  or delivery date for any individual airframe. ROCAF tactical numbers and ROKAF serials are
  allocation numbers, not build dates, and were not converted into one.
- **`access_type` is blank for the Beian Road F-86 display in Taipei** because the host institution
  could not be identified and guessing between "public park" and "inside a military compound" would
  mislead.
- **`operator_country` is blank** on the Yeouido C-47 replica; on the civil light aircraft
  (Cessna 172 at Jeju; Cessna 206 at Tamkang; FA-200 at Korea Aerospace University) and on the
  unidentified airframes at Ershui; Caotun; Tainan Juancun and the four low-confidence Korean sites,
  in every case because no operator is established.

### Needs a human — ranked
1. **National Aviation Museum of Korea (국립항공박물관), Gimpo.** The flagship modern Korean aviation
   museum, opened 2020, and this pass could not obtain a single confirmed real airframe from it.
   One phone call closes it. **+82-2-6940-3198 / https://www.aviation.or.kr/**. Ask specifically for
   the 옥외전시장 (outdoor exhibition area) inventory and for registrations.
2. **ROKAF Air Force Museum (공군박물관), Air Force Academy, Cheongju.** Two heritage-listed
   airframes are confirmed and nothing else. The Academy publishes a visit-request procedure at
   **https://www.afa.ac.kr/**. A single visit would produce 20-40 rows with serials and would also
   settle the F-51D and Buhwalho identities.
3. **Boramae Park Air Park, Seoul.** Eight airframes; eight types known; zero serials. Seoul city
   parks administration (**https://parks.seoul.go.kr/**) holds the lease agreement with the ROKAF
   and therefore holds the serial list.
4. **US Air Force bases in Korea — Osan (51st FW) and Kunsan (8th FW), plus Camp Humphreys.**
   The brief expected gate guards and this pass found none in any accessible source. The 51st and
   8th Fighter Wing public affairs offices would confirm or deny in one email each. Any USAF
   airframe found there is `operator_country: US`.
5. **The 2024 F-4E and 2025 F-5E ROKAF retirement hand-outs.** The brief is right that these
   postdate every compilation, and this pass could not reach the Korean news coverage that would
   list the recipients (WebSearch was exhausted; Naver; Daum and namu search are not directly
   fetchable). ROKAF public affairs, or a Korean-language search for `F-4E 퇴역 기증` and
   `F-5E 퇴역 기증`, would produce a fresh set of monument sites that nobody has recorded yet.
   **This is the single highest-yield remaining lead for South Korea.**
6. **Beian Road F-86 display, Taipei (25.0769,121.5271).** Two Sabres, one numbered 6134, 100 m

   checking: the ROCAF Command Headquarters area; the Martyrs' Shrine precinct; a Zhongshan
   District civic park. Google Street View along 北安路 would settle it in two minutes.
7. **Jaundae, Daejeon (36.4143,127.3470).** Six aircraft-tagged building objects in two rows of
   three inside a joint-service education complex. Satellite imagery would immediately show whether
   these are airframes or hangarettes.

### A note on the Overpass sweep, as instructed
`overpass-api.de` is blocked by this environment's proxy as the brief warned. `overpass.kumi.systems`
and `overpass.private.coffee` both returned **HTTP 504** for these queries. The **maps.mail.ru**
mirror (`https://maps.mail.ru/osm/tools/overpass/api/interpreter`) succeeded for every query, with a
browser User-Agent and the query POSTed from a file. Bounding-box queries were used throughout; no
`area["ISO3166-1"]` query was attempted after the 504s.

Yield: **71 objects for South Korea** and **85 for Taiwan** on the primary tag sweep
(`historic=aircraft`; `memorial=aircraft`; `memorial:type=aircraft`; `aeroway=gate_guardian`;
`historic=memorial`+aircraft), plus a further **196 objects for South Korea** on a Korean-language
name-regex sweep (`전투기|비행기|항공기|안보공원|군사공원|전투비행` and type-designation prefixes) of
which about 25 were genuinely relevant. `aeroway=gate_guardian` returned **zero objects in both
countries** — the tag is essentially unused in East Asia.

**OSM coverage of both countries is thinner than Europe's and an empty Overpass result is NOT
evidence of absence.** This matters most for South Korea. The brief's expectation that Korean civic
parks plinth retired aircraft "on a scale comparable to Turkey" is almost certainly right, and this
pass found roughly twenty such sites where the true number is plausibly several times that. Korean
OSM mappers have simply not surveyed monument aircraft the way Taiwanese; Polish or Czech mappers
have. The Taiwanese picture is much closer to complete because one dedicated surveyor did the work
and because Chinese Wikipedia maintains real per-airframe survivor tables.

### A note on the research constraint that shaped this pass
The session's WebSearch budget (200 calls) was exhausted before this task began. All research was
therefore done with direct fetching: the MediaWiki API against `zh.wikipedia.org`; `ko.wikipedia.org`
and `commons.wikimedia.org`; WebFetch against `namu.wiki` (which blocks plain curl behind Cloudflare
but is reachable through WebFetch); Overpass against the mail.ru mirror; and Nominatim forward and
reverse geocoding. Bing; Mojeek and DuckDuckGo all refused or returned bot-detection pages to direct
requests, so no general-purpose Korean or Chinese web search was possible. **The Korean half of this
file would be substantially larger with working search** — specifically the Naver blog ecosystem and
Korean news coverage of the 2024-2025 F-4E/F-5E hand-outs, both of which the brief correctly
identified as the high-yield material and neither of which could be reached.
