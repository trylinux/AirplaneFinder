## NOTES

### Sources and their weight

**What carried the region.**
1. **muzeumlotnictwa.pl WordPress REST API** — the single highest-yield source in this pass. The
   Kraków museum runs WordPress with a public `eksponaty` custom post type. `GET
   /wp-json/wp/v2/eksponaty?per_page=100&lang=pl` returns 725 exhibit records with an
   `typ_eksponatu` taxonomy (`samoloty` 138, `smiglowce` 21, `szybowce` 32, `silniki` 115,
   `inne` 13). De-duplicated across the Polish and English Polylang copies this gives exactly
   **191 airframes**, which is the roster used here. The `acf` field is empty, so the structured
   feed gives titles and prose but not a serial column; 22 of the 191 prose bodies do state a
   construction number, registration or tactical number and those were mined directly.
   *Probe first, scrape never* — this took one request to discover and eight to harvest.
2. **muzeumsp.pl (Dęblin) Yoast sitemap** — `eksponaty-sitemap.xml` (114 URLs, lastmod
   2026-08-24). The museum puts the **tactical number in the page title and the slug**
   (`lim-6-bis-nr-boczny-427-kod-nato-fresco-e`), so the sitemap alone yields an airframe-level
   inventory with serials. The page bodies are type-generic spec tables and add nothing.
   Its WordPress REST API exposes no custom post type, so the sitemap is the right door.
3. **pl.wikipedia per-type articles** — the decisive source for the rest of Poland. Articles
   `MiG-21`, `Su-7`, `Su-17`, `MiG-23`, `MiG-29`, `Ił-28`, `PZL TS-11 Iskra`, `PZL TS-8 Bies`,
   `An-2`, `An-26`, `Mi-2`, `Mi-8`, `Mi-24`, `Jak-23`, `Jak-40`, `PZL SM-2` and `PZL I-22 Iryda`
   each carry a "W muzeach" / "Zachowane egzemplarze" section listing **museum, tactical number
   and construction number**. This is how Polish enthusiasts actually record their heritage and
   it is far richer than any English-language directory. The `PZL I-22 Iryda` list is explicitly
   refreshed "stan wiedzy na rok 2025" and is the freshest cross-country list found.
4. **zabytki-techniki.org.pl** — a Joomla "virtual museum" project with per-object articles
   grouped by museum. Its aviation categories carry serials in the article titles for MPTW
   Warszawa, Muzeum Wojska Polskiego, Muzeum Orła Białego and Muzeum Marynarki Wojennej.
   Useful but **not current**: it still lists the four Gdynia naval airframes that left for
   Dęblin in 2012.
5. **sslwrp.goleniow.net.pl** — a compact nationwide MiG-21 census. Independently corroborated
   the Kraków MiG-21PFM 4205 and MiG-21MF 6504 against the museum's own pages where Wikipedia
   disagreed, and supplied the Zabrze, Rogowo, Muzeum 303 and Mrągowo placements.
6. **Museum own sites for current status** — muzeum.drzonow.eu (rebuilt 2025), wmn.poznan.pl,
   muzeumwp.pl, 1944.pl.

**What proved stale, and how.**
- `zabytki-techniki.org.pl` Gdynia aviation category (Lim-6bis 316, TS-11, SM-1 4028, Mi-4ME 042):
  three of those four have been at Dęblin since October 2012 (Dziennik Bałtycki 2012-11-12);
  Dęblin now publishes its own exhibit page for Lim-6bis 316. Only the TS-11 and the SM-1 are
  recorded at Gdynia here.
- The pl.wikipedia article `Muzeum Lotnictwa Polskiego w Krakowie` "Lista eksponatów" is
  referenced to museum pages accessed in **2008** and is materially wrong today: it omits the
  F-4E (2023), the Harrier, the Jaguar, the Sea Venom, the F-105D, the Mirage 5BA and the whole
  amateur-construction and NATO-project collection, while listing a Farman IV, a Levavasseur
  Antoinette, a Heinkel HE-5f, an LWD Szpak 4T, an LWD Żuraw, a MAK-30 target drone, a P-39Q
  wing, a PZL M26 Iskierka, a PZL S-4 Kania 3 and an SZD-18 Czajka that the museum's current
  feed does not publish. **The 2008 Wikipedia list was not used as the roster.**
- `aviationmuseum.eu` was deliberately not used as a fact source for any row.
- `lotnictwo.net.pl` hosts a per-airframe register of Polish military aircraft ("Spis statków
  powietrznych Wojska Polskiego") with build dates, unit histories and final fate. It is
  forum-threaded so throughput is low, but it is the best verification source in the country.
  **Access note for the next pass:** the site serves an incomplete TLS chain. Fetch the
  intermediate from `http://repository.certum.pl/cyberfolks2.cer`, append it to the proxy CA
  bundle and pass that as `--cacert`; also send a normal browser User-Agent or LiteSpeed
  returns 403. Do not use `-k`.

### Judgment calls

- **Replicas recorded and flagged**: Blériot XI (built 1967 by Paweł Zołotow), Etrich Taube
  (historical replica), RWD-8, SL-1 Akar (built 2014 by Warsaw University of Technology
  students), and the Muzeum Powstania Warszawskiego **B-24J Liberator full-scale replica**. Each
  says REPLICA in the description. The Liberator is a purpose-built museum object, not an
  airframe, but it is a deliberately retained and publicly presented full-scale aircraft and the
  brief asked for a decision — it is IN, clearly labelled.
- **Fragments and sections recorded**: Zeppelin-Staaken R.VI 36/16 (engine nacelle only, Kraków),
  Stinson L-5 (fuselage only, Kraków), SZD-27 Kormoran (fuselage only), TS-11 012 (sectioned
  instructional airframe), B-17 and B-24 fragments at MPTW Warszawa, the Fw 190 wreck recovered
  from the sea at Kołobrzeg, the AN001-04 Iryda fuselage at Rzeszów. All flagged in-line.
- **Composite / mis-identified**: the Kołobrzeg "Po-2LNB" is a Polish-built **CSS-13** made up as
  a Po-2. Recorded as CSS S-13 with the false identity explained.
- **Airworthy aircraft recorded on_display**: the Muzeum 303 TS-11 (SP-YII) is flyable but is a
  museum-held and publicly presented airframe. The Fundacja Biało-Czerwone Skrzydła Iskras
  (SP-YBC, SP-YTH) and the Fundacja Eskadra Lim-2 SP-MIG and SBLim-2 SP-UTI at Modlin were
  **excluded** — those are operational warbirds based at a field, not displays.
- **access_type**: museums with normal ticketed or free walk-up access are `public`, including
  outdoor military-equipment parks (Poznań Cytadela, MWP, Drzonów). University and technical-school
  airframes that sit inside a campus or school compound are `appointment` (LZN Wrocław, PANS Chełm,
  Politechnika Rzeszowska, Politechnika Wrocławska, Instytut Lotnictwa). Politechnika Poznańska's
  Su-22 stands on an open campus and is `public`.
- **operator_country**: `PL` for everything that served the Polish armed forces or Polish civil
  operators, including Soviet-built types. `DE` for the Deutsche Luftfahrt-Sammlung Berlin
  airframes and the Luftwaffe PWS-26 (one of two PWS-26 the Luftwaffe used — its Polish
  construction is stated in the description). `RU` only for the Grigorovich M-15 (Imperial
  Russian Navy) and the Soyuz 30 descent module. `GB` for the Sopwith Camel, Spitfire, Harrier,
  Jaguar, Sea Venom, Tiger Moth, Skeeter and the Liberator replica's commemorated operator.
  `BE` for the two ex-Belgian Mirage 5BA. `CH` for the Swiss Alouette III and Vampire.
  `SE`, `IT`, `US`, `CA`, `PT`, `FR`, `CZ`, `IN` as appropriate. `SU` was never used.
- **Polish designations**: Polish-built Soviet types are recorded with the **Polish designation in
  `model`** (Lim-1, Lim-2, Lim-5, Lim-6, SBLim-2, SM-1, SM-2, B-33) and the Soviet original in
  `aliases`, with the factory named in the description (WSK Mielec for the Lims, WSK Świdnik for
  the SMs and Mi-2). Where a museum explicitly displays a **Soviet-built** MiG-17PF rather than a
  Lim (Dęblin nb 305 and nb 948), the row says MiG-17 and the description says so.
- **Four-digit tactical numbers**: the derivation (Lim-5 "1809" from c/n 1C 18-09) is stated where
  the source supports it. It is NOT asserted where the construction number was not published,
  because the mapping is not uniform across factories and blocks.

### Blank fields, deliberately

- **Coordinates are blank for every site except Kraków.** The contract forbids inventing them and
  the brief specifically warns that directory coordinates run kilometres off. Kraków's are given
  because the museum occupies the whole former Rakowice-Czyżyny airfield and the location is
  unambiguous. Every other site needs a geocode from an authoritative source; this is the single
  cheapest remaining improvement to this file.
- **`tail_number` is blank on ~70 Kraków rows** — mostly the western NATO-project jets (Harrier,
  Jaguar, Draken, Viggen, F-104S, F-4E, F-105D, F-84F, F-5E, A-7P, Mirage, Fouga, T-6G, A-37B,
  Vampire, Sea Venom) and the pre-war and glider collection. The museum does not publish them and
  no second source was found in this pass. **These are the highest-value single-airframe lookups
  left in Poland** — abpic, airfighters and the Polish spotter forums will close most of them.
- **`tail_number` blank on Kraków Su-7BM, Su-7BKŁ, Su-20, TS-11 bis B and TS-11R** because
  Wikipedia lists *more* airframes of each type at Kraków than the museum publishes exhibit pages
  for, and picking one would be a guess. The candidate numbers are named in each description.
- **`year_built` is blank on most rows.** It was populated only where a source gave a
  construction, roll-out, first-flight, delivery or acceptance date. No Polish four-digit tactical
  number was ever read as a year.
- **`model_name` and `aircraft_name` are blank throughout.** Popular names sit in `aliases` per
  the alias rule; individual aircraft names (Drzonów Li-2 "Stasiek", Kraków Mi-2T "Wiarus",
  "Czerwona 1", "Irena") are described in prose because the source is a nickname in running text,
  not a formal name field.
