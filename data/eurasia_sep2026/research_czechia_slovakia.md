## NOTES

### Sources and their weight

**What carried Czechia.** Three operator-published inventories did most of the work and they are the
best material in this pass:
- **Nadace LHS Vyškov** (lhs-vyskov.cz/muzeum/letecke-exponaty/) publishes all 35 airframes with the
  *evidenční číslo* — the works number — for every one. That is a first-party inventory with serials
  and it is rare. It also publishes its own GPS to fifteen decimal places.
- **Air Park Zruč** (airpark.wz.cz) publishes a three-part numbered inventory — základní oddělení,
  speciální oddělení, depozitář — with a construction number against nearly every airframe, plus its
  own GPS in DMS. Its weakness is currency: the footer reads "Aktualizace: květen 2021".
- **Letecké muzeum v Kunovicích** publishes a complete exhibit list, and OpenStreetMap independently
  carries 31 individually surveyed airframe nodes at the same coordinates that match it type for type.

**What carried the monuments in both countries.** The **OpenStreetMap Overpass sweep** was decisive.
Czech and Slovak OSM aviation mapping is exceptional: nodes routinely carry `inscription` with the
placard text transcribed verbatim (including construction numbers — Trenčín's "L29-R v.č. 892823",
Modlany's "0602", Medzianky's "3156"), `start_date` with the erection date (Příbor 6 May 2011;
Bubovice 1 October 1986), `check_date` with a re-survey date (Raná 26 May 2026), and links to
Mapillary, Panoramax and freemap.sk photographs. Where a node carries a transcribed inscription I
have treated it as a read placard; where it carries `source=uhul:ortofoto` or

### Judgment calls

- **Czechoslovak licence designations are recorded in BOTH places, as instructed.** `model` carries
  the base designation (MiG-15, MiG-19, Il-10, Il-14, Bf 109, Me 262, Fw 190, Yak-11, Bü 131, Si 204,
  Bü 181, Fi 156); `aliases` carries the Czechoslovak designation and its dashless form (S-102/S102,
  S-103/S103, CS-102/CS102, S-105/S105, B-33/B33, Avia 14/Avia14, S-199/S199, CS-199, S-92/S92,
  CS-92, C-11/C11, C-104/C104, C-3/C3, Z-381, K-65); and the `description` states which designation
  the airframe actually carries where a source establishes it. The Avia S-199 "Mezek" rows carry the
  1948 Israeli connection in the description because that is the airframe's international
  significance.
- **`manufacturer` for licence-built types** is set to the actual builder where the airframe is the
  Czechoslovak product (Avia for the S-92/S-199/CS-92/B-33/Avia 14; Let for the C-11; Aero for the
  C-3A/C-104) and to the original designer where the airframe is Soviet-built (Mikoyan-Gurevich,
  Ilyushin, Mil, Sukhoi). Where a source does not establish which, the description says so.
- **Czechoslovak c/n-as-tactical-number.** Where the operator publishes both (Vyškov, Slavnica) the
  description states the relationship explicitly — e.g. MiG-21MF 9502 from c/n 969502, Il-14 3133 from
  913133, MiG-8T 0818 from 10818. Where only a four-digit number is known it goes in `tail_number`
  with no claim about the c/n.
- **`operator_country` and Czechoslovakia.** There is no ISO code for Czechoslovakia, so pre-1993
  airframes are `CZ` with the description stating Czechoslovak service where a source establishes it.
  Post-1993 Slovak airframes are `SK`. Presidential-gift aircraft at Košice carry the donor's code
  (CH, FR, IT, DE, GR, SE, AT, CN, RO, HU, BE, US, RU, UA, PL) because the *operator* was that
  country's air force. The Košice Su-15TM is `UA` because Ukraine operated it after 1991 and Ukraine
  gave it. The Air Park's ex-Polish MiG-17R, MiG-21R and MiG-21UM are `PL` — the operator's own
  inventory marks each of them "(PL)".
- **No `RU` operator_country was assigned anywhere in this pass**, because no genuinely ex-Soviet
  Milovice or Mimoň/Hradčany airframe was located (see exclusions). The Košice Po-2 is `RU` only
  because it is a Russian Federation state gift delivered in flying condition in 2004.
- **Replicas and mock-ups are recorded and flagged in the description**, never dropped: Kbely's A-10,
  two A-12s and the Ab.11 mock-up; Košice's B-534, SPAD XIII, Deperdussin and Demoiselle; Bratislava
  airport's Caproni Ca.33; Air Park Zruč's Fokker Dr.I, P-40 and the Spitfire film prop; Mladá
  Boleslav's Vlach 1912 replica.
- **Nose and cockpit sections are recorded**: Kbely MiG-21MFN 4017 and MiG-23BN 9863; Air Park Zruč's
  Tu-134 OK-DFI nose; Kunovice's two L-610 fuselages; Kbely's L-159A 6027 fuselage.
- **An aircraft converted into a café or restaurant and publicly visitable is a display record**, per
  the contract. Two are recorded: the Il-18 café at Bakov nad Jizerou and Reštaurácia Lietadlo
  (Il-14 "3156") at Medzianky.
- **Air Park Zruč's depozitář** contains 31 aviation entries of which 20 are complete or near-complete
  airframes with serials. Those 20 are recorded with `display_status: in_storage`. Loose wheels,
  propellers, wings and engine parts are not aircraft and are not recorded. The B-33/Il-10 5542, which
  the operator's own inventory calls a *vrak* (wreck), is recorded `under_restoration` rather than
  `on_display` — it is a retained airframe in a museum collection, not a scrap-line hulk.
- **Airworthy aircraft are recorded `on_display` where they belong to a museum collection** — Kbely's
  Aero C-104S — per the contract's convention. Kbely's other flying aircraft (L-200 Morava, Zlín Z-126,
  Zlín Z-226) are *not* recorded because no serial or registration for them is published anywhere I
  could reach; a row with a blank identity at a museum with 275 aircraft adds nothing.
- **access_type judgments.** `public` where a member of the public walks up or buys a ticket. `restricted`
  for Čáslav (VHÚ states the garrison museum is inside the closed compound of the 21st Tactical Air Force
  Base and requires prior arrangement), Náměšť, Prešov air base, the Záhorie military district, the
  Bratislava Police Academy, and the Caproni at Bratislava airport (the map object records it as
  `airside=yes`, i.e. past security). `appointment` for the private yard at Orechová Potôň.
- **Site names.** Every generic name carries a ` -- <City>` suffix: Národní technické muzeum -- Praha,
  Letecké muzeum Metoděje Vlacha -- Mladá Boleslav, the monument sites, and the Slovak displays.
  Site names that are already distinctive (Letecké muzeum Kbely, Air Park Zruč u Plzně, Múzeum
  letectva Košice) are left alone.
- **`Unidentified` rows.** Fifteen airframes across nine sites are mapped but untyped. Each is recorded
  as `Unidentified` with a descriptive model and every other identity field blank, per the contract,
  with the OSM node ids in the description so a later pass can go straight to them. That is deliberate:
  a blank imports safely and a dropped site gets re-researched from scratch next year.

  exclusion in this file, and the reason is fresh. Founded in Olomouc in July 2009 by Zdeněk Svobodník;
  evicted when Olomouc city terminated the hangar lease in 2015; reopened at the Radarka site near
  Koněšín (okres Třebíč) in July 2016. iDNES reported on **7 November 2025** ("Unikátní letecké muzeum
  končí. Co bude s exponáty, zatím není jasné") that the museum is finishing and the fate of the
  exhibits is unclear; the Czech Wikipedia article, last checked 5 June 2026, records that the Studenec
  sawmill depot was cleared completely in November 2025 and that **the museum lost the Radarka site on

  else — XL-29 č.003, XL-39 č.007, XL-410 OK-020, XL-610 OK-UZB, Ae-270 č.0010, MiG-21 trainer TL-21

### Blank fields, deliberately

- **`year_built` is blank on all but two rows** (the Bratislava Police Mi-2, built 1987 per the
  operator's own video, and the Soyuz 28 capsule, flown 1978). Czechoslovak and Soviet construction
  numbers do encode build blocks, but decoding them into a year without a published cross-reference
  would be a guess dressed as a fact. Where a c/n is known it is in `aliases` so a later pass can
  decode it properly. **No US fiscal-year serial has been used as a year** — the Kbely T-33A 53-5211
  and F-5E 73-0878 both have blank `year_built`.
- **`tail_number` is blank on roughly 90 rows.** For Košice this is deliberate and total for the
  Soviet-type airframes, because the only serial list available is the column-shifted one. For the
  monuments it is because OSM records a type and not a number. A blank here is the correct answer.
- **`postal_code` is blank on no site but several are the municipality's general code** rather than a
  building-specific one; treat them as navigational, not postal.
- **`operator_country` is blank on the Unidentified rows and on three Czech monument rows** where the
  airframe's nationality genuinely cannot be inferred, and on the Cessna 150 and Cessna 402B at Zruč
  and Kunovice which have no known national operator.
- **Vojenské historické múzeum Piešťany has a site record and NO aircraft rows.** That is deliberate.
  The museum reopened its rebuilt permanent exhibition on 8 September 2026 — two days before this pass —
  and VHÚ SR publishes no per-airframe list. Recording a pre-modernisation roster would have imported
  stale data into a database that had none.
