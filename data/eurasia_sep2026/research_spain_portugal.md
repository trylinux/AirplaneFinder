## NOTES

#### Sources and their weight

**What carried the region**
1. **An OpenStreetMap Overpass sweep** was the single highest-yield source and produced roughly 60 percent of the sites here. 184 elements were returned for `historic=aircraft`, `memorial=aircraft`, `memorial:type=aircraft` and `aeroway=gate_guardian` across mainland Iberia (35.9,-9.6 to 43.9,3.4), the Canaries (27.4,-18.4 to 29.6,-13.2), the Azores (36.8,-31.4 to 39.9,-24.9) and Madeira (32.5,-17.4 to 33.2,-16.2); 164 fell inside Spain or Portugal after French border tiles were dropped. Every point was reverse-geocoded through Nominatim at zoom 14 and the ambiguous ones again at zoom 18, and a follow-up Overpass `around` query was used to identify the institution enclosing four unnamed clusters. Spanish and Portuguese OSM mappers record far more than presence: several nodes carry the full monument inscription, the installation date, the previous Luftwaffe serial, the construction number, or an explicit `ruins=no`.
2. **outono.net/elentir 2025-04-02**, "Los cazas F-4 Phantom II españoles, su avión cisterna y el triste final de muchos de ellos" — the best single Spanish source found. It gives the dual serial of every preserved Spanish Phantom with its location, and it is only seventeen months old. It supplied 14 of the serialled Spanish rows.
3. **pasionporvolar.com "Aeronaves conservadas en Valencia"** — a province-level Spanish survey that gives both halves of the dual serial and, critically, flags where a displayed serial differs from the true identity (the Valencia Sabre and the Valencia Kiowa). Its weakness is that it is undated and appears to be the only province in that series.
4. **Official military and municipal sources**: ejercitodelaireydelespacio.defensa.gob.es and patrimoniocultural.defensa.gob.es for Cuatro Vientos; museudoar.pt for the three Portuguese sites (current addresses, GPS, prices, and the Alverca opening hours); ejercito.defensa.gob.es for the Toledo Bo 105 (Oct 2024); ayto-torrejon.es for the 2013 Phantom roundabout; emfa.pt for the Beja Alpha Jet monument (Oct 2019).
5. **Dated specialist and regional press**: defensa.com (León academy 2021-08-01; Getafe Super Saeta), infodefensa (Albacete Mirage restoration 2024-03-10), La Mira (Sant Pere dels Arquells 2026-09-05), O Mirante (Alverca T-38 2019-08-20), Observador/DN/Visão (Museu de Marinha Santa Cruz 2021-09-25), madridactual.es and Noticias para Municipios (Getafe 2023).

**What proved stale or unreliable**
- **The English Wikipedia "Museu do Ar" per-site aircraft lists** are the only published per-site inventory and are heavily used above, but they are stale and partly derived from `aviationmuseum.eu` and from Força Aérea pages archived in **2012-2013**. Concretely: the museum's own current Alverca page describes the indoor holdings as documents, trophies, a colonial-war collection, aircraft armament, a coin collection, **one Alouette II** and models — nothing like the 21-aircraft list Wikipedia gives for Alverca. The most likely explanation is that most of the Alverca airframes moved to Sintra when that site opened in 2009 and the list was never re-cut. Four Alverca airframes (T-38, F-86, A-7P, T-37) ARE independently confirmed by a recent OSM survey of the museum grounds and are the ones I would trust.
- The same Wikipedia lists contain an internal contradiction: **Cessna T-37C serial 2420 is given for both Sintra and Alverca**. Both rows are kept with the conflict stated in the description rather than silently dropping one.
- **es.wikipedia's Museo de Aeronáutica y Astronáutica article carries no aircraft inventory at all**, and the ministry's own "Colecciones" page is descriptive rather than catalogued. There is no structured feed and no published inventory for a museum of about 200 airframes. The English article's 45-row "selected aircraft" table is the only serialled list in existence that I found.
- **aviationmuseum.eu was not used as a fact anywhere.** It appears only as an ancestor of the Wikipedia Museu do Ar lists, which is flagged above.
- **Grokipedia** surfaced in a search result for Monte Real Air Base and was not opened or used.

#### Corrections made

- **Museo del Aire, "HA-200 Saeta A.10C-104"** → corrected to **Hispano Aviación HA-220 Super Saeta**. In the Spanish designation system the HA-200A is E.14, the HA-200D is A.10B and the HA-220 Super Saeta is **A.10C**. The serial the source itself quotes therefore contradicts the type it names, and the independent OSM survey of the outdoor park records an HA-220 Super Saeta on site.
- **Museo del Aire B-25J "130338"** → tail number left **blank**. 130338 is not a valid USAAF B-25 serial (they run 43-xxxxx / 44-xxxxx) and Spain never operated the B-25, so the marking is treated as false and carried as an alias.
- **Museo del Aire MiG-21 "22-26"** → tail number and `operator_country` both left **blank**. English Wikipedia calls it East German; a Russian press account (RBTH, Nov 2017) describes the museum's seven Soviet-built aircraft as arriving from Russia in 2017. The two accounts cannot both be right and neither was confirmed.
- **Valencia Parque del Oeste F-86F** → `tail_number` set to **C.5-101**, with the worn **C.5-5 / 1-005** carried as aliases, because the source explicitly says the formal identity differs from the marking.
- **Valencia Museo Histórico Militar OH-58A** → `tail_number` set to **HR.12B-17**, with the displayed **HR.12-12 / ET-126** carried as aliases, on the same reasoning.
- **Getafe Plaza de Pinto** → OSM says "HA-200 SAETA"; defensa.com, madridactual.es and Noticias para Municipios all say **Súper Saeta**. Recorded as **HA-220** with the discrepancy stated.
- **Museu do Ar "Hawker Hurricane IIIb"** → recorded as **Hurricane IIb**. The Hurricane III was a projected Packard-Merlin variant that was never built, so "IIIb" cannot be right as printed.
- **Museu do Ar stored Mirage IIIR "313" and CF-104D "104750"** → `operator_country` set to **FR** and **CA** respectively. Portugal operated neither type; those serials are French and Canadian.
- **Museu do Ar Spitfire ML255** → `operator_country` **GB**. ML255 is an RAF serial.
- **Colégio Militar Fiat G.91 "0547"** → carried as given but flagged: Portuguese G.91 serials sit in the 54xx block, so 0547 is probably a rendering of a 54xx number.
- **Spanish licence-built types** are named as such throughout, per the brief: CASA 2.111 = licence-built He 111, CASA 352 = licence-built Ju 52, CASA 1.131/1.133 = licence-built Bü 131/133, CASA C-127 = licence-built Do 27, CASA SF-5/RF-5/SRF-5 = licence-built Northrop F-5. The museum holds **both** a German-built He 111E-3 (B.2-82) and a Spanish-built CASA 2.111E (T.8B-97); they are separate rows and the distinction is stated in both descriptions.
- **Spanish dual serials** follow the brief: the type-code serial (C.12-37, CR.12-48, A.10C-104, HR.15-21, AR.9-064) is the `tail_number`; the unit code (12-29, 12-57, ET-140, 212-064) goes in `aliases`.

#### Judgment calls

- **Fundación Infante de Orleans recorded as a site with its aircraft `on_display`.** The contract excludes "airworthy privately owned warbirds based at a field". FIO is not that: it is a registered foundation whose stated purpose is heritage conservation, whose own strapline is "Museo de aviones históricos en vuelo", whose statutes commit it to "una exposición estática permanente", which is listed by esmadrid.com as a Madrid tourist attraction, and which opens to the public on the first Sunday of every month except January and August. Deliberate retention plus public presentation is satisfied. **The counter-argument is real** — these aircraft fly, several are on loan from private owners, and the borderline is genuinely close. If the database's rule is strictly "does it fly", this whole block should be dropped; it is contained in one site block so it is easy to remove.
- **FIO's location is a live risk.** An agreement with Airbus to move the collection to the Base Aérea de Getafe was signed in October 2024 and re-reported in February 2025. I could not establish whether the move has happened (fio.es blocks automated fetching via robots.txt). The site is recorded at Cuatro Vientos because that is still what the foundation's Wikipedia infobox and the Madrid tourist board give, but **this needs a human check before import**.
- **`year_built` was left blank on every FIO row.** The Spanish Wikipedia article's "entrada en servicio" field is the *type's* introduction year, not the airframe's build year — exactly the trap the contract warns about. The only `year_built` populated anywhere in this file is 1964 for the Palma CV-990, taken from an OSM `manufacture_date` tag.
- **Replicas are flagged in the description, not dropped**: the Museo del Aire's Breguet 19 GR "Cuatro Vientos"; Sabadell's Monocoque Hedilla II and three-quarter-scale Polikarpov I-15; the Museu do Ar's 14-bis, Demoiselle XX, Maurice Farman MF.4, Caudron G.3 and Blériot XI full-scale replicas (for the last three the museum holds both a replica and an original-numbered airframe, and which is on show at which site is unresolved).
- **Nose and cockpit sections recorded, and said so**: the Las Palmas DC-9 EC-DGB (walk-in forward fuselage, no wings) and the Museu do Ar's Boeing 707 9T-MSS cockpit.
- **Composite recorded**: Museu do Ar Alouette III 9258 with the tail boom of 9265.
- **`Unidentified` used 22 times** rather than guessing. Every one of those is a real airframe whose presence is attested but whose type nobody has recorded. Per the contract these are rows with a descriptive model, not dropped rows.
- **`access_type` was judged by how a member of the public actually gets in**, not by ownership: school-yard airframes (IES Barajas, Escola Básica de Alfeite) are `restricted`; the Sabadell museum is `appointment` because its own page requires advance booking with an ID number to pass the airport perimeter; the Museu do Ar Ovar núcleo is `appointment` because the operator names it as visitable but publishes no hours and it sits on a military installation; base displays (León academy, Torrejón, Zaragoza, Los Llanos, Son Sant Joan, Alfragide, Colégio Militar, Monte Real, Ota, INTA) are `restricted`; factory sites (Airbus Getafe, Airbus Sevilla) are `restricted`.
- **Sites with two or more airframes in one enclosure were grouped as one site** (Barajas airport, INTA/Base Príncipe, Torrejón air base, Zaragoza air base, Monte Real, Colégio Militar, Alfragide, Camino de la Malanoche). Airframes on separate streets in the same town were kept as separate sites, which is why Albacete yields seven monument sites and Getafe four.

#### Blank fields, deliberately

- **`tail_number` is blank on the great majority of rows.** Spain's two biggest holdings — the Museo del Aire's roughly 200 airframes and the León academy's 16 — have no published serial inventory. Where a type is confirmed but the serial is not, the type is recorded and the serial left blank rather than reconstructed from a series pattern.
- **`year_built` is blank on all but one row**, for the reason given under Judgment calls.
- **`operator_country` is blank** on the Museo del Aire's four Soviet-built aircraft and its B-25, on Sabadell's An-2 and I-153, on FIO's Po-2, Fairchild 24 and I-16, and on every `Unidentified` row. In each case the previous operator is genuinely unknown; the contract's instruction is that a blank here is the correct answer.
- **`variant` is blank** on many monument rows where the mapper recorded only "Mirage F1" or "Fiat G.91" or "T-33". Guessing a sub-variant from the fleet's most common mark would be exactly the kind of authoritative-looking error the contract warns about.
- **`wing_type` is blank on all rotary-wing rows**, per the contract.
- **`postal_code` is approximate** for Portuguese sites where OSM returned a district-level code rather than a street-level one.
