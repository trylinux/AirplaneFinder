# CHINA (People's Republic, incl. Hong Kong and Macau) — Museums, Base Displays and Monuments

Database was at ZERO records for China. Research done in Simplified Chinese (Chinese Wikipedia raw
wikitext via the MediaWiki API, museum operators' own .edu.cn / .mil.cn / .cn sites), plus an
OpenStreetMap Overpass sweep, plus a Wikimedia Commons filename/category sweep used specifically to
recover PLAAF tactical serials that no text source publishes.

`region` is the literal string `Asia` on every row, per the brief's override of the contract.

**THE HEADLINE FINDING IS A CURRENCY FINDING, NOT AN INVENTORY ONE:** the China Aviation Museum at
Datangshan — the flagship, and the single largest aviation collection in Asia — **closed to the
public on 9 October 2025** and is being relocated to Changchun. It is recorded here as a site with
`access_type: restricted` and every airframe as `on_display`, because the collection physically
still exists and the closure is explicitly framed as temporary; but no visitor can enter it today
and any row from this site must be re-checked before it is treated as a place a person can go.
See NOTES.

## NOTES

### Sources and their weight

**What carried this pass, in order of usefulness.**

1. **Chinese Wikipedia raw wikitext, fetched through the MediaWiki API rather than through a page
   renderer.** This was by far the highest-yield source and it is the reason the closure of
   Datangshan was caught. `zh.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=<中文标题>&action=raw` returns the full
   article including the reference apparatus, which is where the dated news citations live. The
   Chinese article on 中国航空博物馆 carries the 2025 closure and the Changchun relocation with three
   separate citations; the English article on the same museum carries neither and still reads as
   though the museum is open. **The English article is now materially wrong and anyone working from
   it will produce a stale record.**
2. **Chinese Wikipedia CATEGORY enumeration**, specifically `Category:中国航空航天博物馆` and
   `Category:中国军事博物馆`. This produced the site list far more efficiently than searching would
   have, and surfaced sites that no English-language aviation directory lists at all: 沈飞航空博览园,
   东北航空历史纪念馆, 中国航空工业历史博物馆, 立巢航空博物馆, 空军新疆航空队纪念馆,
   中国人民解放军空军编余飞机储存中心. Recommended as the first move for any future Chinese pass.
3. **Museum operators' own sites**, where reachable. njhkhtg.nuaa.edu.cn gave NUAA's current
   exhibit list, its address, its three-day advance booking rule and the fact that it now charges
   admission (fee notice dated 2025-12-24) — all things no third party publishes. caacmuseum.cn and
   airandspacemuseum.buaa.edu.cn were partially reachable.
4. **Wikimedia Commons filenames and category trees.** This was the ONLY way to recover PLAAF
   tactical numbers. A single photographer's 2012 set is titled in the form
   `<serial> <type> Chinese Airforce (<flickr id>).jpg`, and enumerating it through the Commons
   search API yielded twenty-odd serials at Datangshan that appear in no text source in any
   language: Il-12 35046 and 35141, Il-18 208 and 232 and 226, Li-2 3029 and 5070 and 8205, An-24
   71291 and 50954, Trident 50051 and 50055, Tu-124 50256 and 50257, Be-6P 9886, Il-14 4202,
   Viscount 50258, Tu-4 4114, An-12 B-1059, Convair 240 XT-610, DC-3 XT116, Il-62 B-2024, and six
   Y-5s (3129, 3139, 10996, 60066, 10953, 21397). Commons also produced the Beihang P-61's identity
   plate (P-61B-15-NO, c/n 1234, 42-39715) and the four **2026-04-01-dated** photographs that are
   the freshest evidence in this file, at Shenfei.
5. **OpenStreetMap.** See the dedicated section below — it did much better than the brief expected
   in a few specific places and exactly as badly as the brief expected everywhere else.

**What did NOT carry it.** Baidu Baike was unreachable throughout. Zhihu, the museum object
catalogue at kmycjng.com and several .gov.cn pages returned robots or connection refusals. No
Chinese enthusiast forum was reachable. **The web-search budget for this session was exhausted at
the halfway point**, after which all further discovery was done by API enumeration (Wikipedia
categories, Commons search, Overpass, Nominatim) rather than by search. That constraint is the
single largest cause of the gaps listed under NEEDS A HUMAN, and it is why there are no rows for
Northwestern Polytechnical University, Shenyang Aerospace University, Nanchang Hangkong University,
the factory museums at Chengdu / Xi'an / Harbin / Nanchang, Zhuhai, or the Tianjin Kiev carrier park.

### The OSM sweep — what it actually returned

Queried `historic=aircraft`, `memorial=aircraft`, `memorial:type=aircraft` and
`aeroway=gate_guardian` over China split into four bboxes. `overpass-api.de` is blocked by this
environment as the brief said; `overpass.kumi.systems` and `overpass.private.coffee` both worked but
BOTH time out repeatedly on quadrant-sized Chinese bboxes and had to be retried on alternating
mirrors with a browser User-Agent and the query POSTed from a file. Raw returns: SE quadrant 196
elements, NE quadrant 331, SW quadrant 104, NW quadrant 28. After filtering out Taiwan, Japan,
Korea, Vietnam, Russia, Kyrgyzstan, Kazakhstan, India, Pakistan and Mongolia, **398 elements fell
inside the PRC including Hong Kong and Macau — and 223 of those are a single site.**

Three findings worth carrying forward:

- **There IS a full OSM survey of Datangshan.** Nodes 12052761665 through 12053057023, mapped in
  2024, place **223 individual aircraft** on the open-air park and give an explicit type for about
  sixty of them. This is an independent second source for the Datangshan inventory and it is the
  spine of the list above. It also gives a hard count: 223 airframes outdoors, against the museum's
  own "近300架" and the relocation reporting's "149 types / 376 aircraft" (which will include the
  cave hall and stored airframes). The three numbers are consistent.
- **NW China is a genuine, well-searched zero.** The 36-53.6°N / 73.5-105°E quadrant — Xinjiang,
  Tibet, Qinghai, Gansu, Ningxia, western Inner Mongolia — returned 28 elements and **every single
  one is in Russia, Kyrgyzstan or Kazakhstan**. Not one aircraft node inside China in that entire
  quadrant. **Do not read that as absence.** The Urumqi memorial hall recorded above holds three
  aircraft and is not in OSM at all.
- **Outside Datangshan and NUAA, Chinese OSM aircraft coverage is thin and mostly untyped.** Roughly
  half the Chinese nodes carry no name, no model and no type at all. Those are leads, not records,
  and they are listed under NEEDS A HUMAN rather than being turned into invented rows.

### Corrections made

- **China Aviation Museum status.** English Wikipedia, and every English aviation directory
  downstream of it, presents Datangshan as an open museum. It **closed on 9 October 2025** with the
  notice "因建设任务需要" and is being moved to Changchun Dafangshen with a stated 2027 target. The
  site is recorded with `access_type: restricted` and this is stated at the top of the file, in the
  site's confidence line, and here. This is the most consequential correction in the pass.
- **"Ilyushin Il-2" at Datangshan → Il-10.** Both Wikipedia collection lists say Il-2. Every
  photograph of the aircraft on Commons is captioned Il-10 by multiple independent uploaders, and
  the PLAAF operated Il-10s, not Il-2s. Recorded as Il-10 with the correction stated in the row.
- **"Shenyang built DF-102 (MiG-17)" → MiG-19 family.** A 2012 Commons caption calls the museum's
  Dongfeng-102 a MiG-17. The DF-102 was the pre-J-6 designation of the first Shenyang-built MiG-19
  series. Corrected in the row, and the same correction applies to the DF-102 at Shanghai.
- **"Harbin Y-8" at Datangshan → Antonov An-12.** Several Commons captions label the museum's
  four-engined turboprops Y-8. The 2024 OSM survey types them as An-12 and An-12BP, and one carries
  the registration B-1059. Recorded as An-12 with `Y-8` in aliases, because both labels are in
  circulation and someone will search for either.
- **"歼5教练机" at Chengdu → JJ-5.** In Chinese usage a "J-5 trainer" is the two-seat JJ-5, not a
  single-seat J-5. Recorded as JJ-5 with the reasoning stated in the row so a later pass can
  overturn it from a photograph.
- **Y-12 registration is left BLANK because the two sources conflict.** Chinese Wikipedia's
  collection list gives the museum's Y-12 II as **B-3804** ("环球飞行", the round-the-world aircraft).
  Wikimedia Commons files the museum's Y-12 under a category **B-3884**. One of these is a typo and
  I could not determine which. Rather than pick, the tail number is blank and both are named in the
  row's description. **This is exactly the kind of one-digit error that would look authoritative and
  collide with the real airframe later.**

### Judgment calls

- **Datangshan recorded as `restricted`, not `public` and not deleted.** The airframes exist and the
  closure is officially framed as temporary. Deleting the site would lose 122 well-sourced rows;
  calling it public would be false. `restricted` plus an explicit description is the honest middle.
  Display status is left `on_display` throughout because no source says the collection has been
  crated; but if the Changchun move is under way in 2026 then some of these are in transit and a
  future pass may need to flip many rows to `in_storage`.
- **Serials are NOT matched to airframes where the count exceeds the certainty.** Three Il-18s stand
  at Datangshan and three serials (208, 232, 226/B-226) were photographed on Il-18s there, but no
  photograph ties a serial to a variant. Rather than invent three pairings, all three Il-18 rows
  carry a blank tail number and the row descriptions state the three observed serials and say the
  matching is unresolved. Same reasoning for the two unserialled Li-2s. **The serials are preserved
  as information without being falsely attached.**
- **Korean War MiG-15 markings treated as commemorative, not as identity.** As the brief warned, the
  Datangshan MiG-15 line wears historically significant numbers that are frequently not the
  airframes' own. No serial is asserted for any of them. The one MiG-15 serial in this file — 079,
  Wang Hai's aircraft at the Military Museum — comes from a Party-organ feature that treats it as
  the aircraft's own number, and even that is stated as sourced rather than confirmed.
- **Chinese designation in `model`, Soviet original in `aliases`, dashless form always included.**
  J-5 carries `J5;MiG-17;MiG17;F-5;Fresco`; H-5 carries `H5;Il-28;Il28;IL-28;Beagle`; and so on
  throughout, per the brief.
- **Replicas and reproductions are recorded and flagged in the description, not silently dropped:**
  the Wright Flyer, the Rosamonde (乐士文一号), and the Vought O2U "Lenin" (列宁号), which is a
  reconstruction of the Red Army's first aircraft rather than the 1930 machine itself.
- **Mockup risk is flagged rather than resolved where the source itself is ambiguous.** AVIC's own
  newspaper says the ten outdoor exhibits at the Miyun history museum are "实物和全尺寸仿真模型" — real
  aircraft AND full-scale mockups — without saying which is which. All four rows there say so. The
  same flag is on the FC-31 at Shenfei, the FC-1 at Datangshan, the AG600 and FC-1 at NUAA
  Jiangjunlu, and the AC313 at NUAA.
- **`operator_country` for defected and captured airframes follows the airframe's service identity,
  not its builder or its current location.** The F-5F 5361 at Datangshan is `TW` because it served
  with the ROCAF until Huang Zhicheng flew it out on 8 August 1981. The RB-57D is `TW` for the same
  reason. The Ki-55 is `JP`. The D-21 and the Beihang P-61 and 147H are `US`. The Canberra is `ZW`,
  the F-86 `PK`, the MiG-23 `EG`, the F-104 `IT`. Hong Kong airframes are `HK`.
- **`operator_country` left BLANK where it is genuinely unknown** rather than defaulted to CN: the
  Datangshan Mirage III, F-5A, F-5E, Percival Provost, L-29, Alouette III, Mi-24, DHC-2 Beaver; the
  Shanghai MiG-15UTI and Beaver; the NUAA AS350; and both Enping unidentified airframes.
- **The two Enping airframes are recorded as `Unidentified` rows rather than dropped**, per the
  contract's instruction, with every identity field blank.
- **Single displayed airframes at parks, roundabouts and county squares ARE recorded as sites.**
  Fourteen of the thirty-two sites in this file are one-aircraft monuments. In China as in Europe
  the monuments outnumber the museums, and the OSM sweep only scratches them.

### EXCLUDED, and why

- **Minsk World / 明斯克航母世界 (Shenzhen, then Nantong).** The park closed on 14 February 2016 over a
  land dispute with Vanke; the carrier was towed to Zhoushan in April 2016 and has lain at Nantong
  under the Sutong Bridge since May 2016. A major fire in August 2024 badly damaged the island
  superstructure. A July 2026 report states plainly "目前已经不能再登上去参观了" (you can no longer board
  it) and "开园时间遥遥无期" (the opening date is indefinitely postponed). Its historic fixed exhibits
  included a MiG-23, a MiG-27, a Yak-38 and a Mi-24. **Not a site today: no public access, no
  presentation, indefinite stall.** Revisit if the Nantong park ever opens — if it does, those four
  airframes become records immediately.
- **PLAAF Surplus Aircraft Storage Centre, Rangshe Township, Lushan County, Pingdingshan, Henan
  (33.685°N 112.889°E).** Asia's largest and the world's second-largest military aircraft storage
  centre; hundreds of retired J-5s and J-6s on the ramp under vapour-phase preservation. This is a
  **live military storage facility inside an air base**, not a display: the airframes are held for
  potential recall to service, not for public presentation, and there is no public access. Excluded
  under the display-versus-graveyard test. The Chinese Wikipedia article has an empty
  "航空展览馆" (aviation exhibition hall) heading which suggests something display-like may exist on
  site; if it does, it would be a separate record. Flagged under NEEDS A HUMAN.
- **Lin Biao's Trident crash site (47.648°N 111.194°E).** OSM node 12808323126 marks where PLAAF
  Trident 256 crashed on 13 September 1971. It is in **Mongolia**, not China, and it is a wreck
  site, not a display. Excluded on both grounds.
- **戴笠坠机处 (Dai Li crash site), Jiangning, Nanjing (31.925°N 118.707°E).** OSM node 12328542835 is
  tagged `memorial=aircraft` but `material=stone` — it is a stone marker at a 1946 crash site with
  no airframe. Excluded: a memorial *to* an aircraft is not an aircraft.
- **Shenyang J-11 at Datangshan.** Listed on Chinese Wikipedia's collection page, but the only
  Commons photographs of it are explicitly titled "Shenyang J-11 Model". Excluded as a scale model.
  The J-11 will be a real airframe somewhere in China; it is not this one.
- **Chinese Wikipedia's Datangshan "Il-2".** Excluded as a mis-identification of the Il-10; see
  Corrections.
- **Lichao Aviation Museum, Chengdu — site recorded, ZERO aircraft rows.** China's first private
  aviation museum, 5000 m², open daily, and the only description of its holdings anywhere I could
  reach is "数千件航空相关文物" (thousands of aviation-related artefacts) with not one airframe named.
  Recording invented types would be worse than recording none.
- **Northeast Aviation History Memorial Hall, Faku — site recorded, ZERO aircraft rows**, for the
  same reason: two dated 2015 news pieces establish the museum, its hours and its free entry, and
  neither names an aircraft.
- **Taiwan.** The OSM sweep returned a very large and unusually well-serialled set of preserved
  aircraft on Taiwan (F-5E 5283/5308/5198/5262/5253/5119/5197, TF-104G 4193/4189/4179/4147, F-104J
  4519 repainted in error as 4522, F-100A 0234/0218, T-33A 3326/3325, F-86F 224/6134, PL-1 5823/5804,
  T-CH-1 0701/0744, AT-3 0802/0825, XA-3 0902, XC-2, C-119L, Boeing 727 2722, and more).
  **Deliberately excluded from this file** — the brief scopes to the PRC including Hong Kong and
  Macau. **This is a large ready-made dataset for a future Taiwan pass and should not be
  re-researched from scratch.**
- **Hong Kong Museum of Coastal Defence** — no aircraft found in any reachable source. **Macau
  Science Centre** — OSM way 1352850371 at 22.1278°N 113.3629°E is tagged `historic=aircraft` +
  `exhibit=science` at the Science Centre, but nothing establishes whether that is a real airframe,
  a cockpit section or a model, so no row was created. **Macau Grand Prix Museum** — motorsport, no
  aircraft.
- **Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean, Kyrgyz, Kazakh and Russian nodes** returned inside the query
  bboxes were filtered out and are not in this file.

### Blank fields, deliberately

- **`year_built` is blank on every row but one.** Chinese sources essentially never publish
  construction dates for these airframes, PLAAF tactical numbers encode nothing about build date,
  and the contract forbids deriving a year from a serial. The one exception is the Shenzhou 4 return
  capsule (2002, its flight year, which is also its build year within a few months). The Mooney's
  1987 was **removed** from `year_built` after drafting: 1987 is the year of the Lisbon-Macau flight,
  not of construction.
- **`tail_number` is blank on the great majority of Datangshan rows**, and on every civic-monument
  row. Chinese monument aircraft are repainted freely and the PLAAF reassigns five-digit tactical
  numbers between airframes; a number read off a repaint is not an identity. Where a number was
  actually read off a dated photograph it is recorded; otherwise blank.
- **`postal_code` is blank for Hong Kong and Macau** (neither uses postcodes) and for a few mainland
  monuments where Nominatim returned no postcode.
- **`website` is blank** for Shenfei, the Northeast Aviation History Memorial Hall, the Xinjiang
  memorial hall, the Miyun history museum and every monument — none has one.
- **`aircraft_name` is blank except for the seven airframes that genuinely carry a name**: Betsy,
  Sagres, Lenin, Mao Zedong's Il-14 and Il-18, Wang Hai's MiG-15, and the ORBIS DC-8.
