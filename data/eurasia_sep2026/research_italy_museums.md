## NOTES

### Sources and their weight

**What carried this pass**
1. **it.wikipedia "Museo storico dell'Aeronautica Militare"** — an unusually good article: a sortable table of ~80 airframes, each row citing the Aeronautica Militare's own per-aircraft museum page or the reference work *Ala* for the serial. This is effectively the AM's catalogue re-tabulated, and it is the only place the MUSAM inventory exists in machine-readable form. I pulled the raw wikitext rather than the rendered page, because the rendered page loses the serial column's footnote.
2. **volandia.it's `velivolo` custom post type** — the single highest-yield discovery of this pass. Volandia's WordPress exposes `velivolo-sitemap.xml` (79 aircraft pages, `lastmod` 2026-05-28). Each page ends with a *Storia* paragraph that names the specific airframe: construction number, MM serial, unit codes, first-flight date, donor and arrival year. This is a museum publishing airframe-level provenance and almost nobody indexes it. The WP REST API does **not** expose the type, so it must be reached through the sitemap.
3. **A dated September-2024 Wikimedia Commons walkaround of Volandia** — file titles of the form `<operator> <type> <serial> Volandia 2024 (01).jpg`, with a consistent operator-prefix scheme (AM = Aeronautica Militare, EI = Esercito, MM = Marina Militare, CC = Carabinieri, GdF = Guardia di Finanza, VVF = Vigili del Fuoco, RAI = Regia Aeronautica, SP = Siły Powietrzne/Polish AF). Independent of the museum's own text and it agreed with it every time the two overlapped. It is also what dates the collection to 2024.
4. **museoaviazione.com per-exhibit portfolio pages** for Rimini — narrative provenance for nearly every airframe, and materially **more current** than the Italian Wikipedia list.
5. **museomotori.unipa.it** — a proper university object catalogue with MM serials, service histories and a dated 2023 repaint.
6. **volandia.it/rete-nazionale-dei-musei-aeronautici/** — the AM's official seven-museum network page. This is where the Baracca SPAD's serial (S2489) comes from, and it is how I found two sites that were not in my brief at all (Palermo and Rocchetta a Volturno).

**What proved stale or thin**
- **it.wikipedia and en.wikipedia on the Museo Caproni** are misleading in a specific way: both give two bulleted "collection" lists that are actually *historical* snapshots — the 1920s-30s Taliedo holdings and the 1970s-80s Vizzola holdings — not the current Trento display. A reader would import a Ca.42, a Ca.22, a Roland VIb and a Siemens-Schuckert D.IV that are not on show. I did not record those.
- **it.wikipedia's Rimini list** contains at least four errors the museum's own pages contradict (see Corrections).
- **aviationmuseum.eu** was used only for Piana delle Orme, where nothing else airframe-level exists, and every row is flagged in its description as single-source.
- **museocaproni.it is unreachable** from a modern TLS client (`dh key too small`), which is probably why Caproni Trento is so poorly covered online.

- **Volandia F-104G**: it is **not** an Italian aircraft. Museum text: "F-104G S/N 683-2044 appartenuto alla Luftwaffe", and the 2024 photo survey reads the German serial **20+37**. `operator_country` DE, tail_number 20+37, not an MM.

### Judgment calls

- **`year_built` is blank for nearly every MUSAM airframe, deliberately.** The Italian Wikipedia table has an "Anno" column whose own footnote reads *"Se possibile di immatricolazione dello specifico esemplare, altrimenti del progetto"* — where possible the registration year of that airframe, **otherwise the year of the design**. Spot checks confirm it is mostly the design year (the C-47 is given as 1935, the Tornado as 1974, the M.C.202 as 1940). Importing that column as `year_built` would push a plausible-looking wrong date onto ~70 airframes. I populated `year_built` only where a source states an airframe-specific date: the Tornado (first flight 23 Sep 1988), the SPAD S.VII S-153 (September 1916), the CC.2 (first flight 27 Aug 1940). Volandia's dates, by contrast, are airframe-specific and I used them.
- **Replicas and mock-ups are recorded and flagged in the description, never in the aliases.** Volandia's Ca.1 (2010 reproduction), its S.V.A.5 (9/10-scale, 2001), its M.C.205 (full-scale simulacro), its BA609 and AW609 (mock-ups), its Ornithopter; Vigna di Valle's Wright No.4 and Forlanini helicopter; Rimini's Fokker Dr.I; Caproni Trento's Re.2005 and Leonardo glider; Milan's Farman III.
- **`operator_country` is blank for replicas and mock-ups that never had an operator** (Volandia BA609/Ornithopter/Bleriot XI, Vigna di Valle Wright No.4 and Forlanini, Milan Farman III, Caproni Trento Leonardo glider). Blank, not a guess.
- **Racing seaplanes** (M.39, M.67, M.C.72, Fiat C.29) are `experimental`, the closest fit in the closed role list to a purpose-built record/racing machine. Sailplanes and gliders are `other`, since the list has no glider role.
- **Frecce Tricolori airframes take their base role** (G.91PAN → ground_attack; MB-339 PAN → trainer) with the aerobatic-team service in the description, rather than all being forced to `other`.
- **The Yak-40 at Volandia is `operator_country: LR`** — its last and displayed identity is the Liberian registration EL-CAR flown for Ales Airlines. Aeroflot service is in the description. This is a judgment call; someone might reasonably prefer SU.
- **Volandia's Stinson L-5 is `US`**: it is 42-99119, a USAAF airframe delivered March 1944; its later Italian civil marks I-AEEL are in aliases.
- **Volandia's Douglas Dakota IV is `GB`**: built for the USAAF as 43-48983 but transferred straight to the RAF as KJ952 and served there; the later Italian civil registration I-RAGF is the tail_number because that is its current and displayed identity.
- **Missile rounds on plinths inside museum grounds are recorded** (Nike Ajax and Nike Hercules at San Pelagio, Nike Hercules at Volandia) as `missile_rocket` / `surface_to_air`, because they are deliberately retained and publicly presented. Ground vehicles, guns, radars, tanks and engines at Rimini and Piana delle Orme are **not** recorded — out of scope.
- **`access_type` calls**: Rivolto is `restricted` (inside a live military base). Rocchetta a Volturno is `appointment` (visits are arranged by phone; no published hours). Palermo is `public` — it is a university museum with published opening arrangements, though group booking is normal. Everything else is `public`. Rimini is `public` but **seasonal**: its own site banner reads "Riapertura 1 MAGGIO 2026".
- **Cockpit and fuselage sections are recorded as airframes** where they are the museum's presentation of that type: the Junkers J.I fuselage and engine at Vigna di Valle, the Fokker D.VIII fuselage and the Ca.60 hull section at Trento, the Do 328JET fuselage no. 232 at Volandia. Each says so in its description.

### Blank fields, deliberately

- **`tail_number` is blank across most of Rimini, Caproni Trento and San Pelagio.** This is not an oversight: none of those three publishes serials for any airframe, and I would not invent them. Where a **unit code** is known but the MM is not (Volandia's F-104S "9-51", F-84F "50-30", F-86K "51-3"), the unit code is in `aliases` and the tail_number is blank — per the brief, the unit code is not an identity and must never be promoted into `tail_number`.
- **`year_built` blank** — see the judgment call above.
- **`aircraft_name` is blank everywhere.** No Italian museum airframe I found carries an individual name distinct from its type name; the "Pony 0" / "Pony 10" style designators are display numbers and are in the description.
- **`model_name` is blank for types whose popular name IS the model field** (Ariete, Sagittario 2, Ghibli where it is the AMX's name — that one I put in model_name since the model is "AMX").
- **`operator_country` blank** on the six replicas/mock-ups listed above, on Rimini's Il-28, MiG-21UM, Su-7, Su-17, DC-3 and Volandia's Dornier 328 fuselage, Jodel D.9, Hummel Bird, Monerai and NH90 prototype, where no single national operator is established.

### EXCLUDED, and why

**Sites considered and rejected**
- **Museo Storico dell'Arma Aeronautica, Roma (Palazzo Aeronautica, Viale dell'Università 4)** — every search for this name in Italian resolves to Vigna di Valle. The Associazione Arma Aeronautica maintains a relic and uniform collection at Palazzo Aeronautica, but I found **no evidence of a complete airframe**, and access is by arrangement through the association. Not recorded as a site because I could not confirm a single airframe. See NEEDS A HUMAN.
- **Museo Storico Navale, Venezia** — the Marina Militare's naval museum. I found no evidence of a complete aircraft in the collection; the aviation content appears to be models, engines and *mezzi d'assalto*. Well-searched zero. See NEEDS A HUMAN.
- **Museo dell'Aeronautica Militare di Taranto/Grottaglie** — MARISTAER Grottaglie is a live Marina Militare air station (GRUPAER Harrier base). There is a *Sala Storica dell'Aviazione Navale*, but it is inside the base, access is by organised visit only, and I found no published airframe inventory. Not recorded as a site. See NEEDS A HUMAN.
- **Museo Storico dei Vigili del Fuoco** — the Corpo Nazionale dei Vigili del Fuoco's historical collections are dispersed across Rome (Capannelle) and provincial commands and are fire-appliance collections. The one VVF *aircraft* I traced (AB.204B VF-31) is at **Volandia** and is recorded there. No separate site record.
- **Bunker Soratte, Sant'Oreste (RM)** — a member of the AM's museum network, but it holds Cold War *cimeli* in tunnels, not airframes. No aircraft rows.
- **Politecnico di Milano and Politecnico di Torino aeronautical faculties** — I found no evidence of a publicly presented complete airframe at either. The comparable university case in Italy that *does* qualify is Palermo, which is recorded. Well-searched zero.
