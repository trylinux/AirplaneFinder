## NOTES

### Totals
61 sites — 33 in the Netherlands, 24 in Belgium, 4 in Luxembourg.
321 airframe rows across 47 of those sites. 14 sites carry no aircraft rows; each is explained below.

### Sources and their weight

**What carried this region:**

1. **The NMM's own online collection catalogue (collectie.nmm.nl).** This is the single most valuable
   find of the pass and it answers the brief's question directly: yes, the Dutch flagship publishes a
   collection database. The facet `subcollection_exact:vliegtuigen` returns the whole aviation
   subcollection; I paged it and recovered 85 object records, most with registration, construction
   number and often the US fiscal-year serial in the object title. This is a primary institutional
   source and it beats every directory. Two objects in it are duplicates (the T-37B 65-10825 appears as
   records 480206 and 953974; the F-4E 67-0275 as 471977 and 954007) and one — record 471471,
   "Artificial Horizon" — is an instrument that has been filed under aircraft. Those three are excluded.
2. **OpenStreetMap via overpass.kumi.systems.** The Low Countries are as well mapped as the brief
   predicted. `historic=aircraft` plus `memorial=aircraft` plus `memorial:type=aircraft` plus
   `aeroway=gate_guardian` returned 39 objects inside Belgium and 67 inside the Netherlands, a large
   share of them carrying `ref` with a real serial (D-8279, D-8318, P-191, P-248, J-228, J-246, FX-45,
   I-320, 4R-15, H-08, MT30, A-80, 1914, DM-STD, 919, PH-NIV, PH-EAG). Airframe-level coordinates
   throughout.
3. **The International F-104 Society (i-f-s.nl) "Preserved in Belgium" and "Preserved in Netherlands"
   pages.** A specialist type register with per-airframe locations and status notes, several dated
   2022-2025 (FX15 installed 11 June 2023; FX99 moved indoors September 2024; D-8268 removed from
   Zwolle 22 April 2025; 28+22 left Baarlo August 2025; 22+65 sold by PS Aero 2024). This is the most
   current source in the whole pass and it is the reason the Starfighter coverage here is much stronger
   than the coverage of any other type.
4. **The Brussels Air Museum friends association (brussels-air-museum.be).** Publishes the Air and
   Space Hall's own inventory in three period pages — roughly 120 airframes with types, national
   origin and year of accession, but no serials at all.
5. **targeta.co.uk's photo tour of the Brussels Aviation Hall.** The only source I found that carries
   serials for the Cinquantenaire collection (S-85, SC-11, N5024, SP-49, HD-78, C.2531, 5.160,
   3471/18, SG55, SM15, LF658, MB24, H21, O-16, P-4, D04, OL-L87, OO-SRA, the V-xx Stampe codes).
   Single-source, and that is stated on every row that uses it.
6. **gras-spotters.nl "Wrecks & relics"** for the Gilze-Rijen gate guards.
7. **KNVOL / onzeluchtmacht.nl** and **upinthesky.nl** for the Volkel roundabout F-16 J-246 — three
   sources agreeing on serial, date and provenance make it the best-documented monument in the file.
8. **photon.komoot.io reverse geocoding.** Turned about 100 bare OSM coordinates into street names,
   towns and postcodes. This is what let me tell Peer from Kleine Brogel, Teuge from Twenthe, and
   Baarlo from the German border. Nominatim itself returned 403 through the agent proxy; photon worked.

**Which compilation proved stale or wrong, and in what specific way:**

- **aviationmuseum.eu** gives Aviodrome's coordinates as 52.45788, 5.25065. The museum is at
  Lelystad Airport at 52.4582, 5.5310. The longitude is wrong by 0.28 degrees — about **19 km**, which
  puts the pin in the Markermeer. I used the site only for registrations of aircraft whose *type* is
  independently confirmed by the Dutch Wikipedia collection list, and I flag the one registration
  (Fokker 70 PH-MKC) whose type is not on that list as the weakest entry in the set.
- **aviationmuseum.eu's Museum Bevrijdende Vleugels page** returns a visibly column-shifted table:
  the C-53C Skytrooper row carries the serial string "290321/UA-D, N32MS, D-HEFO, OO-GEI" while the
  C-47 row above it carries "N32MS" alone. That is four identities smeared across two rows. I recorded
  the D-Day display marking only and asserted no registration. This is exactly the failure mode the
  contract warns about.
- **whichmuseum.com** lists three aviation museums in Belgium. The real number is at least eight.
  Directory coverage of Belgium is thin in both languages.

**National spotter registers — what I found and what I did not.** Belgium has two serious specialist
sites, **flying-zone.be** and **ailes-militaires-belges.be**; the latter is disallowed by robots.txt
and I did not fetch it. **hangarflying.eu** maintains a Belgian aviation-heritage inventory
("Patrimonium") whose `erfgoedsites` index is mostly memorial plaques, crew graves and crash
memorials rather than airframes — valuable for a war-memorial pass, thin for this one. For the
Netherlands, **scramble.nl** (Dutch Aviation Society) publishes no free preserved-aircraft register
that I could reach, and the **IPMS Nederland "NedMil Luchtvaart"** pages turned out to be narrative
type histories, not per-airframe fate registers. Both national registers remain the best lead for a
follow-up pass — see "Needs a human".

  carried as an alias with the reason given in the description.
- **Oosterland F-104G.** Painted as Dutch **D-8030**. OSM states flatly "D-8030 is een nep registratie"
  and the F-104 Society lists the same airframe at Bruinisse-Oosterland as **FX-45**. Recorded as
  Belgian SABCA-built FX-45 with `operator_country` **BE**, the false Dutch serial as an alias.
- **Gilze-Rijen NF-5A.** Wears **K-3066**; the true identity per gras-spotters is **K-3003**. True
  serial in `tail_number`, worn marking in `aliases`.
- **Brussels Halberstadt C.V.** Displayed marked 3470/18, true identity 3471/18. Same treatment.
- **Oude Meer PH-NIV.** Not the Fokker Friendship prototype. It is a former Air France and Farner

- **Teuge, not Twenthe.** The F-86F "FU-012" and the Ilyushin Il-18 "DM-STD" sit at 52.241 N, 6.05 E,

- **Poortwachterslaan is Eindhoven, not Best.** The OSM cluster of six airframes at 51.447-51.449 N,

- **Vodecee roundabout F-84F.** Belgian press (L'Avenir, 21 November 2017, "Vodecee: un avion, mais pas
  le bon") reports the aircraft on this roundabout is not the airframe it was believed to be. No serial
  is asserted.
- **Overloon's recovered four-engined bomber.** Dutch Wikipedia calls it a Lancaster recovered from
  Glabbeek in Belgium. I recorded Avro Lancaster and flagged that the identification needs a check on

### Judgment calls

- **Militaire Luchtvaart Museum — confirmed merged.** The former MLM at Soesterberg is gone as an
  institution; its collection is the aviation subcollection of the Nationaal Militair Museum, and the
  NMM's own catalogue still carries MLM provenance in object descriptions (record 471934 credits the
  "Stichting Vrienden van het Militaire Luchtvaart Museum"). No separate site record is created for it.
  "Nationaal Luchtvaart-Themapark" is the older formal name of **Aviodrome** and likewise gets no
  separate record.
- **Airworthy museum-owned aircraft are excluded.** The NMM catalogue includes six airframes it owns
  but which fly with the Koninklijke Luchtmacht Historische Vlucht or comparable foundations:
  Harvards PH-MLM (471510), PH-LSK (471512) and PH-TBR (571441), Beaver PH-DHC (471511), Fokker S-11
  PH-SII (471513) and Spitfire Mk.IX MK732 / PH-OUQ (571438), plus Catalina PBY-5A BuNo 48317 (471491)
  which flies as PH-PBY. The contract says operational aircraft are not displays, so all seven are
  excluded. Aviodrome's DC-2 "Uiver" and F27 PH-FHF are the mirror case and I made the opposite call:
  the museum presents them as exhibits in its own halls and hangar between flights, so they are
  recorded on_display. This is an inconsistency I am flagging rather than hiding — a reviewer may want
  one rule for both.
- **Replicas** are recorded and flagged in the description in every case: NMM's Fokker D.XXI "221"
  (1988), Fokker G.I "330", Fokker C.X, Koolhoven F.K.51 "429", Curtiss P-6E "C-319", Brewster B-339C,
  the 1:1 Spitfire "3-WB" and the Wright Flyer; Brussels' Fokker Dr.I 425/17 (1983), Voisin De Caters
  (rebuilt 2008), the Stampe SV-4s the museum's own page marks as replicas, and the V-1; the
  1 Wing Historical Centre's Spitfire Mk IX; Museum Bevrijdende Vleugels' Horsa; and both Euro Space
  Center launchers.
- **Nose and cockpit sections are recorded as rows**, with the description saying so: Aviodrome's Jet
  Provost, Buccaneer, Dove and Caravelle cockpits; the Schiphol terminal Fokker 100 PH-OFE (mapped
  indoors on level 3, so almost certainly a forward fuselage); the TU Delft Boeing 737. NMM's Harvard
  B-175, an airframe stripped of skin and wings, is recorded `under_restoration`.
- **Aircraft converted into other things and publicly visible are recorded**, per the contract: the
  Teuge Il-18 hotel suite; the Jauche Caravelle "Alsace" becoming a hostel; the Overboelare C-45 at a
  disused pub; the Balen F-84F at the Fly Coffee & Lunchbar; the Breda Fokker 100 on a factory roof.
- **`access_type`.** `restricted` is used only where a member of the public must pass a military gate:
  Vliegbasis Leeuwarden's static park, the 1 Wing Historical Centre at Beauvechain and the Musee
  Spitfire at Florennes. `appointment` for Kleine-Brogel Air Museum and the Chievres base museum, both
  of which sit beside sensitive installations and publish no walk-up hours. Eindhoven's and
  Gilze-Rijen's gate guards are `public` because they stand on named public roads outside the wire.
- **`operator_country` on allied airframes.** The Brussels Fairey Battle carries an RCAF serial and is
  coded **CA**; the Bolingbroke likewise. Its Mi-24D was donated by the Luftwaffe as inheritor of the
  East German fleet, so **DE**; its MiG-23BN is of Egyptian origin, so **EG**; its F-86F was a
  Portuguese gift, **PT**; the RF-4C is a USAF Museum loan, **US**. Leeuwarden's MiG-21SPS and Su-22
  are ex-East German and coded **DE**. Historyland's MiG-21M is ex-Polish, **PL**. The NMM's Fouga
  MT-51 is ex-Belgian Air Force and coded **BE** even though it sits in a Dutch museum. The Oosterland
  Starfighter is Belgian, **BE**, not Dutch. Aviodrome's Hunter WV395 wears an RAF serial, **GB**;
  its 22+90 Starfighter is ex-Luftwaffe, **DE**.
- **`operator_country` deliberately left blank** on the NMM's MiG-21PFM "47" (the museum says only
  "Soviet Union and Warsaw Pact"), the NMM Auster MZ-236 (RAF-style serial, operating service not
  stated), the Zelhem MiG-21PF and L-29, the Tilburg MiG-21F-13, the Helchteren MiG-21, the Luxembourg
  City MiG-21, the 1 Wing MiG-21bis, Aviodrome's MiG-21PFM and An-2, the Jauche Caravelle, the
  Overboelare C-45, the TU Delft exhibits and both Euro Space Center launchers.
- **`year_built` is blank almost everywhere.** I populated it only four times, each from a sourced
  build or completion statement: Historyland's MiG-21M (1969, stated by the museum), the Fligermusee
  Klemm (1934, stated by the museum), NMM's Fokker D.XXI replica (completed 1988) and Brussels' Fokker
  Dr.I replica (entered 1983). Every Dutch and Belgian letter-number serial and every US fiscal-year
  prefix in this file is an order or allocation number, not a build year, and none was converted.
- **Unidentified rows.** Four rows use `Unidentified` with a descriptive model, per the contract: three
  at TU Delft (a helicopter, a light aircraft, and by extension the untyped fourth exhibit) and one at
  the Ypenburg monument in Nootdorp. These are sites I am confident exist with airframes I could not
  type.

### EXCLUDED, and why

**Excluded outright:**

- **PS Aero, Baarlo (NL, 51.325 N, 6.087 E).** Sixteen OSM-mapped airframes — a Caravelle III F-BHRA
  (the first SE 210 built), Eurofighter Typhoon, F-104G ex-26+44, MiG-23, two Su-22s, Alpha Jet, Fouga
  "Red Devils", Cessna Citation, ATR 72, three airliners, two helicopters. This is a **trading company**,
  not a display: the F-104 Society records 22+65 "sold by PSAero in 2024" and 28+22 leaving Baarlo for
  a new owner in August 2025. Stock in a dealer's yard fails the "deliberate retention plus public
  presentation" test. Worth revisiting if PS Aero ever formalises a museum — the Caravelle prototype
  alone would be internationally significant.
- **Madurodam DC-3 "Albert Plesman" (Den Haag).** OSM way/1308800647 records a Douglas DC-3 ref
  PH-APM operated by Madurodam, but the OSM note explains it is the themed shell of an attraction
  called "The Flying Dutchman". I could not establish whether any real airframe is involved, and
  PH-APM is a suspicious registration. Left out rather than guessed. **Worth one phone call.**
- **Aruba.** OSM node/13690348134 (`historic=aircraft`, 12.5006 N, 70.0192 W) falls inside the
  `ISO3166-1=NL` area because of the Kingdom's constituent countries. Out of scope for a European pass
  and flagged here so a Caribbean pass picks it up.
- **Weiswampach, Luxembourg (50.148 N, 6.058 E).** Two OSM `historic=aircraft` nodes, LX-PEZ (Sky Arrow
  650T) and LX-AIB (Cessna 172), at what appears to be an active ULM field. Almost certainly
  operational aircraft, not displays.
- **Wings of Memory.** The brief lists it as a Brustem museum. It is not: it is a research and
  commemoration organisation for WWII aircrew lost over Belgium, and hangarflying.eu reports it opened
  a museum at the **Hunnegem site in Geraardsbergen**, not at Brustem. No airframe list is published
  and its known exhibits (a V-1 replica presented in 2022) are memorial pieces. Recorded here as an
  exclusion so the Brustem lead is not chased again; the Geraardsbergen museum needs a fresh look.
- **Musee National d'Histoire Militaire, Diekirch (LU).** Checked as the brief asked. The museum's
  own description and the Wikipedia article list wheeled and tracked vehicles, weapons, a photographic
  archive and life-size dioramas. **No aircraft.** A well-searched zero.
- **Luxembourg-Findel.** Checked as the brief asked. No preserved airframe found at or beside the
  airport. Luxembourg's aviation museum is at **Mondorf-les-Bains**, 30 km south — the brief's
  assumption that it would be at Findel is wrong and that is worth recording.
- **Musee de la Bataille des Ardennes (Clervaux), Museum of the Battle of the Bulge (Wiltz), Musee
  385th Bomb Group (Perle), Bastogne War Museum (BE).** All four mapped as military museums in the
  Ardennes sweep. None publishes an airframe. Ground-war and air-crew museums, not aircraft
  collections.
- **Vliegermuseum Space Art, Ommen (NL)** — a **kite** museum. `vlieger` is Dutch for kite, not
  aviator; it is tagged `museum=aviation` in OSM and will trap any automated sweep.
- **Astropolis Space Science Center, Oostende (BE)** — a planetarium, no vehicles.
- **Sterrebeek/Zaventem ASCO (FX76 dismantled, restoration on hold; FX100 under restoration), Bilzen
  (FX99, inside a private covered hangar since September 2024), Gosselies (FX69, status uncertain),
  Lint AED Studios (23+94), Beervelde (FX79 on a company fence), Sint-Truiden Airport (26+72, private
  owner since 8 February 2022), Evere Queen Elizabeth HQ (FX21).** All from the F-104 Society list.
  The last three or four are arguably displays and the Evere one certainly is a gate display, but I
  could not establish public visibility or a usable street address for any of them within this pass.
  They are the single densest block of easy wins for a follow-up.
- **Nieuw Milligen (D-8053, pole-mounted) and Harderwijk (24+46, relocated from Teuge in 2022), and
  Hasselt Overijssel (26+02, gate guard at a company).** Same reason: real displays named by the F-104
  Society, but I had no coordinates and did not want to invent any. **These three are certain records
  waiting for a coordinate.**

**Recorded as sites with no aircraft rows, and why:**

- **CRASH Luchtoorlog- en Verzetsmuseum '40-'45 (Aalsmeerderbrug)** and **Museum Vliegbasis Deelen**.
  Both are genuine, open, ticketed museums. Neither publishes an airframe list, and both are
  wreck-recovery collections. The contract's test — a recovered and displayed airframe or major
  section is a record, an unidentified fragment in a vitrine is not — cannot be applied from a
  distance. Rather than invent rows or drop two real sites, the sites are recorded and the aircraft
  question is left open. **The same reasoning applies to Overloon's recovered Lancaster, which I did
  record because the museum lays the recovered parts out in the aircraft's contours — that is a
  displayed major structure, not fragments in a case.**
- **Luchtvaart- en Oorlogsmuseum Texel**, **Vliegend Museum Seppe**, **Museum Stampe & Vertongen**,
  **Musee International de la Base Aerienne de Chievres**: real sites, addresses and hours confirmed,
  no published inventory. Seppe and Stampe are largely flying collections whose aircraft would in any
  case be excluded as operational.
- **Aircraft Display -- Koksijde-Dorp, -- Lokeren, -- Sint-Gillis-Waas; Kokorico Aircraft -- Beke;
  Gowalt Vliegtuig -- Kwatrecht; First Stop GT Pneus -- Brugelette; Pairi Daiza Hangar Hydravion;
  Sanicole Aeroclub**: eight Belgian OSM `historic=aircraft` nodes with a confirmed location and no
  type. Each is a real preserved aircraft that somebody bothered to map. Recorded as sites so the next
  pass has coordinates to work from; no aircraft row, because guessing a type is worse than a blank.

### Blank fields, deliberately

- `tail_number` is blank on 96 rows. In every case no source gave one. This is most acute at the
  **Brussels Air and Space Hall**, where the museum's own inventory publishes no serials at all and
  only about a third of the 120 airframes appear in the photo tour that does. It is also true of the
  whole **Eindhoven** and most of the **Kleine-Brogel** and **Florennes** lines.
- `year_built` is blank on 317 of 321 rows — see the judgment call above.
- `website` is blank on all monument sites; only institutions have one.
- `postal_code` is blank on the 1 Wing Historical Centre, the Musee Spitfire, the Chievres museum and
  the three Luxembourg monument sites, where I had a town but no reliable code.
- `state_province` for Belgium uses the province names in the language of the region (Limburg,
  Hainaut, Namur, West-Flanders spelled "West Flanders", etc.); "Luxembourg (province)" is written
  that way for Libin to distinguish the Belgian province from the country.
