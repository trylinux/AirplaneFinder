# Missouri

33 museums, 136 aircraft. Greenfield — the state held nothing before this pass.

## Sources and their weight

Three institutions publish usable inventories: the **Historic Aircraft
Restoration Museum** at Creve Coeur (via Wikipedia's FAA-cited roster), the
**National Museum of Transportation**, and the **Museum of Missouri Military
History**. Beyond those, Missouri is unusually badly served — AMMO Springfield,
Nicholas-Beazley, and Freedom of Flight publish no inventory at all, and their
holdings here rest on directory compilations.

The **FAA registry** settled the Kansas City airliner questions, which mattered
more than usual (see below). **silverhawkauthor's Missouri page** was lead
generation only; several of its entries are wrong in ways documented below.

## Two collisions, resolved

**F/A-18E BuNo 165164** came back from two researchers at two different St.
Louis sites. It is at the **National Museum of Transportation** — the museum's
own page, the August 2024 dedication reporting and the arrival chronology all
agree. The Lambert attribution is an artefact of the aircraft's history: E1 is
the first Super Hornet built, it made its maiden flight **from Lambert Field on
29 November 1995**, and Boeing's St. Louis plant sits on Lambert airport
property. One airframe, one place.

**F-84F 51-9422** was reported at Boeing Park, Spirit of St. Louis Airport. It
is at **American Legion Post 253 in Festus** — it is the former St. Louis ANGB
gate guard, dedicated to Maj Julius V. Santschi, with its 110th FS Missouri ANG
history documented. Boeing Park holds the F-101B and the Harrier mockup only.

## Corrections made

- **The Missouri History Museum's "Spirit of St. Louis" is a replica** — a Ryan
  B-1 Brougham modified to NYP dimensions for the 1957 Jimmy Stewart film,
  acquired 1962. The original is at the Smithsonian. **But the museum does hold
  a genuine Lindbergh aircraft**: his personal 1934 Monocoupe D-127, built in
  St. Louis by Lambert Aircraft and donated by Lindbergh himself in 1940. It
  hung at Lambert Airport for decades and was withdrawn to climate-controlled
  storage on 12 June 2018 — recorded `in_storage`.
- **L-1011 N91011 never came to Missouri.** Acquired in 2014 by what is now
  TriStar Experience with the intention of ferrying it to Kansas City in TWA
  colours, it has sat at Victorville ever since and the FAA now shows Orbital
  Sciences of Mojave as owner. The TriStar physically at Wheeler Downtown is a
  different aircraft — **N31019**, and it belongs to the **Airline History
  Museum**, not TriStar.
- **The Whiteman B-29 is an SB-29 repainted as "The Great Artiste"** — the real
  Great Artiste was scrapped.
- **The Whiteman F-100D, F-15A and F-4E are the former Lambert pedestal jets**,
  craned down in April 2015 and re-erected at the 131st Bomb Wing Heritage Park
  in June 2015. There is no separate Lambert display record.
- **Springfield's F-84F 51-1639 is at the Downtown Airport**, not at Central
  Missouri State in Warrensburg as an older survey has it, and it is a separate
  site from AMMO despite being in the same city.

## Judgment calls

**HARM's roster is 66 rows against a museum that says it currently holds 49.**
The list is the FAA-cited Wikipedia roster, last comprehensively verified in
2019; the collection rotates seasonally across four hangars. Everything is
recorded `on_display` and the count discrepancy is flagged here rather than
guessed at row level.

**The Airline History Museum is recorded `restricted`, not public.** It has been
locked out of its hangar since 2023 and has no regular hours. Its L-1011 N31019
was reported at risk of scrapping after a June 2026 deadline — recorded
`in_storage` with that flag, and worth re-checking before anyone relies on it.

**The Boeing Prologue Room is `restricted`** — free, but open to walk-in
visitors only June through August; the rest of the year it is group access on
one Wednesday a month. Its two items are Mercury and Gemini boilerplate
mockups; everything else there is scale models.

**Four serials were deliberately discarded, not recorded.** AMMO Springfield's
reported markings — 70-16086 for an L-3B, 53-6055 for a Stearman, N25191 for a
Cobra — are not plausible for their types, so those rows carry blank tails with
the reason in aliases. The Caruthersville Huey's "0-61193" is a painted marking
whose underlying fiscal-year serial is not established.

**A name collision surfaced on import**: "Veterans Memorial Museum" already
existed for Chehalis, Washington. The Branson record was renamed **Branson
Veterans Memorial Museum**.

## Excluded, and why

- **Wings of Hope, Chesterfield** — an operating humanitarian air-transport
  charity, not a museum. Its aircraft are active mission airframes.
- **Greater St. Louis Air & Space Museum** — it is in **Illinois** (Cahokia
  Heights), having moved across the river in 2005. Route it to an Illinois pass.
- **Farmington's Lim-5** — relocated to Planes of Fame, Chino; its US
  registration was cancelled in 2012.
- **Vichy / Rolla National Airport P-51D 44-73350** — a privately owned
  airworthy warbird based there, not a display.
- **National Personnel Records Center Huey** — the NPRC vacated 9700 Page Avenue
  in 2011 and no source confirms a Huey at either address.
- **Branson's PV-2 Harpoon** — one source, which also miscalls it a "P2V
  Harpoon" (P2V is the Neptune). The museum closed in 2013 and reopened with a
  reduced collection. Not recorded.
- **The St. Louis Science Center's F-15A 71-0286 and PGM-17 Thor** — on older
  surveys but absent from the Science Center's own current grounds page. Only
  the F/A-18B is recorded.
- **The CAF Missouri Wing's P-63, EA-1E, AT-6 and A6M** — on older gazetteers,
  absent from the wing's own 2026 site.
- **Whiteman's "B-2 static display"** is a sculpture; there is no B-2 airframe
  anywhere on display. The "Minuteman" at the heritage park is the ANG emblem
  statue, not a missile. UH-1P 65-7941, AH-1F 68-17040 and A-10A 76-0551 have
  left the base.
- **Rosecrans' KC-97L** — scrapped.
- **Ozark Air Lines Museum** — no current web presence; presumed defunct.
- **National WWI Museum, Kansas City** — verified to hold no aircraft.
- **The National Museum of Transportation's inbound Harrier** — announced for
  summer 2026, no serial published, not yet confirmed on site.
- **VFW Post 3944, St. Louis** — an AH-1 70-15944 from a single source with no
  geocodable location. Held.

## Blank fields, deliberately

`year_built` is populated only from sourced dates. The FAA's bulk registry
returned HTTP 403 from this environment, so the ~30 HARM N-numbers could not be
matched to their YEAR-MFR values — **that single lookup is the highest-value
follow-up for this state.**

Tails are blank across most of AMMO, Nicholas-Beazley's glider and Flying Flea,
Freedom of Flight's T-33, Rosecrans' C-130H (only the tail marking 1391 is
published), Fort Leonard Wood's Huey, the CAF wing's three unregistered
airframes, the TWA Museum's 707 cabin section, and the Spirit of St. Louis
Harrier mockup — whose "7487" is a painted marking, not a BuNo (the two genuine
YAV-8B prototypes were 158394 and 158395).

**Wentzville has no postal code** — the geocoder returned none and no
authoritative value was found.
