# East Africa and the Horn — Kenya, Ethiopia, Eritrea, Uganda, Djibouti, Somalia/Somaliland, Rwanda

18 sites, 45 airframes, imported September 2026. Greenfield.

This region contains the two most famous aircraft graveyards in Africa, and the
single most consequential decision in this package was **not** recording most of
them. Where the display/derelict line was drawn is documented below.

---

## A note on Somaliland

Two sites — the Hargeisa War Memorial and the Guluf Airline Restaurant — are in
**Somaliland**, which is not internationally recognised. They are filed with
`country = Somalia` and `state_province = Somaliland`, so that the country list
stays aligned with recognised states while the region is still visible and
searchable. This is a database-consistency choice, not a political one, and it is
recorded here so nobody "corrects" it without knowing why.

## Sources and their weight

**Spottingmode's Wrecks & Relics Online (`spottingmode.com/wro`) was the single
most valuable source in this region** and was weighted highest after dated
photography. It is contributor-maintained and carries per-location coordinates,
per-airframe construction numbers, explicit status codes (preserved / stored /
dumped / instructional / deleted), per-location "last update" dates, per-airframe
"last noted" dates, and a visible comment thread where contributors argue about
identifications. **A status vocabulary that distinguishes display from dump, plus
dated currency fields, is exactly what this work needs**, and nothing else in
East Africa has it. Several of its East African locations were updated in 2025
and 2026, including Kenya entries credited to the site admin's own March 2026
visit. **It is not on the methodology's source list and it should be.**

Practical note for the next pass: `spottingmode.com` negotiates a Diffie-Hellman
key that modern OpenSSL rejects (`dh key too small`) and its `robots.txt` fetch
fails, so WebFetch and plain curl both fail. It is reachable with
`curl --ciphers "DEFAULT:@SECLEVEL=0"`.

Also used, in order: **airhistory.net photo captions** (a March 2014 caption
independently confirmed the Aero Beach Boeing 707's identity, BOAC origin as
G-APFL, construction number, *and* that it is preserved rather than dumped);
**Ruud Leeuw's Asmara field notes**, a photographer's own dated observations from
2003 and 2006, which corroborated the Asmara Expo Park group by a completely
independent route; the **Kenyan Ministry of Defence and DefenceWeb** for the Moi
Forces Academy Bulldog handover; **Ethiopian Monitor and Together We Learn
Ethiopia** for the Tsehai repatriation. **Tripadvisor reviews were used only as
dated eyewitness testimony, never as description** — one April 2024 review is the
sole post-war evidence that the Mekelle memorial is operating again.

### aviationmuseum.eu was close to useless here, and wrong where it spoke

- Its Africa index links only **Djibouti** and **Somalia** out of fifteen
  countries in this slice. There is no Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Eritrea, Rwanda,
  Madagascar, Mauritius, Seychelles, Comoros, Burundi or South Sudan page at all.
- **The Djibouti index page returns HTTP 404** — the country is linked from the
  continent index but the page behind it is gone.
- **The Ethiopia page carries no sites whatsoever**, only the country name and a
  bare mention of "Mek'ele". Last update 27 November 2015.
- **The Somalia page (last update 6 December 2016) lists exactly one aircraft: a
  "MiG-15" at Hargeysa. It is a MiG-17.** Wikipedia, Somaliland Standard,
  Somaliland Current, Saxafi Media and the Somaliland public all say MiG-17, and
  the airframe shows MiG-17 planform.

Net: aviationmuseum.eu contributed **one lead to this whole region, and that
lead's single fact was wrong.**

**Wikipedia survivor lists were empty where they should have been full.** `List
of surviving North American F-86 Sabres` has no East African entry at all. `List
of displayed Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-23s` has no African entry at all.

## The biggest finding: there is no evidence for an Ethiopian Air Force Museum

The research brief called Ethiopia the richest country in the region on the
strength of an Ethiopian Air Force Museum at Debre Zeit / Bishoftu (Harar Meda)
holding F-86 Sabres, F-5s, MiG-21s, MiG-23s, Il-28s and Mi-24s. **That museum
could not be substantiated, and there is a specific and convincing explanation
for how the claim arose.**

What was looked for and not found: any museum website or Ethiopian Air Force
heritage page; any tourist listing, Tripadvisor entry, Lonely Planet entry or
tour-operator itinerary; any visit report; any Wikipedia entry; any
Amharic-language reference; any dated photograph of a curated collection. (A
Lonely Planet "Air Force Museum" hit turned out to be *Delhi*.) A recent
first-hand look inside Harar Meda, October 2021, describes only operational
transports and helicopters and mentions no museum.

What Harar Meda actually holds, per Spottingmode, is **thirteen separate
locations scattered across an active air base** with stored, dumped and scrapped
airframes — MiG-21bis, MiG-21R, An-12, DHC-5, DHC-6, Mi-8, Mi-17, Tu-154, Yak-40,
Su-25 — statused `std`, `dum` and `dlt`. That is a withdrawn-fleet population
inside a restricted military airfield, not a museum, and none of it is a record.

**Where the F-86s and F-5s actually are: Asmara, Eritrea, in the graveyard.**
Spottingmode's Eritrea list carries six Ethiopian Air Force F-86F Sabres (252,
257, 258, 264, 268, 270), five Ethiopian T-33As (211–214, 216), an Ethiopian F-5A
(674), a C-119K (910), an Mi-24A (1620) and nine Ethiopian MiG-21s — **every one
statused `dum`, at Asmara.** These are the airframes Ethiopia left behind when
Eritrea separated: American types in Ethiopian markings, exactly as the museum
claim describes, sitting in a scrap line rather than a Bishoftu museum. The
museum claim appears to be a conflation of those two facts.

**No Ethiopian Air Force Museum row was created.** If the museum exists it is
invisible to every channel available here, and it is the top item for a human on
the ground.

## Where the display / derelict / graveyard line was drawn

The rule applied is **deliberate retention plus public presentation.**

**Recorded as displays:** plinthed monuments and memorial exhibits (Hargeisa
MiG-17; the Mekelle and Bahir Dar martyrs' memorial open-air exhibitions); base
gate guards and heritage airframes (Djibouti BA 188; the Moi Air Base Provost;
the Laikipia Strikemaster); school and club exhibits (Moi Forces Academy Bulldog;
Mang'u High School; the Aero Club of East Africa Jodel); and curated attractions
built around retained airframes (Aero Beach; Asmara Expo Park; Club 034; the
Guluf Airline Restaurant; the Stanley's Haven playground; the Massawa roadside
An-12).

**One curated wreck was recorded: the Kigali Falcon 50.** It is a crash site, not
a preserved aircraft — but the wreckage sits inside a ticketed museum's grounds,
is interpreted for visitors, and admission and a photo permit are charged for it.
That makes it an exhibit. Had the wreckage simply been left in a field it would
not be here.

**The Asmara aircraft graveyard is not recorded — none of it.** This was the
brief's headline site. Spottingmode holds roughly twenty-four airframes across
ten Asmara airport locations and statuses **every one `dum`**. It is a scrap line
on and around an airfield. (The separate Asmara *tank* graveyard, which does
function as a war memorial and requires a Ministry of Tourism permit, contains
per a first-hand account **no aircraft at all** — only an airport flight-stair
car.) The three intact airliners at the **Expo Park** are a different matter:
they were deliberately moved into a public exhibition compound in the city and
repurposed as playground equipment, and those three are recorded.

**Restaurant and playground conversions are the closest call here, and this
package went the permissive way.** Club 034 (Kitengela) and the Guluf Airline
Restaurant (Hargeisa) are commercial venues, not museums. They are recorded
because the airframes are intact, deliberately retained, publicly accessible, and
catalogued as `preserved` by the one database in this region that distinguishes
preserved from dumped. **A reviewer who wants a stricter rule should drop those
two sites and the Stanley's Haven playground; nothing else in the package depends
on them.**

**Excluded:** instructional airframes in technical training halls (the Nord 262
5Y-DCA at the East African School of Aviation, `i/a`); the **Kenya Airways Pride
Centre**, which holds the *fuselages only* of 737-700 5Y-KQG and Embraer 190
5Y-KYR as training rigs; the **Jomo Kenyatta International Airport stored-airliner
lot**, a disposal yard of sixteen-plus airliners being progressively removed to
Ruiru, Riara Ridge and Kiambu — and also the origin of the Club 034 Boeing 720;
the **Mogadishu MiGs**, a UNOSOM-era wreck line, gone now; the **Ugandan Air Force
airframes at Entebbe outside Aero Beach** (MiG-15UTI U-618, MiG-17F U-614,
MiG-23MS 6908 and 8707, MiG-21bis 9811/9818, MiG-21UM 9307, a Fouga Magister) at
four separate airport-side locations 500–1000 m north, which a careless read
merges into Aero Beach; and **two F-100 airframes in a scrapyard at Balbala,
Djibouti** (11.54439N 43.12070E), named here so they are not confused with the
five preserved Djibouti airframes.

**Madagascar was reversed mid-research and is now a documented negative.**
Spottingmode statuses six Malagasy airframes `pre` and site records had been
drafted for them. Then Ruud Vos's Dakota Hunter account (republished by War
History Online, July 2017) describes "20+ derelict War Birds" — C-47s, An-24s,
MiG-21s and a Mil helicopter — "rotting away since many years" and cannibalised
for spares just outside the international airport, and Nominatim confirms the
Arivonimamo coordinate resolves to an active military airfield. That is a boneyard
on a restricted field. All six rows were removed.

**Operational aircraft.** The clearest case is the row of six F-5s at Laikipia
(Spottingmode 32165, updated March 2026), where a contributor wrote in March 2026
*"these F-5 should be still active. So not sure if they should be listed."* 925
is a serving Kenya Air Force aircraft. Excluded.

## Corrections made, with evidence

- **Hargeisa: MiG-15 → MiG-17.** Evidence above. Serial blank; none has ever been
  published.
- **Mekelle: MiG-27 → MiG-23BN.** Dark-tourism.com lists "MiG-21 and MiG-27".
  Ethiopia never operated the MiG-27; it operated the MiG-23BN and MiG-23ML, and
  the BN is routinely misreported as a MiG-27. MiG-27 kept in aliases as a
  designation in circulation.
- **Kigali: "Falcon 60" → Falcon 50.** Two Rwandan tour operators call it a
  Falcon 60, one using both names in the same article. Dassault has never built a
  Falcon 60. Wikipedia gives Falcon 50, registration 9XR-NN.
- **Addis DC-3: ET-T-1 is a marking, not an identity.** ET-T-1 was Ethiopian
  Airlines' *first* DC-3; this airframe is ET-AIA. Real identity in
  `tail_number`, marking in aliases.
- **Mang'u High School: 5Y-MHS is probably a fabricated registration.** A
  Spottingmode contributor observed in May 2026 that the same registration has
  been applied to *both* the school's Beech 65 and its Boeing 737, which cannot
  both be true and is almost certainly a school marking (MHS = Mang'u High
  School). The Beech's `tail_number` is blank; the 737 keeps its real
  registration 5Y-KQH with 5Y-MHS in aliases.
- **Aero Beach is a monument, not a graveyard.** Every travel source calls it an
  "airplane graveyard". A Spottingmode contributor recorded in November 2018,
  sourced *from the owner*, that "although most planes look like wrecks, they are
  preserved as a monument for the Israeli raid in 1976." It charges admission, is
  fenced and guarded, and visitors climb into the cockpits. Note that Atlas
  Obscura explicitly debunks the local legend that one airframe is the hijacked
  Air France jet — none of them was involved in the 1976 incident.
- **Aero Beach type list.** Two travel blogs give incompatible inventories (a
  2018 post lists a Caravelle and MiG-17s; a July 2026 post lists a MiG-19 and an
  L-29). Spottingmode's airframe-by-airframe list has MiG-17Fs and one L-29 and
  **no MiG-19**. Travel-writer type identifications are not evidence.

## Currency, stated plainly

**Strong.** Djibouti — all five military airframes carry a **February 2025**
last-noted date. Kenya — Moi Forces Academy (March 2026, on-site), Mang'u High
School (May 2026), Aero Club and Stanley's Haven (October 2025), Club 034 (March
2025). Uganda — Aero Beach refreshed February 2025, and a July 2026 travel
account confirms the site is operating with a raised entry fee. Ethiopia — the
Bole DC-3 refreshed July 2025. Somaliland — the Hargeisa memorial appears in 2026
listings and Nominatim carries an OSM feature literally named "War Memorial
(MIG)" at the exact coordinate.

**Weak, and flagged in the rows themselves.** **Mekelle is the worst case in the
package**: the airframe list dates from June 2015, the museum was deliberately
targeted and destroyed as an act of cultural destruction during the Tigray war,
and the only post-war evidence is an April 2024 Tripadvisor review saying the site
"has been totally looted by the government army after Tigray war, but I highly
recommend to visit the museum." That the site operates is established; that the
four specific airframes survive is not. They are recorded because heavy outdoor
hardware is the least removable part of such a site — and every one of those four
rows says so in its description. **Bahir Dar** rests on a 2018 entry plus 2016 and
2019 visitor reviews, in a region that has since had its own conflict. **Asmara
Expo Park** rests on a 2014 entry and 2006 field notes. **Massawa** rests on a
2014 entry and one undated field note.

**Satellite imagery could not be used in this pass** — the browser extension was
unavailable and the Overpass API was unreachable through the proxy. **All four
weak-currency sites above could be settled in about ten minutes by someone with a
browser and Google Earth's historical-imagery slider.** That is the single
highest-value follow-up here.

## Deliberate blanks

**`year_built` is blank on every row but one.** The only sourced construction date
in the whole slice is Tsehai — assembled in Addis Ababa during 1935, first flight
December 1935. No serial or construction number anywhere was converted into a
year.

**`tail_number` is blank on six rows**, in every case because no identity was
established rather than because nobody looked: the Hargeisa MiG-17 (no serial ever
published), the Mekelle MiG-23BN, the Bahir Dar Mi-24A, the Mang'u Beech 65
(wears a marking judged false), the Stanley's Haven PA-31 (no visible ID), and
the Aero Beach MiG-21MF U-919 (serial known, construction number not).

**Postal codes are blank for Somaliland, Uganda, Eritrea and Djibouti.** The
Ethiopian and Kenyan codes present came from reverse-geocoding the display
coordinate and are **P.O. Box district codes rather than delivery postcodes** —
lower confidence than a US ZIP.

## Coordinate provenance

Every coordinate is a fix on the airframe or display group, or a named map
feature — never a town centroid. Sixteen of the eighteen come from Spottingmode's
per-location coordinates, which are contributor-placed positions derived from
satellite imagery. Adwa and Kigali are named OSM features (Adwa matching the
Amharic-named building, preferred over Wikipedia's coordinate 130 m away; Kigali
being the OSM "Rwanda Art Museum" feature, which is the same institution on the
same site). Hargeisa is independently corroborated: Wikipedia and Spottingmode
agree to within 5 m and Nominatim reverse-geocodes that point to "War Memorial
(MIG)".

**Two caveats.** **Djibouti BA 188's five display points are spread over about
2 km**; they are recorded as one air park using the Mirage IIIC position, but the
Beech 18 at 11.55291, 43.15002 is 1.7 km west of the rest and may sit on the
Djiboutian or US side rather than the French base — if so it should become a
separate record. **Bahir Dar's coordinate** reverse-geocodes only to "Bahir Dar"
with no feature name; it was matched to the Amhara martyrs' monument on the
strength of a description it fits. That is an inference, not a confirmed feature
match.

## Documented negative results

**Tanzania** — no display. Military airframes at Ngeregere and Mwanza are dumps;
Dar es Salaam and Zanzibar are stored civil aircraft. One unresolved lead below.
**Burundi** — no display; all five Bujumbura entries are `std`. **South Sudan** —
nothing; searches returned only accident records. **Comoros** — no display.
**Mauritius and Seychelles** — nothing; Spottingmode has zero entries for either
and neither national history museum has an aviation holding. **Rwanda** — one
record only; Spottingmode has zero Rwandan entries. **Madagascar** — one boneyard,
no displays. **Somalia proper**, as distinct from Somaliland — no display;
everything at Mogadishu, Berbera, Galkayo, Guriel and Bardere is a wreck, a dump
or stored.

## Ranked open questions

1. **Do the Mekelle Hawulti aircraft still exist?** Mi-24A 1621, MiG-21bis 1113, a
   MiG-23BN and Cessna 421 ST-PRC, last recorded June 2015, at a museum
   deliberately looted during the Tigray war. One Google Earth historical-imagery
   comparison at 13.49238, 39.46076 answers all four rows.
2. **Does the Ethiopian Air Force Museum exist at all?** If it does, it is the
   largest collection in the region and this package misses it entirely. One call
   or email to the Ethiopian Air Force at Bishoftu, or to the Ethiopian Ministry
   of Tourism, closes it either way.
3. **What aircraft are in the Military Gallery at Uhuru Gardens National Monument
   and Museum, Nairobi** (-1.3205, 36.7961, open daily, ~12,300 artefacts)?
   Wikipedia says the gallery holds "KDF military hardware, aircraft, artillery"
   but names nothing. This is a major new purpose-built national museum and the
   most likely place in Kenya to hold unrecorded airframes. **No site record was
   created because not a single aircraft could be named.**
4. **Bahir Dar and Asmara Expo Park currency** — 2018 and 2014 respectively, both
   in regions that have seen conflict since. Satellite check at 11.60065,
   37.41259 and 15.31698, 38.91755.
5. **Where is Tsehai actually displayed today?** Ethiopian Monitor reported in
   February 2024 that it went to the Adwa Victory Memorial Museum, and it is
   recorded there. But a detailed first-hand visit account from December 2025
   describes the galleries at length and never mentions an aircraft — though
   photography is prohibited inside and the writer was not looking for one.
6. **What is the An-28 JW9033 at Dodoma?** Statused `pre` at -6.15935, 35.76532,
   which reverse-geocodes to a residential street in central Dodoma, ~2 km from
   the airport. A Tanzanian Air Force An-28 in a city block is very likely a
   monument or a business landmark. **The most likely missing Tanzanian record.**
7. **What are the two MiG-21s at -18.99887, 47.53018?** 169 (MiG-21bis) and 291
   (MiG-21UM), statused `pre`, at Imerimanjaka south of Antananarivo — 22 km from
   the Ivato boneyard, so not part of it. If it is a display it is Madagascar's
   only one.
8. **The Djibouti Beech 18 F-BEHI** — the only airframe in that group without a
   2025 last-noted date, and 1.7 km from the others.
9. **The two Djibouti Mirage IIIC identities.** A February 2025 contributor could
   not tell which of 22 and 82 stands where; serial 22 on the complete airframe
   is provisional.
10. **The Burayu Boeing 737 J2-KCC** (9.05924, 38.60841, west of Addis) —
    statused `pre` and repainted in current Ethiopian Airlines colours, but last
    updated October 2016 and the site could not be identified. No record created.
11. **The Nairobi billboard 737** (-1.34414, 36.91470, unveiled December 2024,
    painted in advertising colours) — identity actively disputed between 5Y-BXZ
    and 5Y-KQK with no resolution as of October 2025, and an advertising hoarding
    built from an airframe was judged to fall outside the display line. Worth a
    second opinion on both counts.
