# Oklahoma

41 museums, 176 aircraft, built in three phases: public civilian museums,
military-base collections, and a park/monument sweep.

Oklahoma was a greenfield build — the database held nothing in the state
before this pass.

## Sources and their weight

Two compilations dominate the published record for Oklahoma, and **both are
stale**:

- **silverhawkauthor.com's "Warplanes preserved in the USA: Oklahoma"** is by
  far the most complete single listing, and it is the ancestor of most other
  lists. It is also undated and demonstrably wrong in places: it still carries
  the Claremore B-17 that left for Palm Springs in 2021 and the Bristow museum
  that died around 2011; it lists four Altus AFB aircraft last evidenced in
  1987; it double-counts a Vance T-37B; it gives a painted marking as the
  A-26's identity; and it misfiles the Oklahoma National Guard Museum's
  airframes under "Tinker ANG Base". Nothing from it was imported unless a
  second source agreed or a photograph settled it.
- **aviationmuseum.eu** is the other. Its Tulsa page is not trustworthy at the
  row level — its registration column and its type column do not align (N2237C
  is given to a DC-3 there and to an ultralight elsewhere). Its coordinates are
  wrong for both Stafford (longitude off by 0.3 degrees) and Science Museum
  Oklahoma (off by ~25 km).

Against these were run **Joe Baugher's serial pages**, **Aerial Visuals**
airframe and location dossiers (which carry per-airframe display coordinates),
**ABPic** dated photographer logs, the **FAA registry**, the **NMUSAF loan
list**, HMdb markers, and each museum's own site. Where an agent could read a
serial off the airframe in a photograph, that is noted in the record.

## Corrections made

- **The Army Air Defense Artillery Museum does not exist as a museum.** This
  closes the thread Texas opened. The Fort Bliss collection did move to Fort
  Sill under BRAC, but Fort Sill's own page says the museum "is currently in the
  planning stages"; the collection sits in the **ADA Training Support Facility**,
  opened December 2020, which is not open to the general public and has roughly
  6% of the holdings on display. Not recorded — see exclusions.
- **The 45th Infantry Division Museum is now the Oklahoma National Guard
  Museum**, and it moved. It left 2145 NE 36th Street and opened a new building
  at 3301 NE Grand Boulevard to the public on **12 June 2026**. No aviation
  directory reflects either change.
- **Stafford's F-117A is 85-0819, not 82-0819.** The wrong serial was printed by
  Vintage Aviation News and inherited by Wikipedia.
- **Stafford's T-38 is 59-1595 wearing false marks 67-14956.**
- **Brenham-style false serials are endemic at the Guard museum**: the A-7D is
  74-1756 wearing 72-0240; the OH-58A is 70-15257 wearing 70-15425; the F-86D
  wears 52-4043 and its true identity is disputed between two sources; and the
  "OH-6A 68-17193" is a composite parts airframe with no single true serial. The
  genuine 68-17193 became N5185H and flies for USDA Wildlife Services in Utah.
- **Tinker's C-47 is BuNo 50761, an R4D-6**, wearing the false USAAF serial
  2150761. It is widely misfiled as 43-49012.
- **Vance's A-26 is 43-22444**, displayed marked as 44-34156.
- **Altus's B-47E wears 52-0413**, which is not its own serial; and the
  surviving-B-47 list itself notes manufacturer records may make the true
  identity 51-7052 rather than 51-7071. Both facts are in the record.
- **The C-47A 42-92838 has moved twice.** Fairgrounds until about 2006, then
  Metro Tech, then **Midwest City** since December 2017, dedicated 9 November
  2018 at the Veterans Memorial in Joe B. Barnes Regional Park. Every list that
  places it at Will Rogers World Airport is two moves out of date.
- **The Aero Commander prototype "Blue Goose" NX1946 left the State
  Fairgrounds**; it was restored at Wiley Post Airport and dedicated at **Eldon
  Lyon Park, Bethany** on 14 October 2024.
- **Woodring's F-4C 63-7440 is an EF-4C**, converted to the Wild Weasel IV
  configuration. Recorded as F-4/C per the base-designation rule with the
  EF-4C identity in aliases.
- **The Lexington "Hiller H-12"** is impossible as written: H-12 is the civil
  UH-12 designation, the military type is H-23/OH-23, Lexington is not on
  OK-59, and Lexington is Chickasaw Nation not Citizen Potawatomi. At least
  three of the entry's four facts are wrong.
- **The Charles B. Hall Air Park is outside the Tinker perimeter** and needs no
  base access — recorded `public`, not `restricted`. Same for the **Vance air
  park**, which was built on Gott Road specifically so the public could see the
  aircraft; Vance's other display airframes are inside the fence and are a
  separate `restricted` record.

## A collision worth recording

**T-33A 52-9846** was already in the database at the **Air Force Flight Test
Museum, Edwards AFB** — which is exactly where the Stafford entry's own
provenance says the airframe came from ("formerly on a pole outside the Test
Pilot School building"). The Stafford claim was last photo-confirmed in 2008 and
the airframe never appeared alongside the museum's Canadair CT-133 in the 2024
photo sweep. The two records are almost certainly one airframe, and the
Weatherford entry is the conflation. Dropped from Stafford; Edwards keeps it.

## Excluded, and why

**Not museums / no aircraft**
- **US Army ADA Training Support Facility, Fort Sill** — not a museum, not open,
  and with 94% of the collection off display there is no way to tell a display
  item from stored training material. Recorded nowhere. Worth revisiting if the
  planned museum is built.
- **Muskogee War Memorial Park (USS Batfish)** — holds no aircraft, and has
  relocated: it left Muskogee on 1 July 2025 after 52 years, to a temporary
  address at Three Forks Harbor, Fort Gibson, with a permanent site funded but
  not built.
- **The Ninety-Nines Museum of Women Pilots**, OKC — a real and current aviation
  museum with no full-size airframes. Louise Thaden's Travel Air, often
  attributed to it, is at the Kansas Aviation Museum.
- **Alva Regional Airport Museum** and **Waynoka Air Rail Museum** — aviation
  heritage, no airframes.
- **Stillwater Airport Memorial Museum** — no airframes, and no evidence it
  survived the airport's new terminal around 2020.
- **137th Special Operations Wing, Will Rogers ANGB** — no display airframe
  found. Absence of evidence, not proof; ANG bases are poorly documented.
- **Oklahoma Aviation and Space Hall of Fame** — housed inside Science Museum
  Oklahoma; recording it would duplicate that museum.
- **Oklahoma History Center** — its *Winnie Mae* is a replica (the original
  Lockheed Vega NR105W is at the Smithsonian) and its other aviation holding is
  a Skylab command module of unverified provenance. Left for a later pass.

**Closed or dispersed**
- **Heartland Air Museum, Bristow** — no evidence of operation after about 2011;
  its DC-3 N231GB is now registered to a private individual.
- **Phillips Petroleum Company Museum, Bartlesville** — closed permanently in
  2025 after 17 years. Its Parker Pusher's disposition is unknown, and note that
  Science Museum Oklahoma also lists a Parker Pusher — the two records may
  collide.
- **U.S. Veterans Museum, Granbury** style cases elsewhere in Oklahoma: none
  found beyond the above.

**Not displays**
- **Broken Arrow TC-45J BuNo 23791** — a derelict hulk beside a parking lot,
  last photographed September 2000, and its registration N10909 has since been
  reissued to a 2006 Cessna T182T, which is the paper trail of a scrapping.
- **Cushing Municipal Airport UH-1H** — single stale source, no serial, no
  photograph, no second source, and the city's own airport page does not mention
  it. Held.
- **Muskogee Davis Field T-33 53-4971** — same lineage, same problem: one
  undated text claim, no photograph.
- **Oklahoma State Fairgrounds** — excluded as a site. The Aero Commander left
  in 2024, and the "AT-6D 44-80858" is N107FG, a privately owned airworthy
  warbird photographed there during an event, never a monument.
- **Lexington Hiller** — see corrections above.
- **Inola, Dobies Heliport UH-1H 64-13628** — appears to be based there, not
  displayed.
- **Camp Gruber, Braggs UH-1** — no serial recorded, and it is an active
  Guard post.
- **Fire-training, battle-damage-repair and maintenance-training airframes** at
  Tinker (a B-52 and a KC-135 near Runway 35, Chilean T-37 fuselages and a
  KC-135 forward fuselage at the ARINC facility) and the four SilverHawk Altus
  entries last evidenced in 1987.
- **The Tinker "C-141B"** listed by the American Legion is a granite-and-steel
  silhouette monument dedicated in 2017, not an airframe.
- **Two simulators** at the American Military Heritage Museum, including one
  built around a real F-4 cockpit section. Not airframes — and the F-4
  simulator must not be conflated with the genuine EF-4C 63-7440 outdoors at
  the Woodring park.
- **Engines and components without an airframe** at Stafford (V-2 engine, F-1,
  J-2, SSME, an SRB segment), plus free-fall weapons and scale models.
- **Private collections with no public access**: the Alva P-51Ds, the Tulsa Sea
  Fury / A-7D / P-51D / F4U-4, and Dr John Swartz's Grove collection.

## Judgment calls

**Replicas are recorded and flagged.** Stafford holds a genuine flown Gemini
VI-A on loan from the Smithsonian alongside full-scale Apollo, Gemini, Sputnik
and Explorer mockups; every replica says so in its aliases. The Titan II is a
genuine vehicle.

**Science Museum Oklahoma is recorded conservatively.** Its own site names only
two aircraft. Of the roughly thirty airframes the directories attribute to it,
ten were imported: the three with two-source or Baugher backing, the two the
museum names itself, and five listed by both directories independently. The
remaining homebuilts, hang gliders, gliders and the FB-111A cockpit section rest
on a single undated directory entry each and are not recorded. This collection
has been reorganised repeatedly — Kirkpatrick to Omniplex to Science Museum
Oklahoma, with the Oklahoma Air and Space Museum folded in — and no post-2020
account enumerates the airframes.

**The Guard museum's airframes are recorded at the museum with a location
flag.** Only the F-86D is confirmed at the new Grand Boulevard site; a June 2026
visitor account reports the outdoor vehicle collection "largely not present" and
possibly still at the old site. Each of the other eleven carries
"location after the June 2026 museum move unverified" in its aliases.

**The El Reno A-26 outlived its host.** VFW Post 382 sold the property in March
2025 under IRS pressure, and the building is being renovated into a restaurant —
with an explicit commitment that the aircraft stays, confirmed in place as of
March 2026. The record is named for the display, not the post.

## Blank fields, deliberately

`year_built` is populated on only a handful of records, all from a sourced
build, first-flight or delivery date: Stafford's Sabre (1954) and F-100
completion (April 1957), the Tulsa Spartans (1945, 1941, 1931), the A-26B
(August 1944), the AURA (FAA 1990), the CAF C-45H (FAA 1954), the two 1941 CAF
trainers, the Caldwell Collection's museum-stated years, the Woolaroc Travel Air
(1927, the Dole Race year), Blue Goose (1948, first flight 23 April), and the
Midwest City C-47 (1944, from attested D-Day service). **No fiscal-year serial
prefix was ever laundered into that column** — which is why every military
airframe at Tinker, Vance, Altus, Fort Sill, Tulsa ANGB and every monument has
a blank year.

Tail numbers are blank where no serial could be sourced: the Guard museum's
F-86D, T-33A and composite OH-6A; Tulsa's A-10, UH-1, Gulfstream II and
Sabreliner; Vance's A-10; the Shawnee Huey (the visible "282" is a nose number,
not a serial); the four American Military Heritage Museum airframes; 24 of the
Caldwell Collection's 29; both WWII ADT Skytrains; and both CAF Tulsa trainers.

## Needs a human on site

- **The single highest-value follow-up is a phone call to four museums** for
  registrations: the Caldwell Collection (24 unregistered airframes), the
  American Military Heritage Museum (4, plus its sub-types), the WWII Airborne
  Demonstration Team (2), and CAF Spirit of Tulsa (2-3). All are solvable with
  one FAA registry pull by owner name or one email each.
- **Where the Oklahoma National Guard Museum's eleven unconfirmed airframes
  physically are** after the June 2026 move.
- **Whether Tinker's AT-38B 61-0817 is inside or outside the fence.** If it is
  outside at the Welcome Center it belongs in the public air park record.
- **Stafford's MiG-21 and MiG-23** carry display numbers only, with no registry
  backing, and one source calls the MiG-21 an MF rather than an R.
- **Huntsville-style serial conflicts**: Vance's T-41A registration (N5168F vs
  N5111F), the Salina display (an Aerial Visuals dossier says T-38A 61-0926, a
  photo-bearing source describes a T-33A — possibly one airframe misidentified),
  and the Tinker EC-121's false serial, reported three different ways.
- **Single-source serials wanting a photograph**: Stillwater's RF-84F 53-7604,
  Tishomingo's BuNo 141859, Woodring's T-37B 56-3519, Elk City's and Muskogee's
  T-33s, and Vance's T-37B 56-3498 and T-38A 65-10426 (both omitted from the
  import for exactly this reason).
- **Currency spot-checks** on Elk City, Ames, Medford, Glencoe, Roland and the
  Okmulgee HU-16 — all rest on Aerial Visuals dossiers with coordinates but no
  recent dated photograph.
- **Coordinates that are site centroids rather than fixes on the airframe**:
  Jet (town centroid — the town is tiny, so the error is small), Elk City and
  Okmulgee (aerodrome reference points), the American Military Heritage Museum
  (mall building centroid), Tinker/Vance/Altus base displays (installation
  centroids), and Bartlesville, Glencoe, Roland, Stroud and Salina, which use
  Aerial Visuals' recorded airframe positions.
- **Clarence E. Page Airport's postal code**, 73099, is the commonly published
  Yukon ZIP for the field and was not confirmed against an authoritative record.
- **Three unresolved F-86 leads** from the Aerial Visuals index with no serial
  and no host site: Kingfisher, McAlester and Stroud. Not recorded.
