## NOTES

### Sources and their weight

**What carried this region: spottingmode.com "Wrecks and Relics Online".** It is not a museum directory — it is a georeferenced field survey. Every location carries decimal coordinates, a free-text description, a last-update date, and a per-airframe table with registration, construction number, operator country, service branch and a status code (`pre` preserved / `std` stored / `dum` dump / `dlt` derelict / `i/a` instructional). Roughly 80 per cent of the rows in this file come from it. It is by a very wide margin the best source that exists for African preserved aircraft and it is essentially invisible to English-language search.

Mechanics, for the next pass: the site rejects modern TLS because its Diffie-Hellman key is too small, so `curl --ciphers "DEFAULT@SECLEVEL=1"` is required; WebFetch fails on it outright (robots.txt fetch throws an SSL error). URL shapes are `https://www.spottingmode.com/wro/<country>/` (locations table with coordinates and dates), `https://www.spottingmode.com/wro/<country>/planes/` (all airframes for that country), and `https://www.spottingmode.com/wro/location/<id>/` (per-location airframe table with status and remarks). 517 location pages were pulled for the eighteen countries in this brief. The per-location pages are essential: the country plane list gives only a city name so a base with six separate plinths collapses into one ambiguous string.

**Second sources used.** The Egyptian Ministry of Defence's own museum index (`mod.gov.eg/modwebsite/MilMuseumAr.aspx`) names exactly five public military museums — بانوراما أكتوبر (October Panorama), متحف العلمين (El Alamein), متحف بورسعيد (Port Said), المتحف الحربى (the Citadel Military Museum) and the Gamal Abdel Nasser museum. That independently confirms four of the Egyptian sites here as public institutions rather than spotter guesses. The Tunisian MoD heritage site (`hmp.defense.tn`) does the same for Manouba. The Algerian MDN site does the same for the Musée central de l'Armée. OSM/Nominatim reverse geocoding was run on about forty coordinates and independently returned the institution by name for Manouba, Tizi Ouzou, the Cairo Citadel museum, the Casablanca aviation museum, Mekelle's Hawelti Museum, Arivonimamo air base, Moi Forces Lanet and Zomba Airport — that is a genuine second source for each of those, and it also settled several ambiguous cases (Dodoma's An-28 is in a city block not at the airfield; Egypt's "Hulwan" MiG-21 is on a public road; Luxor's monuments are on the Airport Road).

For Aero Beach Entebbe a first-hand blog visit published 27 Jul 2026 confirms it is still an operating paid public attraction — that is the freshest independent confirmation anywhere in this file.

**Compilations that proved thin, and how.** `aviationmuseum.eu` lists only twelve African countries in total (Angola, Djibouti, Egypt, Ghana, Mali, Nigeria, Somalia, South Africa, Sudan, Tunisia, Zambia, Zimbabwe) — it has no Morocco, no Algeria, no Kenya, no Ethiopia, no Eritrea page at all, and its country index pages return HTTP 403 to automated fetches. It contributed nothing that spottingmode did not already have. Arabic, French, Portuguese and Swahili web search returned almost nothing usable for monument aircraft: the Arabic searches surfaced only the already-known Cairo museums, the Portuguese searches for Mozambique returned Brazilian museums, and the Swahili search for Tanzania returned the Askari Monument and the Village Museum — no aviation content. **English coverage of African preserved aircraft is thin; local-language coverage is thinner still.** That is a finding, not a failure of effort.

Wikipedia's API returned HTTP 429 for the whole session (shared egress with parallel researchers) and `ar.wikipedia.org` is marked cache-only for WebFetch, so Arabic Wikipedia could not be read directly.

### OSM Overpass sweep — result and what it means

`overpass-api.de` is blocked here as documented. `overpass.kumi.systems` returned HTTP 504 (dispatcher timeout) on every attempt. `maps.mail.ru` returned an unfollowable 301. **`overpass.private.coffee` worked** and is the mirror to reach for first next time. `area["ISO3166-1"="XX"]` area lookups resolve correctly on it; a single query unioning all eighteen country areas completes in seconds for simple tag filters, but adding a `name~` regex over whole-country areas blows the timeout, so keep name regexes out.

The sweep of `historic=aircraft`, `memorial=aircraft`, `memorial:type=aircraft` and `aeroway=gate_guardian` across **all eighteen countries** returned **exactly one element**: node 869845606, "Abandoned Caribou aircraft", `historic=aircraft` + `tourism=attraction`, at -25.44447 / 31.92708 in Mozambique near the South African border. It is tagged "abandoned" and no operator, type or registration is recorded, so it is NOT emitted as a record here — a DHC-4 hulk in the bush fails the deliberate-retention test on the evidence available. It is worth a human look.

**One OSM hit across eighteen countries and roughly 250 real preserved airframes is a statement about OpenStreetMap, not about Africa.** An empty or near-empty Overpass result over Africa is not evidence of absence and must never be reported as one. The comparable European tag sweep would return hundreds. Do not let a future pass conclude "OSM shows nothing, therefore nothing is there".

### Corrections and identity work

- **Sudan's Wadi Seidna "F-5" and "FT-5" are not Northrop.** Serials 710 and 719 are Chinese Shenyang F-5 and FT-5 — the licence-built MiG-17F and MiG-15UTI. The source's own location note calls the first one "MiG-17". Recorded with manufacturer Shenyang. A pass that reads "F-5" as Freedom Fighter will put two Northrops in Sudan that never existed.
- **Egypt's Citadel Military Museum airframes almost all wear false numbers.** MiG-17F 2481 is displayed as 1901 (which is the Almaza museum's MiG-15UTI serial) and was previously displayed as 752; MiG-21F-13 5908 is displayed as 658; Su-7BMK 7320 is displayed as 7751 (which is the Almaza museum's Su-17K serial); Yak-18A 621 as 46; the Wilga as 42; the Zlin as 61. True identity is in `tail_number`, the worn marking is in `aliases`, and the reason is in `description`. This is exactly the repainting trap the contract warns about, and Egypt does it systematically.
- **Egypt's Almaza F-4E 7827 is displayed as 67289** — a USAF-style serial that is not its Egyptian identity.
- **Luxor's four monument aircraft carry 2355 / 2356 / 2357 / 2358.** Those are a consecutive display-numbering block for a monument group, not serials. Same for Minya's L-29 "1973" (the October War year), Sebha's SF.260 "1969" (the Al Fateh revolution year), and Mersa Matruh's Su-17 "977".
- **Benghazi has two different aircraft both displayed as "319"** — a MiG-21bis (whose true serial the source suspects is 701) and MiG-23MS 6907. Recorded separately with the duplication flagged.
- **Algeria's Su-7UMK GL-55 may read SL-55.** Both forms are in `aliases`.
- **Algeria's T-34C 7T-WPD is displayed as 7T-WBD** — one letter off, and 7T-WBD is not its registration.
- **Tunisia's Alouette II L51-003 (c/n 1811) and L51003/3 (c/n 1795) are two different airframes** at two different sites (Cité des Sciences in Tunis and Sousse). Any pass that dedupes on the serial string will merge them wrongly.
- **Tunisia's HH-3E c/n 61.620 appears twice** — once at Bizerte as L81108/TS-SRE and once at Sidi Ahmed as L82105/TS-SRE marked "Removed". Only the Bizerte record is emitted; the Sidi Ahmed entry is treated as the same airframe before it moved.
- **Zambia's Chipmunk AF-504 has moved** — logged at the ZAF headquarters site and again at Twin Palm, both in June 2025, with the headquarters entry marked removed. Emitted once, at Twin Palm.
- **Madagascar's Broussard 5R-MND** likewise appears at two Ivato points with one marked removed. Emitted once.
- **Malawi's Do 27** was rebuilt by Malawi Army Air Wing technicians from one or two crashed airframes serialled MAAW-12 and MAAW-13 specifically in order to preserve it. Which serial it now wears is not recorded, so `tail_number` is deliberately blank and both candidates are in `aliases`. This is a composite and is flagged as one.
- **Egypt's Su-7BMK 7320 vs Su-17K 7751 and MiG-15UTI 1901 vs MiG-17F 2481**: the Citadel and Almaza collections cross-contaminate each other's serials through repainting. Import both museums in the same transaction or the uniqueness key will fight itself.

### Judgment calls

- **Gate guards on closed military bases are recorded, with `access_type: restricted`.** The contract is explicit that a plinthed gate guard is a record. In the Maghreb and Egypt this is where nearly all the volume is: Tafaraoui alone has twelve plinthed airframes, Mecheria eight, Bousfer eight. Excluding them would have produced a near-empty file.
- **Multiple plinths on one installation are grouped into one site.** Tafaraoui's five separate survey points become one Tafaraoui site with twelve airframes. Splitting them into five "sites" 300 m apart would be noise. The exception is where a geocode showed the points are genuinely different places — Livingstone airport vs the Chipmunk 4 km away in town; Ivato vs Imerimanjaka; the two Helwan sites (a public road and the aircraft factory).
- **Aircraft converted into restaurants, bars, playgrounds and billboards are recorded.** Kitengela's Boeing 720, Hargeisa's Il-18 "Guluf Airline Restaurant", Murang'a's Navajo playground, and the Kenya Airways 737 billboard on Mombasa Road all qualify as deliberate retention plus public presentation.
- **Aero Beach Entebbe is recorded as a separate site from the Entebbe air base gate guards.** They are 800 m apart, differently owned and differently accessed. The brief lists "Uganda's Entebbe display" as already covered; whichever of these two the existing record means, the other is new.
- **`in_storage` used once**, for the Lady Be Good remains at Tobruk, because Wikipedia says "stored at Jamal Abdelnasser Air Force Base" while the field survey files it under "Tobruk - museum". Recording it as `on_display` would assert a public exhibition nobody has documented.
- **Instructional airframes (`i/a`) are excluded** unless the setting is itself a display — so Mang'u High School's 737 is in (it is a school landmark that the source's own observer photographed) but Blida's Ecole Nationale de Techniques Aéronautiques hangar contents are out, as are Marrakech's ex-French Mirage F1Cs and Sfax's indoor school airframes.
- **Sub-variants left blank where the source only said "MiG-21".** Guessing PF vs PFM vs bis from a satellite description would manufacture a fact.

### EXCLUDED, and why — a well-searched zero

- **Rwanda: zero.** The Rwandan country survey has exactly one location (Kigali, "several small props", civil, Aug 2025) and **zero** airframes with preserved status. No museum, no monument, no gate guard was found in any language. The database's existing single Rwandan record is all there appears to be. This is a genuine zero, not a gap.
- **OSM node "Abandoned Caribou aircraft"** (Mozambique, -25.44447 / 31.92708) — excluded as a bush hulk with no type, operator or retention evidence. Worth a human look.
- **Storage and scrap concentrations, all excluded**: Libya's Misrata Galeb and MiG-23 dumps (twenty-plus airframes), Tripoli Mitiga's MiG-25 line, Al Jufra, Birak's L-39s, Gamal Abdul El Nasser's MiG-21s, Beni Walid's L-410s; Egypt's Cairo West F-4 storage line (ten-plus airframes) and Bilbays' thirteen stored L-29s; Ethiopia's Harar Meda storage (eight Mi-24 and eight MiG-23 fuselages plus a dozen more), Dire Dawa; Eritrea's Asmara airfield boneyard (the "several T-33, F-5, F-86, MiG-21, MiG-23, helicopters and C-119" cluster — twenty-four airframes, and it is a graveyard not a display); Uganda's Entebbe MiG-21/MiG-23 storage line; Tanzania's Mwanza MiG-21s and Ngeregere MiG-17/MiG-19 lines; Zambia's Mbala Jastreb line and Mumbwa's MiG-19/MiG-15/MB-326 field; Mozambique's Nacala MiG-21 line (eight airframes) and Beira's collapsed hangar; Sudan's Wadi Seidna wreck fields; Morocco's Marrakech stored T-37/CM.170/T-6 park (twenty-nine airframes) and Rabat's Chinook dump; Algeria's Laghouat and Blida Il-28 dumps and Ain Arnat's Mi-8 rows.
- **Airliner storage at Khartoum, Tripoli, Nairobi, Lokichogio, Maputo, Addis Ababa, Mogadishu, Berbera** — all working or abandoned commercial parking, none of it display.
- **Djibouti's Balbala scrapyard F-100s** (two airframes) — excluded as scrap; the plinthed F-100D at the base is recorded.
- **Sudan Police "Joker" 201** at Khartoum — the type designation could not be resolved to a manufacturer at all and inventing one would have been worse than omitting it. Also **Sudan's SAFAT 01** (an instructional airframe at Khartoum) and the Antonov An-2 instructional airframe at Sudan University of Science and Technology.
- **Egypt's As Salihiyah, Gebel El Basur, Wadi Abu Rish and Bilbays entries are recorded with types but no serials** — a decision to record rather than exclude, because the contract says a single plinthed aircraft is a site record. They are marked low confidence.
- **The "Ethiopian Air Force Museum at Bishoftu" was NOT created.** As the brief states, it does not exist. What is at Bishoftu/Harar Meda is a plinthed MiG-21MF (serial 1083, sighted Feb 2025) inside the air base perimeter, plus a large storage and scrap area. It is recorded as a base display, not a museum.
- **The Royal Moroccan Air Force aeronautics museum in Rabat does not exist yet.** A 2018 lepetitjournal.com piece describes an FAR project in cooperation with Le Bourget; nothing since. Separately, Le Desk (8 Apr 2026) reports Royal Air Maroc will build a headquarters tower **and an aviation museum** at Casa Anfa. Neither is open. Do not create either.

### Blank fields, deliberately

- `year_built` is blank on all but one row. Not a single African source in this sweep gave a sourced construction, roll-out or delivery date. Soviet construction numbers are recorded in `aliases` (they encode build dates for anyone who wants to decode them) but they were not converted into `year_built` values, because the contract forbids inferring a year from a number without a source. The one exception is the Lady Be Good, whose 1942 build is documented.
- `postal_code` is blank almost everywhere — postal codes are not in general use for these addresses and the few returned by geocoding are recorded.
- `website` is blank for every gate-guard and monument site and for most museums; the Egyptian, Tunisian and Algerian ministry pages are the only official web presences found.
- `tail_number` is blank wherever the source recorded "..." — that is the survey's explicit marker for "airframe present, serial not read", and filling it would be invention.
- `aircraft_name` is populated once (Lady Be Good). No other airframe in the region carries an individual name in any source.

### Needs a human — ranked

1. **Mekelle, Ethiopia — the Hawulti aircraft park.** Four airframes (Mi-24A 1621, MiG-21bis 1113, a MiG-23BN, and Cessna 421 ST-PRC) last confirmed 26 Jun 2015. Mekelle was besieged and shelled 2020–2022. Nothing current could be established in any language. One phone call to the Tigray Bureau of Culture and Tourism or a single dated photograph would settle whether these four rows should exist at all. The site itself is confirmed present in OSM as "Hawelti Museum" at 13.49238 / 39.46076.
2. **Casablanca — Musée Royal Air Maroc / متحف الطيران.** Five airframes including a Lockheed L-749 Constellation, a genuinely rare survivor. Last airframe sighting Oct 2024, but Royal Air Maroc announced in April 2026 that it is redeveloping the Casa Anfa site with a new headquarters tower and a purpose-built aviation museum. The collection is very likely to move. Contact Royal Air Maroc corporate communications; one call establishes both the current location and the future one.
3. **Tobruk — Lady Be Good.** Two sources disagree on whether the B-24D's remains are on museum display or in military storage at Jamal Abdel Nasser air base. This is one of the most historically significant airframes in Africa and the record should not stay ambiguous. Try the Libyan Antiquities Authority or the US Air Force Historical Research Agency, which tracked the recovery.
4. **Asmara Expo Park, Eritrea.** Three large airframes (a DC-3, a 727 and an An-12) last confirmed 2014. The Expo grounds are demonstrably still in annual public use (Eritrea Festival footage 2023) so someone photographs this site every year — the photographs just are not indexed in English. Eritrean diaspora media or the Eritrean Ministry of Information would close it.
5. **Sousse, Tunisia.** A Saab 91D and an Alouette II confirmed as recently as 21 Apr 2026 but the institution at 35.82281 / 10.63338 could not be named. Somebody local knows in one sentence whether it is a barracks, a school or a public park — and that decides `access_type`.
6. **Dodoma, Tanzania.** An-28 JW9033 in a city block, 2018 evidence, and it is the country's only record. A second Tanzanian airframe (a Shorts SD.330) is logged 2 km away on the same date and was not emitted for lack of any status. Tanzania at 2 records instead of 0 would still be badly under-represented for a country that flew MiG-17s, MiG-19s and MiG-21s.
7. **Khartoum — Sudan Military Museum.** Four airframes, record refreshed Dec 2025, but the museum sits in a city that has been a battlefield since April 2023. `access_type` is recorded as restricted on that basis. Someone should establish whether the museum and its aircraft still stand.
8. **Egypt's unserialled monuments** — As Salihiyah, Gebel El Basur, Wadi Abu Rish, Bilbays, the Nasr City group, the Military Technical College, and both Al Tayaran Street airframes. Every one is a real aircraft on a plinth photographed from the ground or from satellite; none has a serial. A single Egyptian enthusiast with a camera would convert about a dozen low-confidence rows into high-confidence ones. The Egyptian Air Force Museum's own Facebook page (facebook.com/museum.eaf) and Instagram (@museum.eaf) are the obvious point of contact.
9. **The OSM Caribou in Mozambique** at -25.44447 / 31.92708 — type, ownership and whether anything is being done with it.
