# NOTES

## Sources and their weight

**What carried the Caribbean.** The single highest-yield instrument by a wide margin was the
**OSM Overpass sweep**, not any museum directory. `overpass-api.de` is blocked as briefed;
**maps.mail.ru** answered every Caribbean query first time with a browser User-Agent and a
POSTed query file, and the union-of-`area["ISO3166-1"=...]` form worked for all 24 Caribbean
country and territory codes in one request (39 elements). It found, with no English-language
equivalent anywhere: the Santa Clara aircraft cluster, the Holguin monument MiG, the Camilo
Cienfuegos military school airframe, the Mayari Arriba helicopter, the La Cabana missile-crisis
aircraft, the eleven-airframe Dominican museum park, the two-airframe San Isidro housing-area
display, and the three-airframe Bayamon science-park group. Eight of the fifteen Caribbean site
records in this file exist only because of that sweep.

**Vietnam.** The same mirror answered Vietnam only via a **bbox** query: `area["ISO3166-1"="VN"]`
timed out with 504 on all three mirrors, twice. `[bbox:8.0,102.0,23.6,110.0]` returned 48
elements in one shot; five of those were in China, Laos and Thailand and were discarded by
coordinate (listed under EXCLUDED). Recommend the bbox form as the default for Vietnam in
future passes.

**Vietnamese national press was the best serial source and the only one I trusted.** Thanh Nien
(2 April 2025) and VnExpress (25 April 2025) independently published the Southern Air Force
Museum roster a month apart, and an OSM surveyor with photographs recorded two of the same
numbers. That is the only Vietnamese serial set in this file I consider safe.

**Which compilation proved stale, and exactly how.**
- `aviationmuseum.eu` on Museo del Aire Cuba says flatly "Permanently closed" and stops there —
  it does not know the collection was relocated to San Antonio de los Banos, which airhistory.net
  and Russia Beyond both record. A researcher trusting it would have deleted a site that moved.
- `aviationmuseum.eu` is also the ONLY source anywhere for a Beechcraft D50 Twin Bonanza N10AM at
  the Ramey AFB Museum. Per the contract I did not import it. See "Needs a human".
- `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_displayed_Lockheed_T-33_Shooting_Stars` has **no Cuban, Dominican,
  Puerto Rican or Vietnamese entries at all**, despite Cuba certainly having at least one (Museo
  del Aire, serial 703, photographed 2003) and probably three. The list is Euro-American in scope
  and should not be used to conclude "no T-33 here".
- `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_surviving_de_Havilland_Vampires` likewise has zero Caribbean
  entries. The Dominican Air Force flew Vampires very late and the brief expected survivors; I
  found none in any source. Recorded as a well-searched zero, not a gap.

## Judgment calls

- **Bay of Pigs B-26 operator_country left BLANK.** The brief flags this and it is the right call:
  the FAR flew B-26s and so did the CIA-supplied Brigade 2506, both in Cuban markings. Without a
  construction number the operator cannot be settled. Blank, with the reason in the description.
- **Captured US aircraft in Vietnam: `US` by default, `VN` where a Vietnamese operational history
  is documented for THAT airframe.** So A-37B 0475 (flown by Tu De on 28 Apr 1975) and F-5E 7579
  (flown in the 1978–79 Cambodia border war) are `VN`; every other captured Huey, Skyraider,
  Dragonfly, Bird Dog and F-5 is `US`. This follows the brief's rule while honouring the contract's
  "the air force that flew it" definition where the evidence supports it.
- **Huu Tiep Lake B-52 — RECORDED, flagged as in-situ wreckage.** The display-versus-wreck test in
  the contract is deliberate retention plus public presentation, not completeness. The lake has been
  kept unclear around the wreck for 54 years, the section is heritage-listed (OSM `heritage=2`), it
  has a walled viewing surround and interpretation, and it is a standing tour stop. It passes. The
  B-52 Victory Museum's assembled wreck two blocks away passes on the same test. Both are marked
  WRECKAGE in the description so no later reader mistakes either for an intact airframe.
- **Santa Clara airliner-cafe RECORDED.** The contract explicitly makes an aircraft converted into a
  cafe and publicly visitable a record. So is the Mi-8PS turned into a children's playground.
- **Concorde G-BOAE recorded `restricted`, not `public` or `appointment`.** The attraction is closed;
  the hangar is used as a cruise-season departure terminal. A member of the public cannot buy a
  ticket to see it, so `public` is wrong; there is no appointment route either. `restricted` is the
  honest coding and the reason is in the site record.
- **Parque Museo Aeronautico FARD recorded `restricted`.** It is inside an active air base.
- **Nha Trang Air Force Officers' College recorded `restricted`.** Military academy.
- **`Unidentified` used 20 times and never a guessed type.** Every Cuban monument MiG is tagged
  "MIG" in OSM, which is a local generic (Cubans call any Soviet fighter a *mig*), not an
  identification. Guessing MiG-21 would have looked authoritative and been unfalsifiable.
- **Zone 5 Museum 2 + 2 + 1 split is flagged as an inference in every row.** Five OSM airframes;
  a guide naming UH-1s and MiG-21s in the plural. The count is sourced; the split is not.
- **No coordinates invented.** Muniz ANGB, the Museo del Aire at San Antonio de los Banos, and
  the two Pakistani-style base cases are all left blank rather than pinned by eye.
