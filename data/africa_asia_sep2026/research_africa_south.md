## NOTES

**Totals: 112 sites, 384 airframes.** For comparison the brief reported the whole
continent standing at 107 sites / 360 airframes, of which these nineteen countries held
roughly 47 sites and ~180 airframes. South Africa alone here is 45 sites and 262
airframes against the 20 / 160 recorded.

### Sources and their weight

**`spottingmode.com/wro` carried this pass almost single-handed, and nothing else came
close.** It is the only source anywhere in this region with a display-versus-derelict
vocabulary: every airframe carries one of `pre` (preserved), `std` (stored), `dum`
(dumped), `dlt` (derelict) or `i/a` (instructional), a per-location coordinate to six
decimals, a construction number where one is known, a free-text location description and
a dated "last update". The whole file is built on its `pre` and `i/a` populations. Its
TLS handshake still fails modern OpenSSL — `curl --ciphers "DEFAULT:@SECLEVEL=0"` is
required, and the `/<country>/planes/` sub-page returns the entire national airframe
table with status codes in one request, which is far cheaper than walking locations.
Country slug notes: Guinea-Bissau is `guinea_bissau`, not `guinea-bissau` (the hyphenated
form 404s to the index and looks like a successful fetch by byte count); Côte d'Ivoire
must be percent-encoded.

Status counts it returned for the nineteen countries, which is itself the shape of the
region: South Africa 185 `pre` + 19 `i/a` out of 347; Nigeria 35 + 6 of 101; Ghana 23 +
12 of 50; Angola 23 + 1 of 55; Zimbabwe 18 of 31; Senegal 6 of 7; Mali 5 of 43 (32 of
Mali's rows are `dlt`); Côte d'Ivoire 5 of 23; DRC 5 + 1 of 54; Botswana 2 + 2 of 10;
Namibia 1 of 8; Guinea-Bissau 1 of 3; São Tomé 2 of 4.

**The OSM Overpass sweep was the second pillar and found things no directory has.**
`historic=aircraft`, `memorial=aircraft`, `memorial:type=aircraft`, `aeroway=gate_guardian`
over all nineteen country areas returned 33 elements. Mirrors: **overpass-api.de is
blocked by this environment's proxy; `maps.mail.ru/osm/tools/overpass/api/interpreter`
worked every time; `overpass.kumi.systems` and `overpass.private.coffee` both returned
"Dispatcher_Client::request_read_and_idx::timeout" runtime errors during this session** —
use mail.ru first. Prefer `area["ISO3166-1"="XX"][admin_level=2]` over a bbox; without
the `admin_level` filter the Namibia query silently matches nothing because `NA` collides
with other tagging.

What OSM contributed that W&R did not: **both Senegalese airframes inside Camp Lieutenant
Amadou Lindor Fall at Thiaroye** (the first Senegalese record found anywhere, and traced
as ways, not points, meaning a mapper drew the actual wing outlines); the **Yaoundé Fouga
Magister memorial**; the **Sikorsky H-19 at Aburi**; the **"Military Museum" tag at
Okahandja and the Outapi War Museum in Namibia** (both investigated and both excluded —
see below). What it *confirmed* is more valuable still: seven airframes where an
independent OSM node sits within 1-8 m of the W&R coordinate — the Thebephatshwa
Strikemaster (2 m), the Franceville Fouga (1 m), the Kumasi L-29 (3 m), the Legon MB-339
(3 m), the Lubango MiG-21 (2 m), the Jos L-29 (8 m), the Komatipoort Caribou (5 m). In a
region where most claims rest on one compiler, that agreement is the strongest evidence
available.

**South Africa is well mapped by OSM and the rest of the region is not. An empty Overpass
result in Namibia, Botswana, Angola, Senegal, Chad, Niger, Guinea-Bissau or São Tomé is
NOT evidence of absence** — it is evidence that nobody has surveyed there. The whole ZA
`historic=aircraft` population is one node (the Komatipoort Caribou); the SAAF Museum,
Ditsong and the SAA Museum are not tagged as aircraft at all. Anyone reading Overpass
counts as coverage will conclude South Africa has one preserved aeroplane.

**Known OSM mis-tagging, re-confirmed:** three DRC nodes at Butembo (`Avuenue mabakanga`,
`Avenue kathi kameru`, `Avenue volcan`) and three Chadian nodes with Arabic names are
street junctions tagged `historic=aircraft`, exactly as the project's earlier Africa notes
recorded. They are excluded again here. Do not re-investigate them.

**`aviationmuseum.eu` failed again in the measured way.** Its Cuito Cuanavale page gives
longitude 18.9974 E; the real memorial is at 19.1646 E — **18 km out**. Its aircraft list
for that site is correct and matched W&R and OSM exactly, so it is usable as a lead and
never as a coordinate.

**Sources that could not be reached:** `faa.ao` (the Angolan armed forces' own site,
which hosts a `museu_fan` page) resets the connection from this environment; the SAAF
Museum's own `saafmuseum.com` and the Outapi Town Council site both fail robots.txt
fetches. The SAAF Museum's three-branch structure was therefore confirmed from Wikipedia
plus `saafmuseum.org.za` rather than from the museum itself.

### Corrections made

- **The SAAF Museum is three separately recorded institutions here, not one.** Swartkop
  (Centurion), Ysterplaat (Cape Town) and Port Elizabeth/Gqeberha each get their own site
  record with its own coordinate, access type and airframe list, because they are 1 400 km
  apart and a researcher looking for Ysterplaat's Shackleton must not be sent to Pretoria.
  Port Elizabeth's airport has been renamed **Chief Dawid Stuurman International**; the
  volunteer body there now presents itself as the **South African Aviation Foundation
  Museum** at `saafmuseum.org.za`, which is a different domain from the SAAF Museum's own
  `saafmuseum.com` and is easy to confuse.
- **Swartkop is two W&R locations, not one.** Location 8778 is the museum display area;
  location 8793, 600 m north, is its storage and restoration line and holds the Venturas,
  the Vampires, the Whirlwinds and a Rhodesian Vampire T.11. Both are recorded under the
  museum, the second as `in_storage`. Ysterplaat and Port Elizabeth likewise each have a
  second W&R coordinate folded in.
- **The Me 262 at Ditsong is confirmed as the world's only surviving Me 262 B-1a/U1** —
  Werknummer 110305, the two-seat radar-equipped night fighter. The brief asked this to be
  verified carefully; W&R records the sub-variant explicitly and the museum's own exhibit
  list names a Messerschmitt in the GE Brink Hall. It is the single most significant
  airframe in Africa.
- **Impala recorded as an Atlas type, not an Aermacchi one.** Impala Mk I = Atlas
  licence-built Aermacchi MB-326M; Impala Mk II = licence-built MB-326K. `manufacturer` is
  Atlas Aircraft Corporation, `model` is Impala, `variant` is Mk I / Mk II, and the MB-326
  designation is in `aliases` in both dashed and dashless form plus in the description.
  W&R lists all of them under the Italian designation, which is why an MB-326 search does
  not find South Africa's largest preserved type population.
- **Cheetah recorded as a distinct model.** Cheetah C / D / D2 / E / R are Atlas rebuilds
  of Dassault Mirage III airframes with canards, a new nose and Israeli-derived avionics;
  `manufacturer` Atlas, `model` Cheetah, variant in its own field, with "Mirage III
  rebuild" in aliases and an explanation in every description. **Cheetah R 855 is the sole
  reconnaissance prototype** and the rarest of the four.
- **Buccaneer S.50 is the South African land-based variant** and is recorded as such, not
  as S.2. Six survive in this file — three at Swartkop and Waterkloof, one each at
  Ysterplaat and Ditsong — plus two ex-RAF S.2Bs on the South African civil register.
- **Zimbabwe Military Museum serial conflicts are recorded rather than resolved.** The
  zimfieldguide visit account and W&R disagree on three airframes: the Harvard (SR48 vs
  SAAF 7397 with c/n 88-13395), the Vampire T.11 (2406 vs 4220 with c/n 15373) and the
  Canberra (R2504 vs 2504). Where W&R supplies a construction number its identity is used
  as `tail_number` and the other reading goes in `aliases` with the conflict stated in the
  description. The Flying Flea appears only in the visit account; Hunter 1827 only in W&R.
- **Angolan Lubango MiG-21 is a PFM, not an MF** — W&R's own comment thread corrected this
  in 2019 and the country table still shows the corrected type.
- **The Lobito PC-7's serial is left blank.** W&R's compiler and a contributor argued in
  2019 over whether the airframe is R-417 or R-419 and the thread ends unresolved. Both
  strings are in `aliases`; neither is asserted as the identity.
- **The Sepupa R44's registration is left blank.** The source's own contributors state the
  colour scheme does not match A2-HLA, so the marks are treated as false and carried in
  `aliases` per the painted-markings rule.
- **The Kücki's Pub Jabiru is recorded without a tail number** because it is a cabin and
  rear-fuselage section, not an airframe; V5-RIZ is in aliases.
- Ghana's Mi-2s G-660 (Takoradi) and G-661 (Kumasi) are given the *same* construction
  number 536824090 by W&R. That is an evident source error; both rows are kept with the
  error flagged in the Takoradi description.

### Judgment calls — display versus derelict

The test applied throughout was deliberate retention plus public presentation, and it was
applied hard, because this region is full of intact airframes that are simply abandoned.

**Recorded despite looking marginal:**
- **Red Clay Savannah Centre, Tamale (6 airliners) and Ejisu (2).** These are the artist
  Ibrahim Mahama's acquisitions of retired airliners as public art and education pieces.
  Unambiguously deliberate retention plus public presentation, and by airframe count the
  largest civil preservation site in West Africa.
- **Kücki's Pub, Swakopmund.** A fuselage section inside a bar, recorded under the
  explicit contract rule about bar and cafe conversions. It is Namibia's only record.
- **Komatipoort Caribou.** OSM names it "Abandoned Caribou aircraft"; W&R codes it `pre`;
  OSM also tags it `tourism=attraction`. The public treats it as a landmark, so it is a
  record, with the tension stated in the row.
- **Instructional airframes (`i/a`) at Kaduna, Accra, Lyttelton, Thebephatshwa, Gaborone
  and Olifantsfontein** are recorded as `in_storage` rather than dropped. They are
  deliberately retained, intact and identified; they are just not presented. Marking them
  `in_storage` lets a later pass promote or drop them without re-researching.
- **Kingankati, DRC (727 + 737 + TriStar on a private estate).** The least secure
  display-versus-derelict call in the file. Three airframes all coded `pre` and grouped
  reads as a collection, not a scrap line, but nobody has photographed it. Flagged in the
  site record.
- **AFB Hoedspruit's two airliner hulls.** Coded `pre` by a source that uses `dum`
  elsewhere in the same country, so recorded — but the likeliest truth is fire-training
  and the row says so.

**Excluded, and why:**
- **The Wonderboom storage lines, Pretoria (W&R locations 22617, 18789, 30031 — 21
  airframes).** Kudus, Bosboks, Albatrosses, five C-47s. All `std`, at a commercial civil
  airport, in rebuilders' yards. Stored inventory, not display. This is the largest single
  exclusion in South Africa and it is deliberate.
- **Jan Smuts / OR Tambo airline storage (W&R 8772, 8804-8806, 22578-22579, 23000, 29273-29274).**
  Withdrawn A340s, DC-9s, a 737-300, an ERJ-145. Parked airliners awaiting disposal.
- **Kruger Mpumalanga (19901), Lanseria (19944-19946, 21991), Rand (32741, 32756, 12410),
  Mahikeng (11809), Pierre van Ryneveld/Upington (8791 — four MD-82s).** Same reasoning:
  stored or withdrawn commercial airframes on operating airfields.
- **Oudtshoorn (32739) — two ex-Gabonese Mirage 5s coded `std`.** Their four sisters are
  recorded here as displays at Sandton, Milnerton, Bonaero Park and Stellenbosch; these
  two are stored. Worth re-checking in a year.
- **Okahandja Military Museum, Namibia.** Built 2004 by Mansudae Overseas Projects for
  US$4-5 m, tagged `tourism=museum` in OSM at -21.98085, 16.91644 — and **it has never
  opened**. Armed guards turned visitors away in 2008 and Wikipedia records it still
  closed through 2022. No aircraft attributed to it by any source. A well-searched zero.
- **Outapi War Museum, Namibia** (-17.5042, 14.9793; museums.com.na; outapiwarmuseum@gmail.com;
  065-251567). A real, open war museum in an ex-SADF base with bunker accommodation — and
  its published exhibit list is small arms, shell casings, handcuffs, tear gas canisters
  and photographs. **No aircraft.** Checked precisely because Outapi sits in the Ondangwa
  operational area the brief flagged.
- **Grootfontein and Ondangwa air bases, Namibia.** Searched in English and German; no
  preserved airframe, gate guard or museum found at either. W&R's entire Namibian
  population is eight airframes, of which four are `dum` at Windhoek Eros, two `std`
  (a DC-6B at Eros and an Il-76 at Rundu), one `dlt` (An-26 NAF-3-642 at Omega) and one
  `pre` (the pub section). **Namibia genuinely appears to preserve almost nothing.**
- **Windhoek Eros dump (D2-END Be.200, V5-JJL Ce.310R, V5-POC PA-31, N781K Be.95).** Four
  `dum` airframes at a working airport. Textbook scrap line.
- **The Omega An-26 NAF-3-642**, the only Namibian Air Force airframe W&R lists, is `dlt`
  at a disused Caprivi strip airfield. Derelict, not preserved.
- **Angolan derelicts.** Angola's 55 W&R rows include 11 `dlt` and 4 `dum` at Catumbela,
  Cabinda, Cabo Ledo, Huambo and Benguela. The brief warned that Angola is full of
  Soviet-era wreckage; it is, and none of it is here. Only the 23 `pre` plus the Cuito
  Cuanavale group were taken.
- **Mali's 32 `dlt` rows.** Mali has more derelict airframes than any other country in
  this set and only five preserved ones. All 32 excluded.
- **Nigeria's 27 `dum` and 7 `dlt` rows**, including the Makurdi and Maiduguri MiG-21
  storage that was advertised for sale in December 2020.
- **The São Tomé crash site at 0.3775, 6.7097** (OSM node 13687762807: "The orange remains
  of an aircraft that crashed 29 July 2019"). Wreckage, not a display. Note it is only
  ~400 m from the Super Constellation location and the two are easily conflated.
- **The UTA Flight 772 desert memorial in Niger** (OSM nodes 11417119810 / 11417125791 at
  16.8654, 11.9537, tagged `memorial=aircraft`). A 61-metre compass rose built in the Ténéré
  from a wing of the destroyed DC-10 and 170 mirrors. It is a genuine and important
  memorial, but it is not a retained airframe — it is a monument constructed from debris.
  Excluded on the same principle that excludes wreck sites, and named here so nobody
  re-researches it. The mapped Niamey Noratlas 5U-MAF (`i/a`) is likewise instructional.
- **Butembo DRC and N'Djamena Chad `historic=aircraft` nodes** — street junctions, as
  above.
- **Guinea-Bissau's other two W&R rows** are one `dlt` and one `std`; only the Do 28
  J5-AAC survives the test.

### Blank fields, deliberately

- **`latitude` and `longitude` on the Bissau Dornier site.** W&R's country index does not
  expose a coordinate for location 16089. Guessing a Bissau airport coordinate would look
  authoritative and be wrong.
- **`tail_number` blank on eleven rows** — the Dunnottar Harvard, the Luanda museum PC-7,
  the Lobito PC-7, the Franceville Fouga, the Yaoundé Fouga, the Aburi H-19, the Gweru
  Flying Flea, the Sepupa R44, the Kücki's Pub Jabiru, the second Jos P.149D and both
  Thiaroye airframes. In every case the source either records no serial or records one it
  distrusts.
- **`operator_country` blank on three rows** — the Gweru Flying Flea (a homebuilt with no
  national operator), the Aburi H-19 (nationality unknown) and nothing else. Every other
  row is attributed.
- **`year_built` is blank on every row in this file.** No source consulted gives a
  construction, roll-out or delivery date, and the contract forbids deriving one from a
  serial. Construction numbers are recorded in `description` and `aliases` instead, which
  is where a later pass can convert them.
- **`postal_code` blank across most of the region** — outside South Africa these countries
  either have no postcode system in general use or none discoverable per site.

### operator_country decisions

SAAF-operated British types are `ZA`, per the brief: the Shackletons, the Canberras, the
Buccaneer S.50s, the Vampires, the Mosquito PR.9 LR480 (flown by 60 Squadron SAAF in RAF
markings), the Spitfire Mk VIII and the Hurricane IIc. `GB` is used only where the airframe
never served South Africa: the Ysterplaat Whirlwind WV224 (Royal Navy), the Port Elizabeth
Sioux XT562 (British Army) and Oxford ED290, and the Swartkop Argus KK476 (RAF). `DE` for
the four Ditsong Luftwaffe airframes and the Swartkop Storch; `DZ` for the Swartkop Mi-24A;
`BH` for the Bahraini Scout; `PT` for the two Fortaleza de São Miguel Harvards, which are
Portuguese colonial-war airframes and not Angolan; `GA` for the five ex-Gabonese Mirage 5s
now in South Africa; `CA` for the São Tomé Super Constellations (Canadian-registered
airlift aircraft); `IN` for the Ghanaian HAL HT-2; `LT`, `TR`, `KE`, `MZ`, `LS`, `ET`, `BJ`,
`DJ` and `US` where the airframe's only operator was of that nationality.

### Needs a human — ranked

1. **Photograph the two aircraft inside Camp Lieutenant Amadou Lindor Fall, Thiaroye,
   Dakar (14.7566, -17.3766).** This is Senegal's first record of any kind and both rows
   are currently `Unidentified`. One ground photograph converts two blank rows into real
   airframes. The camp is a Senegalese army installation; the approach is via the Armée de
   l'Air's public affairs office rather than the gate.
2. **Confirm the SAAF Museum Port Elizabeth's lease and current status.** The 25-year
   lease signed February 1991 expired in 2016 and no source found says what replaced it.
   Contact: `saafmuseum.org.za`. One call settles a 15-airframe site.
3. **Is the Gweru collection intact in 2026?** Still open after this pass. Nine of the
   twelve W&R rows rest on photographs shot 2000-2006 and uploaded 2013-14. The National
   Museums and Monuments of Zimbabwe central region office (`nmmz.co.zw`) is the one call
   that closes thirteen rows including a Spitfire F.22.
4. **Are the two São Tomé Super Constellations still standing?** Unchanged from the
   project's earlier open item; nothing found in 2024-26. CF-NAL and CF-NAM are the last
   physical remnant of the Biafran airlift and would be a significant loss.
5. **Identify the Kingankati, DRC estate (-4.3066, 15.7252).** Three airliners including a
   TriStar. Either a private collection worth a proper record or a derelict group that
   should come out.
6. **Identify the venues behind fourteen South African single-airframe records** —
   Randfontein, Kimberley, Saldanha, Kempton Park, Muizenberg, Brooklyn Pretoria, Farmall,
   Krugersdorp, Boksburg, Benoni, Strand, Bokrivier, West Village and the Kameeldrift
   five-airframe group. W&R gives a coordinate but no venue name for each. A single pass
   through Google Street View plus the SAAF Association's regional branches would name most
   of them and let the site names lose their generic form.
7. **Angola's Museu da Força Aérea Nacional (-8.8495, 13.2282).** Ten airframes, no
   opening hours, no website reachable from here, no visit report. Contact via the FAA
   press office; `faa.ao/fan/museu_fan` exists but refuses foreign connections.
8. **Botswana's Thebephatshwa Strikemaster OJ3** was last noted in 2018. Botswana Defence
   Force Air Arm public affairs would confirm it in one exchange, and Botswana currently
   stands at zero in the database.
9. **Whether the Lyttelton Cheetah group (-25.8023, 28.1915) is a training school or the
   SAAF Museum's overflow.** Seven airframes hang on the answer, including three Cheetah Cs.
10. **The Nigerian Air Force's own view of the Kaduna gate guards.** Nineteen airframes over
    9 km, all W&R-sourced. NAF Directorate of Public Relations could confirm the plinthed
    ten in one reply and would also settle whether the "NAF Museum Kaduna" that directories
    claim exists in any form (the project's earlier notes concluded it does not).
