### Sources and their weight

**What carried this brief.** Three things did most of the work, and none of them was a directory:

1. **The OSM Overpass sweep.** It carried Myanmar outright. The `maps.mail.ru` mirror answered a
   17-country multi-area query in under two minutes and returned 64 elements. Thirty-four of those

2. **Primary institutional publishing.** The Australian Department of Defence releases of

3. **The Wrecks-and-Relics-derived register at spottingmode.com** for Malta — 32 airframes with

**Which compilation proved stale, and exactly how.**

- **`aviationmuseum.eu` — column shift, twice, on the same page.** Its Malta page assigns
  **EN199 to a North American T-6G Harvard** and leaves the Supermarine Spitfire Mk.IX with no

  Italian military serial block and the airframe is the Texan. This is precisely the failure mode
  the contract documents, found twice in one table. Anyone who imports that page unchecked will
  create a phantom Harvard, a serial-less Spitfire and a mis-serialled flea.
- **`aviationmuseum.eu` — false-serial propagation.** Its Malta Meteor F.8 is listed as **WF714**.
  WF714 is the marking painted on the aircraft. The airframe is **WK914**. The directory records
  the repaint as the identity.
- **`aviationmuseum.eu` — transcription noise.** Lightning **XN7699** (one digit too many;
  correct XN769); Beech **N495F** (correct N495FA); Malta's second Dakota given as **F-GILV**
  where the Wrecks-and-Relics register has **T9-ABC**.
- **`aviationmuseum.eu` Kuala Lumpur — geographically stale by eight years.** It gives the RMAF
  Museum's address as Jalan Lapangan Terbang Lama, Kuala Lumpur. **That museum closed on
  15 March 2018.** Its page date is December 2016 and it has not been revisited.
- **English Wikipedia's Lao People's Army museum article — not usable.** It lists the museum's
  aircraft as "Bell UH-1 Helicopter, F-4 Phantom, F-105 Thunderchief, **MiG Bison** fighter jet,
  Biplane". "MiG Bison" is not an aircraft: *Bison* is the NATO name for the Myasishchev M-4
  bomber, and "MiG-21 Bison" is an *Indian* upgrade standard that has no connection to Laos.
  An F-105 in a Vientiane museum is extraordinary and uncorroborated. **I imported none of it.**
  Only the MiG-21 (independently photographed) and the UH-1 (consistent across accounts) are
  recorded, both flagged.
- **`asisbiz` PNG museum page** is a genuinely good photo essay but its images are from
  **2002** and it documents a large 2000-2001 dispersal of the collection to Australian
  collectors. Treated as historical, not current.

### Judgment calls

- **Display versus wreck — Laos, Plain of Jars.** The brief asked for this test explicitly.
  **Result: no records.** The Plain of Jars and Xieng Khouang carry enormous quantities of
  American ordnance and airframe debris, and bomb casings are reused locally as fence posts,
  planters and building piles. That is **material reuse of scrap, not deliberate retention plus
  public presentation of an identified airframe.** The UXO Visitor Centre in Phonsavan and the
  UXO Lao Information Centre in Luang Prabang interpret the bombing campaign through ordnance,
  photographs and prosthetics — no aircraft. I found **no plinthed, curated or identified airframe
  anywhere in Xieng Khouang province.** In-situ crash debris in the jungle fails the test on both
  limbs: nobody retained it and nobody presents it. **Zero records, and the zero is the finding.**
- **Display versus wreck — Papua New Guinea.** Applied hard, because PNG is the one place where
  getting it wrong would generate hundreds of false records. The Pacific war left thousands of
  airframes across PNG; Pacific Wrecks catalogues them by crash site. **An in-situ crash site is
  not a record** — it fails deliberate retention. **A recovered relic curated inside a museum is
  a record** — hence the B-24 tail fin, the G4M wing panel and the B-17 parts at Kokopo are all
  in, each labelled as a fragment. The famous **"Swamp Ghost" B-17E 41-2446** is *excluded*: it
  was recovered from the Agaiambo swamp and shipped to the United States; it is not in PNG.
- **Fragments and nose sections recorded, not dropped.** Malta's two Hastings noses, the Lightning
  cockpit and the BAC 1-11 nose; PNG's B-24 tail fin, P-38 centre section, Ford 4-AT undercarriage
  and G4M wing; Kokopo's B-17 "small parts". Each says so in `description`. A reviewer who wants
  whole airframes only can filter on that word; a reviewer who deletes them loses identified
  material that no other record captures.
- **Unidentified rows rather than dropped sites.** Twelve rows use `Unidentified` plus a
  descriptive model, per the contract. Every one of them is an OSM `historic=aircraft` node with
  no type tag. Dropping them would have understated Kuantan by four airframes and Kabul by ten,
  and would have left no coordinate for anyone to check. Guessing types would have been worse.
- **`access_type` reasoning.** `public` where a member of the public walks or buys a ticket
  (Malta, the Singapore museums, the PNG National Museum, the War Museum Cambodia, Hong Kong,
  the Malaysian park monuments, Naypyidaw as reported in 2016). `restricted` for anything behind
  a military or gendarmerie gate (Kuantan, Kuching, Kabul airport, Phnom Chum) and for Pyongyang,
  where entry is possible only on a state-approved guided tour — that is a controlled gate in
  substance whoever owns the land. `appointment` for the RMAF collection at Sendayan: a temporary
  display on an operating air base during restoration is not a walk-up. **Blank** for four sites
  where I genuinely do not know (Chaman-e-Hozori, Mawlamyine, the two unhosted KL nodes).
- **`operator_country` reasoning.** Malta is almost entirely `GB` as briefed, with an Italian
  pocket (`IT`) for the G.91, AB.204, AB.47 and T-6G — MM- serials make that unambiguous — and
  `US` for the Hueys. Maltese civil homebuilts and the Cub take `MT`. Blank where the airframe
  is carried on a foreign civil registration with no established national operator (the Dakotas,
  the Beech 18, the BAC 1-11 nose, the Kestrel, the Centaur, the K-8 glider). PNG's Pacific-war
  airframes take `US`, `JP` and `AU` by the flag that flew them, never `PG`. The Lao UH-1 takes
  `US` — it was American-supplied and captured, and captured aircraft keep their operator.
  Myanmar's UB- and numeric serials are all `MM` including the ex-RAF Spitfires and Seafire,
  because they served the Union of Burma Air Force.
- **Kosovo: `XK` never used.** As briefed, `XK` is not a valid ISO 3166-1 code. The question is
  moot — Kosovo produced **zero records**, so no row needed a country code and no airframe had
  to be tested for demonstrable Yugoslav service.
- **The ITE College 737 is deliberately borderline.** An airliner fuselage on a technical-college
  campus is a teaching aid. It is deliberately retained and precisely identified, which argues
  for inclusion; it is not publicly presented, which argues against. Recorded with
  `access_type: restricted` and the reasoning stated so a reviewer can drop it in one move.
- **Airworthy aircraft:** none encountered in this brief. No row is an operational airframe.
- **Replicas:** none identified in any source. If Pyongyang's "downed UN aircraft" turn out to
  include mock-ups, that would not surprise me — but I have no evidence either way and have not
  recorded them at all.

### EXCLUDED, and why

**Countries searched to a genuine zero.** Each of these was searched in English and, where a
distinct search language exists, in the local one. None produced a single defensible record.

- **Bhutan** — no aviation museum, no preserved airframe, no monument. Bhutan's civil aviation
  begins with Drukair in 1983 and its fleet has been continuously renewed and sold on; there is no
  retired national airframe. Paro is the only international airport. Searched: English, plus the
  Drukair fleet history. **Confident zero.**
- **Maldives** — no museum, no monument, no preserved airframe found. A geography of coral atolls
  with no room for outdoor plinths and no military air arm. **Confident zero.**
- **Timor-Leste** — the museum sector is the Timorese Resistance Archive and Museum, the Chega!
  exhibition and the Xanana Gusmão Reading Room; the Wikipedia list of museums in Timor-Leste
  contains no aviation collection. Nothing at Dili or Baucau. **Confident zero**, though Baucau's
  Indonesian-era airfield is the one place worth a human eye.
- **Fiji** — searched the Fiji Museum (a cultural and maritime institution: the Bounty rudder, not
  aircraft), the RFMF and its air wing, Nadi and Nausori. The RFMF air wing is tiny and has no
  heritage display. Nadi was a major wartime staging field but nothing is preserved there.
  **Confident zero** for museums; **low-confidence zero** for gate guards, which are exactly the
  thing thin OSM coverage hides.
- **Guyana** — the Guyana National Museum and the National Trust's monument register contain no
  aircraft. Georgetown's monuments are the 1763 Monument and similar civic sculpture. Guyana
  Airways' fleet dispersed on liquidation. Nothing at Cheddi Jagan International or Ogle.
  **Confident zero** for institutions.
- **Suriname** — searched in Dutch as well as English. Nothing at Paramaribo, Zanderij or
  Zorg en Hoop; no aviation museum; the Surinamese Air Force is a handful of light aircraft with
  no heritage collection. **Confident zero.**
- **Kosovo** — searched in Albanian (`aeroplan`, `avion`, `përmendore`, `muze ushtarak`) and via
  the Serbian/Yugoslav literature. Slatina Air Base is the obvious candidate and it is genuinely
  empty: the Yugoslav air force withdrew its aircraft in June 1999 and the base has been KFOR and
  then civil since; the underground Objekat Slatina complex is closed. Prizren and Gjakova have
  no airframes. The Pristina memorials that exist are the Brotherhood and Unity monument, Gazimestan
  and the memorial to the two fallen US pilots — **none of them incorporates an aircraft.**
  **Confident zero for museums; genuine unknown for gate guards.**

### The OSM caveat — state this plainly

**An empty Overpass result in this brief is not evidence of absence.** The sweep returned
**zero** `historic=aircraft`, `memorial=aircraft`, `memorial:type=aircraft` or
`aeroway=gate_guardian` features in **Laos, Cambodia, North Korea, Bhutan, the Maldives,
Timor-Leste, Kosovo, Fiji, Guyana and Suriname** — and yet Cambodia demonstrably has at least
three displayed airframes (the Siem Reap MiG-19 and Mi-8 and the Kampong Chhnang MiG-21) and
Laos at least one (the Vientiane MiG-21). **OSM simply has not been surveyed for aircraft in
those countries.** The tags are volunteer-contributed and hobbyist-driven, and the hobby is
concentrated in Europe, North America, Australia and a handful of Asian cities.

Conversely, where OSM *is* surveyed the yield is exceptional: Myanmar returned 39 aircraft nodes,
Malaysia 13, Afghanistan 10. Note what that distribution actually reflects — **one enthusiastic
mapper per country**, not the underlying density of airframes. Myanmar is not richer in preserved
aircraft than Cambodia by a factor of thirteen; Myanmar has a mapper and Cambodia does not.

Treat the OSM layer as a **high-precision, very-low-recall** instrument throughout this brief.
