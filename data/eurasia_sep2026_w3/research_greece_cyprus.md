## NOTES

### Sources and their weight

**`spottingmode.com/wro` carried this package outright.** It lists **245 locations in Greece**
and **27 in Cyprus** with per-location coordinates, and each location page carries a table of
registration, construction number, type, operator country, service branch, a status code
(`pre` preserved / `std` stored / `der` derelict / `dum` dumped / `i/a` instructional /
`dlt` deleted) and a dated **last-noted** field. Roughly two thirds of the airframes recorded
here carry a last-noted date in **2025 or 2026**, which is what makes the currency claims in
this file defensible. Reached with `curl --ciphers "DEFAULT:@SECLEVEL=0"` per the note carried
forward from the Middle East pass. The status vocabulary is what let the display test be
applied airframe by airframe rather than site by site — several Greek air bases have a
preserved gate line and a scrap dump within 800 m of each other.

**The International F-104 Society (`i-f-s.nl/preserved-f-104s/preserved-in-greece/`) is the
second pillar.** It names roughly **48 preserved Greek Starfighters** with prose locations,
repaint history, arrival dates and condition notes. Greece plinthed more F-104s than any other
European country and this page is the only source that tracks them individually. It supplied
locations that no map source has — Trikorfo, Kapnochori, Georgitsi, Vasiliada, Ellinopyrgos,
Leuctra, Athikia, Psachna, Tragano — and it independently corroborated 19 of the serials
spottingmode gives. Where the two disagree it is noted in the row.

**OpenStreetMap Overpass sweep.** `historic=aircraft`, `memorial=aircraft`,
`memorial:type=aircraft`, `aeroway=gate_guardian` and `tourism=attraction`+`aircraft:type`,
bbox 34.7,19.3,41.8,29.7 (Greece) and 34.5,32.2,35.8,34.6 (Cyprus). `overpass-api.de` is
blocked by this environment's proxy; **`maps.mail.ru/osm/tools/overpass/api/interpreter`
worked first try** with a browser User-Agent and the query POSTed from a file. The Greek bbox
returned **169 elements**, but the bbox spills into Turkey, Albania and North Macedonia and
roughly 100 of those are Turkish (the Istanbul air museum), Albanian (Kuçovë and Rinas MiG
lines) or Macedonian (Bitola). **41 elements are actually in Greece**; 5 in Cyprus. OSM's
value here was corroboration and Greek-language naming, not discovery — it names only about
a sixth of what spottingmode carries. Every OSM point was reverse-geocoded through Nominatim
to place it in a named municipality before it was matched to a register entry.

**`aviationmuseum.eu` behaved exactly as the contract warns.** Its Greece index gave seven
useful leads (War Museum, Chromonastiri, HAF Museum, Elefsis Heritage Park, Larisa Base
Museum, Tanagra Base Collection, P.A.L.I.S. Foundation) and its collection tables are
genuinely two-column HTML that has to be zipped line by line — flattening it produces the
column shift the contract describes. Its **Cyprus page has no content at all** ("We haven't
found any aviation museum, or museum with aircraft in Cyprus") and was **last updated
12 August 2009**. Nothing from aviationmuseum.eu is recorded here as fact without a second
source; where it is the only source the row says so.

**Wikimedia Commons** supplied dated photographs with provenance — in particular Alan Wilson's
4 November 2022 Greek trip, whose captions carry full delivery histories for F-84F 26837 and
RF-104G 6691. Commons GeoSearch around each OSM point was run and was mostly unproductive
(Greek monument aircraft are almost never geotagged) but it did confirm the War Museum and
Tatoi inventories.

**Greek-language searching was attempted throughout and was the least productive channel in
this environment**, for a tooling reason rather than a coverage reason — see "Needs a human".
Greek Wikipedia did supply the museum articles, the branch list of the War Museum, and the
in.gr report of the December 2019 aircraft delivery.

### Corrections made

- **HAF Museum F-84F.** aviationmuseum.eu gives `26919`. The register gives the true identity
  **26361** with the explicit remark "As 26959". Recorded as 26361 with 26959 and 26919 both
  in aliases.
- **HAF Museum Canadair Sabre 19169.** Wears **19202**; aviationmuseum.eu renders this as
  "19169/202" which reads like a unit code rather than a false serial.
- **HAF Museum Sabre 19168 is not Greek.** The register gives country **Canada**, type
  **Sabre Mk.6**, displayed as USAF 12910. `operator_country` set to `CA`, not `GR`.
- **HAF Museum RF-104G 6677** was displayed for years wrongly marked **7415** — and 7415 is a
  real and separate F-104G also at Tatoi. The Society records that the museum has since
  corrected the marking. Both airframes are recorded separately.
- **HAF Museum HU-16B.** aviationmuseum.eu gives `15289/289` and `17204/204`; the register
  gives `517190` (c/n G-250) and `517204` (c/n G-274). The c/n-backed register forms are used.
- **Leuctra F-104G is German, not Greek.** The register states plainly: **2680**, country
  Germany, "As HAF 7426, never flown in Greek service". `operator_country` set to `DE` and the
  worn Greek serial 7426 moved to aliases. Every other Greek F-104 monument is `GR`.
- **Perea is a Canadair Sabre, not an F-5.** OpenStreetMap node 6799186227 names the point
  "F5". The register identifies **Sabre Mk.2 19294 c/n 194 wearing the civil marks SX-JGM**.
  OSM is wrong and the row says so.
- **Acharnes F-104G removed in 2016, not still there.** OSM node 2802119739 still shows an
  "F-104G Starfighter" at 38.0741 23.7399 in Acharnes. The Society records that 7435 was taken
  away from Acharnai in **June 2016** and has been the **Elefsis gate guard since 2024**. The
  Acharnes node is stale; no site was created for it and the airframe is recorded at Elefsina.
- **Thermo Mirage F1CG wears 115**, which is a real and separate Mirage F1CG at Tanagra. True
  identity 124 recorded; 115 in aliases.
- **Sedes T-33A 35629 wears "626"**; **Souda TA-7C 156774 wears "155774"** (a one-digit
  transposition of a real BuNo); **Chromonastiri U-17A ES293 wears "ES309"** — which is why
  aviationmuseum.eu lists it as ES309. All three recorded with the true identity in
  `tail_number` and the worn marking in `aliases`.
- **Athens War Museum F-86 is a D, not an E.** in.gr's December 2019 report calls it an
  "F-86E Sabre" flown by the Ελληνική Φλόγα display team. Both the register and the Commons
  photographs show **F-86D 51-8404 / FU-404**. Recorded as the D; the E claim is in the
  description, not the identity.
- **Makedonitissa Noratlas wears 52-133**, the serial of an aircraft lost in the 1974 airlift.
  The displayed airframe is **52-128, c/n D030**.
- **Lefke F-100 wears 55-2766**, an aircraft that crashed in 1964. The displayed airframe's own
  identity is unpublished, so `tail_number` is blank and the worn marking is in aliases.
- **Fouga Magister at P.A.L.I.S.**: aviationmuseum.eu gives serial `42`; the register gives
  `427` with matching c/n 427. The register form is used.
- **A-7D 75-0390 at P.A.L.I.S.**: aviationmuseum.eu attaches this serial to a "Sukhoi Su-22M-4"
  — a textbook one-row column shift. The register has it as an ex-USAF A-7D, with Su-22M4 3909
  as a separate Polish airframe. Both recorded correctly.

### Judgment calls

- **Airworthy aircraft recorded `on_display`.** Spitfire LF Mk.IXc **MJ755** and T-6G
  **49-3424** both fly from Dekelia (photographed airborne 24 May 2026 and 25 January 2026
  respectively). They are museum-held historic aircraft rather than an operational fleet, so
  they are recorded, per the contract's instruction to record airworthy museum aircraft as
  `on_display`.
- **Wrecks under restoration recorded, with `display_status: under_restoration`.** The Blenheim
  Mk.IVF raised off Rethymno in 1996, the Ju 52 raised off Leros in October 2003, the Ju 87D-3,
  and TF-104G 62-12274. These are deliberately retained by a museum and publicly known; they
  are not scrap-line hulks.
- **Replica flagged.** The War Museum's Farman MF.7 "Δαίδαλος" is a full-size replica and the
  description says so; `year_built` left blank.
- **P.A.L.I.S. Foundation recorded as one site with `access_type: appointment` and every
  airframe `in_storage`.** Three sources confirm ~32 airframes at Kalyvia; aviationmuseum.eu
  says "Not open yet" and the F-104 Society says the foundation "became under Government
  control after a financial scandal became clear, museum is closed". It is deliberate retention
  by a constituted foundation but there is no evidence of public presentation, so it is neither
  dropped nor recorded as a public museum.
- **Messolonghi aeroclub recorded, marginally.** The register codes all five airframes `std`,
  but the F-104 Society describes a deliberate lawn arrangement of jets at the aeroclub since
  around 2012 and notes the F-104 came from a museum. Recorded as a display with the condition
  caveat in each row. If a later pass finds it is a dumping ground, delete the site.
- **Elefsis Heritage Park cut back to two airframes.** aviationmuseum.eu lists eight
  (C-47A 92626, Do-28D-2 41-38, F-102A 0-61059, Noratlas 53-258, T-33A 54951, TF-102A 0-62326,
  YS-11A 2137, HU-16B 510070). The register confirms only F-84F 26659 and T-33A 16771 at that
  coordinate, both April 2025. The other six are listed here rather than invented into rows.
- **Nord Noratlas 53-258 appears in two lists** — aviationmuseum.eu puts it at both the HAF
  Museum and Elefsis Heritage Park. It is recorded once, at the HAF Museum, where the Greek
  Wikipedia exhibit list also places an outdoor Noratlas. This is a low-confidence placement.
- **Two separate F-5As near Pedion tou Areos, Thessaloniki.** 01380 at 40.62317 22.95756 and
  13353 at 40.62427 22.95988 are 200 m apart with different serials and different last-noted
  dates. They are recorded as two sites, not one duplicated.
- **`operator_country` for ex-Netherlands NF-5s left as `GR`.** NF-5A/B airframes (3005, 3033,
  3047, 3061, 3062, 3073, 4027) were built for the Netherlands but transferred to and flown by
  the Hellenic Air Force before withdrawal, so the operating air force at retirement is Greek.
  The Dutch origin is in the description. This differs from the Leuctra F-104G, which the
  register says never flew in Greek service at all.

### Blank fields, deliberately

- **`year_built` is blank on every row in this package.** No source consulted publishes a
  construction, roll-out, first-flight or delivery date for these airframes. Construction
  numbers are recorded in `description` where known (Lockheed 683D-/583F- blocks, Northrop
  N./RF. blocks, Lockheed 580- T-33 blocks) precisely so a later pass can derive build dates
  from them. **A US fiscal-year serial prefix was never converted into a year_built** — 62-12274
  is an order-year serial, not a 1962 build.
- **`aircraft_name` is blank except for the two commemoratively-named Tatoi Starfighters**
  ("Tigris"/ΤΙΓΡΗΣ and "Olympus"/ΟΛΥΜΠΟΣ) which carry the names as painted markings.
- **`tail_number` blank on three rows by design**: the Agios Georgios Tymfristou F-5A (no source
  publishes a serial), the Lefke F-100D (wears another aircraft's identity; its own is
  unpublished), and the Limassol Baby Great Lakes (a civil registration, placed in aliases).
  The Ju 52 and Ju 87 at Tatoi are also blank because their only claimed identities come from a
  single lead-list source.
- **`postal_code` blank** at Lefktra, Terpsithea, Skyros, Aktion, Araxos, Tympaki, Agios
  Georgios Tymfristou and Tanagra/Schimatari — Nominatim returned no postcode for those points
  and none was guessed.
- **`website` blank on every monument and base site** — none has one.

### CYPRUS TOP-UP WARNING

Ten Cypriot sites are proposed above and **all ten are marked `TOP-UP`**. The nine already in
the database were not enumerable from this session, so **overlap is likely and must be checked
before import**. Ranked by probability that the row already exists:

1. **RAF Akrotiri Lightning / Whirlwind / Wessex** (three sites) — the most famous preserved
   aircraft in Cyprus and near-certain to be present already. Check first.
2. **Makedonitissa Tomb Noratlas** — a national monument; likely present, but if it is, check
   whether it carries `52-133` (the marking) rather than `52-128` (the airframe). If so, that is
   a correction to make rather than a row to add.
3. **Özgürlük Milli Parkı** — the northern displays are the likeliest genuine gap.
4. **Lefke F-100D**, **Kato Deftera Sea Harrier**, **Lakatameia PA-22**, **Lefkoşa Car Museum
   glider**, **Limassol Cyprus Motor Museum** — small, recently registered, most likely new.

`region: Middle East` is used on all ten to match where the existing Cypriot rows sit, exactly
as the brief directs. This is a deliberate departure from the geographic truth and from the
`Europe` value used on every Greek row in the same file.
