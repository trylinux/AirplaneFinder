Database held ZERO records for all three countries before this pass.
`region` is the literal string `Europe` on every row. `operator_country` is set on every
airframe row without exception (RO / BG / MD / RU / DE / CS / PL / GB) because Romanian and
Bulgarian tactical numbers are 2–4 digits and repeat endlessly across types and countries.

### Sources and their weight

**iar93.ro carried Romania.** The single most valuable source in this pass was
https://iar93.ro/avioane-iar-93-expuse/ — a dedicated type site run by people who worked the
programme, last updated 9 Oct 2024. It publishes all 31 surviving IAR-93s with the exact
factory roll-out date of each airframe (day/month/year), the series and single- or two-seat
configuration, and the current holder. That single page produced 25 airframe rows and 20 site
records, and it is the reason `year_built` is populated on every IAR-93 row here: those years
come from published factory completion dates, not from serials. The ro.wikipedia IAR-93
article reproduces the same register with geohack coordinates for most entries, which let me
tie register entries to OSM objects. Where the two disagreed I preferred iar93.ro.

**bg.wikipedia carried Bulgaria's flagship.** "Музей на авиацията (Пловдив)" reproduces the
Krumovo museum's own accession register as a table with four identifier columns: board
number, nomenclature number, construction number, and building plant. That is far better data
than anything English-language coverage of Bulgaria offers, and it produced 71 rows with a
construction number on most of them. The museum's own site airmuseum-bg.com (checked
September 2026) confirms the collection narrative, the 59-aircraft count, opening hours and
euro-denominated ticket prices — so the museum is demonstrably still operating.

**The museums' own websites were reliable and current.** airmuseum-bg.com and
militarymuseum.bg both itemise their own outdoor exhibits; roaf.ro publishes the Romanian
museum's history and accreditation record. All three were fetched in September 2026.

**OpenStreetMap did the monument work.** The Overpass sweep is the backbone of everything
outside the four museums. See the next section.

**aviationmuseum.eu proved stale and thin, exactly as warned.** Its Romania page (last updated
2016) lists four Bucharest sites and nothing else in the country — it misses every one of the
20 IAR-93 monument locations, the Tecuci museum, the Pucioasa museum and the Coloneşti
memorial centre. Its Bulgaria page misses the Burgas museum entirely. Its Moldova page lists
one museum and gets its name wrong (National Museum of History of Moldova, which is the
history museum, not the army museum that actually holds the MiG-21PF). It was used only to
generate the lead "Grupul Scolar de Aeronautica Henri Coanda" in Bucharest, which OSM then
confirmed as the Colegiul Tehnic de Aeronautică. No fact in this file rests on it.

**Wikimedia Commons supplied the Romanian museum's serials.** A 2024 Flickr-derived photo set
uploaded to Commons has the tactical number in the file name for sixteen airframes at Pipera
(Yak-23 14 and 52; MiG-15bis 246 and 2713; MiG-15UTI 2579; MiG-17F 442 and 444; MiG-17PF 0904
and 502; MiG-19P 750; MiG-21F-13 10; MiG-21R 2007; MiG-21RFMM 8006; L-29 15 and 50; L-39ZA
140). Commons registrations likewise carried the Burgas museum (LZ-BTU, LZ-ANL, LZ-1089,
LZ-7001, LZ-ILE). Commons category "Monuments featuring aircraft in Bulgaria" produced eleven
Bulgarian monument leads that appear in no other source and are not in OSM.

**Grokipedia was not used.** Neither was any AI-generated compilation.

### The OSM Overpass sweep — method and result

overpass-api.de is blocked by this environment's proxy as the brief said. maps.mail.ru
returned 504 on every attempt. **overpass.kumi.systems and overpass.private.coffee both
worked**, POSTing the query from a file with a desktop-browser User-Agent.

Two passes were run:
1. A bbox pass over the three bboxes in the brief for `historic=aircraft`,
   `memorial=aircraft`, `memorial:type=aircraft`, `aeroway=gate_guardian` and
   `tourism=museum` + `museum=aviation` — 252 elements.
2. An `area["ISO3166-1"]` pass for the same tags — 146 elements.

**The bbox pass was 42% contamination.** The Romania bbox (43.6,20.2,48.3,29.7) reaches into
Hungary and Serbia and swept up the entire RepTár Szolnok collection (Tu-134 HA-LBF, Il-18,
Il-28, Il-14, An-24, An-26, Saab Viggen, Hawker Hunter, Mi-24) and the entire Belgrade Museum
of Aviation (60+ nodes, a Caravelle, a Soko J-21 Jastreb). The Moldova bbox reaches into
Ukraine and swept up a Su-15 at Kuchurhan and a MiG-15UTV at Odesa oblast. Every one of those
would have been filed as a Romanian or Moldovan record on a naive bbox read. **The
`area["ISO3166-1"]` filter DID work on both mirrors** and is what the country attribution in
this file rests on; the brief's advice to avoid it is not correct for kumi.systems or
private.coffee, though it is sound for maps.mail.ru which timed out on everything.

A third pass for `man_made=aircraft` and for museums whose name matches авиац/Aviaţ/Aeronaut
found four extra named museum objects (Tecuci, Coloneşti, Pucioasa, and the Krumovo museum
relation) and no extra airframes. Regex-across-all-keys queries reliably timed out at 250 s;
splitting them into single-tag queries fixed it.

Every OSM cluster was then reverse-geocoded through Nominatim at 1.1 s intervals, and eight
ambiguous clusters were resolved with an `around:` context query for named neighbours. That
last step is what identified the Bucharest Strada Biharia group as the Colegiul Tehnic de
Aeronautică „Henri Coandă”, the Sofia Tsarigradsko shose group as Площад на авиацията, the
Bacău Strada Condorilor pair as the Aerostar works, and the Buzău county group as the Boboc
Air Force training school.

### Judgment calls

- **Replicas and reproductions flagged, not dropped.** The Vuia 1 and the IAR 80 at Pipera are
  both reproductions and say so in their descriptions. The IAR 80 reproduction is the single
  most important exhibit in Romanian aviation heritage precisely because no original survives.
- **The Sofia LAZ-7 is a sculpture, not an airframe**, and its description says so; it is
  recorded because it is a deliberate, publicly presented aviation monument.
- **20 `Unidentified` rows.** Every one is at a site where OSM or a dated photograph proves an
  airframe exists but no reachable source names the type. Per the contract these were recorded
  with a descriptive model rather than dropped or guessed. They are: 2 at Clinceni; 1 at
  Ariceştii Rahtivani; 1 at Sofia Terminal 1; 1 at Buhovo; 2 at Omurtag; 6 at Graf Ignatievo;
  1 each at Kovachevo, Semchinovo, Zelenikovo, Krushovene, Gara Oreshets, Uzundzhovo,
  Svilengrad, Batak and Aleksandrovo.
- **Additional un-serialled rows at Pipera.** OSM individually maps 5 MiG-15, 3 MiG-19 and
  9 MiG-21 in the museum's open-air park while the 2024 photo set identifies only 3, 1 and 4
  of them. The unmatched airframes are recorded as rows with a known type and a blank
  tail_number rather than left out. If the importer later gets a full museum inventory these
  are the rows to reconcile first.
- **`access_type` reasoning.** `restricted` was used for anything inside a military or
  industrial perimeter (Boboc, the Air Force General Staff, the Braşov academy, Avioane
  Craiova, Aerostar Bacău, the Bucharest aeronautics college). `appointment` was used for
  private sport aerodromes where an airside visit has to be arranged (Clinceni, Şiria,
  Zenair-Gagu, Vădeni, Barza). `public` was used for everything on a street, in a park, in a
  hospital or university yard, or in a ticketed museum — including the Pipera museum, whose
  normal mode is public even though it is shut for rebuilding right now.
- **Graf Ignatievo recorded as `public`.** The six mapped airframes fall in the village, about
  1.3 km east of the runway, next to mapped restaurants and a hotel. If a site visit shows
  they are inside the wire this should flip to `restricted`.
- **Missiles at the two national military museums** are recorded (S-75, S-125, 2K11 Krug,
  OTR-23 Oka, R-17 Scud, 9K52 Luna-M) because `missile_rocket` is a valid aircraft_type and
  the surviving Bulgarian Oka and Scud rounds are historically significant — Bulgaria
  destroyed both systems in 2002 under western pressure.

### Blank fields, deliberately — do not read these as oversights

- **`latitude`/`longitude` blank on 10 sites**: Novi Han, Gara Oreshets, Uzundzhovo,
  Svilengrad, Dabravite, Batak, Aleksandrovo (all Bulgaria, all evidenced only by an
  un-geotagged Commons photograph) and Muzeul Armatei Naţionale Chişinău (Nominatim returned
  nothing and no source gave a coordinate). Guessing a village-centre point would have been
  easy and wrong.
- **`tail_number` blank on the Krumovo CF-104.** The museum register gives board number 8-899,
  nomenclature 63-899 and construction number 683A-1199. The Canadair CF-104 line ran
  683A-1001 to 1200, so the c/n is genuine and this is one of the last built — but which air
  force's marking "8-899" is could not be established, and inventing a Canadian, Norwegian or
  Turkish serial would have looked authoritative and been wrong. All three published
  identifiers are in `aliases`.
- **`operator_country` blank on 4 rows**: the Krumovo CF-104 (donor air force unknown); the
  Ariceştii Rahtivani airframe marked 5N-TSA (Nigerian registration on an unidentified type in
  a Romanian industrial park — no service history established); the Kilifarevo Saab 340 (no
  registration, no operator); and the three Bulgarian village airframes at Kovachevo,
  Semchinovo and Zelenikovo, which cannot even be typed.
- **`wing_type` blank on the 20 `Unidentified` fixed_wing rows.** The contract requires
  wing_type for fixed_wing, but monoplane-versus-biplane is exactly what an untyped OSM node
  does not tell you. If the importer rejects a blank here, `monoplane` is the safe default for
  all of them except possibly the Bulgarian village airframes, which could be An-2s.
- **`year_built` blank on everything except the IAR-93s and the 1912 Bozhinov machine.** The
  Krumovo register publishes construction numbers whose blocks do encode build dates for the
  Soviet types, but decoding them confidently needs a block table I could not verify against,
  and the contract forbids inferring a year I cannot source. The c/ns are all in `aliases` so
  a later pass can decode them properly.

### EXCLUDED, and why — a well-searched zero is a finding

- **Avia B-534 in Bulgaria — DOES NOT EXIST.** The brief asks for it. bg.wikipedia's history of
  the Krumovo museum explains why there is nothing to find: the Treaty of Neuilly required the
  destruction of Bulgaria's entire air force in 1919; the 1935 Bozhurishte museum attempt lost
  its wood-and-fabric exhibits to weather; and in the 1970s the commandant of the Dolna
  Mitropolia flying school ordered the surviving historic airframes destroyed to free hangar
  space — **the Bf 109 „Стрела”, the Ju 87 „Щука” and the Ju 52 „Сова” were all scrapped
  then**. Bulgaria's inter-war and WWII fleet is gone. The Arado Ar 196A-3 at Krumovo survived
  only because it was recovered from the sea afterwards, and it is now the only one in the
  world. Do not re-research this.
- **DAR-9 Siniger — nothing found.** The brief asks for it. It appears nowhere in the Krumovo
  register, nowhere on the museum's own collection pages, and nowhere in the OSM sweep. The
  same 1970s scrapping order is the likely reason. Treat as extinct unless a replica turns up.
- **Muzeul Militar Naţional Bucharest aviation hall — under-recorded, not excluded.** Only the
