## NOTES

### Sources and their weight

**OpenStreetMap carried this region.** A single Overpass sweep over bbox 39.6/13.3/46.9/23.1 for
`historic=aircraft`, `memorial=aircraft`, `memorial:type=aircraft` and `aeroway=gate_guardian`
returned 281 elements of which 242 fell inside or adjacent to the eight target countries. Follow-up
sweeps for `man_made=aircraft`, `exhibit=aircraft`, `attraction=aircraft`, `historic=airplane`,
`tourism=attraction`+`aircraft:type`, `memorial=plane` and for aviation-named museums added the
Kapela memorial park, the Rijeka F-84G, the Maribor and Dolenjske Toplice airframes, the Skopje
park and the AeroPark at Vrsar. **overpass-api.de is blocked by this environment's proxy;
maps.mail.ru worked first time on every query** and is the mirror to use here. Queries were POSTed
from a file with a browser User-Agent and a bbox, never `area["ISO3166-1"]`.

Roughly two thirds of the sites in this file exist in NO English aviation directory. Several were
recovered only by reverse-geocoding an untitled OSM node: the "Novi Sad" airframe is actually at
**Irig**, the "Batajnica" memorial sits in **Stara Pazova** municipality, the An-2 monument is at
**Divčibare** near Valjevo, and three airframes I initially bucketed as Croatian — the Otok
"Piccadilly Hope" Dakota, the Dolenjske Toplice CRJ-200 and the Dolenje Kamenje airframe — are in
**Slovenia**. Anyone repeating this work should reverse-geocode every node before assigning a
country; the Croatia/Slovenia and Serbia/Vojvodina boundaries in particular will mislead a
bounding-box guess.

**Local-language Wikipedia was the second pillar.** sr.wikipedia's „Музеј ваздухопловства — Београд“
carries the museum's full published inventory with registrations and construction numbers and cites
the Museum Association's *Vodič kroz muzeje Srbije* and the Belgrade heritage institute dossier.
sl.wikipedia is unusually strong on Yugoslav types: the **Soko 522** article maintains a worldwide
survivor list which is the only source I found anywhere for the Mostar airport gate aircraft 60143.
hr.wikipedia gave the Otočac DC-3's true identity, the Perešin aircraft's post-1991 history and the
protected-monument status of the Gornja Stubica park. sq.wikipedia was thin; the Albanian material
came from en.wikipedia's aerodrome articles plus OSM.

**Museum websites checked live in September 2026:** parkvojaskezgodovine.si (2026 opening hours and
a news item dated 1 September 2026), mgk.hr (2026 opening hours for Karlovac–Turanj). The Belgrade
museum's own domain **www.muzejvazduhoplovstva.org.rs now 302-redirects to a myhosting.rs parking
page** — a hard signal, independent of the press reporting, that the institution is not operating
normally.

**Compilations that proved stale or useless.** `aviationmuseum.eu` and `silverhawkauthor.com` were
not used as facts anywhere in this file, per the contract. The OSM `opening_hours=Mo-Su 09:00-17:00`
tag on the Belgrade museum polygon is stale and contradicted by the museum's own dead website and by
gradnja.rs; do not import it. General web search was unavailable for this pass (the session's search
budget was exhausted before this brief started and every alternative engine — DuckDuckGo, Mojeek,
Brave, Ecosia, several SearXNG instances — was blocked or rate-limited by the proxy; Bing returned a
geo-generic ad page with no organic results), so this file rests on OSM, Wikipedia in six languages,
Wikidata, direct site fetches and the OSM/Nominatim APIs. That gap is the single biggest weakness
here and is why so many rows carry medium rather than high confidence.

### The Belgrade museum — what the evidence actually says

The brief asked for careful verification. Here it is.

- The building is a protected cultural monument (Government of Serbia; *Službeni glasnik RS* 72/13;
  national register SK2145; heritage dossier at nasledje.gov.rs spomenik_id 109018). Ivan Štraus's
  torus won the federal Borba prize for the best architectural work of 1989.
- Founding rights passed from the City of Belgrade to the Republic of Serbia in March 2017.
- The museum's own published figures: **over 200 aircraft, of which visitors could see about 50 in
  the building and a dozen on the apron around it**; the remainder sit in the depot awaiting
  restoration. The OSM survey independently maps roughly **110 airframes in the open** inside the
  compound, which is consistent with a large outdoor reserve rather than a display.
- **gradnja.rs, 27 August 2025:** the museum "ostao zatvoren" — has remained closed for over a year;
  the valuable collection is increasingly endangered by poor conditions; the facade is damaged;
  aircraft have stood exposed to the weather for years. A contract has been signed for adaptation
  and modernisation of about 6000 m² of exhibition space plus new multipurpose hangars, with
  completion expected in 2027. The published renderings carry no named architect.
- I found **no evidence of a 2026 reopening**, and no evidence of a closure date more precise than
  "over a year before August 2025".

`access_type` is recorded `public` because that is what the institution is; the closure is recorded
in the site's `confidence` field and in every one of the 210 Belgrade aircraft descriptions. If the
importer would rather a closed museum read `appointment`, that is a one-field change and I have
flagged it rather than silently choosing.

### Corrections made

- **Perešin's aircraft is not in Gornja Stubica.** Several popular accounts conflate them. The MiG-21
  in the Spomen-park Rudolfu Perešinu came from the overhaul plant at Velika Gorica when the park
  opened on 24 June 2004; Perešin's actual defection aircraft was held at Klagenfurt, then
  dismantled and stored at Grossmittel near Wiener Neustadt, and was returned to Croatia only in
  2019, where it is displayed at the Ministry of Defence in Zagreb. Two separate site records.
- **Zagreb MiG-21bis: true identity 113, painted as 115.** The OSM surveyor caught this. `tail_number`
  is 113; 115 is recorded as an alias with the reason in the description. Exactly the trap the
  contract warns about.
- **Albania's fleet is Chinese, not Soviet.** The Rinas line is recorded as **Shenyang J-6** (the
  licence-built MiG-19; exported to Albania as the **F-6**) and **Shijiazhuang Y-5** (the licence-built
  An-2), with `manufacturer` set to the Chinese builder and the Soviet ancestor carried only as an
  alias. The OSM tagging at **Kuçovë** labels the stored fleet "MiG 15" and "MiG-19" throughout;
  those airframes are in reality overwhelmingly Chinese **Shenyang F-2/F-5/F-6** (J-2/J-5/J-6) —
  en.wikipedia's Kuçovë article lists the 2004 scrapping batch as "MiG-15s, MiG-19s and Yakovlev
  trainers as well as Chinese-made Shenyang F-5s and F-6s", i.e. both, mixed. Because I cannot say
  which airframe is which, I have written **no aircraft rows for Kuçovë air base at all** rather than
  import fifty rows with the wrong manufacturer.
- **Pivka's jet is Romanian.** Slovene Wikipedia calls it "OREL/IAR93 VULTUR" and the OSM node names
  it IAR-93 with tactical number 206. It is an **Avioane Craiova IAR-93**, the Romanian half of the
  joint programme that produced the Soko J-22 Orao — not a Yugoslav-built Orao. `operator_country` RO.
  (A separate genuine IAR-93 with number 201 is mapped at Timișoara in Romania, outside this brief.)
- **Otočac's "DC-3" is a C-47.** hr.wikipedia gives the ex-JRV evidencijski broj **71255** and a 1944
  build; a Yugoslav Air Force 712xx number on a 1944 airframe is a military C-47, so it is recorded
  with manufacturer Douglas; model C-47; variant A and tail number 71255; DC-3 is an alias.
- **Belgrade's "Li-3" is a Li-2.** The museum lists 7011 as a Li-3; the note explains it is a Li-2
  re-engined with C-47A powerplants. Recorded as Li-2 with Li-3 as an alias.
- **Belgrade's R-25 Vulkan is not a SAM.** The English Wikipedia table calls it a "SAM missile"; it is
  a Yugoslav unguided rocket. Recorded `artillery_rocket` with the error noted.
- **Belgrade's Spitfire wears two RAF serials.** The museum's own list gives JK808 *and* RAF serial
  JK448 for the same airframe. `tail_number` is JK808 (the worn marking); JK448 is an alias; the
  description says plainly that one of the two is not this aircraft's identity.
- **Belgrade Stearman YU-BAI.** The museum's own list writes the US registration as "N5536O???" with
  query marks. I did not import it; the description says why.
- **Bitola's "F-86E".** Yugoslav day-fighter Sabres in the 11xxx block include large numbers of
  Canadair CL-13 Sabre Mk.4 airframes. The Macedonian OSM label F-86E may be the Yugoslav service
  designation rather than the build standard. Recorded as North American F-86E as tagged, with the
  doubt stated and CL-13 as an alias.

### Judgement calls

- **Replicas recorded and flagged:** Sarić No I (replica of the 1909-1910 original, Belgrade);
  Nieuport 11 "12" (recorded by the museum itself as a replica, ex F-WZBC).
- **Not complete airframes, recorded anyway with the fact in the description:** the F-117A and F-16CG
  wreckage and the RQ-1/MQ-1 Predator and BGM-109 Tomahawk remains at Belgrade; the Soko G-4 23733
  which is a tail section only; the Rogožarski R-100 (uncompleted) and Fizir FP-2 (incomplete); the
  second Karlovac MiG-21, which the surveyor calls "demolished".
- **display_status at Belgrade.** With the museum shut, no status is fully honest. I used: `on_display`
  for the headline dome and apron exhibits, `in_storage` for the bulk duplicate blocks (17 Kraguj,
  15 J-21, 10 IJ-21, the MiG-21 variants, the Mi-2s, the whole glider collection), and
  `under_restoration` for the five the museum itself lists as awaiting restoration (Fiat G.50,
  Ju 87B-2, P-38L, P-51D, Hiller UH-12A). Every one of the 210 rows says in its description that the
  museum is closed and the collection was reported deteriorating in August 2025. A reader who wants
  "everything at Belgrade is effectively in storage right now" can get there from the descriptions.
- **The underwater Soko J-21 off Split** is recorded. It is deliberately placed and maintained as a
  dive attraction — deliberate retention plus public presentation — but it is reachable only on an
  organised dive, so `access_type` is `appointment`. If the database's convention is that submerged
  wrecks are excluded, this is the one row to drop.
- **"Party avion"** (Fokker 100 event venue, Strmec Stubički) is recorded because the contract
  explicitly counts an aircraft converted into a bar or cafe and publicly visitable.
- **Airworthy aircraft:** none recorded. Nothing in this sweep is a flying warbird.
- **Unidentified rows.** Ten airframes are recorded with manufacturer Unidentified and model Aircraft; every other
  identity field blank, per the contract's instruction not to drop a well-sourced site's airframe and
  not to guess a type: Gospođinci, Batajnica, Kuršumlija, Irig, two at Zemunik, Split airport,
  Maribor, Dolenje Kamenje and Kralje.
- **`operator_country` for pre-1991 airframes** is `RS` throughout with the description stating
  explicitly that the aircraft served **Yugoslavia**, as the brief directed — there is no ISO code for
  the SFRY. Post-1991 Croatian-operated airframes are `HR`; Slovenian `SI`; Albanian `AL`; the two
  1999 shoot-down wrecks and the Gjirokastër T-33 are `US`; the Polish-supplied Mi-8 at Pivka and the
  Polish-built Po-2V at Belgrade are `PL`; the Fiat G.50 is `IT`; the DFS 230 at Sarajevo is `DE`;
  the Pivka IAR-93 is `RO`.

### Blank fields, deliberately

- **`year_built` is blank on almost every row.** Soviet and Yugoslav construction-number blocks in
  this collection do not decode to a build date the way Czech or Polish blocks do — a MiG-21bis c/n
  75095222 encodes the *izdeliye* type number 75, not a year. The only populated `year_built` in the
  file is the Otočac C-47 (1944, stated in the source).
- **No serials were invented anywhere.** Where a monument aircraft has no published serial the field
  is blank — that is the majority of the non-museum rows in this file.
- **Latitude and longitude are blank for two sites:** the Perešin aircraft at the Ministry of Defence
  in Zagreb (the source says only "in front of the building") and the Soko 522 at Mostar airport
  (the source says only "at the entrance"). Both would be trivially fixed by one photograph.
- **`aircraft_name` is blank throughout.** None of these airframes carries a published individual name.
- **`wing_type` is blank on every rotary-wing and missile row**, per the contract.

### EXCLUDED, and why

- **Kuçovë Air Base storage line (LAKV, 40.7738-40.7751 / 19.9289-19.9332), about 50 airframes.**
  OpenStreetMap maps roughly fifty Chinese-built fighters here (tagged MiG-15/MiG-19 but really
  F-2/F-5/F-6 in the main). These are aircraft withdrawn from use in 2004 and parked pending disposal;
  40 were prepared for auction in 2016 with a stated intention to sell 100 more. Kuçovë was rebuilt
  to NATO standard with about €50 m of NATO money and reinaugurated on 4 March 2024 as a NATO
  tactical air base hosting Balkan air policing. This is a disposal line inside an active NATO
  installation, not deliberate retention with public presentation — a graveyard under the contract's
  test — so it is excluded. **It is the single highest-value thing in Albania for a future pass to
  re-check**, because if any part of it is ever formally preserved it becomes a major site overnight.
- **Gjadër Air Base (LAGJ, 41.895/19.598).** The famous cavern base. Flying ceased in 2000; the base
  "is now used as a storage facility for fighter aircraft withdrawn from service" with no Albanian Air
  Force aircraft permanently based there, and the tunnels hold up to about 50 airframes. Nothing is
  presented to the public; OSM maps no aircraft there at all. Excluded as storage. The site has since
  become the Italy–Albania migrant processing centre, which makes any future public access less
  likely rather than more.
- **PMK301 / PH-KXL memorial, Skopje (41.97790/21.61576).** Tagged `memorial=aircraft` but it is the
  memorial to the victims of the 5 March 1993 Palair Macedonian Fokker 100 crash, not a preserved
  airframe. Excluded.
