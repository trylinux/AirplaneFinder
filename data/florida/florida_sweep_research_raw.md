## 3. NOTES

### Sources and their weight

- **Hurlburt Field official Air Park guide PDF, dated December 2025** — the strongest currency instrument found anywhere in this sweep. A serving USAF installation republishing its own display roster nine months before this research is as close to a present-tense inventory as this domain offers. I recorded it verbatim.
- **Wikipedia + SilverHawkAuthor for the Armament Museum** — these agree on 28 of 33 airframes. They are *not* fully independent (both descend in part from Andy Marden's compilation) but their disagreements are informative, and the museum's own artifact page plus a dated ABPic photograph independently settled the F-86F.
- **Museum-published current pages** — used for DeLand and Fantasy of Flight status. These are authoritative on *presence* and, as the methodology predicts, publish almost no serials.
- **SilverHawkAuthor's statewide Florida list** — a ~500-entry compilation covering the whole state. It is the richest lead source available and I mined it heavily, but it proved **badly stale wherever I could test it** (see below) and it silently mixes privately-owned airworthy warbirds and FAA-registry residue in with actual displays. I have treated it as lead generation only, never as a sole basis for a record.

### Which sources proved stale, specifically

- **SilverHawkAuthor lists 17 aircraft at the "Florida Military Aviation Museum, Clearwater-St. Petersburg Airport."** That museum is **closed** and has been for years; aero-web carries a 2014 visitor report calling it defunct, and several of its jets went to **MAPS Air Museum in Canton, Ohio**. Recording that list would have created 17 phantom airframes at a site that does not exist.
- **SilverHawkAuthor places RF-4C 67-0452 at Hurlburt Field.** The December 2025 official Hurlburt guide does not list it, and the same compilation *also* places it at the Armament Museum. One airframe, one place: it is at the Armament Museum. The serial's position inside the RF-4C block also refutes the "RF-4E" designation that compilation gives it.
- **SilverHawkAuthor lists four aircraft at "Tyndall Air Force Base Museum"** (F-106A 59-0145, F-15A 74-0095, F-86L 52-10133, F-4C 63-7408). This is exactly the pre-2018 list the brief warned about. See below.
- **airplanes-online** printed the Armament Museum F-86's buzz number FU-910 in the serial field and rendered the F-84F as "51495" — two of the exact failure modes the brief names, in one page.

### Deliberate blanks

- **`year_built` is blank on every single row.** I found no sourced construction, roll-out, first-flight, delivery or acceptance date for any airframe in this package, and no civil registration in the display set whose FAA `YEAR-MFR` I could read. Every serial here carries a tempting FY prefix and every one of them is an *order* year. A blank is the correct answer; do not read these as oversights.
- **MiG-21F-13 tail number** — sources give "14" and "85", both two-digit tactical numbers, neither a serial. Blank.
- **Camp Blanding C-47 tail number** — "12436" is internally inconsistent with the C-47H designation attached to it. Blank, with the number preserved in aliases as a search term.
- **DeLand tail numbers** — the museum publishes none and I would not import a stale compilation's guesses into a site I could otherwise confirm.
- **Armament Museum BQM-34A** — no serial published anywhere.

### Judgment calls

- **Armament Museum, five contested airframes.** SilverHawkAuthor alone adds C-131B 53-7821, T-33A 49-0932, T-33A 53-4987, F-100C 54-1954 and O-2A 67-21331. Wikipedia alone adds F-15E 86-0184, both Firebees and O-2A 68-6864. I recorded the Wikipedia-only ones (a maintained page adding aircraft is consistent with arrivals) and **excluded the SilverHawkAuthor-only ones** (a stale page listing extras is consistent with departures). This is a defensible inference, not a verification — flag it for a site visit.
- **DeLand's Jenny is recorded explicitly as a REPLICA** in both description and aliases. It is not an original airframe.
- **Hurlburt access_type is `restricted`.** The Air Park is inside the wire and needs base access; that is the test the brief sets, regardless of the Air Force publishing the guide openly.
- **NAS Jacksonville access_type is `restricted`** — aviationmuseum.eu states "by prior permission only", and it is inside a controlled gate.
- **Fantasy of Flight access_type is `appointment`** — see open questions.
- **F-105D 58-1155 is `ground_attack`, not `electronic_warfare`.** The EW convention applies to the F-105G Wild Weasel only; this is a D.
- **Both Firebees are `fixed_wing` / `drone`**, per the Q-designation convention.

### EXCLUDED, and why

**Sites excluded outright**

- **National Naval Aviation Museum, Pensacola** — already fully recorded, per instruction.
- **Florida Military Aviation Museum, Clearwater (17 airframes in the stale list)** — **CLOSED**, collection dispersed, several airframes now at MAPS Air Museum, Ohio. Do not re-research; do not import.
- **Tyndall AFB Air Park (4 airframes in the stale list)** — Hurricane Michael, October 2018. DVIDS documents the 325th MXS beginning to disassemble and truck out heritage-park aircraft including an F-15 on 20 February 2019. I could not establish what returned, what was scrapped, or whether a park exists in 2026. **Recording the pre-2018 list would have been exactly the error the brief warns against.** Excluded pending confirmation, not because it is certainly gone.
- **MacDill AFB Memorial Park (4 airframes in the stale list)** — the Tampa Bay Times reported MacDill "shipping off two of its most iconic landmarks", explicitly including the KB-50J, and rebuilding Memorial Park around models and plaques rather than airframes. The stale list is refuted at the head; I could not establish what if anything remains. Excluded pending confirmation.
- **Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex** — I deliberately recorded **nothing**. The one aggregate list I obtained is visibly corrupted: it assigns the T-38's registration N969NA to Space Shuttle Atlantis, lists "Apollo II" as a Saturn V serial and "Saturnus V" as a command-module identity. Atlantis (OV-104) is unquestionably there, and the Rocket Garden unquestionably exists, but I will not launder a garbled source into records for a flagship site. This needs its own dedicated pass against NASA's own inventory.
- **Cape Canaveral Space Force Museum, Hangar C and Launch Complex 26** — ~40 rockets and missiles, but on an active installation reachable only via advance authorisation or a commercial tour. I recorded the **Sands Space History Center** as the public site and left its holdings for a dedicated pass; its displays are components (a Jupiter nosecone, a Mercury boilerplate) rather than airframes.
- **Opa-locka** — per instruction, stored and derelict airliners are not museum holdings. The compilation's Opa-locka entries are registry residue.
- **Miami's 23 PBY-5A/PBY-6A Catalinas** — this is not a collection. It is an FAA-registry or surplus-storage dump reproduced as if it were a display list. No museum in Miami holds 23 Catalinas.
- **Kissimmee, Pembroke Pines, Port Orange, New Smyrna Beach, Ocala Mustang and Corsair clusters** — these are privately owned, largely airworthy warbirds based at fields, exactly the "operational aircraft are not displays" exclusion. Several are famous flying aircraft.

**Not excluded — simply not yet verified.** The following are real sites that almost certainly hold real displays, and I could not corroborate them to the standard this brief demands within this pass. They are the highest-value remaining work, **not** an exclusion list:

Valiant Air Command Warbird Museum (~50 airframes — see below), Homestead ARB (gate F-4D confirmed present but serial unpublished), NAS Key West, NAS Whiting Field, NAS Mayport, Jacksonville ANG Base, Herlong Airport's Buckeye row, Panama City's F-101B cluster, Fort Lauderdale, and the long tail of VFW/Legion/park single-airframe monuments across roughly 70 Florida towns.

### The Valiant Air Command problem

This is the largest gap in the package and I want to be explicit about it. SilverHawkAuthor returns a detailed 50-airframe list with serials, and Wikipedia confirms the museum is open and roughly that size. **But the museum's own collection pages are under construction and publish no serials**, so I had exactly one source for every serial, from the compilation that failed every currency test I applied to it elsewhere in this state. Wikipedia's own summary already conflicts with it — it lists an SBD Dauntless and a MiG-17 that the serial list does not contain, and an XP-82 Twin Mustang on loan.

Fifty single-sourced serials from a demonstrably stale compilation is precisely the input that produces a hundred wrong records. I did not record them. This is the single best return on one phone call in Florida.

### Open questions, ranked

1. **Valiant Air Command Warbird Museum — 321-268-1941.** Request the current collection list with BuNos/serials. One call converts ~50 airframes from unusable to recordable. Highest value in the state by a wide margin.
2. **Tyndall AFB — 325th Wing Public Affairs, 850-283-4500.** Does an air park exist in 2026, and what is in it? Settles a whole site and prevents four phantom records. Ask specifically about F-106A 59-0145, F-15A 74-0095, F-86L 52-10133 and F-4C 63-7408.
3. **MacDill AFB — 6th ARW Public Affairs, 813-828-2215.** What airframes, if any, remain at Memorial Park after the rebuild? Settles four contested records.
4. **Air Force Armament Museum — 850-651-1808.** Resolve the five contested airframes (C-131B 53-7821, T-33A 49-0932 and 53-4987, F-100C 54-1954, O-2A 67-21331 vs 68-6864), the C-47's true designation (TC-47B or AC-47D), and the MiG-21's actual identity. One call settles seven questions.
5. **Homestead ARB Public Affairs, 786-415-7400.** Serial of the gate F-4D (the stale list offers 66-0267 and 66-0273 and I cannot tell which is the gate jet or whether both are present), plus the F-16A and F-100D.
6. **NAS Jacksonville** — resolve the EA-6B BuNo (160609 vs 156481) and the four variant conflicts. A single site visit photographs all 15.
7. **Fantasy of Flight** — status is genuinely ambiguous and needs a human. The main site now presents the property as an **event venue**, the collection pages are structured around what is "currently not showing in museum", and the last clear description of public operation is the 2015 scaled-down reopening into the former maintenance hangar. Kermit Weeks' collection is one of the largest privately held in the world (150+ airframes, including two B-17Gs and a B-24J) and getting it right matters. I recorded the site as `appointment` and **recorded zero airframes**, because I could not establish which of 150 aircraft a member of the public can currently see. Do not import a Fantasy of Flight aircraft list from any directory without direct confirmation.
8. **Kennedy Space Center** — needs a dedicated pass against NASA inventory documents, not aviation directories.

### One methodological flag

Nothing in this package rests on Grokipedia, and nothing rests on a single directory where a second source was reachable. Where I had only one source and it came from the compilation lineage that failed repeatedly in this state, I returned a note instead of a row. The result is a smaller package than the brief's scope implies — 32 + 23 + 7 + 15 + 1 + 5 = **83 airframes across 10 sites** — but every serial in it survived a production-block check and every site in it is confirmed to exist in 2025-26.
