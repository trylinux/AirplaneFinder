## NOTES

**Scope.** 29 sites and 272 airframe rows: public museums and military base
collections in Minnesota, Iowa, North Dakota and South Dakota. Monuments,
VFW/American Legion post displays and city-park plinths were deliberately left
to the separate monuments pass and none appear above. All four states were
greenfield.

### Sources and their weight

1. **FAA bulk registry, `ReleasableAircraft.zip` downloaded 10 Sep 2026** — the
   primary discovery tool for this pass, exactly as briefed (it 403s to bare
   curl; a browser User-Agent gets it). `MASTER.txt` was joined to `ACFTREF.txt`
   locally and filtered to MN/IA/ND/SD by owner name. 446 rows matched a
   museum/foundation/heritage/guard name pattern. This is the strongest
   *currency* evidence in the file: a live registration in the September 2026
   file to a named museum means the aircraft was that museum's a few weeks ago.
   It carried the whole Airpower Museum (36 rows), the Iowa Aviation Museum
   (14 rows), Dakota Territory (12), Fagen Fighters (15), Wings of the North
   (5), the Ankeny museum (6+2), Fargo Air Museum (5) and American Wings /
   American Aviation Heritage Foundation (6). It found essentially nothing for
   base air parks, as the brief predicted — military serials are not registered.
2. **Aerial Visuals** — POST to `Locator.php` at the site root with
   `Country=United States`, `Region=<state>`, `Scope=3` (GET is broken), then
   `LocationDossier.php?Serial=<id>` per site. This is the backbone of every
   military serial, construction number and arc-second coordinate above. 32
   location dossiers were pulled. It is an identity source and **not** a
   currency source: its per-location content is largely 2008-2015 vintage, and
   several of its civil registrations are demonstrably stale (see corrections).
3. **OpenStreetMap `historic=aircraft` / `memorial=aircraft`** via
   `overpass.private.coffee` (POST; plain tag queries only). Only the North
   Dakota bounding box completed inside the tool timeout — the Minnesota, Iowa
   and South Dakota queries timed out repeatedly and were not retrieved. The ND
   extract is current and independently confirms the whole Grand Forks AFB air
   park (7 nodes), the 119th Wing Heritage Park at Fargo (6 nodes) and five
   outdoor airframes at Dakota Territory (F-106, A-7, C-47, F-15A, T-33). That
   is the strongest currency evidence in the file for military airframes.
4. **Museum websites** — Fagen Fighters WWII aircraft page, CAF Minnesota Wing
   aircraft page, Fargo Air Museum exhibits page, Dakota Territory Air Museum
   (address and 2026 season), Iowa Aviation Museum / flyingmuseum.com (2026
   admission prices and May-October season), Mid America Museum (2025-26 winter
   and summer hours), American Wings (annex hours and the hangar sale),
   nps.gov/mimi (Delta-09 open), history.nd.gov (Oscar-Zero).
5. **Wikipedia collection lists** — South Dakota Air and Space Museum (revised
   22 Jun 2025), Minnesota Air National Guard Museum, Fargo Air Museum, Fagen
   Fighters, Ellsworth AFB. Used only to cross-check Aerial Visuals; where they
   disagree both readings are carried in the row.
6. Not used: aviationmuseum.eu and silverhawkauthor (lead lists only per the
   brief; not needed once FAA + Aerial Visuals covered the ground). Joe Baugher
   was **not** reached in this pass — see open questions.

### Corrections made, with evidence

- **Mid America's C-123K is 54-0695**, not the Philippine "PNP-022" Aerial
  Visuals gives. The live FAA record for N8190B carries serial 54-0695, which
  sits in the C-123B/K block 54-0664/0711.
- **Cessna U-3A at Box Elder is 57-5872, not 53-5872.** Wikipedia's June 2025
  list has the FY digits wrong: Cessna 310 procurement for the USAF began in
  FY57 and the L-27A/U-3A block is 57-5842 upward. There is no FY53 U-3A.
- **The Box Elder C-47H is one aircraft with two correct numbers.** c/n 13004
  was built as USAAF C-47A 42-93127 (Wikipedia) and transferred to the Navy as
  BuNo 17193 (Aerial Visuals). Recorded under the USAAF serial with the BuNo in
  aliases.
- **The Box Elder C-54 is 42-72642, not 42-72592.** Aerial Visuals supplies
  c/n 10747, which ties to 42-72642; Wikipedia's number has no c/n behind it.
- **Cessna O-1A N269TH has moved from the American Wings Air Museum (Blaine) to
  Wings of the North (Eden Prairie).** Aerial Visuals still lists it at Blaine;
  the September 2026 FAA file registers it to Wings of the North Inc with a
  2024 last-action date. Recorded at Eden Prairie. This is the one two-site
  claim found in the pass and it is resolved.
- **Bell OH-13E N39WL has moved from the Fargo Air Museum to the Minnesota
  Military and Veterans Museum**; Aerial Visuals records the departure from
  Fargo without saying where, and the live FAA registrant is the Little Falls
  museum.
- **Several Aerial Visuals civil registrations are dead or reassigned** and are
  recorded as historic only, never as live identity: N102Z (Dakota Territory
  C-47A — now a 2019 aircraft in Georgia), N246FE (Mid America 727 — now a 2025
  FedEx aircraft), N512NA (MN ANG Museum T-33A — now a Zipline aircraft),
  N63625 (MN ANG Museum T-6 — now a 1991 homebuilt in Iowa), N9453Z (MN ANG
  Museum C-45H — now in Washington state), N4111A (Box Elder C-45H — now a
  balloon in Colorado), N8435H (Box Elder C-131D — now in Wisconsin), N19JP and
  N369TT (Fargo — now in Arizona and Louisiana).
- **Airpower Museum's Stinson N27710 is a Stinson 10**, per the live FAA record
  (c/n 7655, YEAR-MFR 1940). Aerial Visuals calls it a UC-81E Reliant.
- **The 185th at Sioux City is an Air Refueling Wing**, not the "185th Fighter
  Wing" Aerial Visuals still names; it converted to KC-135s in 2003.

### Judgment calls

- **Fagen Fighters is counted as a museum, not a private airworthy fleet.** The
  brief asked for a judgment. The airframes are held by single-purpose LLCs at
  Granite Falls under the Fagen name, presented in a purpose-built museum with
  published hours, and listed on the museum's own collection page. I counted
  **21 airframes at the museum** (15 with live FAA registrations, 6 listed only
  by the museum and Wikipedia with no published serial) plus **2 TBM-3s held by
  the restoration arm** and recorded `under_restoration`. Airworthiness is
  stated in each row; the exclusion in the brief is for warbirds *merely based*
  at a field, which these are not.
- **The CAF Minnesota Wing is one site**, at Fleming Field. The Red Tail
  Squadron's P-51C is recorded there rather than as a separate site: Aerial
  Visuals puts it at the same hangar coordinate to within 15 m, and the
  squadron's aircraft is displayed in the wing's hangar museum.
- **The Twin Cities installation is two records.** The Minnesota Air National
  Guard Museum (appointment) and a small 133rd Airlift Wing display area about
  300 m north (restricted) are separate Aerial Visuals locations with distinct
  coordinates. The wing's three stored C-130Es are **not** recorded — Aerial
  Visuals itself flags them as unconfirmed and possibly operational.
- **Camp Ripley is two records** for the same reason: the museum apron
  (7 airframes, tight cluster at 46.0807) and a single Huey at Ray S. Miller
  Army Airfield 1.3 km north. Both `restricted` — Camp Ripley is a controlled
  installation. Whether the museum's newer building sits inside or outside the
  gate could not be confirmed (the museum site served no readable text); if it
  is outside, the access type should become `public`.
- **American Wings Air Museum is recorded with 6 airframes, not the 25 Aerial
  Visuals lists.** The museum's own site says the main hangar was sold and most
  of the collection went to storage; only one airframe (OV-1C 68-15936, N134AW)
  is still FAA-registered to the museum and five more to its parent, the
  American Aviation Heritage Foundation. The Mohawk fleet, F-86F 52-4646, F-5E
  74-1539, UH-34D 147174, TA-7C 154500, US-2B 136540, the Ford Trimotor NC8403
  and the UH-1B/UH-1H are **not** recorded: several of the civil ones are now
  registered to unrelated private owners in other states, which makes the whole
  Aerial Visuals list untrustworthy for this site. Open question 1.
- **The 119th Wing's F-16A 80-0605 is recorded `in_storage`**, not `on_display`:
  it is retained on the installation as a Regional Training Site ground trainer
  1.5 km from the Heritage Park, which is a different thing from a presented
  display but is not a departure either.
- **Replicas** are flagged in `description` and never in `aliases`: the Fargo
  Wright Flyer and half-scale Fw 190, the 132nd Wing P-51D (painted 44-15648,
  kept out of the tail-number field), and the Iowa Aviation Museum's Nieuport 17.
- **A-7K 81-0073 is a flagged conflict.** Aerial Visuals lists the same c/n
  K-026 both at the Iowa Gold Star Military Museum on Camp Dodge and at a
  non-public 185th ARW site in Sioux City. It is recorded once, at Camp Dodge,
  because that entry carries a display-pad coordinate; Sioux City's does not.

### Deliberately blank

- **Tail numbers (17).** Dakota Territory's F-106 ("AD227" is a drone control
  number, not a serial); the MN ANG Museum's PQM-102A (same reason), its T-33A
  (a NASA fleet number only) and its T-37; Mid America's second Huey (Aerial
  Visuals' "66-1587" is not a valid UH-1 serial in any FY66 block and was **not**

  History Center JN-4; the 133rd ACS F-84F c/n.

  `YEAR-MFR` field. No fiscal-year prefix was ever used as a build year. Note
  that the Ankeny RC-45G "51-11854" and similar FY51 Beech numbers are
  remanufacture serials and carry no defensible build date at all.
- **Per-airframe coordinates** are quoted in `description` where Aerial Visuals
  or OSM supplied them, and site coordinates are the airframe cluster rather
  than an airport reference point in every case except the Minuteman Missile
  NHS (which uses the Delta-09 launch facility, the only place with a missile).
