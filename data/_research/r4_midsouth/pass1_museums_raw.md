# NOTES

## Source weighting
- **FAA bulk registry (MASTER.txt joined to ACFTREF.txt, September 2026 vintage)** — primary discovery tool and the strongest currency evidence for civil-registered museum fleets. It carried the whole Beechcraft Heritage Museum roster (35 airframes), the whole Swift Museum Foundation roster, and the civil airframes at the Tennessee Museum of Aviation and Aviation Heritage Park. The owner-name web search is broken exactly as the brief says; the bulk download with a browser User-Agent worked. It found essentially nothing in Kentucky and Mississippi because those museums' collections are ex-military and unregistered.
- **Aerial Visuals `Locator.php` / `LocationDossier.php` POST at the site root** — the single richest identity source for this region: per-airframe arc-second fixes and serials for 24 locations. Vintage roughly 2008-2015 as the brief warns, so used for identity only.
- **OpenStreetMap `historic=aircraft` via overpass.private.coffee** — the currency counterweight. The 2026 extract independently confirms 18 Barksdale air-park airframes, all five Flying Tiger Heritage Park airframes, five Camp Shelby airframes, both Halls TN airframes and the Kentucky Science Center Mustang. Where an OSM node matches an Aerial Visuals entry the airframe is treated as confirmed present in 2026.
- **Museum own sites (2026)** — Barksdale, Tennessee Museum of Aviation, Beechcraft Heritage, Aviation Heritage Park, Swift Museum, Southern Heritage, North Louisiana Military Museum, USS Kidd, INFINITY, Mississippi Armed Forces Museum. Authoritative on presence and access, near-useless on serials.
- **aviationmuseum.eu** — used as a lead list only and it failed the same way as in other states: for Flying Tiger Heritage Park it gives A-10 73-0667, A-7D 66-3234, F-86 52-4931 and F-105G 56-3296, none of which pass a production-block check (56-3296 is a KC-135 serial). Aerial Visuals' serials for that site all pass. Discarded.

## Corrections with evidence
1. **Barksdale is NOT publicly accessible without base clearance.** The task brief stated the museum sits outside the base gate and is publicly accessible. That is wrong as of 2026. The museum's own current site instructs US citizens without a DoD ID to submit a Visitor Request Form to Barksdale Security Forces **at least 30 days prior**, with a separate Foreign Visitor Request Form dated 7 February 2025. `access_type` is recorded as **restricted**. Aerial Visuals still carries the old note "the museum is currently accessible to the public" — it is stale.
2. **F-86L 52-4168 two-site claim resolved.** Both Jackson Barracks and the Chennault Museum have been credited with this Sabre. Aerial Visuals records it as departed from Jackson Barracks and present at Chennault; recorded at Chennault only.
3. **Aero Commander U-4B 55-4648 two-site claim resolved.** Credited to both the Wedell-Williams museum in Patterson and the Regional Military Museum in Houma. Aerial Visuals lists it as departed Patterson and present at Houma; recorded at Houma only.
4. **Beech T-34B 144102 / N8457.** Aerial Visuals lists it as having left the Tennessee Museum of Aviation. The September 2026 FAA registry shows N8457 current to that museum at Sevierville, which is the later evidence; recorded as present.
5. **Chennault A-7 BuNo 154485 variant conflict.** The museum and Wikipedia both call it an A-7E. BuNo 154485 falls inside the 154361-154556 A-7B block. `variant` left blank rather than asserting either.
6. **Barksdale KC-97L "030240" and T-33 "80615"** in museum literature are the same airframes as 53-0240 and 58-0615; the museum drops the fiscal-year hyphen. Recorded in full-serial form.
7. **Barksdale AT-11 "3267"** on the museum's own page is the construction number, not the serial — exactly the failure mode the brief predicts. The serial is 42-36887.
8. **Barksdale B-17 identity.** The airframe is Navy PB-1W BuNo 77244, painted as B-17G "231340 Miss Liberty". The painted number is in `aliases`, not `tail_number`.
