## NOTES

### WAS SEARCH AVAILABLE
**General web search was NOT available.** The shared WebSearch quota for this session was fully exhausted (200 of 200 calls) before I started; my one confirming call returned the budget-exhausted message. Everything below therefore rests on APIs and direct URL fetches:
- **OSM Overpass** — the single most productive tool for this region by a wide margin, exactly as the brief predicted.
- **Wikipedia APIs** in Russian, English, Armenian and Georgian (`list=search`, `prop=extracts`, `list=categorymembers`).
- **Wikimedia Commons API** (category walks, `insource:` search, `generator=geosearch`, `iiextmetadatafilter` for dated photo descriptions).
- **Wikidata SPARQL** (`query.wikidata.org`).
- **Nominatim reverse geocoding** — used on every single OSM coordinate; this is what turned bare `historic=aircraft` nodes into city, region and postal code.
- **api.openstreetmap.org `/map.json`** — used to pull the *context* around unnamed aircraft nodes, which is how the Ashgabat and Tbilisi sites were identified. This is worth remembering: when Overpass is congested, the plain OSM map API on a tiny bbox works fine and tells you what facility an aircraft node sits inside.
- **Direct fetch of aviahistory.ucoz.ru.**

### SOURCES AND THEIR WEIGHT
**1. OpenStreetMap via Overpass — carried the region.** Sweep of `historic=aircraft`, `memorial=aircraft`, `memorial:type=aircraft`, `aeroway=gate_guardian` and aviation/military/technology museums, using `area["ISO3166-1"=...]` per country. Yield: KZ 27, KG 13, UZ 12, AM 10, GE 8, TM 5, MN 2, AZ 2, **TJ 0**.

**Mirror behaviour, for the next agent:**
- `overpass-api.de` — blocked by the proxy as the brief said.
- **`maps.mail.ru/osm/tools/overpass/api/interpreter` — the workhorse.** The `area["ISO3166-1"]` filter works there (the brief said it worked only on kumi/private.coffee — that is not right, it worked on mail.ru too). It served 8 of 9 countries.
- `overpass.private.coffee` — worked, slower; picked up Kazakhstan and Azerbaijan when mail.ru rate-limited.
- `overpass.kumi.systems` — returned `Dispatcher_Client::request_read_and_idx::timeout` for anything larger than a single small country. Effectively unusable for this sweep.
- The Bash tool caps at 120 s, so every Overpass call must be launched with `setsid nohup` into a background script writing a status file, and polled. A foreground `curl` against a nine-country query will always be killed before it returns.

**2. `aviahistory.ucoz.ru` ("Сайт авиационной истории") — the find of this assignment, and the reason the serials in this file exist at all.** It is a Russian monument-aircraft registry organised by type and then by country, with per-airframe construction numbers, factory dates, delivery dates, registration dates, operator chains, write-off dates and installation dates, sourced to *Soviet Transports*, russianplanes.net and the "Авиапамятники" project. It is the ONLY source I found anywhere that gives construction numbers for post-Soviet Central Asian monuments. Crucially it is also **honest about losses**: it marks scrapped airframes "Утилизирован" with dates, which is how I was able to exclude five monuments that other compilations still list as extant.
Its coverage is type-limited — it catalogues transports and trainers (An-8/10/12/28/30/32, Tu-16/114/124/134, Yak-11/12/18P/40/42, PZL-104, M-15) and does **not** cover the MiG/Su/Mi fighters and helicopters that make up most of this region's plinthed aircraft. So it is deep but narrow.
Useful index URLs: monuments index `http://aviahistory.ucoz.ru/index/0-5`, museums index `.../0-4`. Country sub-pages reached this pass: An-12 KZ `0-471` / UZ `0-474`; Tu-124 UZ `0-422`; Tu-134 AZ `0-522` / UZ `0-364`; Yak-40 KZ `0-304` / KG `0-305` / TJ `0-309` / UZ `0-310` / GE `0-336` / AM `0-502`.

**3. Wikimedia Commons category tree** — `Category:Monuments featuring aircraft in Kazakhstan / ...in Uzbekistan / ...in Kyrgyzstan` exist and are the only structured photo index for the region. They are **thin** (Kazakhstan's whole tree is five leaf categories; Uzbekistan's is empty of subcats). What they do give is *dated* photographs with EXIF coordinates, which is the best currency-of-evidence available here — the Baikonur An-12 photos are from April 2026 and the Balkhash MiG-21 from September 2024. There is no Commons monument category at all for Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, Mongolia, Georgia, Armenia or Azerbaijan.

**4. Russian-language Wikipedia** — the brief was right that it is the best encyclopaedic source for the region, but its *monument* coverage of Central Asia is much weaker than for Russia and Ukraine. `Категория:Самолёты-памятники` has exactly one entry in all nine countries (the Baikonur Li-2). Where ru.wikipedia was decisive was on **Baikonur and Buran** (`ОК-МЛ1`, `Буран 1.02`, `ОК-МТ` are three separate detailed articles) and on the **negative finding at Tashkent** (`Парк «Боевой славы» (Ташкент)`).

**5. Armenian Wikipedia** settled the Yerevan aviation museum question that nothing in English or Russian could — see below.

**6. `aviamonuments.ru`** — cited by aviahistory as a partner project and would probably be excellent, but it is **unreachable from this environment** (proxy returns `CONNECT tunnel failed, 502`). Flagged for a human.

**7. `aviationmuseum.eu`** — its Asia and Armenia pages both 404. Not consulted, no facts taken from it.

### CORRECTIONS MADE
- **Aral, not Tokmok (Kyrgyzstan).** Wikimedia Commons files the MiG-23M under `Category:MiG-23M monument (Tokmok)`. The OSM node for a MiG-23 memorial reverse-geocodes through Nominatim to **Shosseynaya street, Aral, Chuy District** — about 8 km west of Tokmok. Tokmok separately has an **Il-28** monument at the Lenin/Gorky junction. Recorded as two distinct sites. If a future pass finds the MiG-23 genuinely inside Tokmok, these two records must be re-checked for a duplicate.
- **The Baikonur An-12 is not the Baikonur Li-2.** Commons carries two separate categories and ru.wikipedia one article; they are two different monuments in two different parts of the city (Aviators' park in the 7th microdistrict vs the plinth at Yangel/Seyfullin). Both recorded.
- **Aktobe Yak-40 "19 yellow" wears a false registration.** The registry states plainly that `UN-87590` was *applied in 2000* to a monument aircraft whose real identity is CCCP-87590 / c/n 9110416, and that it was subsequently repainted again into Kazakh Air Defence Forces colours as bort 19. Per the contract, the tail_number field carries the bort it currently wears and the description carries the true chain; `UN-87590` and `CCCP-87590` are in aliases.
- **Aktobe An-12 "18 yellow" likewise.** True identity CCCP-11985 / c/n 402005, ex-Soviet Air Force; the yellow 18 and the Kazakh colours were applied after 1996.
- **Two Ust-Kamenogorsk Yak-40s, not one.** UN-87492 (c/n 9541545, Left Bank park) and UN-87498 (c/n 9540146, Metallurg park) have adjacent-looking registrations and were both installed in 2011 in the same city. They are two airframes with different construction numbers, different build dates and different parks. Anyone reconciling registers should not collapse them.
- **Semey has two candidate Yak-40 locations.** The registry puts UN-88259 beside the Zhana-Semey airport terminal (September 2011); OSM has an unnamed `historic=aircraft` node inside Semey city at 50.3438/80.2178. I have NOT merged them — the airport lies east of the city and the positions do not agree. Recorded as two sites, with the discrepancy stated.
- **`historic=aircraft` on two Karakalpak airfields is an OSM tagging error, not a display.** Nodes at 42.3666/59.5117 (Takhiatash / Qubla Nokis) and 42.7812/59.6299 (Kegeyli) carry `historic=aircraft` *together with* `disused:aeroway=aerodrome` and `aerodrome:type=military/public`. The mapper tagged the disused airfield, not an airframe. Excluded.

### JUDGMENT CALLS
- **Buran 1.02 and OK-MT recorded as `in_storage` at a `restricted` site.** They fail the "publicly presented" half of the display test, but they are unambiguously *deliberately retained*, they are internationally significant, they have a documented and contested ownership, and the brief asked explicitly what is where and who owns it. Recording them with `in_storage` and `restricted` states the truth without pretending they are on show.
- **OK-ML1 is a mock-up and is labelled as one.** `aircraft_type: spacecraft`, `role_type: space`. It is *finished to look like* the flown orbiter, which is exactly the kind of thing that gets miscatalogued as Buran 1.01. Buran 1.01 was destroyed in the 2002 roof collapse and no part of it is on display anywhere.
- **`BTS-002 / OK-GLI is NOT in this region`** — it is at the Technik Museum Speyer, Germany, and has been since 2008. Recording it here to close the brief's question, not because there is anything to import.
- **Aircraft converted to a restaurant, a hotel, a children's cinema, a playroom or a cafe are recorded**, per the contract's explicit rule. That covers: Gelediz Tu-134 restaurant (AZ), Yenokavan aircraft hotel (AM), Sevan and Rustavi Yak-40s (AM/GE), Karshi Yak-40 children's cinema (UZ), Osh Yak-40 children's cinema (KG), Chimgan Yak-40 cafe (UZ).
- **The Gelediz Tu-134 is a fuselage only.** Recorded as a full record with the amputation stated in the description, following the contract's treatment of nose sections and partial airframes as records.
- **The Chimgan Yak-40 is now incomplete** — undercarriage legs and tail surfaces cut off for scrap by summer 2020. Still recorded: it is a retained, presented hulk in use as premises, not a scrap-line hulk.
- **`Unidentified` used 24 times, deliberately.** Most of this region's OSM aircraft nodes carry `historic=aircraft` and nothing else. The contract's instruction is explicit: use `Unidentified` plus a descriptive model rather than dropping the row or guessing a type. Every one of these is a real, mapped, surveyed object at a known coordinate; they just need one photograph each.
- **`operator_country` rule I applied, so a later pass can audit it.** (a) Where the source gives an operator chain, I used the state that actually flew it — `KZ` for airframes that flew as `UN-`, `UZ` for those handed to the Uzbek Air Force in 1992-93, `TJ` for `EY-`, `AM` for `EK-`, `GE` for `4L-`, `AZ` for `4K-`. (b) `RU` where the airframe demonstrably served only the USSR and was withdrawn or plinthed before 1991 — this covers most of the Yak-40 monuments, both Baikonur airframes, the Akaltyn Tu-124 and the Aktobe pair. (c) `RU` for types that left Soviet service long before 1991 and were never operated by the successor state — the Kant MiG-15, the Tokmok Il-28, the Priozersk MiG-17, the Yerevan MiG-19. (d) **BLANK** for MiG-21, MiG-23, Su-25, Su-27, An-2, Mi-24 and every Unidentified airframe, because those types *were* inherited and flown by the successor states and I have no plinthing date. Blank is the honest answer and it imports safely; guessing `RU` on a Kyrgyz MiG-21 would be the exact collision the field exists to prevent.
- **Access types.** `restricted` for anything behind a military or factory gate (Aktobe institute, Baikonur city and cosmodrome, Chirchiq tank school, TAPOiCh, Priozersk, Ashgabat training school, Tashkent 60th Military Town). `appointment` for private grounds a visitor can reach by asking (Sevan and Chimgan recreation bases, the Rustavi kindergarten, the Dushanbe university campus, the Krtsanisi school, the Zvartnots administration building). Everything else `public`. Judged by how a member of the public actually gets in, not by ownership.
- **Sites recorded with NO aircraft rows.** Astana Military History Museum, Kostanay open-air museum, Pavlodar Museum of Military Glory, Oral military equipment exhibition, Kutaisi Science and Technology Museum, Khankendi Martyrs' Memorial Museum, Museum of the History of Armenian Aviation, Mongolian Military Museum (one Unidentified only). These are real, geocoded institutions in scope, but I could not obtain an inventory and I will not invent one. They import as sites and can be filled in later.
- **Azerbaijan descriptions kept scrupulously neutral**, per the brief. The Baku Military Trophy Park is discussed below in EXCLUDED with its factual content stated and no airframe asserted; the Khankendi museum is recorded by its own name and OSM tags with no political characterisation attached. I did not import contested framing from any side.

### EXCLUDED, AND WHY
**Confirmed scrapped or destroyed — do not re-research these:**
- **Emba-5 (Ploshchadka 10), Kazakhstan — An-12 "48 white"**, ex-165th Independent Composite Aviation Regiment, plinthed in the Victory park of the military town in the late 1970s, used as a cafe, given the name "Наташа", repainted into a camouflage scheme in the 1990s. aviahistory marks it **Утилизирован** (scrapped).
- **Almalyk, Uzbekistan — An-12**, towed 70 km under its own power from Tuzel in 1999 to a roadside teahouse in microdistrict 5/2, extended with a welded plug to make a restaurant, repainted into Uzbekistan Airways colours in 2008. **Scrapped November 2017.**
- **Angren, Uzbekistan — Yak-40 CCCP-87619** (c/n 9132018), in the city park, used as a video salon, a children's cafe and finally a coal store for the "Ангар" cafe. **Burned 2 January 2019 and scrapped.**
- **Fergana, Uzbekistan — An-12B CCCP-11064** (c/n 6343907). A genuine composite: rear fuselage section F-3 from An-12 CCCP-12964 mated to section F-2 of CCCP-11005, producing the hybrid registration. Used as a children's cinema. **Scrapped.**
- **Urgench, Uzbekistan — Tu-134 CCCP-65600** (c/n 5350002) at the Parvoz hotel from 8 September 1983. This was a genuinely historic airframe — the Tupolev OKB high-alpha and anti-spin test aircraft, first flown 14 August 1965. **Scrapped.** A real loss.
- **Kurgan-Tyube (Bokhtar), Tajikistan — Yak-40** in the children's park. **Scrapped in the second half of the 2000s.**

**Removed, not scrapped — the site no longer qualifies:**
- **Tashkent, Park "Боевой славы" / Jasorat bogi.** Opened May 1975 in front of the Turkestan Military District history museum (renamed the **Museum of the Armed Forces of Uzbekistan** after 1991) with open-access outdoor military equipment **including MiG-15 and MiG-21 airframes**. The "Защитнику Родины" monument was dismantled in November 2009 and the park reconstructed; the new "Клятва Родине" monument opened 12 January 2010. **Every piece of military hardware — the MiG-21 mock-up, the Katyusha, the T-34-85, the BMP-2, the SU-100 and the artillery — was removed and never returned**, despite an ambassadorial statement that they had gone for restoration ahead of the 65th Victory anniversary. This is the site the brief named as `Музей Вооружённых сил Узбекистана`; **it no longer has an outdoor aircraft display.** The museum building itself may still hold indoor material — that is the open question, not the park.

**Considered and rejected as not being displays:**
- **Lin Biao Trident crash site, Batnorov, Khentii, Mongolia** (47.6483/111.1935). OSM tags it `historic=aircraft` + `building=ruins` with a Chinese inscription recording the 13 September 1971 crash of Chinese Air Force Trident No. 256 carrying Lin Biao, Ye Qun and Lin Liguo. Wreckage remains on the steppe. Genuinely significant, and visited — but it is crash debris in situ, not deliberate retention plus public presentation. Excluded under the display/derelict test. **Flagged as a judgment call a curator might reasonably reverse.**
- **Mi-8T wreck at Ardoti, Dusheti Municipality, Georgia** (42.5716/45.1886). OSM names it "Remains of a Mi-8T helicopter of the Chechen Republic of Ichkeria" and attaches six Panoramax image sequences. A wreck in the Khevsureti mountains. Excluded as a wreck.
- **Takhiatash and Kegeyli, Karakalpakstan** — mis-tagged disused airfields, covered under CORRECTIONS.
- **Baku Military Trophy Park.** Opened 12 April 2021; more than 300 exhibits including about 150 pieces of heavy equipment — T-72 tanks, MT-LB tractors, 9K33 Osa and 2K12 Kub systems, and fragments of 9K720 Iskander missiles. **No aircraft is documented in the English Wikipedia inventory, and the Wikimedia Commons category for the park (photographed August 2025) shows only ground vehicles, a BM-21, trenches and plaques.** I therefore assert no airframe and record no site. If a human confirms a fixed-wing or rotary airframe there, it becomes a site; the Iskander fragments would qualify as `missile_rocket` / `ballistic` if they are presented as retained exhibits rather than debris. Left out rather than guessed.

### WELL-SEARCHED ZEROS
- **TAJIKISTAN returned ZERO objects from the OSM sweep** — the only country of the nine with nothing at all in `historic=aircraft` / `memorial=aircraft` / `gate_guardian` / aviation museums. All three Tajik records in this file come from the aviahistory registry alone, and one of those three (Kurgan-Tyube) is scrapped. Dushanbe, Kulyab and Khujand produced nothing beyond that. The registry's Dushanbe monument (December 1998) has no stated address.
- **TURKMENISTAN.** Five airframes at one location — the Turkmenhowayollary aviation training school in Ashgabat — and **nothing anywhere else in the country**, including Mary. No museum, no monument, no gate guardian in OSM; no Commons category; no Russian Wikipedia coverage. This is the "expect very little" outcome the brief anticipated, and it is a real finding rather than a gap in effort: the same query shape that returned 27 objects for Kazakhstan returned five clustered nodes for Turkmenistan.
- **MONGOLIA.** Two objects nationwide. The provincial-centre MiG-21s and An-2s the brief expected **are not in OSM at all**, and Mongolian-language sources were not reachable through the APIs available. Nalaikh, Buyant-Ukhaa and the Zaisan memorial area returned nothing. This is under-searched rather than empty — see NEEDS A HUMAN.
- **A "Tbilisi Aviation Museum" could not be confirmed to exist.** Nothing in OSM, Wikidata, Commons or any Wikipedia. The Su-25 at the Delta plant is real and is recorded; a general aviation museum in Tbilisi is not evidenced by anything I could reach.
- **The Yerevan aviation museum is real but holds NO airframes.** The Armenian Wikipedia article for `Հայկական ավիացիայի պատմության թանգարան` (Museum of the History of Armenian Aviation, founded 1998, in the Civil Aviation Administration building at Zvartnots) describes documents, photographs, uniforms, literature and **"small models of a number of aircraft"**. The brief's expectation of "a real museum with a decent collection" is **not supported** — the actual Armenian airframes are the MiG-19 at Mother Armenia, the Hrazdan Tu-134, the two Yak-40s and the Yenokavan hotel conversion.
- **Erebuni and Gyumri (Armenia), Vaziani (Georgia), Kant beyond the two town monuments, Karshi-Khanabad and Nukus (Uzbekistan), Ganja / Nakhchivan / Kurdamir (Azerbaijan), Taldykorgan and Karaganda (Kazakhstan), Osh and Tokmok beyond what is listed** — all searched, all returned nothing beyond what is recorded above.

### BLANK FIELDS, DELIBERATELY
- **`latitude`/`longitude` blank on every record whose only source is the aviahistory registry.** The registry has "location" links but they are javascript map pop-ups the fetcher cannot resolve, and the prose gives street-level descriptions ("beside the House of Officers", "in the Khlebzavod district"). Inventing a coordinate from a district name is exactly the failure mode the contract warns about. Roughly 20 sites are affected and each needs one map lookup.
- **`year_built` blank wherever the source gave only a registration or delivery date and I judged the gap too large to infer a build year.** Where I did populate it from a delivery date rather than a manufacture date, the description says so explicitly.
- **`tail_number` blank on all 24 Unidentified airframes and on most OSM-only records** — no serial was visible or recorded. Blank, never guessed.
- **`variant` blank on the Aktobe Su-17** (the source says "Су-17" with no suffix, and the M-series suffixes matter), **on the Ashgabat Boeing 737** (no -200/-300/-500 stated) and on most monument fighters.
- **`operator_country` blank** per rule (d) above — 24 records. This is a deliberate decision, not an oversight.
- **`postal_code` blank** where Nominatim returned none, notably in rural Kyrgyzstan, at Baikonur and for all registry-only sites.
- **`website` blank throughout.** None of these sites has a web presence I could verify, and the Tashkent Polytechnic Museum's site (politomuseum.uz) belongs to a museum I did not record for lack of an aircraft.
