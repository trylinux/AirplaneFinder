## NOTES

### Search availability — read this first
**General web search was UNAVAILABLE for this entire task.** The session's shared WebSearch quota was
already exhausted (200 of 200 calls) before I started; my one probe returned the quota error, so **not a
single general web search was performed**. Everything below rests on:

- **English Wikipedia API** (`action=query`, `prop=extracts`, `list=search` with `insource:`) — worked
  perfectly and carried the whole task. The `insource:"Pakistan Air Force Museum"` /
  `insource:"Sri Lanka Air Force Museum"` sweeps were the single highest-yield technique: they surface
  the per-type **survivor lists** (`List of surviving Folland Gnats`, `List of surviving Lockheed F-104
  Starfighters`, `List of displayed Lockheed T-33 Shooting Stars`, `Hawker Siddeley HS 748`, `IAI Kfir`,
  `Boulton Paul Balliol`, `De Havilland Dove`, `Kaman HH-43 Huskie`, `Martin B-57 Canberra`,
  `Vickers VC.1 Viking`, `Auster Autocar`) which is where nearly every **serial** in this file came from.
- **Wikimedia Commons API** — category walks plus `prop=imageinfo&iiprop=extmetadata` to pull the
  free-text photo descriptions. This is what produced the Bangladesh serials and, crucially, their
  **locations and dates**: one Dhaka-based aviation photographer's 2014–2018 captions ("BAF Base ZHR
  2016: Preserved at the Children's park", "Preserved at the Falcon park, BAF MTR", "Preserved at
  Shaheed Flt Lt. Aynul Habib square, BAF BBD") are the entire evidential basis for four Bangladeshi
  base sites that appear in no directory anywhere.
- **Overpass** via the **maps.mail.ru** mirror (overpass-api.de is proxy-blocked here; kumi.systems and
  private.coffee both timed out repeatedly on the first attempt and I did not get a usable response from
  either — **maps.mail.ru was the only mirror that worked**, and even it needs the query POSTed from a
  file with a real browser UA and ~2–3 minutes).
- **Nominatim reverse geocoding** — essential, see the bbox warning below.
- **Wikidata** `wbgetentities` — used to resolve the OSM `model:wikipedia=Q152976` tag at Hingurakgoda
  to *Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-17*.
- **WebFetch** on known URLs — worked for `airforcemuseum.lk` (gave current hours, phone, email and the
  "40+ aircraft" figure) but **failed for `paf.gov.pk`** (`ROBOTS_DISALLOWED`, robots.txt fetch timeout).

### OSM sweep — results and the bbox trap
`historic=aircraft`, `memorial=aircraft`, `memorial:type=aircraft`, `aeroway=gate_guardian` and
`tourism=museum + museum=aviation` were all swept over the four supplied bboxes, then re-swept with
name-regex queries (English plus Urdu `طیارہ`/`جہاز` and Bengali `বিমান`).

**The supplied Pakistan bbox 23.5,60.8,37.1,77.9 is mostly India and Afghanistan.** It returned 53
`historic=aircraft` nodes of which **only six are actually in Pakistan**. Delhi (Palam, IAF Museum area,
Rajpath), Chandigarh, Amritsar, Bikaner, Pilani ("Birla Aircraft") and a nine-node cluster at 34.518/69.196
which is **Kabul, Afghanistan** all fell inside it. I reverse-geocoded every borderline node through
Nominatim. Two that look Pakistani are not: `31.6055,74.5738` and the tagged **MiG-23BN at
31.6345,74.8261** both geocode to **Amritsar district, India** — anyone re-running this sweep will hit
those two and must not record them as Pakistani. I narrowed the eastern edge to 75.5°E for the follow-up
queries.

The Bangladesh bbox is likewise dominated by India: of nine hits, eight are Guwahati, Shillong, Kolkata
and Siliguri. The single node inside Bangladesh (`22.6979,90.3696`, name "plane ground", alt_name "short
pitch") is a **cricket ground mis-tagged `historic=aircraft`** — excluded. The Nepal bbox returned exactly
one node, `26.338,85.088` "British Faram Bazar Airport", which is in **Bihar, India**, not Nepal.

**OSM coverage across South Asia is very thin and an empty result here is NOT evidence of absence.**
Pakistan's flagship museum is a single untagged `tourism=museum` building polygon with none of its ~40
airframes mapped; the Bangladesh Air Force Museum has no aircraft nodes at all; the Sri Lanka Air Force
Museum node carries no `museum=aviation` tag. The six Pakistani monument nodes that do exist are almost
certainly a small fraction of what is on the ground — the PAF distributed retired F-6s, T-37s and Mirages
to town parks, colleges and cantonment roundabouts across Punjab and Sindh for decades and essentially
none of that is in OSM. **Treat Pakistan's monument count in this file as a floor, not a census.**

### Sources and their weight
- **Wikipedia per-type survivor lists carried Pakistan and Sri Lanka.** They are the only place serials
  exist for these two countries. They are also mostly **undated** — that is the single biggest weakness
  in this file and is reflected in the confidence fields.
- **Commons photo captions carried Bangladesh.** Dated, first-hand, and they give location precision no
  directory offers. Ten-year-old evidence, but real.
- **The official SLAF museum site (airforcemuseum.lk) is live and current** but publishes no inventory —
  only "40+ Aircraft". Its hours (Tue–Sun 08:30–16:15, closed Mondays and public holidays) and contact
  (+94 77 2 444 445, museum@airforce.lk) are as of 2026-09-10.
- **No lead-list directory was used.** `aviationmuseum.eu` does not cover Asia and I never reached
  `silverhawkauthor.com`; nothing in this file traces to either.

### Corrections made
1. **Zlín Z 43, not Z 143, at the SLAF Museum.** The brief specified "captured/recovered LTTE light
   aircraft (Zlin Z-143)". The Commons photograph taken at the museum on 2022-09-24 is captioned
   **"Zlin Z 43 of Air Tigers"**, and English Wikipedia's `Zlín Z 43` article explicitly attributes the
   20 February 2009 Colombo suicide raid to Z 43s while the `2009 suicide air raid on Colombo` article says
   the raid aircraft "are believed to be Zlín Z 143". The two designations are being used
   interchangeably in the literature and they are different aircraft. I recorded the displayed airframe as
   **Z 43** (what the photograph of it says) and put **Z 143** in aliases with the discrepancy spelled out
   in the description. Do not silently normalise this to Z-143.
2. **Jinnah's Viking is recorded as J-750, not VP-905.** The brief gave VP-905. Wikipedia's
   `Vickers VC.1 Viking` survivors section gives **"J-750 – Viking 1B on static display at the Pakistan
   Air Force Museum"**. I could not reconcile the two within budget, so `tail_number` is the sourced PAF
   serial J-750 and VP-905 sits in `aliases` with the description saying plainly that it is unconfirmed.
3. **The Gnat at the PAF Museum is IE-1083**, forced down at Pasrur on 3 September 1965 by Flt Lt
   Hakimullah Khan Durrani of No. 9 Squadron. Both the Commons photograph taken at Pasrur that day and
   the surviving-Gnat register agree. Recorded with `operator_country: IN` and the capture documented.
4. **The Bangladesh Air Force Museum is at Agargaon on the *west* side of the Tejgaon runway, not at
   Tejgaon airport itself.** The brief said "Agargaon/Tejgaon". OSM puts it at 23.7790/90.3810 on Begum
   Rokeya Sarani, and a 2016 Commons caption records it as having moved from "No-3 hangar of BAF Base
   Bashar" to the western side of the runway adjacent to Taltola gate. The 1987 foundation and the 2014
   public reopening are two different events at two different places.
5. **`A-5111` is not a serial in the ordinary sense.** The Nanchang A-5III at the BAF Museum is
   photographed as "A-5111" / "A-5111 A", which reads as the type designation A-5 run together with the
   airframe number 111. Recorded exactly as worn with the ambiguity stated.
6. **The "Bomber Aircraft" node at PAF Masroor is left as `Unidentified`.** The only jet bomber the PAF
   flew was the B-57, so that is the obvious answer — and I did not record it, because "obvious" is how
   invented identities get into databases.
7. **`historic=aircraft` on `22.6979,90.3696` in Barisal is a cricket ground**, name "plane ground",
   alt_name "short pitch". Mis-tagged in OSM. Excluded.

### Judgment calls
- **The Fazaia Gujranwala JF-17 is recorded as a replica.** OSM tags it `artwork_type=installation`
  rather than `artwork_type=aircraft` (which the Hala F-7P and Jagatpur Mirage 5 both carry), and no
  JF-17 has been withdrawn to museum display. It is recorded because a purpose-built full-size type
  monument in an air force housing scheme is a real site — but the row says REPLICA in the description.
- **The two Mauripur Road "Airplane" artworks and the Lower Mall Lahore "Airplane" are recorded as
  `Unidentified` sites at low confidence.** `tourism=artwork` can mean a sculpture. I would rather leave
  three checkable leads with an honest "we do not know what this is" than delete them.
- **`operator_country` for the LTTE Zlín is blank.** The Tamil Eelam Air Force was not a state and has no
  ISO 3166-1 code. Blank is the correct answer, not `LK`.
- **Chinese-built airframes are recorded under their Chinese builders** — `Shenyang` for F-6/FT-6/FT-5,
  `Nanchang` for Q-5 and CJ-6, `Chengdu` for F-7 — with `MiG-19`, `MiG-17U`, `J-6`, `J-7` etc. in aliases.
  `operator_country` is the national air force that flew them (`PK`, `BD`, `LK`), never `CN`, exactly as
  the brief requires.
- **Indian airframes displayed in Pakistan and Bangladesh carry `operator_country: IN`**: the Gnat
  IE-1083 and the MiG-21 Bison wreck at the PAF Museum (captured/downed), and the Hunter, HAL Chetak
  Z1829, Mil Mi-4 Z2401 and Gnat/Ajeet in Bangladesh (**gifted** by India, not captured — the
  descriptions say which, because conflating the two would be a factual and political error).
- **The MiG-21 Bison at the PAF Museum is a wreck display**, fuselage and tail sections only, in the
  "Operation Swift Retort" gallery. Recorded as a row because it is deliberately retained and publicly
  presented, with the description saying it is not a complete airframe.
- **The PAF Museum MiG-15's `operator_country` is blank.** The PAF never operated the MiG-15. It is
  either a captured airframe or a Chinese-built J-2/JJ-2 and I could not establish which.
- **The A-26 Invader at the PAF Museum is recorded with a stated contradiction.** The museum's Wikipedia
  exhibit list includes it; Wikipedia's `List of surviving Douglas A-26 Invaders` has no Pakistan entry
  at all. Both facts are in the description and `operator_country` is blank. This is exactly the kind of
  row a later pass should verify with a photograph.
- **Balliol CA303 is `in_storage`**, per the source wording; CA310 at Diyatalawa is `on_display`.
- **`access_type` for base displays is `restricted`** (Masroor, Nur Khan, Bashar, Zahurul Haque, Matiur
  Rahman, Katunayake, Diyatalawa) — a member of the public gets in only through a military gate.
  **Hingurakgoda's MiG-17 is `public`** because the node is on the public airport road, and **Koggala's
  HS 748 is `public`** because OSM records a 20 LKR admission charge. Judged by how a visitor actually
  gets in, not by who owns the ground.
- **Two Jet Provost T51s and two Shenyang FT-5s at the SLAF Museum are recorded as two rows each**, with
  blank tail numbers, because the sources say "two" without serials. Two blank-serial rows is the honest
  representation; one row would undercount and invented serials would be worse.

### Blank fields, deliberately
- **Coordinates are blank for eleven sites** (PAF Nur Khan; BAF Bashar; BAF Information Centre; BAF
  Zahurul Haque; BAF Matiur Rahman; National Science Museum Dhaka; SLAF Colombo; SLAF Diyatalawa;
  Aviation Museum Nepal). In every case the site is real and sourced but I have no verified point, and
  most are inside closed military bases. A guessed pin inside a base is worse than no pin.
- **`year_built` is blank on 104 of 105 rows.** The only populated value is 1968 on HS 748 CR-833, taken
  from an OSM `start_date` tag on an aircraft node. No serial anywhere in this file was converted into a
  year — the F-104 `56-798`, T-33 `53-5259`, B-57 `53-3957`, HH-43 `62-4556` and C-47 `43-48203` all
  carry US fiscal-year *order* prefixes and none of them is a build date.
- **`tail_number` is blank on most Pakistani and Sri Lankan museum rows** because the sources list types,
  not serials. Blank is correct; the airframes are real.
- **`postal_code` is blank almost everywhere** — South Asian postal codes are not carried by any source I
  used except where Nominatim volunteered one.
- **`website` is blank for every site but three** (PAF Museum's parent service site, Army Museum Lahore,
  SLAF Museum). The PAF Museum's own site could not be fetched (robots).
- **`operator_country` is blank on eight rows**: the six Unidentified monument airframes, the PAF Museum
  MiG-15, the PAF Museum A-26, and the LTTE Zlín — each for the reason stated in its description.
