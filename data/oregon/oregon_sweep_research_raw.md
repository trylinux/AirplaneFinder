# 3. NOTES

## Sources and their weight

- **The live FAA releasable database, downloaded 9 September 2026 (file dated 8 September 2026).** This carried the most weight and did most of the work. It supplied 160 confirmed WAAAM airframes, 29 Erickson airframes, the Tillamook civil fleet, the Cottage Grove and Hillsboro collections, and legitimate `year_built` values from `YEAR-MFR`. The `DEREG` file was equally valuable: cancellation *with the museum as last registrant* is a positive presence signal, not a negative one.
- **The NMUSAF "Aircraft on Loan by Location" list.** Authoritative on custody of the USAF-owned civic loans, and the single most productive lead source for Oregon monuments — it yielded Baker City, Vale, Ontario, Medford, Clackamas, and both Guard bases. It is undated in circulation and treated here as ~2016.
- **Aerial Visuals per-airframe dossiers.** Used to production-block-check every military serial and to date the last sighting. Its "unreviewed imported data" flag proved a useful reliability signal (see the F-86 correction below).
- **Wikipedia.** The *Tillamook Air Museum* and *Western Antique Aeroplane & Automobile Museum* articles are unusually well maintained — the Tillamook article carries cited edits through August 2026 and was a genuine currency source, not just a lead. The *survivor* lists were error-prone as expected.
- **Primary press** — NAVAIR, KGW, KATU, Port of Tillamook Bay, General Aviation News, Herald & News, DVIDS — used for anything acquired or moved after 2020.

### Sources that proved stale, specifically

- **Wikipedia's `Oregon Air and Space Museum` collection list** is sourced entirely to Skaarup's *Oregon Warbird Survivors 2003*. It still lists a Globe Swift, Nieuport 17, Rutan Quickie, Mitchell Wing B-10 and Smith Termite. The museum's own current sitemap has pages for only five aircraft. I recorded only what the museum's own current pages plus the FAA deregistration trail support, and left the 2003 list out.
- **Wikipedia's `Classic Aircraft Aviation Museum` article** states "Tillamook air museum is Permanently closed as of 5/21/2026." That was true of the Port board's repair vote but has been overtaken: FEMA funding arrived in August 2026 and a sale to the Vintage Aviation Museum is in progress.
- **AeroCorner's "8 Aviation Museums in Oregon"** lists a Supermarine Spitfire T.8 and a "Vanek Gyrocopter" at Tillamook. Neither appears in the museum's own list or in Wikipedia's; the Spitfire is almost certainly an Erickson-era holding that left in 2014. AeroCorner also places a Rutan Long-EZ at OMSI. All three treated as leads only and excluded.
- **Wikipedia's A-7 survivor list** prints `158319` in the serial field for the Tillamook aircraft while its own cited source and the Aerial Visuals dossier both read **158819**. Corrected.

## Corrections made, with evidence

1. **Tillamook's MiG-17 / Lim-6bis has left the state.** Wikipedia lists a PZL-Mielec Lim-6bis at Tillamook, citing an Aerial Visuals dossier for c/n 1F 03-19, registration **N2503N**. The live FAA registry shows N2503N (serial 1F0319, "Poland MiG17 LIM 6") registered valid to **MM 37 LLC, Moses Lake, Washington**. A registration naming an out-of-state owner is strong evidence against presence. **Excluded** — this is the clearest stale-data catch in the state.
2. **The Erickson B-17 is a B-17G, not an F.** Every secondary list calls it a B-17F. The FAA registers N3701G as a **B-17G, serial 44-8543**. It is painted as *Ye Olde Pub*, a B-17F. Recorded as G with the paint scheme in the description.
3. **Erickson's Mustang identity is unresolved and I did not force it.** Wikipedia and Aerial Visuals both describe a CAC CA-17 Mustang Mk 20, c/n 1364, ex-RAAF A68-13, registered N551D. The FAA registers N551D as a **North American/Aero Classics P-51D, serial 44-14826, built 1944**. These cannot both be the airframe's identity. Recorded as the FAA gives it, with both identities in aliases and the conflict stated.
4. **F-86F 52-4755 stays at Clackamas.** Aerial Visuals shows it moved on to the Oregon Air and Space Museum in Eugene — but that dossier is explicitly flagged "based on unreviewed imported data," OASM's own current site has no F-86 page, and a March 2020 DVIDS report has the jet being reassembled at the Oregon Military Museum. I kept it at Clackamas and noted that it wears **12892**, which is not its serial.
5. **Ontario's Phantom is an F-4D, not an F-4C.** Wikipedia's F-4 list has it under an ambiguous heading; both the NMUSAF loan list and the Aerial Visuals dossier (F-4D-29-MC, c/n 1932) give **F-4D**.
6. **Tillamook's A-4B is no longer the gate guard.** It was dismounted from its roadside pole in September 2022 and replaced with a metal scale blimp model. The museum still lists it, so it is recorded, with the dismount in the description.

## Production-block checks

Every military serial recorded was checked against its block via Aerial Visuals: 53-0781 → F-86D-55-NA c/n 201-225 ✓; 66-0266 → F-4D-29-MC c/n 1932 ✓; 81-0759 → F-16A Block 15 ADF c/n 61-440 ✓; 51-6653 → T-33 c/n 580-5985 ✓; 52-4755 → F-86F ✓; 58-0301 → F-101B ✓; 63-7679 and 63-7479 → F-4C ✓; 82-0910 → F-16A ✓. A-4C 148491 falls inside the A4D-2N block 148435–148612, which independently corroborates the museum's own claim that it is "number 337 of 638 A4D-2N built." AV-8B 165002 is from the NAVAIR primary release. The two **F-15A serials (76-0009, 76-0066) could not be block-checked** — Aerial Visuals has no dossier for either, and searching those numbers returns Sikorsky S-76 helicopters whose construction numbers collide. Both fall inside the FY1976 F-15A range and rest on the loan list alone.

## Judgment calls

- **Tillamook is recorded `public` but it is not open.** It closed in December 2025 when a 170×30 ft section of the Hangar B roof folded over in a windstorm; repairs are estimated at $20M+; the director resigned in May 2026 and the Port board voted against repairs the next day; FEMA money arrived in August 2026; and on 20 August 2026 the Port approved a term sheet to lease and eventually sell Hangar B to the Vintage Aviation Museum, a Utah nonprofit that plans to move most of *its* collection in. OpenStreetMap currently labels the site "Tillamook Air Museum (Temporarily Closed)." The vocabulary has no closed state, so I used its normal access model and flagged the closure here and in the site record. **Its collection may change substantially again within a year.**
- Erickson's aircraft are airworthy and flown; recorded `on_display` per convention.
- Reproductions and replicas (Fw 190 A-8, Ki-43, Bleriot XI, Sopwith Pup, Nieuport 11 and 17, A6M2, Dormoy Bathtub, Heath Super Parasol, both Curtiss pushers) are recorded with the reproduction status in the description, not hidden.
- Gliders take `role_type` `other`, there being no glider term in the vocabulary.
- The Tillamook F-14A is `under_restoration` — the museum began fundraising for its restoration in 2025.
- Fuselage- and cockpit-only exhibits (B-52G 59-2579, Convair 880, Boeing 727, F-4N) are recorded as records with the partial nature in the description.
- Classic Aircraft Aviation Museum is `appointment`: free, but by appointment or a three-hour Tuesday-morning window. Its Corvallis holdings are a **separate site record** because they sit at a different airport under a different operator (Corvallis Aero Service).
- Both Guard bases are `restricted` — the displays are inside the wire, reachable by the public only at open-house events such as Sentry Eagle.

## Excluded, and why

- **Evergreen Aviation & Space Museum, McMinnville** — already recorded, per instruction. (Its NMUSAF loans were nevertheless read to keep them from being duplicated elsewhere.)
- **Tillamook's Lim-6bis / MiG-17F** — FAA shows N2503N now owned in Moses Lake, WA. See correction 1.
- **Tillamook's HU-25 Guardian.** Announced May 2025, delivered to the USCG 28 February 1982, last on static display at Coast Guard Station Astoria, to be moved slung under a CH-53K on a date "to be announced." No arrival was ever reported, and the hangar has been open to the weather since December 2025. **Pre-shipment press is not arrival** — excluded, with no serial published anywhere I could find.
- **Erickson's Cessna 525C N464EG** — an operational Erickson Aviation business jet at the same holding address, not a collection piece.
- **Camp Rilea, Warrenton** — no aircraft display found in any source. Camp Rilea Heliport is operational.
- **Pendleton Air Museum** — real and open (21 SW Emigrant Ave, downtown, Mon/Wed–Sat), but it is an artifacts-and-history museum about the WWII B-25 training base. No airframe could be confirmed on site, so no site record is offered. See open questions.
- **North East Oregon Aviation Foundation, Joseph** (N2022R, Fisher Classic, 2004) and **Soaring by the Sea Foundation, Eugene** — foundations holding flyable aircraft, no evidence of a public display collection.
- **OMSI, Portland** — a Long-EZ is claimed by one unreliable aggregator only; nothing on OMSI's own site or in the Oregon Encyclopedia entry.
- **Independence, Oregon** — the ~30 "TRUSTEE" registrations there are Independence State Airport residential airpark homeowners, not a museum.
- **Grokipedia** returned a result for the Oregon Air and Space Museum in one search. Not opened, not used.

## Deliberate blanks

- `year_built` is blank wherever only a fiscal-year serial prefix was available. Not one military `year_built` in this package is populated, because no acceptance or delivery date was sourced for any of them. That is deliberate, not an oversight.
- `year_built` is also blanked for six WAAAM aircraft whose FAA `YEAR-MFR` contradicts the type's production run (Aeronca K 1945, Waco YPF 1972, Swallow TP 1917) or reads `0000` (Stinson L-5 N54921, Pietenpol Sky Scout N1933A). The Erickson P2V-7's 1963 is *recorded* but flagged as doubtful.
- `tail_number` is blank for ~15 Tillamook airframes, the OASM A-6E and T-34B, three Hillsboro jets, both Corvallis jets, the Clackamas O-1A, the Erickson P-39 (which carries no civil registration at all), and the Klamath Falls park F-15. In every case I could establish presence but not identity.
- Coordinates are blank nowhere, but Baker City and Canby are town centroids and are flagged as such.
