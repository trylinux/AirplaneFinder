# Texas — build notes

Texas is being built in three phases by category. **Phases 1 and 2 are done:**
public civilian museums, then military base air parks. Phase 3 is the park and
monument sweep.

**Texas total so far: 41 museums, 515 aircraft** — 27 public, 2 appointment,
12 restricted.

## Museums that have CLOSED or DISPERSED — excluded, and time-sensitive

- **1940 Air Terminal Museum, Houston — CEASED OPERATIONS 2 MARCH 2026.** Its
  own site: *"The museum has ceased operations at this time… liquidating
  non-core assets and working to pay off debts."* Financial collapse, not a
  move. The NRHP-listed building may pass to the Texas Historical Commission.
  **The fate of its Lodestar and Sikorsky S-58 is unresolved and they are
  actively at risk.** Worth re-checking within months, not years.
- **Cavanaugh Flight Museum, Addison — CLOSED January 2024** after 31 years.
  The whole collection moved to North Texas Regional Airport, Sherman/Denison,
  and the new site still says *"this location is not open to the public."*
  Held F-4 64-0777, F-105 63-8343, F-104s 56-0780 and 56-0786, A-26C 44-35710,
  an F-86. Not recorded — no public access. Revisit if it reopens.
- **Cold War Air Museum, Lancaster** — about 22 Soviet-bloc airframes including
  two Mi-24 Hinds, a MiG-21 and seven L-29s. Wikipedia now uses the past tense
  and the website is dead. **Dispersal destination not established** — worth
  a dedicated trace, since two flyable Hinds do not vanish quietly.
- **Pate Museum of Transportation, Cresson — closed 24 December 2009**, 43
  vehicles auctioned 2010, aircraft dispersed individually. **Pate is the
  common ancestor of a surprising share of surviving Texas collections**:
  HAMM's T-33, F-105D and F-4D, HAMM's F-101, Caprock's F9F-8, and Goodfellow
  AFB's EC-47Q all came from Pate.
- Also confirmed closed and still directory-listed: **Hangar 10 Flying Museum**
  (Denton), **Flight of the Phoenix** (Gilmer), **The Pierce Museum**
  (Corsicana), **Rattlesnake Bomber Base Museum** (Pyote), **Edward H. White II
  Memorial Museum** (Brooks, San Antonio).

## Ownership is the structural story here

Texas collections are unusually loan-heavy, and two are almost entirely so.

- **The USS Lexington owns nothing.** All 19 aircraft are National Naval
  Aviation Museum loans — the museum says so, and it is independently
  confirmed by the lapsed FAA registrations of its N3N and Howard GH-3, both
  of which show NNAM as last registrant.
- **The Vintage Flying Museum legally owns exactly one aircraft** — Beech E18S
  N13JC, the "Hollywood Bomber", once owned by Jackie Cochran and later Merv
  Griffin. Everything else in that hangar is private, CAF, Smithsonian, or on
  loan. Its F-86F 52-4689 belongs to the **Smithsonian**.
- **Fort Worth Aviation Museum** holds 12 NNAM loans plus GSA and NMUSAF loans;
  it owns comparatively few outright.
- **Mid America is the opposite** — 61 FAA registrations across six registrant
  identities, all Scott Glover: the museum corporation, three spellings of his
  own name, Pleasant Aviation LLC, 250 Logistics LLC, Next Level Marine LLC.
  One collection, six names. Overwhelmingly airworthy.
- **Frontiers of Flight** owns only two of its aircraft outright; the Apollo 7
  command module and the V-173 Flying Pancake are Smithsonian loans, and the
  RF-8G is an NNAM loan.

## Corrections to what the sources say

- **The CAF's Tippie National Aviation Education Center is built and open** —
  a 47,000 sq ft facility that opened November 2021, not a fundraising project.
  ZIP is 75237, not 75232.
- **B-29 *FIFI* and B-24 *Diamond Lil* are at Dallas Executive, not the Vintage
  Flying Museum.** Wikipedia still associates them with Fort Worth. The CAF
  **Invader Squadron and its A-26B *Night Mission* do remain** at VFM.
- ***Columbine II* is NOT at Mid America.** Eisenhower's Constellation
  (VC-121A 48-0610, N9463) was bought by Karl Stoltzfus of Dynamic Aviation and
  moved to Bridgewater, Virginia in March 2016. Mid America collaborated on the
  restoration; it never owned the aircraft. N9463 appears nowhere in the FAA
  registry under any Glover entity.
- **Frontiers of Flight's "40+ air and space vehicles" is about 28 aircraft.**
  The higher figure counts a 737-200 nose section, two replicas, a 1/5-scale
  Vimy, a large-scale B-17 model, 200+ display models, and a **DFW Airtrans
  people-mover car**. Note the museum has taken its per-aircraft pages offline
  entirely, so that collection carries lower confidence than the others.
- **Fort Worth's "over 36" is about 28 complete airframes** — the rest are an
  A-12 full-scale mockup, an OV-10 factory engineering mockup with wooden
  tires, a composite O-2A, an incomplete BT-13, two OH-58 parts hulks, and an
  **O-11A crash fire truck** listed on the aircraft page.
- **Pioneer Flight Museum has 12 aircraft, not 28** — the higher count includes
  vehicles, engines and non-museum aircraft based at the field.
- **HAMM's "F-111E" is an F-111A, 67-0051.** Baugher records it as an F-111A
  that became a GF-111A ground trainer at Sheppard. The actual F-111E 67-0050
  left for Tucson in 2012 and was scrapped.
- **The Lexington's AH-1 is 77-22754, not "AH-1S 76-22574"** — that serial does
  not exist in the FY1976 list. FAA DEREG confirms Bell AH-1P 77-22754, ex-N4144.
- **The Lexington has one F-14, 160694.** The 160414 that some lists place there
  crashed off Virginia on 23 March 1995.
- **Silent Wings has about 10 airframes, not 2–3**; the **National WASP Museum
  has 5, not 2–3**; **Perrin has 2, not 4**; **Highland Lakes has 8, not 4**.
- **No. 1 BFTS Museum (Terrell) has no airframe at all** — it is an artifact and
  archive museum. Recorded with zero aircraft, which is the finding.

## Data-quality warnings for later phases

- **Do not use aviationmuseum.eu civil registrations without checking.** Spot
  checks resolved two of its Amarillo N-numbers to a Cessna 177B in Illinois
  and a cancelled reservation. Its Midland page is a pre-2015 CAF-HQ snapshot.
  Its military serial and marking data is sounder.
- **Grokipedia's Fort Worth page contradicts both Wikipedia and the museum on
  six airframes.** Do not use it.
- **silverhawkauthor is a cumulative photo log, not a roster** — for Fort Worth
  it lists an F-86H, F-8H, UH-34, AV-8B and YOV-10A the museum does not have.
- **Reused N-numbers are a live trap.** Fort Worth's O-2A once wore N37542,
  which is now on a 2022 Boeing 737-9. Always match serial plus type, never an
  N-number alone. Others confirmed reassigned: N4144, N1040Z, N31SA, N56547,
  N152RA, N551JC.

## Four base-adjacent sites are genuinely PUBLIC

The most useful finding of this phase. These are not behind a gate:

- **Texas Military Forces Museum, Camp Mabry, Austin** — free, open seven days
  10:00–16:00, outdoor exhibits dawn to dusk. Adults need photo ID at the gate
  and **must enter by 15:00** when the Visitor Control Center closes. Foreign
  passports without a US visa do not grant entry. 13 airframes — more than
  double what the directories list.
- **National Mounted Warrior Museum, Fort Cavazos** — opened 19 September 2024
  on 17 acres **outside the main gate**. No visitor pass, no government ID, no
  manned security gate, free parking. One aircraft indoors.
- **Dyess Military Memorial Park, Abilene** — see the F-4 finding below.
- **Lackland OTS Parade Ground** — its displays are reached by following signs
  from I-410 Exit 4 and are visible from outside the fence.

## Fort Cavazos — the air park was broken up, and nobody says where it went

The 1st Cavalry Division Museum's 8 outdoor airframes did **not** move to the
new National Mounted Warrior Museum. **Department of the Army cancelled the
move on cost grounds.** A museum docent's account: *"the original plan was to
move the outdoor park to the new museum, but that plan was cancelled by the
Dept. of the Army... Some of the original items were moved to be displayed in
front of various unit HQs around Ft. Cavazos, others went to places unknown."*

The eight — AH-1S 68-17113 and 68-17147, OH-13E 51-13993, OH-58C 69-16369,
UH-1A 59-1625, UH-1H 73-21839, OV-1C 60-3747, OH-6A 65-12920, CH-54A 66-18409
— are recorded nowhere. Not entered. Aerial Visuals has silently re-tagged the
OV-1C to the new museum, but that is a custodian rename, not evidence of a
physical move: the old park sits about 4 km away inside the base.

**This is the highest-value open item in Texas.** The Army Center of Military
History tracks macro-artifact transfers and could resolve it.

## Two corrections that remove sites from Texas entirely

- **The US Army Air Defense Artillery Museum is in Oklahoma, not Texas.** It
  moved to Fort Sill under BRAC in 2009 and reopened there in 2013. Sources
  placing it at "Building 500, Pleasanton Road, El Paso" are pre-2009. Its
  Firebee, Hawk, Nike Ajax and Nike Hercules went with it. Only the **V-2** in
  Fort Bliss's Combined Arms Heritage Park is confirmed still in El Paso.
- **The 3rd Cavalry Regiment Museum at Fort Bliss is defunct.** The regiment
  was at Bliss only c.2011–2016; its museum was folded into the Fort Cavazos
  museum in 2024. The surviving directory entry gives ZIP **76544 — Fort
  Hood's, not Fort Bliss's 79916** — which is direct evidence of a corrupt
  Hood/Bliss conflation. Its claimed H-19 and H-34 are not there. (An H-34
  *does* exist in Texas, serial 71726 at Camp Mabry, which is the likely
  origin of the confusion.)

**Also lifted:** the Army museum-closure programme that threatened Fort Bliss
was **paused** — Congress inserted NDAA language requiring formal museum
systems and proof of artifact disposition before any closure. Both Fort Bliss
museums remain open.

## The Dyess F-4 moved off base

**F-4D 65-0796 is not in the Linear Air Park.** It moved about 100 yards in
February 2022 into the new **Dyess Military Memorial Park**, beside the
relocated elementary school, **adjacent to but outside the base perimeter**,
on loan to the City of Abilene. So Abilene has a publicly accessible F-4 —
and it is the aircraft credited with the **last official USAF F-4 MiG-21 kill
in Southeast Asia, January 1973**. Recorded as its own public site.

## Structural findings

- **The Kelly Field Heritage Foundation is a virtual exhibit.** Its own site
  describes it as *"a virtual exhibit... featuring photos and history."* There
  is no staffed museum building. The airframes attributed to it are outdoor
  gate guards and unit-area displays scattered across the annex and Port San
  Antonio. The B-58 *Firefly II* and the FB-111A sit on the **Port San Antonio
  commercial campus with public roads** and are probably viewable without base
  access — worth splitting out if that is confirmed.
- **JBSA-Randolph does have a display collection** — about 10 airframes, a
  coherent trainer-type retrospective. The 1958 consolidation of its museum
  into Lackland's explains why there is no building, but not an absence of
  aircraft. Its planned Heritage Park was never funded and holds nothing.
- **The Lackland off-base museum still has not been built.** The Airman
  Heritage Foundation's own page shows only pre-construction milestones. The
  2013 MOU targeted 2017, then 2022. Access classification unchanged.
- **The Southeast Asia Airpark no longer exists** — nine aircraft moved to the
  Parade Ground in 2020 for new Airman Training Complexes. Any source
  describing it as a separate site is stale.

# Phase 3 — parks, monuments and civic displays

24 museums, 38 aircraft. These are the sites that are not museums in any
ordinary sense: veterans-post lawns, city parks, airport entrances, two school
grounds and one state residential campus. They are recorded because the
airframe is the point of the site and is publicly viewable, which is the same
test Phase 1 applied to the park monuments.

## Sources and their weight

The spine of this phase is the **NMUSAF "Aircraft on Loan (by Location)" list**,
which names the host organisation for a USAF-owned airframe. It is
authoritative on custody and useless on currency — the copy in circulation is
dated April 2016. Against it were run **Joe Baugher's serial pages** (which
carry disposition notes, often more recent), the **Aerial Visuals** airframe and
location dossiers (which carry per-airframe display coordinates and dated
photographs), **ABPic** photographer logs, and where they existed, historical
markers and local news.

The strongest currency evidence in this phase is a single documented 2024 survey
trip through Texas by one photographer, John Bennett, which independently dates
**Port Arthur (11 May 2024)**, **Smithville (9 May 2024)** and **Dumas / Window
on the Plains (29 April 2024)**. Amarillo has an April 2024 photograph and a
2022 on-site report; Manchaca a May 2024 photograph.

## Corrections made to the incoming research

- **Brenham's F-111A is 66-0012, not 67-0012.** 67-0012 is a C-141B. Baugher's
  FY66 list ties MSN A1-30 to Brenham explicitly. The wrong serial is on the
  on-site marker and propagates from there; the airframe also wears the false
  serial **67-0068**, which is the aircraft the marker actually commemorates.
- **Hallettsville is VVA Chapter 854, not 824.** Only Waymarking says 824; VVA's
  own directory, the chamber listing and Aerial Visuals all say 854.
- **Dumas is American Legion Post 224, not 244** (Baugher and the Texas American
  Legion directory).
- **Smithville's airframe is a UH-1V, not an AH-1.** It is a Huey, not a Cobra —
  built as a UH-1H and converted to the aeromedical configuration.
- **Amarillo's QF-100D is not at the Texas Air and Space Museum.** The museum's
  own page says so: it holds the model, the aircraft is at the Texas Panhandle
  War Memorial a few miles away. Recorded as a separate site.
- **Big Spring's memorial airframes are not at Hangar 25.** The Vietnam Memorial
  of Big Spring is on the old Webb AFB grounds, about 1.5 miles from the airport
  museum. Recorded as a separate site.
- **Amarillo's "QF365" is a marking, not a serial.** The serial is 56-3046;
  QF365 is the 1989 drone-conversion marking.
- **Dumas has two aircraft at two sites**, 0.9 miles apart on the same street:
  the RF-4C at Post 224 (901 S Dumas Ave) and the F-84F at the Window on the
  Plains Museum (1820 S Dumas Ave). Post 224 restored and is custodian of both,
  which is what produced the conflicting single-site reports.
- **Huntsville holds five airframes, not two** — the two Hueys plus an AH-1P, an
  OH-58 and a GF-16A.
- **The Mi-24D at Lancaster Regional is not the CAF's.** It belongs to the Cold
  War Air Museum, a separate operator on the same field, and is not recorded
  under the CAF wing.

## Excluded, and why

- **Science Spectrum, Lubbock** — the "aircraft" is most likely a model, and it
  sits inside a paid-admission science centre. Not a display airframe.
- **Granbury, U.S. Veterans Museum** — the host closed in early 2014 and merged
  into the Mineral Wells veterans museum; TripAdvisor and Yelp both carry closed
  banners. C-119F 51-2675 was last documented there in 2019, **dismantled**, on
  private ground. Not publicly viewable and not confirmed present.
- **Port Lavaca, VFW Post 4403 AH-1** — four independent negatives: no Aerial
  Visuals location record for Port Lavaca at all, no Texas entry in Wikipedia's
  AH-1 display list outside Austin and Corpus Christi, and the city's own
  veterans memorial is a paver memorial with no aircraft. The HMdb "Bell AH-1
  Cobra, a War Memorial" marker that seeded this report is in **Troy, Alabama**.
- **Alvord T-33A 53-5346** — absent from both master lists. The nearest match is
  a Bowie, TX entry describing several T-33s in outside storage, possibly
  disassembled and possibly not publicly accessible. A storage yard is not a
  display site. Note the report claims it wears false serial 52-9658 — no row
  should ever be created under that number.
- **Odessa / Midland C-97 and B-57** — the strings C-97, KC-97, B-57, Canberra
  and Stratofreighter appear nowhere in Aerial Visuals' type-itemised Texas
  listing. Most likely a garbled memory of the CAF collection that left Midland
  for Dallas.
- **Austin-Bergstrom RF-101C** — went to Maxwell AFB, Alabama by 1989.
- **NAS Dallas F-4N** and **Flying Tiger Airport, Paris** — refuted; no airframe
  found at either.
- **Lago Vista Airpower Museum** — defunct. Its RF-4C 64-1000 survives at the
  entrance to Rusty Allen Airport and is recorded under the airport, which is
  city-owned; the museum itself is not recorded.

## Access

All Phase 3 sites are `public` except **Lubbock State Supported Living Center**,
which is `restricted` — it is a gated state residential facility, not a park.
Whether T-33A 52-9202 is visible from N University Avenue is unresolved; if it
is, the record should move to `public`.

Two school sites — **San Angelo Central High School** and **Del Valle High
School** — are recorded `public` because both airframes stand on open grounds
visible from the street. If the project later decides active school campuses are
categorically restricted, these two move together.

The **CAF Dallas/Fort Worth Wing** is an operating wing, not a static park. Its
five aircraft are recorded `on_display`, which is the closest the enum gets;
three of the five are airworthy and two have no established registration.

## Blank fields, deliberately

`year_built` is populated only where a first-flight, completion or delivery date
was independently sourced — Bastrop (1967), Manchaca (1965), Pasadena (1966),
Brownwood's F-4E (1968), Henderson (1951, from the on-site historical marker),
Amarillo's F-100 (April 1957 completion), Lago Vista (12 December 1964 first
flight) and the two 1941 CAF trainers from the FAA registry. Everywhere else it
is blank: an FY serial prefix is not a build year, and "circa 1968" is not a
date.

Two CAF airframes carry no tail number. The L-5 "Miss Ziggy" flew as N5789,
which is deregistered; the T-41's registration is not published anywhere
reachable. Both are distinguishable by name or by aliases, so neither trips the
untailed-row rule.

## Needs a human on site

- **Plainview, T-33A 51-6753** — the weakest currency in the phase. Newest dated
  evidence is 1989 photography plus the 2016 loan list. Nothing says it was
  removed; nothing says it is still standing.
- **Brownwood, F-111A 67-0046 and GF-4E 67-0249** — newest dated evidence is a
  January 2015 photograph. Both are ground-instructional hulks, the class most
  often quietly scrapped.
- **Odessa, T-33A 52-9233** — the jet is on a pole at Schlemeyer Field, seven
  miles from the Legion post's own hall, and the coordinates recorded here are
  Aerial Visuals' rather than a confirmed fix. One satellite pass settles it.
- **Both Dumas coordinate pairs** are street-address geocodes, not fixes on the
  airframes. OSM also labels the building at 901 S Dumas Ave as VFW Post 9022,
  which is either a shared hall or a stale tag.
- **Sweetwater, Newman Park** — two airframe coordinates sit 16 m apart and it
  is not established which belongs to the Huey and which to the T-33. Both rows
  carry the site coordinate.
- **Huntsville's F-16** — Baugher's FY81 list is internally inconsistent, giving
  81-0788 at H.E.A.R.T.S. Texas and 81-0789 at a "H.E.A.R.T.S Vet Complex" in
  Alabama. The Alabama entry is wrong; 81-0789 is recorded on the strength of a
  2017 photograph, with 81-0788 noted in aliases.
- **Huntsville's OH-58** is catalogued as both an OH-58A and an OH-58C under the
  same registration N60MP.
- **Midland's Huey serial.** 73-21676 comes from a single Aerial Visuals
  location dossier; the memorial's own site does not name it.
- **San Angelo's UH-1 65-9889 build year** — a local news article says 1965,
  which is also its serial prefix. Left blank rather than launder the serial.
